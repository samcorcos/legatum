# Factions: Egypt, Rome and the new-faction search (2026-11-15 to 2026-11-22)

Egypt's Nile redesign and UI, Egypt and Rome approval, the new-faction proposals and the test that picked them, the symmetry ruling, and Rome's veteran founding.

Moved verbatim from SKILL.md on 2026-11-22 (original order kept). The current rules are in SKILL.md; where this file disagrees with SKILL.md, SKILL.md wins.

## Egypt redesigned around Food: the Nile (user, 2026-11-21)
He found the increment-1 rule confusing ("so they just use food for everything? im confused") and asked for "a more aggressive rule break. Maybe only food. double food yield, no production. food surplus is your production (and no growth when building). then maybe a special action in city (like germany's) that lets you focus on growing the city instead of production". He turned down the lead's softer "+50% Food, −25% Production" as not aggressive enough. Built as `FactionRule::EgyptNile` (rules 48; the old rule stays reachable with `--rule-off egypt_nile` for comparison): Food ×2, Production 0, surplus Food → build progress for every item (1:1 as he specified first measured 30% of eight-seat Hard games and he chose 85% (14.5%), but those runs had the AI reading a panel that applied the Nile twice; after that fix 1:1 measured 17.5%, tied top, and 85% 10.5%, even being 12.5%, and he went back to 1:1 the same day: he prefers the simple rule when it lands near the top of the spread rather than above it), the Grow project (a Germany-style never-ending project) to grow. Settled with reasoning rather than a cost: switching to Grow is free, because the Food-to-progress trade is linear and alternating only splits the surplus. Lessons: one resource and one switch read instantly; when a faction loses a yield entirely, remove the buildings and say so (the Workshop line is unavailable) instead of leaving dead options. This breaks the 11-15 test "does not replace an economy" by his explicit choice. Sim comparison: `docs/sim-results/egypt-nile-2026-11-21.md`.

**Egypt doesn't see Production (user, 2026-11-22).** Playing Egypt he asked whether it is "relevant to see all the production things if production is not relevant". Chosen over "turn tile Production into Food" (a rule change that would make Egypt stronger and need new sims): hide Production for Egypt everywhere (map tile icons, tile and improvement yields, build cards) and don't offer Quarries, which would do nothing; Mines stay for strategic supply. Lesson: when a faction loses a yield, the UI must drop it too, not only the rules.

## New-faction proposals from the same search (2026-11-15; Egypt and Rome chosen, the rest not)
He asked for five new factions, rule break first. Seven Astra rounds (generate, critic, revise; `.reviews/astra-new-factions-r1.md` to `-r7-landbuy.md`) ended with two the critics call excellent and no set of five:
- **Egypt** (buildings are paid with surplus Food, 1 Food = 2 progress, instead of growth; no Production or Gold for them) and **Rome** (a full-health melee veteran becomes a city for the Settler price in Gold; no Settlers). Sheets at the end of `-r4-critic.md`.
- Good, with open problems: **United States "Public Lands"** (one land purchase a turn for the whole empire; `-r7-landbuy.md`; note he removed tile buying from the base game as "too in the weeds and i basically never do it"), **Prussia** (upgrades through the production queue, the unit immobile while it refits; `-r6-critic.md`).
- Incomplete: **Hussite Bohemia** (ranged units keep fortification when firing but must start fortified to shoot; needs a goal and a pace check).
- Cut, with reasons in the critic reports: Inca, Achaemenid Persia, Portugal, Russia, Spain, Venice, Revolutionary France.
**What passed and why (use this as the test for any new faction):** the rule break lives inside an action the player already takes every few turns; the cost is the thing itself, so no throwaway unit can pay it and no anti-exploit patch is needed; one sentence states the trade; it is not passive; it does not replace an economy or delete geography.

## Symmetric factions: a cost, a unit and a building for every one, all painted (user, 2026-11-15)
On the redesigned sheets he said "the symmetry is wrong. they should all feel the same in symmetry", asked "aztecs have no building?", asked for "a compensating negative rule" for Germany, and answered the lead's proposals with "ok cool, and make sure the art is there too".
- **Germany:** "each Production building in a German city costs 1 Food" replaces the Ruhr Foundry's own -2 Food (`astra/germany-industry-food`; ablation in `docs/sim-results/faction-ablation/germany-2026-11-15/`). He had suggested moving the Food to the faction; the lead advised against a flat per-city penalty from turn 1 (it slows the first Settlers, and Sparta owns "expands slowly").
- **Byzantium, the Celts, the Aztecs** each get a special building; designs from Astra (`.reviews/astra-three-buildings.md` when it lands), spread across slots and eras, shown to him before building. First sketches: Chinampa, Oppidum, Theodosian Walls.
- **Art is part of the deliverable for every new piece** (Egypt and Rome on `claude/art-egypt-rome`; the three buildings once designed). A faction or building is not done with placeholder art.

## Rome: veterans found cities, no Gold price (user, 2026-11-16, first Rome game on 8.9.67)
"it feels like a real decision... but also, that's a lot of money... having an army means that I don't have a lot of cash to pay for the founding of the cities. I think we should just make it 'veteran soldiers can found cities'. that's already unusual enough, and it's hard to also have to pay for an escalating amount of money for it." The escalating Gold price (the design critic's brake on barbarian-farmed veterans) is removed: the veteran is the whole cost. Lesson: one unusual cost per rule break; a second price stacked on top reads as punishment, and the army's upkeep was already taking the Gold. If Rome then expands too freely, raise the level required, do not bring a currency back. On escalation he answered (2026-11-16): "this is already implicit because the units that are veterans later in the game are more expensive than early units. so i think its fine." Open point the lead raised and he has not ruled on: older, cheaper melee units stay buildable, so a late Spearman with one promotion founds as well as a Rifleman; watch for it in play and in the expansion numbers before proposing anything. Built on `astra/rome-free-founding` (rules 44).

**Rome: rule kept (user, 2026-11-21):** no Settlers, veterans found cities; it forces early war by design ("i played with the rule and had no issues. it's a fun rule break and forces you into early war."). Hard Rome must seek the fight: camps, else a planned early war on the weakest reachable neighbour. Fixes for a slow Hard Rome are AI only, never a rule change.
