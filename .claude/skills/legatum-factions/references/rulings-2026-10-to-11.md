Tribute, Sparta healing, Swiss interest, unique pieces, the Keshig, Celtic hunters, Sakoku, the Heir, India/Japan, the 2026-11-05/06 ablation rulings, Byzantium's Theodosian Walls, Sparta's desertion and upkeep, Germany.

Moved verbatim from SKILL.md on 2026-11-22 (original order kept). The current rules are in SKILL.md; where this file disagrees with SKILL.md, SKILL.md wins.

## Byzantium implemented (2026-10-10, branch `claude/bought-peace`)

**(Superseded 2026-10-22: bought peace and its tribute price were deleted for Mercenary Companies.) Tribute price (user, 2026-10-11):** "I have no idea. We will need to playtest it. We should probably base it on [the] size of the army, not in terms of income given that income can vary wildly." The price already scales with the payee's army (10% of its armed units' Production value, doubling per repeat). Keep it, and tune from playtests rather than an income yardstick.

**Sparta's Scouts heal normally (user, 2026-10-11):** "scouts should be able to heal since they're not primarily combat units." The slow-healing rule covers Sparta's combat units only: Scouts heal 20/10 (and use the current Wait order), and still join the kill-healing share when they damage the victim.

**(Superseded 2026-11-06: slow resting heal is cut; Spartans heal at the ordinary rate.) Sparta's military heals 2 HP a turn by resting (user, 2026-10-11):** "let's not make it 'zero healing' but we can reduce it significantly... 2hp per turn... if you're not at war or something you can still retreat a unit and they might be useful later in the game, but the incentive is still to full attack." Killing stays the main way Sparta heals (up to 100 HP shared), and Scouts heal normally. Implemented on `claude/sparta-scout-heal`: `data::SPARTA_REST_HEAL` = 2 (exported as `RULES.spartaRestHeal`), a flat 2 in own territory and in the field, resting (asleep or not; current Wait order); closed foreign land still heals 0. The old pre-2026-10-04 value was 2 at home, 0 in the field (`git show 1c2922c3^:crates/core/src/rules/units.rs`); the user's "2hp per turn" is applied everywhere. AI: at war an unexposed wounded regiment still stays with the army; at peace it retreats and rests up to 60 HP (`tactics.rs::recover_wounded`).

**Switzerland's interest is uncapped and called interest (user, 2026-10-17):** "'per complete pair' is not normal language... it should also say 'interest'... is there a reason we have a cap at 5? I feel like we shouldn't have a cap." The wording becomes "+1 Gold for every 2 population" and "every 100 Gold in your treasury earns +1 Gold a turn in interest", and the +5 cap is removed. Measured 2026-10-17 (Hard + Search, 256 games a cohort, cap 5 against uncapped, `interest-cap=5` is the control): no runaway. Switzerland's win share 17.1% to 17.4% pooled over 4 and 8 empires, human-seat benchmark -0.4 pp (Hard) and +1.2 pp (Hard + Search), all inside noise. The AI's treasury never passes about 1,200 (median 150 to 290 at T150), so interest is 4 to 6% of its Gold income; interest is not cut by decadence and is not Power. The case not measured is a human who sits on 3,000 Gold (+30 Gold a turn). Counterweights if it ever runs away: a higher Gold threshold per point, or interest subject to decadence. Details: `docs/learnings.md`, 2026-10-17.

**Mongols: the Keshig horse archer, their Classical mounted unit; Scouts are normal (user, 2026-10-17):** "the Mongols are famous for their horse archers... the first mounted unit should be a Mongol horse archer (if they have a special name from history we go with that)... like a normal mounted unit, but they can *also* do ranged attacks in addition to melee." The Outrider no longer replaces the Scout. The Keshig picks melee or ranged each turn, as one order, with no hit-and-run. Built on `claude/mongol-keshig` (lead's calls within the decision):
  - **Moved to the Classical era (user, same day):** "I want this to be a second era (or whatever the first horseman upgrade is) rather than first era. The Mongol horde was in the 1200s so it feels premature to give it right at the start of the game for flavor reasons." The Keshig replaces the Cataphract (Iron Working); in the Ancient era the Mongols build ordinary Horsemen (with their +1 move), which upgrade into Keshigs. Rule (flavour): a faction's unique piece arrives in the era its history belongs to, or as close as the era ladder allows, never earlier just to give a faction an early identity.
  - **Numbers (combat sweep, `docs/sim-results/unit-classes/keshig.txt`; superseded by the doubled price scale: on master the Keshig is 164 Production, 33 melee and 18 ranged at range 2, `data::KESHIG_COST`):** 82 Production (Cataphract 75), the Cataphract's 25 melee and moves (5 with the Mongol move), 18 ranged at range 2 (a Composite Archer has 23). The first, Ancient version (60 Production, the Horseman's 18 melee, 10 ranged) was set by the same sweep and scaled up by the era steps. At equal Production it is at least as good as the bowless rider everywhere and far better against mixed spear-and-bow armies (34% against 1%), and still loses to a spear line. Price dominates: at 55 it beat a mixed army 70% of the time; at 65 it lost the Horseman's own mirror. Range 1 was worse than range 2 at every price.
  - **Order model:** no new order. It shoots with an ordinary `RangedAttack` from where it stands (that is its turn: no move, no retaliation, no capture) or charges by moving onto the enemy (a normal mounted attack that can take a city). Ranged units already could not move and shoot in one turn, so "move to a tile and shoot" is not a thing either.
  - **Triangle:** a shot fights as the ranged class (`combat::fight_class`): no spear bonus against arrows, a mounted target gets no bonus against them either (designer, 2026-11-10: the mounted bonus is for charging), no ranged-vs-melee bonus (none exists). Charging and defending it is mounted.
  - **Upgrade:** to the ordinary Knight, losing the bow (the usual pattern for uniques); Mongol Horsemen upgrade into Keshigs.
  - **Counter:** a spear line still beats it in the open; Horsemen and mixed armies with spears hold it.
- **Faction spread on 8.9.7 (diagnostic, 2026-10-22; docs/sim-results/hard-vs-human/baseline-8.9.7.md):** 2,048 balanced 8-empire Hard games: Byzantium 29.2%, Athens 16.9%, Mongols 16.2%, Switzerland 13.8%, Sparta 10.4%, Germany 8.1%, Japan 3.1%, Celts 2.4% (fair 12.5%; 12x best/worst against pillar 8's 1.5x). Japan's weakness lines up with decadence counting Food in Power (decision waits on his "Japan's Century" run); the Celts' with keeping no cities; Byzantium's likely with bought peace (Mercenary Companies replace it). Per-faction gene climbs did not fix any of them: balance here needs design (deepen drawbacks or make the weak identity pay off), not tuning. Single-map seat numbers (Mongols 3.9%) are map effects; use balanced games.
- **Celtic hunters lose the −1 move (user, 2026-10-22, decisions log):** "the move penalty for celtic archers is pretty rough. is that needed?" They move like any ranged unit; +20 Production cost and +3 ranged strength remain (superseded: the +20 went 2026-11-02 and the +3 with the Ambusher on 2026-11-06). Lesson: a drawback that makes a faction's signature unit unable to keep up with its own army hurts the identity instead of shaping it.
- **Sakoku without the intruder rule (user, 2026-10-22, decisions log):** "it's enough of a penalty to shut down the whole country for 30 turns, so i don't think the enemy troops thing is required." Enemy units in Japanese land no longer break the vow; the 30 closed turns are the cost. Decadence stays as is (user "yes" to the coach's recommendation).
- **Byzantium's 29% is mostly the Heir goal, not bought peace (2026-10-22, mercenaries agent, 2,048 balanced games):** bought peace off moves Byzantium only 30.1% → 27.0%. Byzantium completes the Heir goal in 86% of games (median T138) and wins 34.8% with it against 0.4% without. Matches the user's read ("some sort of rules gimmick that the AI can't figure out"): rivals don't contest the Heir. Fix by making the AI contest it or deepening the goal's cost (pillar 8), not tuning; options go to the user once the no-Heir batch is sized.
- **Heir stays; make it huntable (user, 2026-10-22, decisions log):** "I think Heir is fine. maybe there just needs to be a clearer announcement that the heir has been born and where the heir is so people can try to snatch it." Every empire is told when the Heir is born, its position is public on every map from then on, and the AI values hunting it.
- **Era resources and factions (2026-10-22, lead):** a faction's unique unit uses the same era resource as the unit it replaces (the Keshig and the Cataphract Iron, the Legionary and the Pikeman Iron); no faction gets a resource rule yet. There is no Horses resource, so the Mongols' steppe identity stays mobility, not supply. A resource-flavoured drawback or strength (e.g. a faction that ignores one era's resource) is open design space for the faction reworks, not built.
- **Celts and era resources: decided, no change (user, 2026-10-23):** "they sack the city, then they have to build their own with settlers. its fine." Sacked cities give no deposits; the Celts get their resources by settling. Don't add a sack-supply rule or an exemption.

**Japan becomes India; Japan gets a new identity (user, 2026-10-25, after winning 8.9.10 as Sparta).** "Japan is way overpowered... the rapid population expansion means rapid territory expansion and more production and gold and everything because it means more tiles." Population is territory turns a growth faction into a snowball, and the distance-from-capital drawback isn't paying for it. The population identity moves to **India** ("historically known for having a huge population, and Japan is not"); China was considered and saved for later because it has other identities. India needs a better compensating drawback rooted in Indian history ("What is the cost of this huge advantage?"). **Japan stays** but must be redesigned around something historically Japanese. Sakoku is Japanese, so it shouldn't move to India. Proposal: Astra task `india-faction-proposal` → `docs/design/research/india-and-japan-2026-10-25.md`; nothing is renamed until the user picks.
- **Strengthen the distance-from-capital penalty (user, 2026-10-25):** "we could try making this penalty more significant... it's clearly not enough right now. they snowball way too easily." The drawback should be deepened (pillar 8), not the Food network weakened. Suspect: the +7% per nearby city network bonus stacks in a tight cluster near the capital, where the penalty (beyond 4 hexes) never applies. An interim constant change can ship before the India rename.
- **Decisions 2026-10-25 (user, on Astra's India/Japan proposal):** India is option **B, regional courts (Akbar)**: non-capital cities send no Gold until a Revenue Office, then half; goal the Imperial Diwan. New Japan is **Fortified domains (Tokugawa)** with Sakoku as its goal. The interim Japan fix is an **escalating distance penalty** (10/30/60/100% at 1–4 hexes beyond radius 4), not Astra's radius-2 idea: "2 doesn't make logical sense because cities are min 3 away but we should increase the penalty the further out they are." He wasn't sure how B overlaps the penalty ("maybe we don't need 4"); the lead kept both because B taxes only Gold and leaves the Food/Production snowball unchecked. Re-check after playtesting whether India is now over-penalised.
