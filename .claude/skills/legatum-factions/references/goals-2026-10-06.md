# Factions: the faction goal redesign (2026-10-06) and its rejected goals

Every faction goal's design, implementation notes and later supersessions, the rejected goal ideas with the user's reasons, and Mongol passage history.

Moved verbatim from SKILL.md on 2026-11-22 (original order kept). The current rules are in SKILL.md; where this file disagrees with SKILL.md, SKILL.md wins.

## Faction goal redesign (all 8 decided 2026-10-06; all implemented, with later changes marked below)
- **Where a goal lives in code (2026-10-06):** `FACTION_GOAL` in `app/src/data.ts` holds each goal's name, plain-words rule (a function of the speed's scaling; every number comes from `RULES.factionGoals`, exported from `crates/core/src/goals.rs::spec`), `live` (a live goal is held, not earned, and can be lost: the race shows it outlined like a title), icon and optional public count (unused now). `FACTION_INFO[f].goal`, the faction sheets, the Guide, and the victory race all read it. Redesigning a goal is an edit there plus the core's scoring; nothing in the VP UI is hardcoded.
**Every faction goal is worth exactly 2 VP** (user rule, 2026-10-06). Tune difficulty through the goal's threshold or cost, never its VP. Mongols and Byzantium's 3 VP and Sparta's 4 VP are retired.

**Counter principle** (user, 2026-10-06): every goal either has a super-high cost, or rivals can take the points away. They can steal them outright (the Heir), or knock the faction off the goal and profit doing it (conquering a Swiss drops its treasury below 1,000 and pays the captor).

Test (user): a goal is in theme and inconvenient, "not something I would commonly do in any game". "Save then burn" is the model.
- **Decided:**
  - **Switzerland:** a live goal: have 1,000 Gold in the treasury (superseded 2026-10-22: now 5,000 Gold, `goals::SWISS_HOARD_GOLD`). Spend below it and you lose the VP until you're back up. Supersedes "spend 1,000". User: "you're hoarding gold. and it's a real strategic tradeoff."
  - **Japan distance penalty (now India's corruption, 2026-10-25):** −10% per hex beyond 4 from the capital (was −5%). The user expects Japan to build near the capital, going a little further only for a needed resource.
  - **Sparta: Honour the Fallen (IMPLEMENTED 2026-10-07, goals increment 4).** Retire 3 veterans (promotion level 4+ (superseded 2026-11-06: level 3+); the user kept 4 on 2026-10-07: "level 2 is just way too easy to achieve, and units are cheap... the AI just needs to be better at getting units more experience"; playtest 8.4 confirmed it: "level 4 experience is achievable. I got several to level 3 but I won the game too quickly") in a city with a War Shrine (displayed as the Agoge). You need wars to promote them, then you give them up. The user chose it over raze, the Wall of Men, the Carneia and Iron Peace.
    - **Rules:** `Action::RetireVeteran{unit}` is a unit action on a level-4+ military unit (superseded 2026-11-06: level 3+, `goals::SPARTA_RETIRE_LEVEL`) standing in an own War Shrine city; progress (`Player::veterans_retired`) is kept; the third retirement is permanent. The unit is disbanded, not killed (no healing for others). The unit panel offers "Retire this veteran" on every veteran (greyed with the reason when he is not in a shrine city), the map rings the War Shrine cities while he is selected, and the confirm reads "Retire this veteran: 2 of 3 toward Honour the Fallen (+2 VP)".
    - **AI:** builds one War Shrine (the best-Production city) once a third of the game is gone or a veteran exists; retires only when the 2 VP take the team to the target with time to hold it, or at 70% of the game, never leaving a threatened city empty, and never when the Strongest Army title it would lose costs as many points; gathers its veterans at the shrine city in that window. Rivals value a retired Sparta's thin garrisons (`retired_army_weakness_value`).
  - **Japan (`Japan`): Sakoku (implemented 2026-10-10; simplified 2026-10-22 per the decisions log).** Replaces the Swarm. A live, public vow: `Action::Sakoku{close}`. While closed Japan can't declare war or join an Alliance (its permanent teammate is unaffected, teams are not Alliances; but an Alliance is between teams, so a team with a closed member signs none), gets no tech diffusion, and no foreign unit may enter its land at Peace. After 30 turn ends closed (15 at Quick, speed-scaled) it holds 2 VP regardless of enemy units in its land; reopening loses it and restarts the count. Full rules and AI: game-rules.md §8 "Sakoku". **The Swarm is a goal rule now (`goals::GoalRule::Swarm`), reachable by no faction until the Aztecs take it as "The New Fire"**; its action, UI and AI are kept whole.
  - **(Superseded 2026-10-10) Japan: The Swarm.** A one-time empire action: sacrifice 30 population (calibrated 2026-10-07 from 50) for the VP.
    - The population is taken from the biggest cities first, and every city keeps at least 1.
    - The rule text only says "sacrifice 50 population"; the confirm dialog previews each city's loss.
    - Supersedes the 8-city cluster, which had map luck.
    - **Implemented (2026-10-07, goals increment 2):** `Order::Action{Swarm}`, `goals::swarm_plan`; the race panel's action row opens a confirm with each city's loss and released tiles, the map rings the shrinking cities. The AI fires it when its 2 VP, net of titles it would lose (Most Populous, Most Territory, Largest Economy), takes the team to the target with time to hold, or the game is 75% over, never into an enemy army; rivals add a thin-target value for a swarmed Japan's small cities (`goal_actions.rs`, `victory.rs::swarm_weakness_value`).
  - **Mongols: The Open Road (IMPLEMENTED 2026-10-07, increment 5; Hall cost 300 Production was a placeholder, now `YAM_RELAY_STATION_COST` 600; displayed as the Yam Relay Station and built on a chosen tile since 2026-10-24, with `BuildingKind::YamRelayStation` kept as the internal name).** Build the Courier Hall, a pricey Mongols-only building. While it stands, its holder has 2 VP and every empire gets Peace passage through the holder's land. It's capturable, and the captor holds the 2 VP while they hold the Hall (user confirmed, 2026-10-06), mirroring the Heir. User: "capturable and someone else can take the points".
    - **Rules (lead, 2026-10-06):** one Hall per Mongols empire, rebuildable only if it no longer exists (destroyed by a Celtic sack); if a rival captured its city, retake the city. Every empire gets Peace passage through ALL of the holder's land for every unit type, military included: exposure is the Hall's cost (`GameState::passage_allowed`, `goals::holds_open_road`).
    - **Art (user chose variant C, the Portolan rose):** a compass-rose plaque over the Hall city with its intercardinal points in the holder's tint; faint rhumb lines across the holder's land, stopping at its border; a gilt-ringed badge beside the city seal when zoomed out; a token icon (a fleur on the north point, so it doesn't read as the Wayfinder) for the race panel and cards.
    - **Wayfinder sight (user, 2026-10-07, playtest 8.5; superseded 2026-10-17: Mongol Scouts are ordinary again, the Keshig replaced the Outrider):** the Wayfinder starts with sight 3, not 2, and the Scout sight tech takes it to 4. "There's no point in having 4 moves if he can only see 2 ahead." A unit's sight should match its mobility, so a fast scout can see where it's going.
    - **AI:** Mongols builds the Hall when its VP can reach and hold the target and it can defend its now-open land; rivals value the Hall city by its 2 VP and, at Peace, stage armies inside the holder's open land; the holder defends the city.
    - **Roads (user, 2026-10-07):** when the Hall completes, roads link every city on the map (all empires) in a network growing out of the Hall city, and units on roads get +1 move (Civ 6 rules as a guide). Everyone benefits, and it announces "2 VP available". **Built 2026-10-07 (`claude/hall-roads`).** Rule: a road tile costs 1 move whatever its terrain, and a unit that starts its turn on a road has +1 move (one sentence, integer-exact, no per-turn state). Roads are laid once at completion (and extended each turn to cities founded or captured later while a Hall stands), persist when the Hall is captured or razed, and never open a border. Art: inked road lines through edge midpoints, rooted at the Portolan rose city (see legatum-art). The Open Road card, Hall build card, Guide and tile card all say it. Player-built roads (a second increment, `claude/player-roads`): the Roadworks building (Field Logistics, 70 Production, +1 Gold flat, links its city to every city within 8 hexes of any empire, later cities too, same movement rules; rivals use them). AI: units use roads through the shared cost function; the existing Hall-capture valuation already covers the "2 VP available" announcement.
    - **Rush counter is AI skill:** the user stops the Mongols rush by parking a Scout to watch their cities, and calls it "almost too easy". Don't weaken the rush.
    - **Art:** the Hall needs a clear, distinctive map landmark that every player can read at a glance during play. It also needs a token on the VP race panel.
  - **Athens: Share the Learning.** A one-time broadcast: every rival gains 3 free techs of its own choice.
    - It's equal for everyone and costly early, so the timing is a real choice.
    - **Implemented (2026-10-07, goals increment 2):** `Order::Action{Broadcast}` leaves each living empire outside the team `free_techs_pending`; receivers choose with `ChooseFreeTechs` (prerequisites known or listed first), a human's prompt (leader portrait, picker) returns every turn and nothing auto-picks. The AI broadcasts when it takes the team to the target with time to hold, or at 85% of the game; AI receivers pick by the research valuation (`goal_actions.rs`). Containment of a Athens near the target is the existing leader logic.
    - UI: each human receiver gets a "choose 3 technologies" prompt when it happens; the AI picks its own.
    - The hold-to-win rule gives rivals 10 turns to use the techs.
  - **Celts: Call the Wild (IMPLEMENTED 2026-10-07, simplified 2026-10-31).** A rite the Celts can call once per turn: each call raises a barbarian camp only in Celtic cities that have none. They hold 2 VP while every city has a camp in its territory. There's no minimum city count; user: "if you have one city and you trigger it, it's extremely inconvenient and painful, so you're probably not going to".
    - **Barbarians are no threat to the Celts, both ways (user, 2026-11-03, 8.9.25 Celts playtest):** "since the barbarians are not hostile, the cities should not shoot at them... units should not wake up from sleep / fortify if a barbarian is nearby." Nothing automatic treats barbarians as a threat to the Celts: no city auto-fire at them, no wake-ups, interrupts, danger warnings or AI defence against them. Manual attacks stay allowed (clearing camps, including the Celts' own extra Call the Wild camps).
    - **Sack simplified, technology steal removed (user, 2026-11-03):** "let's remove the tech steal and simplify the gold capture as well. that explanation in the rules is too complicated." The steal fired in 0 of his 2 sacks and 12% of AI sacks. Sack Gold is now 30 per population of the city, nothing else (no hidden treasury share), so the forecast is exact; 30 keeps the average sack within 4% of the old one (178.9 Gold over 1,297 AI sacks, 28.75 per population). The warband camp stays; `RULES_VERSION` 12 (branch `claude/celts-sack-simplify`). Lesson: a rule that needs a paragraph and a hidden number to explain (a quarter of a treasury nobody can see, a technology picked by a cost rule) reads as clutter even when it works; the one-sentence test applies to the take as well as the rule.
    - **Rivals:** any rival that clears a camp gets the normal clearing reward, and the Celts lose the VP until every city has a camp again.
    - **Re-calling:** it adds a camp to every city, so the Celts usually clear their own extra camps first.
    - **Exploit guard (lead):** the Celts earn no clearing Gold from camps their own rite raised, so they can't farm Gold.
    - **Implemented:** `Action::CallTheWild` raises a camp on the farthest free tile of each city that lacks one (the confirm and map preview the exact tiles); camps are ordinary (guard, scout; since 2026-11-02 their raids never touch the Celts), exempt from border dispersal and the natural camp target, and work with barbarians off. Celts territory attrition applies to barbarians in it, so a wild camp's guard sits at 10 HP (open question for the user: exempt wild camps?).
    - **AI:** the Celts call late (the 2 VP take the team to the target with time to hold it, or 70% of the game), only once settling is done (no Settler under way and no room, or 55% of the game), found no city afterwards while they want the goal, keep one camp per city (clearing doubles) and re-call when a rival clears one. Rivals clear the camps of a Celts that scores the goal, from farther away and ahead of ordinary camps, by a short bounded walk into Celts land (`wild_camp_approach`).
    - **Works with barbarians off:** the rite raises its own wild camps.
    - User: "they have to keep them alive, and one of the other players could destroy the barbarians, which would cancel the 2vps and they get the barb encampment bonuses too".
  - **Byzantium: The Heir (IMPLEMENTED 2026-10-06, increment 3; Heir cost 400 Production, calibrated 2026-10-07 from 200; now `HEIR_COST` 800 at the doubled price scale).** Train the Heir, an expensive Byzantium-only civilian. Whoever holds the Heir has the 2 VP. If a rival captures him, the points go to the captor (and back if retaken). User: it "makes them a juicy target once they build that unit".
  - **Germany: Five Ruhr Foundries (designer 2026-11-01; replaced "a Foundry in every city"):** own five cities with a Ruhr Foundry, a live 2 VP goal. The VP is lost when capture or raze drops the count below five, and regained when it returns to five; a city without one does not suspend it. Since 2026-11-05 the Foundry sits in the Forge slot (Iron Working): +6 Production and −2 Food, with the Forge's Iron supply need; the goal counts the Forge-slot building wherever it stands, so a city that adds a Factory keeps counting. "It needs to be meaningfully inconvenient. I feel like 4 would happen by accident"; the lead picked 5 (Hard Germany averaged 4.7 cities and 1.6 Foundries under the every-city rule). Codex `germany-five-foundries`.
- **Rejected:**
  - **Sparta: N deaths in their battles** (withdrawn 2026-10-06). User: it "feels more like a 'do your thing more'". **Rule:** a goal is a cost, never more of the faction's strength.
  - **Celts: sack 2 rival cities.** Accepted at first, then reopened: "feels off".
  - **Mongols: Courier's Oath** (80 turns without declaring war). "They're a rusher, so we're basically saying the only way to play a rusher is to not rush, which is pointless." **Rule:** a goal can't ask a faction to abandon its play style outright. A cost layered on top of it is fine.
  - **Mongols round 2** (End of the Road, Hold the Crossroads, the Express, the Waystations, Caravan of Spoils): all rejected. End of the Road: "you'd take advantage of the early rush, then giving it away is basically free late game." **Rule:** a sacrifice must cost something still valuable when it's paid.
  - **Byzantium: the Long Watch** (a unit next to a rival capital for 40 turns). User: "that's basically 'did they notice that this unit is here?'", not a strategic tradeoff.
  - **Japan: an 8-city cluster** (replaced by the Swarm). It's map luck (a coastal start may not fit 8 cities), and it's arguably "do your thing more".
  - **Celts: keep N untouched Forest/Jungle tiles.** "No guarantee you'll have 12 spaces like that... feels like a dice roll." Goals must not depend on map luck.
  - **Mongols: Markets far apart.** Building Markets is normal play ("I often build markets to get cash").
  - **Mongols: a Grand Tour** (a unit visits every rival capital and returns). "Feels tedious more than a strategic tradeoff." **Rule:** a goal is a strategic tradeoff, never a chore of busywork moves.
  - **Three "burn your specialty" goals** (Switzerland Gold, Germany Production, Athens or Byzantium Science) feel templated. **Rule:** goals must differ in kind across factions, not just in currency.
- **Mongols passage (user, 2026-10-06; landed 2bcb2ddd; superseded 2026-11-06: the Scout and civilian Peace passage is cut, see below):** at Peace, only their Scouts and civilians may cross foreign borders; military can't. The war push-out was rejected as "magic". The Courier Hall opens its holder's land to all units.
  - **It didn't close the rush hole:** Hard's score against the Mongols Proxy v7 went 5.7% → 5.5%. The proxy now stages at the border, and a surprise war at Peace takes effect the same turn, so +1 move still reaches a one-Soldier capital. The next lever is a rules question for the user: war-declaration timing, or capital defence.
- **Implemented (increment 1, 2026-10-06):** the 2 VP rule for every goal, typed VP sources (`VpSource`), `goals.rs` (Permanent, Live, Held), the live Switzerland hoard (AI keeps it once held; rivals value knocking it down through the capture bounty), Japan −10%, and barbarian camps and units denying tile yields. The other seven goals keep their old conditions until later increments.
- **Related rules:** victory needs holding the VP target for 10 turns; barbarian camps and units deny tile yields. Calibration still to do: the Swarm's population, the Heir's cost, the Courier Hall's price.
