## Open questions
- **Sparta healing: blood is the only medicine** (user, 2026-10-04; supersedes "heal normally at home").
  - **Superseded 2026-10-11:** resting heals 2 HP a turn instead of nothing (Scouts heal normally); see the notes at the end.
  - Capturing a city fully heals Sparta military within 2 hexes, as before.
  - Destroying an enemy military unit (Scouts and barbarians included; civilians are captured, so they never count) restores 100 HP, split evenly among the Sparta units that damaged it that turn; excess beyond a unit's max goes to the others.
  - Healing applies only to units that survive the stage: in a mutual kill both units are dead.
  - A garrison killed by combined-assault overflow counts as a kill; everyone who damaged that city tile in the stage shares the healing (lead decision, 2026-10-04).
  - Combined with "infantry ignore wound penalties": winning fights sustains you, losing ones grind you down.
  - War-focused builds are exempt from the +50% non-military surcharge: the Walls family, Citadel and the new War Shrine.
  - The War Shrine is Sparta's military school: units trained in its city start one level higher.
  - Every Settler pays the +50%, the first included (the exemption was arbitrary).
  - The UI shows the Heal action greyed out, with a tooltip explaining that Sparta heal only by kills and captures.
- **Sparta at 1.5–2% (7a baseline).** The diagnosis (2026-10-04) found an early war (median T27) with poor conversion (first capture around T107, 0.74 captures per game). The AI stall fix moved it from 3 to 5 wins in 200. Measured rule options: non-military +25% gives 10/200; 2 field HP gives 4/200; exempting all Settlers gives 4/200. Decision (user, 2026-10-04): hold rule changes until the campaign AI lands in 7b; the user will playtest Sparta in 7b, then decide.

## Historical re-theme (user, 2026-10-10)
Factions are real peoples: Switzerland = Switzerland, Mongols = Mongols, Sparta = Sparta, Germany = Germany, Japan = Japan, Byzantium = Byzantium, Celts = Celts, Athens = Athens. Mechanics are unchanged; names, lore and art follow the history. Portraits pair a man and a woman, with no named leader. Fantasy and alien themes (vault goblins, insect Japan, elves) are rejected.


## Current Mongol rule
The Mongols cannot build Siege Engines and cannot build Walls before the Medieval era. Siege is available from the Medieval era. See the rules reference for current details.

- **Unit rules by class (A2, 2026-10-10):** faction unit rules now read the class, not a unit list: Sparta melee line ignores wounds; the Celts' hunter line (`data::is_hunter_kind`; Archer, Composite Archer, Crossbowman, Arquebusier; +3 ranged; Celtic Ambushers) was removed on 2026-11-06 with the Ambusher cut, and the melee −2 and the +20 cost went 2026-11-02; Byzantium Tagmatic Soldier replaces the Pikeman and the other melee units after the Spearman get +4 strength and +5 cost; Mongols cannot build any siege-class unit (Catapult, Bombard); a faction that cannot build a class (Aztecs and mounted, later) is one line in `data::faction_unavailable_reason`, its Horseman has 5 moves; Germany mounted has 3 (superseded 2026-11-06: German units move normally).
**Open proposal (design agent, 2026-10-10; awaiting the user, not decided):** `docs/design/historical-factions/flavour-redesign-2026-10-10.md` (+ PNG card).
- **Aztecs (9th faction):** Flower Wars, where a kill with an Aztec melee unit taking part gives +1 population in the nearest Aztec city (empire units only, no barbarians or Scouts). **Melee only, confirmed by the designer (2026-11-01, after his 8.9.22 Aztec win):** "ranged units don't take slaves and bombers that kill units also obviously don't take them either. so it's just melee captures." Already the rule in code; never extend it to ranged, mounted or air kills. Shared kills (a melee unit fought it, another unit landed the blow) and defensive melee kills both count; he agreed ("sounds good for melee"). No horses: no Horseman, Knight or Cavalry. Goal: The New Fire, the Swarm moved from Japan (30 population).
- **Byzantium:** drawback "thin levies", at most one military unit per city plus one; the 120% building cost is dropped. Optional "hire an army": a paid call to arms to any met empire, at the bought-peace price, with 10 turns of enforced war.

**Flavour redesign approved to try (user, 2026-10-10: "Sure let's try it"):** the full design is `docs/design/historical-factions/flavour-redesign-2026-10-10.md`.
- **Aztecs (9th faction):** Flower Wars, no horses, and The New Fire, which is the Swarm moved from Japan and shown as torches, never blood. They ship with the mounted class.
- **Japan:** keeps its dense city network; its goal becomes Sakoku, a vow to close the country.

- **Byzantium: Hire an army is retired (user, 2026-10-18), with Call to Arms (calls to arms returned 2026-10-23 for offensive wars, but Hire an army and bought peace stayed gone).** Allies then fought their wars together, so a paid call has nothing to ask of anyone ("'Call to arms' is too complicated"). Bought peace stays until the Mercenary Companies rework lands, adapted to shared wars: tribute buys peace from the payee and all its allies at once (priced on all their armies, no one can refuse) and is refused while Byzantium's own allies are still at war with that side (it must leave the Alliance first, since peace is made by whole sides). Mercenary Companies are not allies: a hired unit is Byzantium's, so the rework needs no rule about wars.
- **Byzantium's forced peace is under review (user, 2026-10-17: "The Byzantium rule break of forced peace feels off").** A Fable research pass (`docs/design/research/rule-breaks-2026-10-17.md`, also the Claude Doc "Legatum Rule Breaks Research") recommends "Set them against each other" (public, refusable war subsidies, relaxing Hire an army), with the fortress (Greek Fire plus the Theodosian Walls) and the mercenary (the Varangian Guard) as alternatives. It is waiting on the user's answers to its questions; don't build a replacement before he chooses. **Update, the same day:** the user rejected "Set them against each other": "only works in theory. They can't actually force someone to move an army and do something." Lesson: a rule break must produce effects the faction itself controls on the map, never depend on another player's cooperation. He leans to the mercenary paymaster: "they can buy units (as mercenaries) for cheaper than normal, but they only last <n> turns... like levying units from city states in Civ... or the money goes to another civ... or buy other civs' special units with gold... it needs to feel like a real rule break." A Fable design pass is in `docs/design/research/mercenaries-and-faction-changes-2026-10-17.md`.
**Athens ruins (user, 2026-10-18):** ruins give the normal reward plus 20 Science, instead of Science only. "It almost feels like a disadvantage to only get science from ruins." A faction bonus should add to the base game, never replace a base reward with something that can feel worse.


**Decided (user, 2026-10-17), from the mercenary research:**
- **Byzantium: Mercenary Companies replace bought peace and Hire an army.** Contracts last **30 turns**, not 20 ("20 turns for mercenaries feels short... I'm not even sure that's enough time for a single campaign"). Companies can capture cities, and there's nothing to hire before first contact ("it's ok"). The Gold goes to the seller in full (the lead's default; the user didn't object).
- **A Company is the seller's own version of the unit (user, 2026-11-14, playing Byzantium on 8.9.35.1):** he hired a Spearman from Sparta and got a plain one: "i should be able to buy the special units." A hired unit carries its seller's unit version (Sparta's Hoplite with its phalanx, the Mongol Keshig, which stops being excluded); the seller's empire-wide rules (ignore wounds, +1 move) still do not travel. Where a seller has a special version it replaces the plain one in that seller's list. He also floated selling *only* special units, to cut clutter and redundancy: **open, not decided**. The fact against it: today only Sparta (Hoplite, Ancient only) and the Mongols (Keshig, Classical) have a special unit; the other six sellers' specials are buildings, so "only" needs a special unit per faction first or it empties the shop that thin levies depend on.
- **He expects the Civ 6 package: every faction has a special unit and a special building (user, 2026-11-14):** "I thought every civ has a special unit and a special building, that's thematic / flavorful to the history of that civilization"; he "didn't realize that only 2 have special units". Today: units for Sparta, Mongols, Byzantium; buildings for Switzerland, Germany, Athens, India; Celts and Aztecs have neither. **Decided the same day ("yes, let's do that"):** fill the gap to one special unit and one special building per faction, Civ-style, units first (six: Switzerland, Germany, Athens, India, Celts, Aztecs), buildings second (five). Each must push the faction further into its style, not only add strength, and the units are spread across eras where history allows. He rules on each draft before it is built. Art is Codex image generation (ChatGPT budget), reviewed by a Claude agent. Treat a faction without both as incomplete when designing.
- **The six special units are approved as drafted (user, 2026-11-14: "keep all six units. do the art"):** Aztecs Eagle Warrior (replaces Spearman: +4 strength, +10 Production); Celts Gaesatae (replaces Phalangite: +8 strength attacking, -5 defending); Athens Peltast (replaces Composite Archer: 3 moves, same shot); India War Elephant (replaces Knight: +8 strength, 3 moves instead of 4, no penalty charging a city); Switzerland Reisläufer (replaces Musketman: +50% against mounted on top of the usual bonus); Germany Panzer (replaces Tank: +1 move; it uses 1 Oil like the Tank, user, 2026-11-15: "there are no other units that require double supply"). Built together with the hire rule (a Company is the seller's version; faction-wide rules don't travel). Numbers are untested first drafts. Art by Codex image generation, reviewed by a Claude agent. The five special buildings are the next pass; he rules on each draft first.
- **Correction to the six: the Reisläufer replaces the Pikeman, not the Musketman (user, 2026-11-14, after asking of the painting "where is the gun, if this is peer to musketman?"):** the Reisläufer were pike mercenaries, so the slot was wrong, not the painting. Stat line: Pikeman plus +50% against mounted on top of the usual bonus. Lesson: pick the slot from what the historical unit actually carried. Same day he chose to repaint the plain Tank as a WWII tank (art only, no new Main Battle Tank unit) so the Panzer has a same-era peer.
- **The hire shop sells only special units (user, 2026-11-14: "only special units", after the lead argued twice for keeping every unit on sale):** each empire sells exactly its own special unit and no plain units; the hired unit is the seller's version. Settled: don't reopen with "most eras would have nothing to hire". The shop is one compact row per seller. Built with the six units as rules 34.
- **Germany: -1 move only outside its own land and roads,** as a trial (superseded 2026-11-05: the movement drawback was dropped entirely and replaced by −2 Food; see the Germany rework entries). The user worries about wording: "I'm always looking for ways to simplify. If rules require explaining, it's hard to make a game work." If it needs more than one plain sentence, revert.
- **Athens stays the science faction** ("someone needs to be the science faction because that's a common thing in games like this"). The Delian League is parked for a possible future faction.
- **Athens needs a real drawback; "School of Hellas" rejected (user, 2026-11-05).** Athens tops every census (20.0% a seat) because its only standing cost is Markets at +1 Gold, and the AI broadcasts Share the Learning at a median T164, when the free techs barely matter. The proposal "rivals research techs Athens knows faster (double diffusion)" was rejected: "it basically just means 'any game athens is in, everyone researches faster.' it lifts all boats that doesn't affect competition." **Rule:** a drawback must change the competition around the faction (cost it relative to its rivals, or give rivals a lever against it), never a table-wide buff or nerf that applies whether or not anyone engages it.

 Its recommendation is "Mercenary Companies": hire any unit a met faction at peace can build, with that faction's rules, at 100% of Production cost in Gold, paid to the seller. Companies don't count toward thin levies, pay 2 upkeep, disband after 20 turns (30 as built), and turn barbarian when unpaid. Hire an army fails the same test as forced peace (another player must choose to act), so both are proposed to retire. Waiting on the user's answers.
