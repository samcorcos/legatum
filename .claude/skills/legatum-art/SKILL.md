# Legatum art

North star (user): refined and grand, "a real 4X that could succeed Civilization": sophisticated and serious, premium, ceremonial, historically weighty. Restrained materials, confident negative space. The map is a place, not a diagram. If a piece of art would look at home in a mobile casual game, a children's book or a clip-art set, it is wrong.

## The house style

- **Map art is atlas ink on vellum:** engraved or pen-and-wash line, hatching for tone, muted earth pigments, one accent red (`--accent`). Think a surveyor's atlas or an engraved estate map, not a painted game board. No glossy gradients, bevels, drop-shadow stickers, cartoon outlines or saturated flat fills on the map.
- **One line language:** the same pen weights everywhere on the map at a given zoom (set in `style.ts`). New sprites reuse those weights, never their own.
- **Player colour is a tint, not a fill:** ornaments, borders and seals take the player colour through `currentColor` or a translucent wash; the terrain still reads underneath (overlays about 0.5–0.6 opacity). Territory is the exception to "only a thin accent line stronger" (revised 2026-10-09, after a target player could not tell whose land was whose): a territory border is a bold seat-colour line with an ink edge over an 0.18 tint, drawn like an atlas's coloured outline and wash. The terrain still reads under the tint; the line is the strong part.
- **Icons:** single-colour `currentColor` glyphs on the 24-unit grid, drawn as solid silhouettes with knocked-out detail (not monoline: that is what the set always was), readable at 16 px, one consistent metaphor per concept everywhere it appears (the same Food glyph in the yield bar, tooltips, city banner and tech card). Every unit, building tier and faction replacement piece has its own icon, and text glyphs (warning, check, cross, arrows, letters) are never used as icons: add a `ui-*` or `mark-*` icon.
- **Paintings** (portraits, key art): the oil technique and parchment-compatible pigments of the current set; no text in the image; prompts saved next to the file.
- **Historical people portraits (user, 2026-10-10):** depict exactly two unnamed adults, one man and one woman, as representative people rather than named leaders. Both are half-length with faces large, distinct and readable at 128 px; keep both hands natural when visible. Use the established refined oil technique and parchment-compatible pigments, but let period clothing, materials and a recognizable historical setting carry the identity. No fantasy, sci-fi, caricature, stereotype exaggeration, text, watermark or additional people. Each portrait's prompt records its people and era; provide a 1024 px original, 512 px PNG and 360 px JPEG.
  - **Every faction ships a `people-<faction>` pair portrait in the same change that adds the faction; there is no single-leader fallback (user, 2026-11-01, after India showed Akbar alone).** India's pair is a Mughal court official (diwan) and a noblewoman of his household, c. 1600. The `leader-*` art and the loader fallback are deleted, and `src/portraits.test.ts` fails if a playable faction lacks its `people-<faction>` files.
- **Celebrations** are the one place for gold glow and particles (see presentation-variants).
- **Zoom tiers:** every map element defines what it shows at each zoom. Detail (hatching, small figures) at normal zoom; a simple, high-contrast mark when zoomed out. Nothing turns into noise or vanishes without a replacement.

## Decisions

- **Resource art (user, 2026-10-10):** painted medallions are the chosen resource art. The six full-colour Wheat, Cattle, Stone, Copper, Iron, and Coal images replace monochrome resource glyphs everywhere. The map draws them at the existing marker size with a device-resolution high-quality downscale; a fine red hairline retains the strategic cue over the painted brass frame. City/banner clearance, hover and selection outlines, improvement marks, and improvement-under-resource-under-unit/banner layering remain intact. Far zoom keeps the simple, high-contrast bonus/strategic category marks, as specified by the zoom-tier rule. UI resource icons and the Guide use the same shipped PNGs (`app/src/assets/resources/README.md`).

- **Landmarks (user, 2026-10-24):** a finished Great Work is a landmark on its own tile beside its city (the painted unlock miniature at about 1.75 hex wide; a gilt-ringed icon badge at the far tier). Its construction plays through its own painted stages (foundations, half built with scaffold hugging the real structure, nearly complete), made from the finished art with an image edit so the framing never jumps (`assets/construction/README.md`). See legatum-ui.
- **Improvements on the map (user, 2026-10-04):** variant C at normal zoom: the land itself transforms (fields ploughed into the hex, a mine cut into the hill, terraces, a quarry face), drawn in the atlas ink style. Variant B when zoomed out: a small corner badge per improved tile. The mock was a direction only; the shipped art must look "sophisticated and serious like the game itself": engraved line and hatching in the map's pen weights and earth palette, no cartoon shapes or bright flat fills. A Claude agent reviews screenshots at both zoom tiers against this skill before it merges.
- **Improvements, as built (branch `claude/p8-improvements`, 2026-10-06; `app/src/map/improvementArt.ts`, reviewed and revised the same day):** drawn live as small cached sprites per (kind, form, size), clipped to the hex and under every lens, overlay, unit and banner, with the colours and pen weights taken from `style.ts` (tone inks, terrain palette, `pen.hair/thin/line`); no solid fills. Normal zoom (hex radius 22 px and up) changes the land: Farm is a light plot of ink contour furrows over a pale tilled wash that keeps the terrain colour, under a light hedge (Irrigated Farm: more rows and a visible cut water channel with sluice); Pasture is a post-and-rail paddock with a single rail, trodden ground, grass strokes and a gate gap (Ranch: a second rail and a trough); Quarry is a crescent of stepped benches cut into one side of the hex, each riser hatched, a few squared blocks on the floor (Deep Quarry: another bench and a crane); Mine is a hillside shaded on its far flank with a timbered adit whose dark is ink hatching, rails and a spoil heap (Deep Mine: a light headframe). **Zoomed out the badge is a ring on the resource's own marker, not a second glyph:** a solid ink ring means improved, a dashed one dormant, on the medallion (18 to 22 px) and on the category dot (below 18 px), so the two read as a pair and nothing competes with the map pass's far-tier marks. **Dormant** is one shared check (`isDormant` in `app/src/improvements.ts`: owned, known, unimproved, strategic deposits included): the medallion fades to 62% with the dashed ring, and the picker count and tile card use the same check. The glyphs `improvement-farm/pasture/quarry/mine` serve the picker rows.

Same engraving language as the seals (`heraldry/src/kit.mjs`), but a bare charge with no rim, so an emblem is never mistaken for a faction seal. Files `assets/rewards/emblem-<kind>.svg`, generated by `rewards/src/build-rewards.mjs`. What each means:

  - `founded` (city founded): the cornerstone, relay star carved on its top, set on the claimed hex with survey stakes.
  - `growth` (a city grows to an unlock): a sprout crowned by the relay star rising from its hex.
  - `milestone` (milestones and titles, anything that adds to the chronicle's VP): the relay star in a laurel tied with a ribbon.
  - `greatwork` (a Great Work completed): a stepped monument under the relay star in the same laurel.
  - `tech` (a partner's technology): the relay star within its orbits. Your own technologies keep the unlock card's rayed seal (accent ring, branch icon), which already shares the double-ring frame.
  - The faction goal keeps the faction crest (it is the faction's own moment). Victory has its own takeover. The lost-city moment is told by the alarm screen first, so its card only exists in the report.

Military counters are the muted seat colour over parchment (about 78%) with a thicker darker rim and the glyph on a small parchment inset, so the colour is the first read (lead, 2026-10-06: the first pass, thin seat rims on parchment, made red, purple and green hard to tell apart; "identify empires everywhere" outranks tint purity for small counters). Civilian counters stay parchment chits with a seat rim, so military and civilian also read. The owner roundel is a parchment disc with a seat ring and an ink numeral; banner population squares are the seat colour thinned toward parchment, ink numeral, with a small three-heads population mark before them and no seat-number circle (2026-11-07, `atlasBanner` in `sprites.ts`); every named banner carries the faction glyph at any zoom. `e2e/art-map.spec.ts` "seat colour strip" shoots all nine factions side by side. ## Process

**Natural wonders (lead choice, 2026-10-10; real-landmark update, 2026-10-10):** Use recognizable real-world landmarks from `docs/design/natural-wonders/`, chosen to read clearly on a hex map. The strategic map shows style B, an ink-and-wash landmark integrated with the terrain at normal zoom; the far tier uses a compact engraved mark distinct from a ruin diamond and resource dot. The tile card and first-discovery notice use style A, the painted vignette. The art stays below roads, banners and units on the atlas. A target player liked Civilization's recognizable strategic-map landmarks with bonuses, and the user chose real natural wonders over the invented concepts.

1. **Mocks first:** 2–3 variants as screenshots in the real map or screen context, at 1000x600 and 1440x900, before implementing any new visual feature. The user picks.
2. **Build to the house style,** reusing tokens, pen weights and icon grid.
3. **Review against this skill** from screenshots (headless Playwright only; never the app's browser pane). Codex-built art is never final until a Claude agent has looked at it. Every branch that changes art, or the names, kinds or wiring art depends on, gets the `legatum-art-reviewer` agent before it merges (user, 2026-11-22); the gate requires its marker. It judges changed art beside its set (`tools/art/art-review.py sheet`) and looks for art the branch should have changed but didn't (`art-review.py impact`: renamed or new kinds, wiring with no file, deleted art still listed).
4. **Record** the pick and any new rule here in the same change.

## Consistency audit (checklist)

Run before a playtest that adds art, and as a whole-game pass when asked. Inventory, then flag each item:
- the same concept drawn two ways (icon vs map sprite vs tooltip);
- icons off the 24 grid, filled where others are monoline, or with hard-coded colours;
- map sprites with their own line weights, saturated fills, gradients or shadows;
- overlays too opaque to read terrain through;
- elements that are noise or vanish at the zoomed-out tier;
- faction art that doesn't read at a glance or doesn't follow from the story;
- raster art with mismatched technique, palette or framing;
- placeholder or emoji art still shipping.
Report each finding with a screenshot path, the rule it breaks and a proposed fix; group fixes by effort.
- **Owned-but-unworked territory is player-tinted hatch** inside the border line (never neutral ink, which reads as fog). Below a zoom threshold it becomes a flat player wash, because constant-pixel hatching turns to noise.
- **Improvement art (review, 2026-10-06):** hair and thin pen weights, no solid black fills, and it must never read as a landmark (a quarry isn't an amphitheatre). Each tech upgrade gets a visible step. Zoomed out, an improvement "badge" is a different shape from the city seal and the resource dot.
- **Every lens is translucent,** including Borders, Threat, Discovery and Movement; audit them all, not just Settle and Resources. Improvement art may skip its fine strokes under a unit counter so the unit and the land don't merge (8.1 review).
- **Polish pass (8.1 review, `claude/p82-polish`):** the Borders lens is an owner wash at 0.3 (`lens.ownerWash`) with a hair-weight owner line; owner numerals sit only on border tiles and only at normal zoom (`nearOnly`), and the far tier is tint alone. Improvement art is clipped out of the unit counters' footprint (`counterFootprint` in `improvementArt.ts`) so a unit never merges with furrows or benches. Zoomed out, a dormant resource marker fades to 45% with a longer dash (3.5 on, 3.5 off) so it never reads as the solid ring of an improved one.
- **Dormant markers at the far tier keep at least about 55% opacity,** so they read on the palest terrain (hills, desert) (8.2 review).
- **Goals 3 art (2026-10-07):** `unit-heir` (crowned head over a cloak with the relay star cut in) and `building-engine` (engine block with three cylinders and a flywheel; not the Foundry's flame). On the map the Heir is a civilian chit with a gilt inner rim; held by a rival it carries Byzantium's glyph on a disc in Byzantium's seat colour at its lower-right corner, and his zoomed-out pip wears a gilt ring (`sprites.ts` `HeirMark`).
- **Decisions (user, 2026-10-10).** The Japan and elf items below are SUPERSEDED the same day by the historical re-theme. Factions are real peoples, portraits pair a man and a woman in each people's most recognisable era, and there's no fantasy or alien art.
  - **Resources:** painted medallions.
  - **Natural wonders:** ink-and-wash landmarks on the map, so they read as geography and stay distinct from the medallions, and painted vignettes on cards and notices (lead's call; the user liked both).
  - **Mongols elves:** being explored. A target player wants a "cute girl" faction (adult, warm and endearing, never sexualised) now that the Japan are insects.

- **New unlock paintings match their set (2026-11-07 art review):** fit the same footprint, earth base and frame coverage as the others in the set (within about 10%), and check at 64 px in greyscale beside the predecessor (Oil Well, Quartz Mine and Saltpetre Works landed paler and smaller than Deep Mine; follow-up).
- **Construction stages match the finished piece's colour, not only its outline** (the Swiss Redoubt's stage 3 is warm tan, the finished fort cool grey; follow-up).

## Finished art, not abstractions (user, 2026-10-10)
"A target player loves the art and beauty of those things, so we need to fix that... it's ok to use placeholders when we're iterating, but once we've nailed it down, we should make sure the art gets updated properly."
- **Placeholders are fine while a feature is iterating.** Once its design is settled (the user has approved it, or it has shipped in a playtest build with no open redesign), its art must be finished, painterly and covetable in the house style. A settled feature that still shows abstract shapes (cubes, plain glyphs) is a defect.
- **Cities:** art per era and per size tier, so cities visibly grow with population and change architecture each era (Civ style). **Done (2026-10-10, `claude/era-takeover`):** the 28 painted miniatures (`app/src/assets/cities/`) are wired into the map by `map/cityArt.ts` and `terrain.ts`; the abstract cubes are gone.
- **The art audit** is recurring: once per release (not weekly, since 2026-11-22 every art PR gets the art reviewer), the ops supervisor tours the screens and a resolved turn and lists every remaining placeholder, abstraction or set misfit, ranked by how visible it is in play, and queues the art.
- **Era advancement** gets celebration art: a full-screen takeover with music. The board changes too, as cities and units take on the new era's look.
- **Art audit status (first audit 2026-10-10):** it ranked 25 placeholders and abstractions (the report and its crops were working files and are not in the repository). Queued from it: `title-keyart-historical`, `terrain-feature-art`, `moment-vignettes`, `camp-ruin-map-art` (each starts with a comparison sheet, no wiring). Deferred until the era overhaul and historical rename land: units, techs, buildings, cities, Great Works, era and victory art, faction ornaments and crests. Re-run the audit after the era overhaul.

## Art direction decisions (designer and a target player, 2026-10-10)
- **Cities:** A, painted isometric miniatures. Seven eras by four size tiers (hamlet, town, city, metropolis).

## Combat animations (2026-10-24, `claude/combat-fx`; details in `docs/design/combat-fx/README.md`)
- A resolved turn plays back in rules order: the volley first (shots staggered about 190 ms), then the moves round by round with each clash on its step, then losses and captures. Each damage cue and HP bar waits for its own blow.
- **The look is engraved, not particles.** Smoke and dust are engraved clouds (round lobes in one ink outline over a pale wash, hatched on the shaded side). Soft gradient puffs read as airbrushed smudges. Impacts are woodcut sunbursts (tapered ink wedges), and red goes only on wedges where blood was drawn: a red-hearted flash read as a target. Dark missiles carry a vellum halo.
- What flies follows the era: arrows, bolts, musket smoke, cannonballs, shells, drones, a bomber pass. A city's walls answer by its owner's era.
- **No real-world national insignia on shared unit art (train-1102 review, 2026-11-02).** Every faction flies the same aircraft sprite, so the US stars on the Fighter and Bomber plan views put Mongol and Japanese planes under American markings.
