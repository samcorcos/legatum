# Art: art direction, terrain and unit art

The 2026-10-10 art direction decisions (cities, units, painted vs engraved), wonder and terrain art, organic terrain, unit distinctness across eras, siege props.

Moved verbatim from SKILL.md on 2026-11-22 (original order kept). The current rules are in SKILL.md; where this file disagrees with SKILL.md, SKILL.md wins.

## Art direction decisions (designer and a target player, 2026-10-10)
- **Cities:** A, painted isometric miniatures. Seven eras by four size tiers (hamlet, town, city, metropolis).
- **Units:** A, painted miniatures, as a zoom hybrid. At normal zoom, a painted full figure on a coloured base. At far zoom, a coloured roundel with a clear class silhouette and the owner rim, so class and owner read at a glance.
- **Zoom-tier rule, general (user: "that rule probably applies to a lot of things, so use your judgment"):** up close, painted detail; far out, a simplified high-contrast mark that keeps class or type and owner legible. Apply it to cities, units, camps, ruins, wonders and resources.
- **Painted versus engraved:** things you act on (cities, units, resources, camps, ruins) are painted and pop. Geography (terrain features, natural-wonder landmarks on the map) is engraved or ink-and-wash and recedes.
- **Camps and ruins:** B, the painted jewel.
- **Title screen:** the target player likes all three directions; lead's pick is C, the road at dawn (a hopeful beginning). The subtitle must lose the sci-fi "star empire" text.
- **Era change imagery:** approved; the target player loves it. **Wired (2026-10-10):** the seven key-art paintings fill the screen in the era-up takeover (legatum-ui, "Entering an era").
- **City art, as built:** a sprite is the owner's era x the city's size tier (hamlet 1-3, town 4-7, city 8-12, metropolis 13+ population). The 512 px canvas is shared: its ground oval is 93% of the width and rests on y = 454, so the renderer draws it 92 world px wide (the oval about 85, a hex and a third, as Civ cities spill past their tile; 84 until 2026-11-22) with the oval's middle 18 px below the hex centre, never clipped by a chunk. A town in sight is drawn above the territory tint (under it, a grown city came out paler than the farms round it); a remembered town stays under the fog wash. **A town is never paler than the improvements around it, and nothing covers its roofs at normal zoom:** the banner block (plate plus meters) ends above the roofline (see legatum-ui). The terrain stays visible around it (no paved-hex fill). Resolution follows the drawn size (64, 128 or the 512 master). **Zoom tier:** at the far tier (the same 22 px per hex radius as the seals) the shared `marks/<tier>-mark` sprite replaces the detailed one, and the seal still carries owner and name. Walls are painted overlays by era (Modern and Future use the Industrial one, the Switzerland' Customs Bastion the Renaissance star fort). A missing file falls back to the nearest finished era, then the nearest size, never to a cube (`cityStem`, `missingCityArt()`); a sprite still decoding is skipped for that frame and the chunk repaints when it arrives.
- **Terrain features, round 1 (in-game screenshots, 2026-10-10):** the target player liked B and A-light, but the user found them "way too busy and also it's kind of hard to tell them apart because they're so busy." Wanted: a middle ground like Civ 6 (he supplied a screenshot of that game as the reference; it is another game's art and is not kept in the repository), "more realistic and beautiful than the fully abstract current version, but not like a fully drawn art scene". Terrain types must be easily distinct by silhouette and colour, with open ground left so units read first. Round 2: three stylized directions (S1 painted toy, S2 flat illustrated, S3 watercolour sprite).

- **Unit art wired (2026-10-10):** direction A (painted miniatures, zoom hybrid) is live on the map and in the unit panel. The stone base wears the owner's colour twice (a wash over the stone and a ring beneath it); far zoom uses the roundel SVG with its `currentColor` as the owner colour plus a 2.6 px owner ring so seats stay apart at 16 px. Engraved icons for the new lineage live in `app/src/assets/icons` (`unit-horseman` ... `unit-bombard`, historical metaphors: bow, pike and buckler, crossbow, musket, bayonet rifle, steel helmet, robot head, quadcopter). Missing: Fighter and Bomber have painted art but no unit yet; the Legionary and Thorn Hunter have no historical figure (the supplied Legionary is Prussian) and use the generic Pikeman and Archer.
- **The five era units are painted (2026-10-11, `astra/unit-art-five-new`).** Phalangite, Composite Archer, Cataphract, Arquebusier and Cuirassier each have their own miniature, panel portrait and roundel (`app/src/unitArt.ts`; prompts in `app/src/assets/units/prompts/`). `unitArt.alpha.test.ts` fails any unit PNG with opaque corners. UI review (2026-10-11) minors, still open: the five roundels' weapons run over the owner rim and the Cataphract and Cuirassier carry parchment-coloured marks (redraw as ink-only silhouettes inside the rim; the Arquebusier reads as a tick at 18 px); Composite Archer shares the Archer's pose and the Phalangite's sarissa is not visibly longer than the Spearman's spear. Rules: check every new roundel at 16, 24 and 48 px beside its predecessor and successor, ink only and inside the rim; a new miniature must differ from its predecessor by silhouette or pose, not only colour, because at 45 px colour is what reads; tall thin figures need a cropped or larger build-card thumbnail (about 24 px today).


**Wonder art on the map, as built (2026-10-11, `claude/wonder-visibility`):** the ink-and-wash landmark is drawn about 2.5 hex radii wide right after the terrain and under everything else the map draws (improvements, lens tint, yields, medallions, borders, units, banners), at about half alpha under any lens; it carries a "+25% [yield]" parchment pill on its own tile in every lens, and at the far tier the compact mark gains a pip in the yield's colour. A painting that spills past its hex must never be drawn above another tile's marks. See legatum-ui, "Map bonuses are visible and explained".

**Map art needs real alpha (2026-10-11):** the natural-wonder map sprites shipped fully opaque and drew as black squares in 8.8. Every map sprite must have a transparent background, so check the alpha channel (e.g. `python3 -c "from PIL import Image; print(Image.open(p).getextrema())"`) before wiring art in.

**Terrain should read as landscape, not tiles (user, 2026-10-11):** "a forest that is multiple tiles should feel like it is a forest covering multiple tiles, not a bunch of forest tiles pushed together... a mountain range that is two or three mountains should feel different than a mountain range that is 12 tiles... something that makes the map feel a little bit more organic rather than hexes pushed together."
- **Forests:** render by connected region, with a continuous canopy across shared edges, dense interiors and thinning edges. **Every feature tile must read as its type at a glance: forests, hills, mountains, marsh and any painted feature (user, 2026-10-17, hills: "I didn't realise these were hills because only a small amount of the hex is covered in hills... if there are similar issues elsewhere in the game fix those too").** Edge thinning never leaves most of a feature hex looking like open ground. **Every forest tile must still read as forest, including edge tiles (user, 2026-10-17: "the 3 hexes that are technically part of the same forest kind of look like those hexes are not forests... we don't have to overcorrect here, but maybe a little more trees on tiles that are forested on the edges").** Edge thinning is gentle: trees cover most of an edge hex, the gaps face the open neighbour rather than the hex centre, and a hex-sized patch of bare grass inside a forest tile is a bug. Built (2026-10-17, `organicTerrain.ts`, measured by `terrainCoverage.test.ts` on the sprite alpha): wood edge hexes cover about 0.75 of the hex against 0.98 inside (floors: mean above 0.68, none below 0.5), every wood tile holds at least three clusters on its inland side, and a grove of three hexes or fewer draws a main cluster plus two small companions per hex. Hills (built 2026-10-17, playtest 8.9.3) follow the same rule with their own numbers, because the hill sprites are only about 60% opaque: lattice step 1.15 R, cluster scale 0.9, edge keep 0.85, a floor of four clusters per tile, lone hexes and groves of up to three get a main cluster plus three companions; edge hexes cover about 0.7, interiors 0.84 (they were 0.28 and 0.6), arms above 0.5, a lone hill above 0.6. UI review (2026-10-17): hill clusters may overhang a hex edge by about 0.15 hex at most and should not poke into a hex of another feature (the fogged forest/hills border shows a little bleed, watch it); in a big block of hills the colour stripes are the loudest texture on the map, so if it is too loud reduce per-cluster contrast, not coverage, and keep the cased grid countable inside it. Not yet shot: hills with a city, fogged hills with a unit, Threat/Movement lenses on hills. Do not go denser: at 0.9 interior a range turns into a busy blanket that hides resource markers. Mountains already cleared the floors (their peaks are big and opaque) and are only guarded by the test; Desert and Tundra are tinted biome colours with a glyph across the hex, and read fine at the edge. `e2e/terrain-edges-shots.spec.ts` shoots the edge hexes of every feature in a real save (EDGE_SHOTS, EDGE_SAVE, EDGE_PREFIX, EDGE_NAME). Review a terrain-density change in three states (visible, fogged, far zoom) on one fixed camera before and after, and shoot a 1-3 hex grove on open ground, not only the unit test (the 8.9.2 save had none).
- **Mountains:** big ranges become one massif along a spine; small groups stay a few peaks. **A lone peak or small group (up to 3 tiles) stands on the land, never as a hex sticker (art audit, 2026-11-22):** its hex takes the commonest open neighbour's ground colour, and its edges to open ground keep the faint grid line, not the cased one; edges inside the group stay cased. **A lone peak or small group (up to 3 tiles) stands on the land, never as a hex sticker (art audit, 2026-11-22):** its hex takes the commonest open neighbour's ground colour, and its edges to open ground keep the faint grid line, not the cased one; edges inside the group stay cased.
- **Base:** soft biome transitions.
- **Legibility:** every tile stays readable under lenses. Explored on `claude/terrain-organic`, round 3.
- **Round 3, built (2026-10-11, `?terrain=s2&organic=1`, any sprite direction; sheet `docs/design/terrain-art/round3-organic.png`):** regions from explored tiles only; woods and hills of 4+ tiles scatter overlapping clusters on a world lattice with a ragged, thinning edge and a canopy wash; groves and ranges of 1-3 tiles stay one cluster per hex; ranges get spine peaks, saddles and (8+) foothills and a stone wash; hills are drawn firmer; the grid is a faint line over the art (the veil's pale edge line is what cut woods into tiles). Lead's recommendation: adopt on S2 after the user sees the sheet; the next increment is art (single trees, wood-edge strips, multi-hex massif peaks; prompts in the README), because generated ranges are one hex wide. Superseded the same day: organic S2 is the default (see Terrain decided).
- **Organic review rules (UI review, 2026-10-11):** a region's wash (canopy, rock, earth) stays inside the region and never darkens a tile of another type; check it on a 2x crop of a forest/plains edge, because a full shot at 0.66 hides the smudge. At far zoom every feature tile keeps a mark at least as bold as the per-hex far mark (organic far uses the per-hex marks over the wash). "Organic" covers open ground too: biome colour boundaries should not read as hex cells. Answered by the user: the grid stays countable (see Terrain decided).

**Unlocks must look covetable (user, 2026-10-11, research screen):** "In Civ, I remember looking at the techs like this and seeing what they enable and thinking 'ooh, I want that'. This one doesn't have that same feeling because the things you're getting feel stale." Every unlock gets painted art at a size that sells it, everywhere it appears: research cards, production, city panel, era-up. That covers buildings, improvements, units, wonders and effects. Small engraved line icons are not enough. Directions in `docs/design/unlock-art/`. Three directions are on `docs/design/unlock-art/directions.png` (2026-10-11): A painted miniature (the city and unit family, frameless), B illuminated roundel, C painted scene plate. The user picked A (below). On the card, the unlock art goes under the tech name, above cost and boost, and the separate Details link moves onto the name to pay for the height at 1000x600.

**Every icon, medallion and miniature must be distinct from the others in its set at display size, in greyscale and for colour-blind players; the distinctness check must pass (user, 2026-10-11, playtest 8.8.1: "Coal and iron look way too similar. I can't tell them apart. All the icons, miniatures, etc need to be visually distinct").** Distinguish by silhouette first, then colour family and value, never by tint alone: two things that differ only in hue are the same thing to a colour-blind player and in greyscale. `python3 tools/art/distinctness.py --render --check` (Node 22 on PATH; guarded by `app/src/artDistinct.test.ts`) draws every set at the size it is shown (resources 14-32 px, miniatures 45, roundels and icons 16-24, seals 40-56, cities 48, unlocks 64), scores every pair (mean CIE Lab difference over the content in the worst of normal, protan, deutan, tritan and greyscale sight; shape and structure for the single-colour icons) and fails when a pair reaches its set's threshold in `tools/art/distinctness.json` (that file's `note` says how the thresholds were calibrated and what the accepted pairs are). A new asset must not look like any existing one in its set; repaint or redraw the newcomer (silhouette first) rather than raising a threshold. `--sheet FILE` shows the flagged pairs side by side, worst first. Painted art is judged at the smallest size it is drawn: Iron and Coal as 14 px discs were the same grey rock. Far-zoom resource marks differ by shape and value (Iron the red square, Coal the near-black disc ringed in red), the far-zoom unit roundels are class symbols (shield, bow, horse, tank, aircraft, drone, a civilian figure), and the colours a lens uses together stay 10 CIEDE2000 apart for every sight (`map/lensPalette.test.ts`).


**Later-era improvements use their own painted unlock miniature:** when a resource work already has a map painting, derive the 64/128/256 px unlock set from that transparent master and use its own slug on every card. A thematically similar earlier improvement is not a payoff placeholder.

**Terrain decided (user, 2026-10-11), built on `claude/terrain-default`:**
- **Organic S2 is the default look** ("yes adopt the look"). `?terrain=current` and `?organic=0` stay for comparisons; rounds 1 and 2 (A, B, A-light, S1, S3) are retired and recorded in `docs/design/terrain-art/README.md`. The sprites live in `app/public/terrain/`.
- **The grid stays visible inside big woods and ranges** ("yes you should still be able to see it"): an edge touching a wood, hills or a range is a cased line (pale casing 2.6 px under an ink core 1 px, `gridArt` in `style.ts`); open ground keeps the faint 0.14 line, and so does the edge between a lone peak or small group of mountains and open ground (`casedEdge` in `terrain.ts`: a full cased outline round one peak cut it out as a hex). Check any new terrain art with the grid on: tiles must be countable at 1000x600 at normal and far zoom.
- **Units must pop on any terrain** ("the miniatures for units are now pretty hard to see"). The map miniature is 20% larger, stands in a soft vellum pool and its stone base wears the owner's colour as a firm ring between two ink rules (`MINI` in `sprites.ts`). Not a dark halo, outline or sticker edge. Rule for any new terrain or unit art: shoot units on forest, range, hills, desert and grass at both window sizes and at far zoom; `e2e/terrain-units.spec.ts` asserts the contrast in a wood (base at least 1.3:1, the pool at least 1.8:1, calibrated: the old look measured 1.0).
- **Lenses win over terrain art:** the Settle lens fades the feature art to 38% (`ART_FADE`, `TerrainCache.setArtFade`), keeping the biome colour. The Yields overlay (off by default; the player can leave it on all game) fades it only to 72% (`ART_FADE_OVERLAY`); Resources and Borders do not fade it.
- Next art, queued for Luna: single trees, wood-edge strips, multi-hex massif peaks (prompts and the file layout in the README; wiring point `organicTerrain.ts` `planOrganic` and `terrainVariant.ts` `loadTerrainSprites`).

**Same-class units must look different across eras (user, 2026-10-11):** "The Hoplite and the plus-one era from the Hoplite looked nearly identical... I just attacked some things not realizing it." Same-class units of different eras must read differently in play: by silhouette and kit, by an era badge or trim, or by a relative-strength cue against your selected unit. Five options were mocked in the real map (`docs/design/unit-art/era-distinct.md`, sheet `era-distinct-options.png`; branch `claude/unit-distinct`, `?unitera=`). The lead recommends three together: a silhouette per era, an era tab on the base, and a strength cue against your selected unit (with the forecast headline). The user's pick is pending.
- **What the mocks taught:**
  - Repaint by pose or weapon angle, not kit: the Phalangite's sarissas lowered in a block, the Pikeman's braced diagonal pike, the Composite Archer kneeling, the Cataphract's couched lance, the Cuirassier's black horse.
  - Miniatures never grow past about 45 px (`tokenHalf` caps at 18), so fine detail never rescues a shared silhouette.
  - An era badge must not look like the route preview's red-ringed turn numerals.
  - An era-metal ring fights the owner colour on the base.
  - The lookalike check scored the confused Hoplite/Phalangite pair at only 0.38, so judge in-play crops as well as the score.

**Units across eras, decided (user, 2026-10-11: "Let's go with your recommendation for the units"):**
- Distinct silhouettes for every era (full lineage repaint).
- A square era-numeral plaque on the base, at normal zoom.
- Strength chips on enemy units the selected unit could attack this turn (the lead's call on scope), plus the forecast headline "one era newer: 26 vs your 22".
- Rejected: an era-metal ring (it fights the owner colour) and a raw strength number on every base (misleading).
- **As built (`claude/unit-distinct`, 2026-10-11):**
  - **Era plaque:** `map/unitEra.ts` `drawEraPlaque`. It sits on the front of the base, filled with the era's metal (`ERA_METAL`, lightness alternating era to era), the numeral light on dark metals and ink on light ones.
  - **Roundels:** silhouettes are scaled to 0.86 and clipped inside the owner rim (r 18.3), with no parchment knock-outs.
  - **Build cards:** use `<slug>-card.png`, a crop of the figure's upper body (`tools/art/unit-derivatives.py --card`); panels come from `--panel`.
  - **Removed:** the unused Legionary, Cairn Singer and spare drone art.
  - **Lineage repaint (`claude/unit-repaints`):** each era's figure has its own pose or weapon angle. Melee: Spearman or Hoplite (upright, round shield), Phalangite (a fan of pikes), Pikeman (steel, braced diagonal pike), Musketman (bayonet levelled at the hip), Rifleman, Infantry (grenade thrower), Humanoid Robot. Ranged: Archer (standing draw), Composite Archer (kneeling), Crossbowman (behind a pavise), Arquebusier (forked rest and smoke). Mounted: Horseman, Cataphract (couched lance), Knight, Cuirassier (black horse, pistol), Cavalry. Faction figures: Tagmatic Soldier (Byzantium; tall almond shield), Celtic Ambusher (crouched in bracken), Mongol Outrider (Parthian shot). The lookalike check enforces `units-miniature`. Its accepted pairs: Hoplite/Spearman (the same unit), aircraft (never on the map), Robot/Settler, Artillery/Bombard.
  - **Miniature rules (repaint review, 2026-10-11):**
    - A figure stands on a full-size base, with a foot or hoof planted on it; a figure floating beside a small disc fails.
    - Weapon lines are at least 2 px at the 48 px size, in a dark value.
    - A wide or low pose is still at least about 36 px tall at 48 px, so a newer unit never reads as smaller or weaker.
    - Smoke and other effects carry an ink edge or a grey-blue value, never pure white.
    - Judge each figure on desert, hills and grass (tan-on-tan fails), and in greyscale against same-era units of the other classes.
    - Open from that review: the Phalangite fades on pale ground, the Arquebusier floats beside a small base, and the Mongol Outrider's base reads as a separate disc.
  - **Repaint rule:** when repainting a unit, change its pose or weapon angle, and check the new figure against its neighbours at 45 px in greyscale. The lookalike check also flags silhouettes (`gist`).

- **Siege gear on upgraded units:** a military unit with Siege displays the era-appropriate siege prop beside it on the map (`app/src/siegeProps.ts`, art in `app/src/assets/siege-props/`). Props are standalone paintings placed inside the hex; the miniature itself is unchanged. A rival’s gear tier is inferred from its era because its technologies are private.
- **Gear matches the unit's own era, never just the technology (user, 2026-10-28, 8.9.15.1: an Industrial Rifleman beside a medieval siege tower, a Field Cannon beside a bombard):** "old timey cannons next to field cannons, and old timey siege towers weren't used by riflemen... something feels off." Every prop, accessory or effect drawn with a unit must belong to that unit's period, and a unit that is itself a gun never gets a second, older gun. Check new gear against the latest unit that can wear it.
- **Siege props stay inside their hex (user, 2026-10-19, 8.9.6, a covered Field Cannon beside Bellinzona):** "it kind of works... but the unit art overlaps too much with the hex to its right." The Engineer's cover prop (the cannon or siege tower drawn with a covered unit) spilled about half a hex into the neighbour, onto the next unit. A unit, its prop and its badges fit inside its own hex at every zoom; shrink or tuck the prop behind the figure rather than widening the group.
