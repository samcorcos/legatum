# Art: November rulings, heraldry and launch audit

Art is the lead's call, recolours are placeholders, the unit-sweep rulings, figure bases, heraldry as built, launch audit fixes and launch reviews.

Moved verbatim from SKILL.md on 2026-11-22 (original order kept). The current rules are in SKILL.md; where this file disagrees with SKILL.md, SKILL.md wins.

## Art is the lead's call, end to end (user, 2026-11-15)
His words, on the full art audit: "don't ask me about the art stuff, just fix it please. I think you hvae anough information to be able to do it properly at this point." The lead audits, repaints, approves and ships art without asking him, including look-alike pairs: this replaces "ask him for any new pair" on `tools/art/distinctness.json`. The bar does not move: a recolour or copy is repainted, never accepted; a pair goes on the accepted list only when both are real paintings of their own subject, with the reason written beside it. Sub-agents still do not edit that file; the lead does. Tell him what changed in the build note, do not ask first.

## A recolour is a placeholder, not art (user, 2026-11-14)
His words, after seeing the Modern era sheet: "you still have some placeholders. biplane and howitzer are just different colors for the same art. can you do a sweep of all the unit art and make sure it meets the requirements?" The Biplane was the Fighter's painting in tan and the Howitzer the Artillery's in sand. Every unit needs its own painting of its own subject (a Biplane has two wings), and so do its icon and roundel. A recoloured or copied piece never counts as done, and a pair that passes only because the tint moved the score, or because it sits on the accepted list, is still a placeholder: repaint it and take the pair off the list. This narrows the 2026-11-14 delegation on the seven look-alike pairs: it covered pairs of two real paintings, not recolours.

## Rulings after the unit art sweep (user, 2026-11-15: "yes to all three")
- **Remove the accepted-pair entries the repaints made unnecessary** from `tools/art/distinctness.json` (miniatures: Infantry/Trench Infantry, Biplane/Fighter, Artillery/Howitzer, barbarian Crossbowman/Trench Infantry, Biplane/Stealth Bomber; icons: Artillery/Howitzer, Biplane/Fighter, Infantry/Trench Infantry, Landship/Tank; the stale Biplane+Bomber+Fighter roundel entry). Landship/Tank miniatures stays with a true reason (two paintings, close silhouettes). This approval covers these entries only; new pairs still need his say-so.
- **The Infantry is repainted in WWII kit.** It is the WWII unit and was painted with a Brodie helmet and puttees; Trench Infantry owns the WWI look.
- **Roundels stay class marks.** A roundel says what kind of unit it is, the miniature says which one; units of one class may share a roundel drawing. Do not draw one roundel per unit.
- **Thin weapons and already-transparent generations (2026-11-15, Egypt and Rome art):** key a figure with a long thin weapon (a pilum, a pike) with `unit-art.py --edge-key`, or the stock key's pull-in leaves a floating tip; composite an already-transparent generation onto flat magenta before keying, or its coloured edge fringe survives. A spear-and-shield miniature that matches a neighbour's silhouette is fixed by a new pose (a low wide crouch), not by mirroring.

## Unit figures: fix what is wrong, do not force a pose (user delegated the call, 2026-11-16: "i trust your judgment")
- The set is mixed by design: some figures stand at rest, many are in action (charging, firing, kneeling, at the gallop). A figure is repainted when something it holds or wears is wrong, its anatomy is broken, or it does not read at map size; not because its pose differs from a neighbour's. The lead's 2026-11-15 instruction "every unit stands upright and calm" was a mistake drawn from two figures and made the second art pass repaint 15 units standing; those 15 stay (each fixed a real error), the rule does not.
- Action poses are welcome where they read better at 48 px; a standing figure with a thin weapon reads slight.
- **Every figure stands on the same base** (one plain round disc, the same warm cream stone, the same height). Mixed bases make the set look like two sets.
- Review standard (user, 2026-11-15): for each thing a figure holds or wears, name it and its real form for that people and period, and look for it in a crop of the painting before accepting; never repeat the painter's description unchecked.

## Heraldry as built (launch audit, 2026-11-21)
- **Eleven peoples, one charge each, three tiers from one drawing.** `app/src/assets/heraldry/src/build-seals.mjs` draws each seal and its 24-grid glyph with the engraving kit (`kit.mjs`); `render-crests.mjs` renders the 512 px gold-on-navy crest from the seal in Chromium with SVG lighting. Nothing in the heraldry folder is painted any more, so there are no crest prompts. Change a charge in `build-seals.mjs`, then run both scripts; never edit a seal, glyph or crest by hand.
- **The charges:** Switzerland a cowbell on its collar, India a lotus, Byzantium a processional cross, Germany a double-headed eagle, the Celts a torc, the Mongols a recurved bow with an arrow nocked, Sparta a Corinthian helmet, Athens an owl, the Aztecs a New Fire reed bundle on temple steps, Egypt a winged sun disk over a temple pylon, Rome a legionary standard within a laurel. The README's table is the record.
- **What a charge may not be:** a real modern national flag or state emblem (no present-day flag device or state arms), a swastika-like or otherwise loaded sign (Sparta wears a helmet, not the lambda), or a copy of another game's faction icon. Generic historical objects only.
- **No per-faction special cases in code.** Every faction's crest is `crest-<id>-512.png` and every icon is looked up by its own name. Egypt once borrowed India's icon and Rome's crest came from a different file name; both special cases hid missing art. A new people ships with its own seal, glyph and crest or it is not added.
- **Distinctness is kept by redrawing.** The thresholds in `tools/art/distinctness.json` were not raised: the closest glyph pair is Byzantium/Rome (0.56 at 16 px) and the closest seals are Aztecs/Rome (0.66 at 56 px) against a limit of 0.70. A glyph has to survive 16 px: thin strokes (a bowstring, an arrow shaft, a staff) vanish there and were thickened until they read.
- **A retired people leaves no files behind.** Japan's seal, glyph, crest and portrait were removed with its last reference; a stale `seal-japan.svg` made the crest renderer produce a crest for a faction that does not exist.

## Launch audit fixes (2026-11-21)
- **Icons drew the retired setting under historical names.** Eleven were redrawn (the rice storehouse, microscope, scroll, water mill, library book, cannon, colonnade, tracked hull, telescope and quartz); the README rows for four unit icons described drawings that had already been replaced. When a concept is renamed, redraw the icon and correct its README row in the same change; `preview.html` is regenerated with `node app/src/assets/icons/build-preview.mjs`.
- **Any raster shown as a plain `<img>` needs real alpha, and a test.** The natural-wonder `-a-512` vignettes were paintings on an opaque black square and showed a black box on the parchment tile card. `tools/art/wonder-vignette-key.py` removes only the black connected to the border (so basalt and deep water inside the painting stay), and `app/src/wonderArt.alpha.test.ts` checks every wonder PNG the way `unitArt.alpha.test.ts` checks the units.
- **Unused art is deleted, after a search that includes computed names.** Icons and unlock art are looked up as `building-${kind}`, so a file with no literal reference may still be live (`building-broodhall`, `ui-sakoku`). Check the enum of kinds and the tests before deleting.
- **Never name a real maker's product in a prompt or a description, and never model a figure on one.** The Hunter-Killer was prompted and documented with a commercial robot's product name and its body follows that robot closely. It is queued for a repaint as a generic armed quadruped.
- **Queued repaints (need image generation; the prompts are in the launch-audit hand-off, not yet in the tree):** the Hunter-Killer; the Aztec portrait first, then the European men who share one face; five unlock pieces with no ground (`building-imperialdiwan`, `building-market-india`, `effect-machinery`, `effect-mathematics`, `effect-metallurgy`) and the faded group (Eiffel Tower, Hagia Sophia, ITER reactor, Pyramids, Relay Uplink, Planetary Foundry, World Exchange, Stock Exchange, Rome's walls, Egypt's granary, oil well, quartz mine, saltpetre works).
- **Provenance has one home:** `app/src/assets/PROVENANCE.md`. Add or correct a row there when a class of art is added or remade; `LICENSE-ART` only points to it.

## Launch reviews (2026-11-21)
- **Judge a crest at the size the app draws it** (40 to 80 px), not on the 240 px sheet. Anything shown under 64 px needs heavier relief or a small-crest variant; Rome's signum, the Celts' torc and the Mongols' bow are thin at pin size and are the first to redo.
- **Rejected tagline (user, 2026-11-21):** "Real peoples. Simultaneous turns. An AI that never cheats." The banner, share image and teaser all use "Empires rise and fall. Some are remembered."
- **Launch media is recorded from the running game** with `docs/media/src/refresh.sh`; anything staged for a shot (scripted orders, a patched save) is listed in the kit's README and the copy says "recorded from the running game", never "unedited".
