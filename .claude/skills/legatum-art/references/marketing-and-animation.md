# Art: marketing, trailers, combat and construction animation, painted sets

Trailer and tagline rulings, music, combat animations, construction rounds, wonder and future-unit painting, figure review standard, barbarians, portraits, renamed units, the Early Tank.

Moved verbatim from SKILL.md on 2026-11-22 (original order kept). The current rules are in SKILL.md; where this file disagrees with SKILL.md, SKILL.md wins.

## Marketing: trailers, README media and taglines (user, 2026-10-24, after the first teaser)
User verdict on teaser v1: "weird choices were made here. please do better."
- **It must make you want to play:** "It should feel inspiring. it should show combat, it should feel cool." Never "here are some random images and some random and often not that important parts of gameplay".
- **Lead with the exciting stuff:** combat, armies, sieges and captures, empires at scale, era-defining moments. Don't feature minor UI mechanics: a whole shot on "you can decline a call to arms" was called "not an interesting feature".
- **Real gameplay only, from real games.** Sparse test fixtures (two cities on an empty map, the hint bar showing) read as "clearly not real gameplay". Use real mid- or late-game states with lots happening.
- **Epic audio:** the teaser needs music (it was silent), plus sound effects where they help.
- **Smooth motion:** zooms and pans must be fluid. The v1 captures were "really choppy" and "low quality when zooming". Capture at 60 fps with real-time or frame-stepped rendering, never a stitched screenshot sequence.
- **Taglines sell the fantasy, not the feature list.** "Real peoples. Simultaneous turns. An AI that never cheats." is "such a weird choice... doesn't feel like the marketing of an actual game." Write like a game trailer: aspiration and stakes.
- **Music direction (user, 2026-10-24):** no harpsichord ("i don't care for harpsichords"). The menu track should feel intense: epic orchestral, with drums and brass rather than gentle or courtly. The previous menu track was rejected as too courtly and not intense enough.
- **Soundtrack (user, 2026-10-24):** "Eclipse Legion" by vitalezzz (CC0, https://opengameart.org/content/eclipse-legion) is the standard track for the game opening and main background music. Its intense later section plays "when you're in a war": the music switches to it while the player's team is at war, and back when at peace.
- **Launch marketing wants several videos, not one trailer (user, 2026-11-01):** for the README and the X post announcing that Legatum exists, he asked for "at least 5 distinct promo and gameplay videos that make people feel like they would want to play this game", in multiple variants: trailers plus gameplay videos. The v3 brief was a working file on the lead's machine and is not in the repository.
- **The main trailer shows the whole game, not only war (user, 2026-11-02):** "the trailer-main is bascially just a bunch of combat footage. it doesn't show anything about getting technologies, making buildings, building an economy, etc... it seems super focused on this one aspect of the game, which is bombarding cities." "Lead with combat" (2026-10-24) means combat is the hook, not the whole film. The main trailer follows the 4X arc: explore about 15%, expand 15%, build and research 30%, compete 30%, victory 10%. The building side must still move: fog lifting, borders spreading, painted construction stages rising in a time-lapse, tech and era cards, city art changing by era. A war-only cut is a separate variant (Conquest, the X cut).
- **Trailer tagline (user, 2026-10-24):** "Empires rise and fall. Some are remembered." The Legacy direction, in the user's own wording. Write captions and the title card around it.
- **Trailer v2, non-combat cut (user, 2026-10-24):** "extremely boring as a trailer." Zooming in and out over still map images must not be the centrepiece. **The trailer needs animation**, either in the game itself or made for the trailer. Showing menus, picking techs, the feeling of gaining something important, and era transitions is fine and is gameplay. Study why Civ VI and VII, Stellaris and similar trailers make you want to play, and work from that.
- **Trailer craft, from the 2026-10-24 study of six 4X trailers** (`docs/media/v2/research-trailers.md`):
  - Something moves in every frame; never hold a still map.
  - About 1.4 s per shot in the middle, slowing only for payoffs, which land on the music's hits.
  - Show a payoff after every process.
  - UI shots are fine when each is one choice with stakes, held 2–3 s.
  - Follow one arc through history, with big title words on the beats.
  - No feature lists, no narrator.
  - Wide shots last 2–3 s and keep the camera moving.
- **Construction should feel built, like SimCity (user, 2026-10-24, after the first moments clips: "The animations are awesome!").** Cities, Great Works, Walls and wonders animate as staged construction (stakes, scaffolding or timber frames, rising courses, then the finished flourish), not a fade-in. Completed Great Works and wonders get **visible landmark pieces on the map**, on their own tile beside the city inside its borders, so a wonder city reads at a glance. Visual only, with no rule change. **Superseded the same day** by "Construction and payoff animations v1 rejected" below: the staging must be the real building's own art, not scaffolds and cranes.

- **Animations get the painted bar too (user, 2026-11-02, watching the promo trailer):** "i noticed you still use the old art for bombers." The air-strike pass drew its aircraft in canvas strokes (flat in October, "engraved" on master), beside painted miniatures. The no-line-drawings audit after the 8.9.22 crane had missed it because it only appears mid-turn. Every object an animation shows (aircraft, drones, anything with a painted unit) uses painted art, and an art audit plays a resolved turn, not just still screens. Being fixed on `claude/painted-aircraft`.

## Combat animations (2026-10-24, `claude/combat-fx`; details in `docs/design/combat-fx/README.md`)
- A resolved turn plays back in rules order: the volley first (shots staggered about 190 ms), then the moves round by round with each clash on its step, then losses and captures. Each damage cue and HP bar waits for its own blow.
- **The look is engraved, not particles.** Smoke and dust are engraved clouds (round lobes in one ink outline over a pale wash, hatched on the shaded side). Soft gradient puffs read as airbrushed smudges. Impacts are woodcut sunbursts (tapered ink wedges), and red goes only on wedges where blood was drawn: a red-hearted flash read as a target. Dark missiles carry a vellum halo.
- What flies follows the era: arrows, bolts, musket smoke, cannonballs, shells, drones, a bomber pass. A city's walls answer by its owner's era.
- **No real-world national insignia on shared unit art (train-1102 review, 2026-11-02).** Every faction flies the same aircraft sprite, so the US stars on the Fighter and Bomber plan views put Mongol and Japanese planes under American markings. The plan views and the Fighter miniature (`app/src/assets/units/fighter-*.png`, repainted 2026-11-02) are now unmarked. **Rule: aircraft and vehicles carry no real-world national insignia** (no stars, roundels, flags or tail codes); every aircraft prompt says "no national markings".
- Effects need about 1.5x the hex-relative size to read at trailer distance (miniatures are capped at 36 px). Keep cloud counts low: each overlapping sprite draws its own outline.
- The capture's gilt sunburst belongs to the celebration, which waits for the taker to arrive. The combat layer adds only ink rings and dust.
- **Construction and payoff animations v1 rejected (user, 2026-10-24):** "the 'simcity' style animations did not turn out great... a lot of the payoffs now feel cheesy, not inspiring and elegant and sophisticated. they feel like animations from clash of clans or some cartoonish game, not a game like civilization." And: "The city building and wonder building animations feel like arbitrary scaffolding with lazy animations. it feels cheap. sim city shows scaffolding that is actually related to the building being constructed. civ does this too to some degree. like you see partially constructed pyramids, and you see them getting built in increments."
  - **Rules:**
    - Construction shows **the actual building in partial stages** (its own art built up in increments), never a generic scaffold dropped on top.
    - Payoffs are **restrained and elegant**: no big sunbursts or cartoon pops, such as the "+3 VP" sunburst medallion.
    - Think engraved atlas, Civ VI wonder build-up and serious strategy, not mobile-game juice.
  - **Combat animations were approved** ("looks awesome").
- **Construction round 2 rejected too (user, 2026-10-24):**
  - "the great library is a cheap reveal animation. feels like you paid an animator $5 to animate an image."
  - The city founding: "doesn't feel like things were actually constructed. it feels more like it was outlined, then filled in."
  - The granary: "feels bolted on and disjointed."
  - His direction: study SimCity and Cities: Skylines construction: "the animations of progress towards building things feel so satisfying in those games."
  - **Lesson:** a 2-second reveal of a finished image at completion is the wrong model. Those games show *progress*: a construction site that visibly advances while it's being built, then a satisfying finish.
- **Construction round 3 direction** (proposal `docs/design/construction/PROPOSAL.md`, the playbook was a working folder on the lead's machine, not in the repository; awaiting the user's approval): construction is a **persistent map state**, not a completion moment.
  - **Wonders and Great Works:** a site stands on the landmark tile from the turn the item is chosen. It advances through painted stages by production (plot, footing, half-built, roofline, finished), with era-appropriate cranes, stock and scaffold that hug the real walls.
  - **Each turn:** about a 1.2 s step. The finish takes about 2.5 s: the scaffold is struck, dust settles, the name is lettered.
  - **Walls** are built section by section with the real overlay.
  - **Ordinary buildings** get no fake site.
- **The construction target (user, 2026-10-24):** "the half-built pyramids is the right path, but honestly all of them feel like in-progress construction." The references are Civ VI's Pyramids at their lower courses (the target), Civ VI's Great Bath under scaffold, a Manor Lords timber frame, and SimCity's steel frame (reference screenshots of other games are never kept in the repository). Each stage is **structurally unfinished**: foundations, then lower courses with scaffold, then a bare roofless shell. It is never the finished art cut off at a line, which "does not look like an in progress building construction." Get the painted stages approved as stills before any animation code.
- **The Great Library construction stages are approved (user, 2026-10-24: "the great library stages look great").** These are the stills in `docs/design/construction/great-library-stages-sheet.png` (branch `astra/art-great-library-stages`): footing, lower courses with timber hoists, an open shell with a crane, then finished. Paint every other Great Work, the capturable faction-goal buildings and the Walls the same way, aligned to their finished art.
- **Max-rank insignia must feel earned (user, 2026-10-25):** the Spartan level-6 (Spartan Veteran) mark on the map is a small grey crossed circle beside the chevron stack, and "doesn't look cool enough". Top ranks get a distinctive, gilded mark in the house engraved style: for Sparta, for example, a lambda shield or laurel wreath. Every faction's highest rank deserves one.
- **Wonder landmarks must match the map's rendering (user, 2026-10-25):** the Great Library landmark "doesn't look like it matches the rest of the map. it sticks out almost like a cartoon against the more refined city and landscape." Landmark and construction-stage art must match the city art beside it in palette (muted, the map's earth tones), saturation, line weight, lighting direction, scale and edge treatment: a soft ground blend, no hard plinth or bright island. Judge every new map piece side by side with a city tile at both zooms before shipping.
- **The seven world wonders are all painted (2026-11-01, `claude/wonder-art-1103`, lead to approve from the shots).** The UI review of train-1103 blocked the release because the Pyramids, Hagia Sophia, the Eiffel Tower and ITER drew as a black icon on a cream ellipse beside three paintings. Each now has a master, 256/128/64 derivatives (map piece, unlock-card tile, Guide row) and three construction stages in the Great Library's pipeline. Historical subjects: Giza's three pyramids with the temple and causeway; Hagia Sophia as Justinian built it (dome, semi-domes, buttresses, west atrium, a gilt cross, no minarets); the 1889 Eiffel Tower of reddish puddled-iron lattice on the Champ de Mars; the real ITER tokamak complex at Cadarache (concrete block, barrel-roofed assembly hall, cooling towers, headquarters). The placeholder fallback in `landmarks.ts` is gone: `landmarks.test.ts` fails if any world wonder or the Yam Relay Station lacks art.
  - **How they were made:** Codex image generation (not Gemini; no key is configured), with the Great Library and Grand Bazaar masters as style references on a flat magenta field, then `tools/art/wonder-art.py` keys the field to alpha, fits the painting to the shared 1254 px footprint (the Great Library's box; a tall piece may rise to the canvas margin) and applies `landmark-atlas-edit.py`'s map-palette grade and ground wash. Prompts sit beside the masters and stages. A stage is an image edit of the raw master and is registered with the master's stored fit and fade box (`masters/wonder-fit.json`), so the patch never jumps (lower-half overlap above 0.96).
  - **Per-piece grade for distinctness:** at 64 px the dioramas share one oval, so Hagia Sophia, ITER and the Royal Arsenal scored 0.67 to 0.71 against the unlock-art bar of 0.66. Fixed by grade, not by raising the bar: ITER is cooled (`wonder-art.py master ... 0.75`: grey concrete and steel, no sandstone) and Hagia Sophia warmed and lightened (`-0.1 1.12`). Photoreal regenerations were worse (a rendered look, and the generator's magenta bled into the crane and flowering scrub).
  - **Rules learned:** the generator leaves a pink glow around the ground patch; key it hard and pull the alpha in a few pixels or a purple rim shows on grass. Ask for the stage as a structural edit (footings, stepped core with white casing laid from the bottom, trestled legs not yet joined, dome as an open timber centring) and it comes back right first time. A label longer than about 14 characters is shortened to its first and last word (`shortLabel`: "ITER Reactor") so it stays on its tile.
  - **Landmark placement avoids unit counters as well as banners** (`landmarkBox` covers now include the footprints of units in view, `render.ts` `unitCovers`).
  - **Review shots:** `e2e/tour/wonder-art-1103.spec.ts` (six sites spaced three hexes apart, two groups so all seven appear; each site's construction stages; the Masonry, Education, Steel and Fusion Power unlock cards; the Guide row).
- **Future units are painted, one art slug per unit kind (2026-11-01, `claude/future-unit-art`; lead to approve from the shots).** The UI review of train-1101d blocked the release because the Hunter-Killer drew as the Infantry's WWI grenade thrower and the Stealth Fighter and Bomber as the Modern Fighter and Bomber. Each now has the full unit set (miniature at 256/64/48, build-card crop, panel portrait, roundel, engraved icon): the Hunter-Killer is an armed four-legged ground robot (a generic legged platform with an autocannon on its back; never humanoid, the designer rejected humanoid robots; never modelled on a real maker's robot, and never described by a product name: the first painting and its docs did both, and the launch audit of 2026-11-21 queued a repaint with different leg geometry, body and sensor head; repainted 2026-11-22 as a crab-legged armoured walker with a turret); the Stealth Fighter a faceted F-22-style arrowhead and the Stealth Bomber a B-2-style flying wing, both charcoal grey on the Fighter's display stand so they read apart from the olive propeller aircraft. Made with Codex image generation from one reference miniature each, keyed and fitted by `tools/art/unit-art.py`; recipe and pitfalls (no thin antennas, the plain cream disc) in `app/src/assets/units/README.md`. **Rule:** `unitArt.distinct.test.ts` fails when two unit kinds share an art slug unless the pair is whitelisted with a reason (reviewer's rule); never map a new unit to a predecessor's art as a placeholder, ship the painting or hold the unit. The Stealth Bomber scores 0.60 against the Bomber miniature (bar 0.60) and 0.78 on silhouette against the Fighter (bar 0.77) only because all aircraft share one display stand; both pairs accepted in `tools/art/distinctness.json` because aircraft never stand on the map and the silhouettes and colours differ.
- **Review unit paintings for whether the figure makes sense, not only for style (user, 2026-11-14, on the first six special units, which the lead had accepted):** three misses in one batch. (1) The Peltast's throw was wrong: "this is not how people throw javelines. please try again and give it a critical eye next time." Check every action pose against how the thing is really done (grip, which way the point faces, arm and body line) at panel size, before showing him. (2) The Reisläufer carried a pike while replacing the Musketman: "where is the gun, if this is peer to musketman?" A unit's painting shows the weapon of the class it replaces; if history and the slot disagree, that is a design problem to raise, not to paint over. (3) The Panzer looked older than the plain Tank, because the plain Tank is painted as a present-day main battle tank: "the panzer looks old by comparison. I think we're missing the ww2 era tank." A special unit is judged beside the plain unit it replaces for era as well as style; never accept a replacement that looks a generation older or newer than its peer.
  - **Second miss on the same painting (user, 2026-11-14, Peltast v4, which the lead had called "right"):** "is that the grip someone has when they throw a javelin? and are those other javelines the same size attached to the shield? something is still off." The body line was fixed but the hand was a clenched overhand fist (a hammer grip) and the spare javelins were half the length of the thrown one and seemed to pass through the shield. Zoom to the hands and to every repeated prop before passing a painting: the grip must be the real one (a javelin lies along an upturned palm beside the ear, fingers wrapped behind the binding, elbow leading), duplicates of a weapon are the same size, and nothing passes through anything. If any of these fail, repaint; do not send it with caveats.
  - **Approved (user, 2026-11-14, Peltast v6, tip d2e710ef8):** "the peltast is fine. ship it". What got it there: the painter never drew the grip in a full-figure render (six tries), but drew it first time when the hand was the subject of a zoomed crop edit, composited back. Use a crop edit for hands and other small details the full render gets wrong.

- **Barbarians are painted, and nothing on the map is a glyph beside paintings (2026-11-01, `claude/painted-placeholders`).** The audit after the ink-crane removal found the last placeholder drawings: barbarian units (a hatched plate with an icon glyph), the city air-wing badge (a "✈" text glyph) and Walls under construction (the finished overlay clipped by angle, though painted stages existed). Now: one painted raider per kind barbarians field (Spearman, Pikeman, Musketman, Archer, Crossbowman, Scout; `assets/units/barbarian-*`), historically grounded (Iron Age tribal raiders, medieval brigands and outlaws, a Thirty Years' War marauder), each with one blood-red cloth accent and a pose that differs from the empire unit of the same kind (except the Archer, which shares the empire Archer's draw: the same unit; repaint it if that ever confuses). On the map the base is charred ink stone in an ink ring with a blood-red notch at its left end, with no owner roundel (a blood-red ring read as the crimson seat, UI review); zoomed out, the class roundel washed blood red under a heavy ink ring with a red notch at the upper left. The air-wing badge wears `unit-fighter` (or `unit-bomber` for an all-Bomber wing) in ink. Walls show `<era>-walls-stage1..3` by production (10%, 40%, 75%), registered to the finished overlay.
- **Ownerless pieces are judged beside the crimson seat (UI review, 2026-11-01).** A barbarian or any other ownerless piece is shot beside the nearest seat colour (crimson) at normal and far zoom in one shot, and ownership must not rest on hue alone (base value, a missing owner roundel, a notch).
- **An accepted lookalike pair needs a reason the map supplies,** and is allowed only when no stated pose or silhouette rule says the two must differ; otherwise repaint the newcomer.
- **A construction stage 3 shares its finished piece's silhouette** (tower count and shape), so completion reads as finishing, not a swap. The Ancient Walls stages now use the finished grey stone and round drum towers, with scaffolds hugging the tower tops.

- **Every faction portrait is a man and a woman, with no exceptions or fallbacks (user, 2026-11-01):** "every portrait is supposed to be a representative man and woman. this is just a man. please fix and remember that for the future." India (added 2026-10-25) shipped without a `people-india` pair and the sheet fell back to the old single-leader Akbar painting. A new faction ships with its pair portrait in the same change; a single person or a named ruler on any faction art is a blocker.
- **Deleting art removes every reference in the same change (UI review, 2026-11-06):** the SVG/PNG, its entry in `tools/art/distinctness.json`, and a regenerated `app/src/assets/icons/preview.html` (`node app/src/assets/icons/build-preview.mjs`).
- **A renamed unit gets new art (user, 2026-11-07, Tagmatic Soldier to Varangian Guard: "should we also redo the art? i suggest yes").** When a unit's identity changes, repaint it to match the new name and its historical look. Never keep the old picture under a new label.
- **Figures have realistic heroic proportions, never cartoonish (user, 2026-11-07, Varangian Guard v1 rejected: "it looks a little cartoonish. small head, almost obese looking body").** Adult proportions (head about 1/7.5 of height), a lean, athletic build, armour fitted rather than ballooning, and a cloak that hangs behind instead of bulking the torso. Compare the silhouette with siblings such as the Pikeman before accepting, and reject stocky or chibi builds.
- **The Landship is shown as "Early Tank" and painted as a WWI rhomboid tank (user, 2026-11-14: "nobody knows what a landship is"; "obviously we need to fix the art").** Code slug stays `landship`. Miniature: tall riveted lozenge hull, tracks all round, side sponson, no turret, dark mud-brown, no markings; the turretless lozenge is what separates it from the Tank and Panzer at 48 px. Icon and roundel are a hand-drawn rhomboid side profile. Lesson: a sepia recolour of a later unit is not a placeholder worth shipping; name units in words players already know.
- **Accepted look-alike pairs need his say-so (2026-11-14).** The art check's accepted list (`tools/art/distinctness.json`) is not the lead's to extend alone. For the seven pairs on the rules-34 branch (Panzer/Tank miniature and icon, Stealth Bomber/War Elephant icon, Autonomous Armour against Panzer and Tank, Barbarian Spearman/War Elephant, Reisläufer/Spartan Hoplite) he said "use your judgment"; all seven were accepted and a Panzer repaint is queued so it stands apart from the WWII Tank. Ask again for any new pair.
