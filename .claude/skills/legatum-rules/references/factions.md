# Factions

Part of the Legatum rules reference (index: `index.md`). Answers: What does each faction do differently (rules, goals, special units, Mercenary Companies)?
Moved verbatim from `game-rules.md` on 2026-11-22, in the original order; the original section headings are kept inside.

**Special units (designer, 2026-11-14: "every civ has a special unit"; `RULES_VERSION` 34; `data::special_unit`, `unit_stats_made_by_rules`, `unit_name_for`, `combat.rs`, `resources.rs`).** Every faction fields one unit that replaces a plain unit for its people only; each has a `FactionRule` ablation switch (`aztecs_eagle_warrior`, `celts_gaesatae`, `athens_peltast`, `india_war_elephant`, `swiss_reislaufer`, `germany_panzer`). The numbers are design numbers and ride the era curve like every faction edge; every one shows as a line of the unit's strength breakdown or its stat line. A special unit upgrades like the plain unit it replaces, into the plain next unit of the line (a Spearman-line piece into the Phalangite, a Reisläufer into the Musketman): the Panzer, a Tank, upgrades into an ordinary Main Battle Tank (rules 35), which loses its extra move. The Mongols' Keshig, the Aztecs' Eagle Warrior, the Celtic Gaesatae, the Peltast, the War Elephant and the Tagmatic Soldier are all before the Modern era and need nothing from it; no special unit exists in the Modern era.

Upgrading a special unit gives the ordinary next unit. A Company hired from a seller is that seller's version (below). Art: `app/src/unitArt.ts` keys the painted figure by faction and kind to the slugs `eagle-warrior`, `gaesatae`, `peltast`, `war-elephant`, `reislaufer`, `panzer`.

**Mercenary Companies (Byzantium / Byzantium only; user, 2026-10-17 and 2026-10-22: "let's do mercenary companies and replace bought peace").** Bought peace (`PayTribute`, `diplomacy/tribute.rs`) and Hire an army are deleted (old tapes drop `PayTribute` on load, `wasm/src/history.rs`; the unknown `tributes` list in a saved diplomacy state is ignored). The Byzantium's field army is hired: `Order::HireCompany { city, unit, seller }` (a city order beside `BuyItem`, `rules/cities.rs`; rules in `crates/core/src/company.rs`, which holds the numbers and the doc comment).
- **Who sells and what** (`company::sellers`, `seller_can_field`, `offers`): any alive empire on another team that the Byzantium has met and is **not at War with** (Peace or Alliance). **Each seller offers exactly one unit: its own special unit** (`data::special_unit(faction)`; the Spartan Hoplite, Keshig, Eagle Warrior, Gaesatae, Peltast, War Elephant, Reisläufer, Panzer, and Byzantium's Tagmatic Soldier), never a plain unit (designer, 2026-11-14; `company::sold_by`, `offers`, and `blocker` refuses anything else: "Sparta sells only its own Spartan Hoplite"). It can be hired when the seller knows the unit's tech and, when the unit needs a strategic resource (`resources::unit_need`), owns an improved deposit of it (`company::seller_can_field` / `seller_refusal`), even if all supply slots are in use. A seller that cannot field it yet is still listed with `Offer::unavailable` ("Panzer needs Armoured Warfare"), which the shop shows as one muted line and which can never be hired. Nothing is on offer before first contact. Published to the viewer as `PlayerView.company_offers` (private, Byzantium only).
- **Price and Gold** (`company::price`, `HIRE_PERCENT` 100): the Production cost of the **seller's version** of the unit (`economy::version_unit_cost`, less the buyer's own Military Science discount), in Gold (everyone else buys at 200%). The Gold goes to the **seller in full** the moment the order is applied (no veto: the seller need do nothing). A hire takes the city's one purchase for the turn (`last_purchase_turn`), needs a free spawn tile, no resistance, and the treasury (`company::blocker`).
- **The Company** (`company::hire`): appears beside the city next turn (moves 0 on the hire turn), promotion level 1 (the Spartan spawn rule), `Unit::contract_until = turn + COMPANY_TURNS` (**30**; user: 20 "feels short") and `Unit::origin = Some(seller faction)` (both public on `UnitView`). It fights, earns XP and **can capture cities**. **A Company is the seller's own version of the unit** (designer, 2026-11-14): its stats, name, picture and the rules of the unit itself follow `Unit::origin` (`GameState::made_by`, `PlayerView::made_by`, `data::unit_stats_made_by_rules`): the Hoplite's phalanx, the Keshig's bow, the Gaesatae's charge, the Reisläufer's bonus, the Panzer's move. The seller's faction-wide rules do not travel and the buyer's do: Mongol +1 move, Spartan ignored wounds, terrain defence, veterans. Combat takes both factions (`combat::unit_attack_strength_for_rules(off, faction, made_by, ..)`).
- **Outside the levy and the supply** (`economy::levy_count`, `resources::current_users` skip Companies): a Company does not count toward thin levies and uses none of Byzantium's strategic supply, so the shop is how Byzantium borrows a rival's era and deposits (era resources: exempt by design; the seller's improved deposit is the check, and the Company reserves no supply slot).
- **Upkeep:** `company::UPKEEP` **2** Gold a turn per Company, its own line "Mercenary Companies" in `economy::upkeep_breakdown`, outside the two free slots. Ordinary military upkeep counts only upkeep-paying land units (`economy::upkeep_unit_count`), not aircraft or Companies.
- **Expiry** (`company::expire`, after `process_cities` in `resolve_turn`): a Company whose `contract_until <= turn` is removed after its last turn's upkeep, wherever it stands (`Event::CompanyDeparted`, visible to its owner's team and anyone seeing the tile). A Company inside a captured city leaves it empty.
- **Unpaid companies turn** (`rules/cities.rs::pay_gold`, `bankruptcy_victim`): on bankruptcy a Company is taken before any levy unit and, instead of disbanding, becomes a barbarian unit where it stands (`company::turn_barbarian`, `Event::CompanyTurned`). With Barbarians Off, mutiny creates the barbarian controller so the loose unit can plan and act on following turns; natural camps remain off (`rules/barbarians.rs::plan_turn,spawn_camps`).
- **Upgrades:** refused for Companies (`rules/units.rs::check_upgrade`: hire the next one instead). A Company can be disbanded (no refund).
- **Events:** `CompanyHired { city, owner, seller, unit, gold, until }` (the buyer's and seller's teams), `CompanyDeparted`, `CompanyTurned`. The numbers are exported as `RULES.company { turns, upkeep, hirePercent }` (`app/src/rules.generated.json`, from `crates/ai/tests/ui_rules.rs`).
- **Tests:** `crates/core/tests/company.rs` (shop, hire and payment, levy and one-purchase rules, War and faction blocks, expiry and upgrade ban, unpaid turning, supply exemption); AI hiring in `crates/ai/src/company.rs`.
- **Measured** (2,048 balanced 8-empire Hard games, seed 9210001; `docs/sim-results/ai-quality/mercenary-companies/results.md`): bought peace carried only about 3 pp of Byzantium's 29-30% (27.0% with it switched off); the Heir goal carried the rest (13.0% with the Heir unavailable and bought peace on).

## 8. Factions

### Rome (2026-11-15; increment 1)

"Settle the frontier with the soldiers who earned it." Rome cannot train or buy Settlers (`data::available_to_faction_rules`); captured Settlers use ordinary founding. A full-health melee unit of level 1 or higher may use the existing `FoundCity` order. Founding consumes that unit without paying Gold or advancing `Player::settlers_trained`; ordinary Settler training and buying keep their own price counter. Roman military foundations do not consume a city's population. Every normal site, ownership, spacing and simultaneous conflict rule still applies (`rules/cities.rs::check_found_city,found_city`, `rules/units.rs::found_city_candidates`). In phase 5 of `rules/mod.rs::resolve_turn`, after ranged fire and movement combat but before healing, the resolver checks the founder's current HP, level and site. If it was wounded below 100 HP or a condition fails, the order fails without consuming the unit. Eligible simultaneous orders within either city's spacing limit all fail; surviving non-conflicting orders resolve in unit ID order, each checking its own eligibility. A Legionary is the Roman Phalangite replacement. Only a unit currently in that slot starts its founded city at population 2, with the corresponding population land claim (`cities::settle_territory`). A Castrum is the Roman Walls replacement. In phase 1, upgrading a Roman melee unit standing in its own city with a Castrum restores 50 HP, capped at 100, once per upgrade order even when it skips several intermediate kinds (`rules/units.rs::apply_unit_orders`). Rome has no faction goal in increment 1 (`GoalRule::None`, zero VP). The sheet uses Rome's own painted portrait, seal, glyph, Legionary and Castrum art; Byzantium's Tagmatic Soldier has its own icon.

Hard Rome uses gene `roman_founders` (on by default for Hard): it trains melee replacements, searches the nearby frontier and visible camps for safe first-promotion fights, and weighs nearby legal city sites more heavily for a healthy spare veteran (`crates/ai/src/production.rs,civilians.rs,military.rs`). It keeps the last defender of a threatened city. Four further Hard genes (on by default; 2026-11-21): `roman_hunt_radius` (8) keeps the promotion hunt within 8 hexes of an own city, drops the frontier reconnaissance and leaves recruits to a planned campaign so a strike force can gather; `roman_soldier_route` lets a veteran founder walk to its site as a soldier, without the Settler's capture-danger detour or trip-risk price (the site's own danger gates stay); `roman_veteran_retreat` steps a wounded threatened veteran out of reach to heal, since it can found only at full health, unless it is a threatened city's last defender; `roman_promotion_value` (100) adds the first promotions a siege should earn (3 XP per city attack a turn, 10 for the capture, 16 for level 1) to strike gains and campaign plans, each priced as a new city (`civilians.rs::roman_siege_promotions`). Two more Hard genes (on by default; 2026-11-21): `roman_early_war` lets Rome without a level-1 melee veteran nominate a conquest city with one city (the shared `conquest_min_cities` is 2), so the ordinary campaign planner can price an early war on the softest reachable neighbour (`conquest.rs::conquest_min_cities`); `roman_found_first` keeps the peace-time supported-defence staging from recalling this turn's veteran founder, which used to overwrite its FoundCity (`hard_defense.rs::stage_supported_defence`). `roman_finish_siege` (on by default; 2026-11-21) finishes the siege those promotions come from: while a Roman campaign is live the one-city conquest gate stays open after the first promotion, and while the campaign's capture forecast holds (`campaign_can_take` or `grind_forecast` on its committed units, or a visible 0-HP target with a committed melee unit) those units are exempt from the wounded-veteran heal and retreat, and a committed veteran founds only if the capture still forecasts without it (`civilians.rs::roman_live_siege`). The planner reads only `PlayerView`, never hidden game state. Other factions' planning stays on their existing paths.

### Aztecs (2026-10-31)

- **Flower Wars:** when a surviving Aztec melee unit helps kill an enemy empire's military unit, the nearest Aztec city gains 1 population, excluding barbarians and Scouts. Melee-only (designer, 2026-11-01): the helper must be `UnitClass::Melee` (Spearman through Hunter-Killer), so ranged, mounted and air units never earn it, though a melee unit's shared or defensive kill does. No prisoners are taken; the victim is simply destroyed (`rules/battle.rs::flower_wars`).
- **No horses:** the Aztecs cannot train Horsemen, Cataphracts, Knights, Cuirassiers or Cavalry; Early Tanks, Tanks and Autonomous Armour are allowed (an Aztec army has no Cavalry to upgrade, so its first armour is a newly built Early Tank); horse-only research is a stepping stone and its unavailable upgrade effects are hidden.
- **The New Fire:** offer 30 population in one rite for 2 VP kept for good, taking from the biggest cities first and leaving each city at least 1.

Flower Wars uses `rules/battle.rs::kill_participants`, shared with Spartan healing: positive damage against the victim, defensive kills, and damage to its city's tile for garrison overflow count; dead participants are removed first. Each qualifying Aztec owner gains once per victim, regardless of the number of its participants. The nearest city is measured from the victim's tile, ties use lower city ID, and an empire without cities gets nothing. Population immediately follows `cities::settle_territory`, with no Food cost or change to stored Food. Economic benefits belong to the owner, not a hired Company's origin. `Event::FlowerWars` is visible to the beneficiary's team and observers of the receiving city, and produces a population map popup.

`data::faction_unavailable_reason` supplies the no-horses reason for production and purchases; blocked horse units remain greyed in the city build list once their technology is known. The New Fire reuses `GoalRule::Swarm`, `Action::Swarm` and `goals::swarm_plan`, preserving the save/action IDs, refusal of a repeated rite, city-by-city loss preview and border release; the presentation is torches, never blood. No unique unit or building is added.

The AI restores the former Swarm timing: gain VP net of lost titles, take a winning opportunity with time to hold or bank a net gain after 75% of the game, and avoid shrinking beside an enemy army until the last 5%. Gene `aztecs_playbook` (default 0, §10) adds the designer's Aztec plan: frontier Walls first, the rite as the closer beside an army at a city it doesn't shrink, and a lean war army. Rivals value the smaller post-rite cities. Flower Wars values melee kills by the nearest city's Food growth cost, discounted by kill and survival chances; the `flower_wars_value` gene also values melee production near rivals and ranged units/Walls near Aztecs. Core availability filters keep horse units out of plans; Tanks are available normally.

`GameSetup::standard`, app co-op setup and online random seats exclude explicit picks; AI seats rotate through the remaining factions. App lobby Random seats remain seeded draws without replacement. There are still at most eight seats, with eleven factions available; simulation rosters and proxy schedules distinguish roster count from seat count. The seeded core AI setup keeps its original nine-faction pool until explicitly given Egypt or Rome, preserving existing pins.

Display names are Switzerland (`Switzerland`), Mongols (`Mongols`), Sparta (`Sparta`), Germany (`Germany`), India (`India`, with `Japan` as a save alias), Byzantium (`Byzantium`), Celts (`Celts`), Athens (`Athens`), Aztecs (`Aztecs`), Egypt (`Egypt`) and Rome (`Rome`). Parenthesized names are stable internal IDs in code and saves; faction-specific display names are listed in `docs/design/historical-factions/rename-map.md`.

**Egypt: the Nile (designer, 2026-11-21; rules 48; `FactionRule::EgyptNile`).** It replaced Food-funded construction (below), which now applies only with `--rule-off egypt_nile` (`RuleExperiments::egypt_construction`), kept as the comparison arm. It all happens in the city's yields (`economy.rs::nile`, after Germany's projects and before decadence), so turn resolution, forecasts, turns-to-complete and the AI read it without special cases: the Food total is doubled (`egypt-nile-food`, percent, default 200), all Production is removed with its own breakdown line, and while anything but Grow is in production the surplus Food (total minus 2 per population, when positive) is removed from Food and added as Production, one for one (`egypt-nile-build`, percent, default 100, rounded down when below; the designer chose 1:1 on 2026-11-21: 17.5% of eight-seat Hard games against an even 12.5%, 85 gave 10.5%). A building city therefore ends each turn with exactly its upkeep Food: it neither grows nor starves, and its stored Food stays put; a deficit is never converted, so it still starves. `IndustryProject::Grow` (Egypt only, `data::available_to_faction_rules`) is a project like Germany's: it never completes, banks nothing, and with it (or with nothing in production) the doubled surplus grows the city as usual. Switching costs nothing: the item keeps its bank, and since the Food-to-progress trade is linear (bar rounding), alternating only splits the surplus. Gold buys as usual. The Workshop line (Workshop, Forge, Factory) is unavailable to Egypt, so Bronze Working gives it nothing and Egypt skips it (rules 49: known from the start, see "Skipped technologies" in §Faction-accurate unlocks); Industrialization still reveals Coal, which counts as a benefit (`data::tech_has_benefit` counts strategic resource reveals). Egypt's cards leave out the effect lines that only add Production (`CardEffect::Production`: Deep Quarry, +1 Production per Workshop family, Railways' Mines, Factories +2, Fusion's +5, Recursive AI's +20%).
