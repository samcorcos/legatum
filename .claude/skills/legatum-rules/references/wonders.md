# Wonders

Part of the Legatum rules reference (index: `index.md`). Answers: How do world wonders, wonder tiles, held wonder VP and natural wonders work?
Moved verbatim from `game-rules.md` on 2026-11-22, in the original order; the original section headings are kept inside.

**Wonder price curve (rules 50, Sam's request 2026-11-22; a first pass the owner may veto).** Every world wonder is worth the same 2 held VP, so its price should follow one curve by era. A geometric fit of the seven prices against the era index (0 = Ancient … 8 = Future), leaving out the two worst, gives about x1.13 per era (Ancient 367, Classical 414, Medieval 467, Renaissance 526, Industrial 593, Machine Age 669, Future 958). Only prices more than ~25% off it moved: the Pyramids 240 → 360 (-35%) and the Great Library 600 → 400 (+45%; set to the Hagia Sophia's 400 so a later wonder is never cheaper). The Hagia Sophia (-14%), Grand Bazaar (+14%), Royal Arsenal (+1%), Eiffel Tower (+8%) and ITER (-6%) are unchanged. The Star Fort moved from Military Engineering (Renaissance) to Machinery (Medieval), one era earlier, because Industrial units ignore every wall tier one era after Military Engineering; Siege still reaches Star Forts only with Metallurgy (Renaissance).

**World wonder race (2026-10-31).** One named wonder unlocks per era (except the World War era since 2026-11-14: the Eiffel Tower moved with Steel into the Machine Age, and its Hoover Dam is increment 2 of the Machine Age, not built; the Modern era's Apollo Program is increment 2 of the Modern era, not built; so there are seven), and each can complete only once worldwide, even after capture or destruction. Each empire may complete one; a captured wonder does not use the captor's completion. The first city to finish wins; if several finish on the same turn (rules 50), the one with more overflow Production (stored plus this turn's Production minus the price) wins and an exact tie is a seeded draw (`rules/cities.rs::wonder_race_order`, `purpose::WONDER_RACE`): only the racing cities swap places in the production phase's city-id order, and the losers are handled by `resolve_wonder_race` as before. Every other city actively building, queuing or banking that wonder loses that order and receives floor(banked Production / 2) Gold, reported to its owner (`data::GREAT_WORKS`, `economy::building_check_at_era`, `rules/cities.rs::resolve_wonder_race`, `Event::WonderRaceLost`). **A city that was actively building it stops for its owner's choice (2026-11-07, Thun T92 in 8.9.26):** the race is recorded on the city (`City.wonder_lost`: winner, wonder, Production, Gold; shown as `CityDetail.wonder_lost`), and its queue does not start in that turn's production phase whichever city resolves first (a loser resolving after the winner holds its turn's Production as unassigned, up to the bank cap). Any production or automation order clears it; otherwise its next production phase clears it and the queue resumes. The app makes it a "Choose production" decision ("India completed the Hagia Sophia first: Thun stopped building it. You got 15 Gold back, half the 30 Production Thun had put into it. Choose what Thun builds next."), with its waiting queue and a one-click "Resume Mill"; the same line in the report and an "A wonder race lost" moment card, never a message about the next queue item (`app/src/wonderRace.ts`). `WonderRaceLost.active` tells a city that was building it from one that only had it queued or banked ("…so it was taken out of Basel's queue"). A met rival's wonder build, progress and Production per turn are public (`projects::great_work_views`), so the build card lists rival builders with their pace and a city building the same wonder warns with the fastest rival's turns left. The original three have no additional yield effect; the four new landmarks give era-scaled city yields (+1 Production, +2 Science, +3 Gold, +4 Science) (`data::building_stats`). Masonry fits the Pyramids' stone construction, Machinery carries Hagia Sophia away from Education's Niter reveal, Steel is the Machine Age metal-frame engineering slot for [the Eiffel Tower's actual puddled-iron construction](https://www.toureiffel.paris/en/news/history-and-culture/15-essential-things-know-about-eiffel-tower), and Fusion Power fits [ITER's real experimental reactor under construction](https://www.iter.org/building-iter) (`data::building_stats`, `data::building_name`).

**Wonder tiles and held VP (2026-10-24; permanent territory 2026-10-31).** World wonders and the Mongols' Yam Relay Station are queued as `ProductionItem::Wonder {building,tile}`. Choose any land tile owned by the building city within its three-hex work radius, excluding its centre, a natural wonder, or another constructed wonder; terrain gives no prerequisite. The site is stored on the order and completed tile (`Tile::landmark`, public in `TileView`). Completion clears any improvement and replaces the tile's Food, Production, Gold and Science with the building's effect. A completed world wonder site is permanent territory of its city: `rules/cities.rs::settle_territory` removes it from population claims, and population loss cannot release it. Capturing the city transfers its sites; razing or a Celtic sack destroys them. A completed world wonder's +2 VP is a held `VpSource::GreatWork {of}`, handled by `scoring::check_great_work` with public lost/completed events and the ordinary ten-turn victory hold. Completion still uses up the builder's one world wonder; capturing one does not use up the captor's choice. The Hall's goal and Peace passage follow its tile owner. AI chooses low-yield, safer interior sites, values the held VP in capture targets, and guards wonder cities with Walls and a garrison.

Buildings form upgrade families (`data::building_predecessor`: Granary→Mill; Workshop→Forge→Factory; Market→Bank→Stock Exchange; Walls→Castle→Star Fort; Library→University→Research Lab); a higher tier satisfies the lower
tier's requirement. Some gate on population (Forge 4, University/Bank 5,
Research Lab 8, Stock Exchange 8, Factory 6).

### Natural wonders (2026-10-10)

Each map seeds distinct wonders from eight real landmarks after resources and starts are fixed: Mount Fuji, Victoria Falls, the Grand Canyon, the Giant's Causeway, Uluru, Ha Long Bay, Iguazu Falls and the Great Barrier Reef (`wonders.rs::place`). At the Standard map size there are six; the count rounds with map area and caps at eight. The derived RNG makes placement stable for a seed without changing terrain, starts, ruins or resources. Wonders use matching terrain (Mount Fuji on impassable Mountains; the Grand Canyon and Uluru on Desert; Victoria Falls, the Giant's Causeway and Ha Long Bay on Hills; Iguazu Falls on Forest; the Great Barrier Reef on Coast), cannot share a ruin or resource, start at least four hexes from every starting capital site and five from another wonder. A wonder occupies one tile; a city cannot be founded on it (`rules/cities.rs::check_found_city`, `settlement.rs::known_legal_site`), and it cannot be improved.

Territory ownership controls a wonder. Its owning city receives **+25% of one city yield per turn**, rounded up from positive output before wonder bonuses: Science for Mount Fuji, the Grand Canyon and the Great Barrier Reef; Production for the Giant's Causeway; Food for Victoria Falls, Ha Long Bay and Iguazu Falls; Gold for Uluru. Multiple wonders each use the same pre-wonder base, so they do not compound one another. The bonus follows a tile transfer and needs no worker assignment or radius limit (`economy.rs::yields_base`). The first empire whose own unit or city sees a wonder receives one reward globally: 50 Science or 80 Gold; simultaneous sightings go to the lower player id. Starting sight is paid with the first turn's report so its notice is visible. Discovery persists across saves and is announced to the discovering empire's team (`wonders.rs::discover`, `event_visibility.rs`). Wonders give no VP. The view carries `TileView.wonder_found` (the discovery reward has been paid to some empire, public like the wonder itself; `view.rs`) so the tile card can show the reward while it is open. A city's yield breakdown lists the bonus as its own source, "<Wonder> (+25%)" (`economy.rs::yields_base`), which the app (`app/src/wonders.ts::wonderOfLabel`) turns into a named line, a city-panel row and a Natural wonders row in the Science hover. Settlement advice values an unclaimed wonder within three hexes (`settlement.rs::site_wonders`); border growth favors it (`rules/cities.rs::claim_score`).

Presets (`crates/core/tests/map_sizes.rs`, `SIZES`): **Small 40×26, Standard 48×32 (default), Large
60×40**, each validated 2-8 players. Generation builds one main continent (~45% land) holding ≥85%
of land, ridge mountains (~6% of land), climate-derived terrain. Ruins ≥4 tiles apart, ~1/60 land
tiles. Resources placed after start sites for fairness. No continent/island choice; only minor
unlandable islands.
The quality-banded start search requires two units of Production above the Plains baseline within
radius 2: two Forest or Hills tiles each provide one, or a visible bonus can make one tile equivalent.
Only passable tiles count. Since
resources are placed after starts, selection relies on terrain. The first 64 map attempts require
the full floor; only after repeated failures does the search retry with one and then zero production
tiles, retaining the quality and spacing rules. The floor is judged on the terrain as generated, before
`balance_founding_sites` rewrites ring 1 (below), so it now mostly screens ring 2.
Quality values Food and Production together but subtracts half their absolute difference, so a
one-yield site ranks lower. The search uses the same 100-point minimum and 24-point maximum spread.
