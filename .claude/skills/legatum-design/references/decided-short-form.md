## Decided (2026-09-30 to 2026-10-10), short form; details in the decisions log
- Defaults: G8 pacing (capital +2; the opening ramp was removed 2026-10-22 and the early prices retuned); balance bundle D (partly superseded by redesigns); one starting Settler (3-Settler start rejected).
- Dropped openings: Tall, DefendedExpand. Economic is on probation (likely cut).
- Factions:
  - Switzerland: tax and turtle, interest, can buy Great Works, treasury bounty, 3 Gold upkeep per military unit beyond the first two, Scouts excepted. (superseded 2026-11-06: Swiss wonder buying was cut, and nobody buys world wonders; the Swiss keep tolls, interest, the Alpine Redoubt and the 5,000 Gold Reserve goal, with triple upkeep and the capture bounty as the price. See the `legatum-factions` current table.)
  - Byzantium: bought peace. At War it may pay the enemy tribute, which enforces 10 turns of Peace and resolves before attacks that turn. (superseded 2026-10-22: bought peace and Hire an army were deleted for Mercenary Companies, which is what master still has; superseded again by the 2026-11-06 and 11-07 decisions, the Theodosian Walls and Constantinople kits, which are decided but not on master.   - Celts: capturing a city sacks it, taking 30 Gold per population and raising a warband camp (the technology steal was removed 2026-11-03). (superseded 2026-11-06: the sack pays 60 Gold per population; barbarians never attack the Celts since 2026-11-02; the Ambusher is cut.)
  - Germany: heavy machines (−1 move); production-to-Gold/Science projects. (superseded 2026-11-06: the movement drawback is gone; Germany is the Forge-slot Ruhr Foundry (+6 Production, −2 Food), double strategic supply and Industry projects, and the 10% first-building discount cut 2026-11-07, RULES_VERSION 22.)
  - Mongols: courier passage and slip-past. (superseded: slip-past was removed 2026-10-31, and Scout and civilian Peace passage was cut 2026-11-06; the Mongols have +1 military move, the Keshig, no terrain defence and no early Walls, and the Yam Relay Station goal.)
  - Sparta: the iron price; veterans from day one with a 6th level (Iron Guard); conquest heals; +50% non-military; desertion when at war with nobody. (partly superseded: desertion was removed outright 2026-11-07, RULES_VERSION 23; before that it needed ten turns without a battle after turn 100; the Hoplite phalanx was added 2026-10-17; slow resting heal was added 2026-10-11 and cut 2026-11-06.)
- Rules: city strike only with walls (walls drawn on the map) merged 2026-10-03 (Rush vs GreedyExpand 25% â 46%; Rush now valid); terrain sight and line of sight queued.

- Scenario training (2026-10-03, the user's idea): short preset scenarios with Claude's written predictions; human runs are compared with AI baselines from the same state to find AI targets (branch claude/scenarios).
- Rush is now viable (playtest 6: the user eliminated 2 AIs by turn 52 as Mongols). The open problem is the AI's counter (claude/ai-threat-response).

- AI training by teacher, critic and student (LLM seats, the Laya model) was abandoned 2026-10-06 for search (see "AI strategy is search" below) and its code removed. What still holds: sims of bad play teach little, so prefer a few high-quality games; every LLM review of an AI recording also hunts for bugs and impossible states, rule inconsistencies, and design flags (dominant or no-brainer choices, mechanics without a real decision). Bugs get fixed before any learning from that data; design flags go to the user. The game sims are branchy CPU code: the GPU is for any later learned evaluation, not the sims.
- **LLM teacher reopened as a trial (user, 2026-11-11):** "can we run Fable in place of an AI for an entire game end-to-end (or many games) and give it game state and ask it for the best move?... fable shoudl be able to make decisoins that are at least as good as me, and then fable can use that discrepancy between it's decisions and the hard ai (which are bad) to tune the hard AI?" The lead's position: the premise (Fable plays at least as well as he does) is untested, so test it first on short Lab cards Hard loses even when told the goal, from the player view only, each run played once; full games only if it wins there. The LLM stays an offline teacher: the live AI never calls a model (pillars 6 and 7), and the gain must be distilled into the valuation. Not a decision yet. **Trial result (2026-11-11, `claude/fable-teacher`, `docs/sim-results/ai-quality/fable-teacher-1111/README.md`):** Fable won 5 of 14 card runs where Hard is 0/50 on all six cards (Two Cities One Turn 3/3, Defend Essen 2/3; 2 of 6 at first sight), and lost the other four cards once each, which look unwinnable from their start positions. Both wins reduce to one idea Hard lacks: a value for a melee unit entering a city at 0 HP (the last garrison walks out of a 0-HP city, shots go at Cannons instead of the melee units beside the city, Bombers are not paired with the unit that enters). The card loop had already found the Essen line without a model, so the LLM's contribution is naming the missing term, not finding the line. Cost: about 31 s a turn; a 300-turn seat is an estimated 10 to 25 hours. The lead's recommendation: no full games; play 10 to 15 turns from positions where Hard and his tapes already disagree, on cards the card loop shows are winnable. Awaiting his call.

- Fortify vs fire is decided by value: in a city the garrison is safe while the city has HP, so it fires; in the field it weighs fortify, heal and retreat against the shot, and fights to the death only when it can't escape.

- **Diplomacy is Peace, Alliance or War** (user, 2026-10-03): an Alliance prevents attacks, shares vision and passage, and allows calls to arms; betrayal is a visible record. (the 2026-10-18 shared-wars rule, which removed calls to arms, was itself superseded 2026-10-23: defence is automatic and an ally's offensive war is a call to arms you may decline; see `legatum-rules` §5.)

- Simplification round (2026-10-03, user):
  - Atlas is the only UI look.
  - The guiding test: does the mechanic add enough to justify existing?

- Production is banked per item, Civ 6 style (2026-10-03, user): switching keeps the old item's progress banked; the new item starts from its own bank; overflow still carries (branch claude/production-banking).

- Opening rule (2026-10-03, user chose B): items with unmet requirements are greyed out (no pre-building a Settler at pop 1); early growth is sped up so pop 2 arrives around T5â6 and the first Settler still lands around T18 at Standard.

- First Settler 25% cheaper per empire (2026-10-04, user OK) keeps the opening around T18 at Standard with Settlers greyed out at pop 1.

- **Goal-driven strategic AI** (user, 2026-10-04: "the AI is not aware that it's trying to score points and win"). Strategic choices (war and peace, campaigns, expansion vs military vs economy, disband or retreat) are scored in one currency: the expected effect on the AI's chance to win the VP race. Plans persist with commitment and explicit abort conditions; no rule may silently override a plan. Tactics stay scripted.
  - First step: war campaigns that play the money clock (assault, make peace or withdraw deliberately; never drift or disband a siege army).
  - Then a shared value function arbitrating plans; a learned evaluation from self-play can later refine it.
  - Self-sabotage detectors (abandoned sieges, disbands at war, peace while winning) measure it.

- **Split counterattack: a unit fights back once per turn** (user's idea, 2026-10-04, after three Oathbearers each took a full counterattack from one Soldier that was already dying). Its melee counterattack is computed once from the stage snapshot and split evenly, rounding down, among every melee attacker that hits it that turn. Every attacker still lands its full hit, with overkill wasted. Cities split their melee counterattack the same way; the wall strike and ranged fire are unchanged. Chosen over "focus fire resolves in order" because ordering reintroduces sequencing that simultaneous turns avoid. Effect: rewards concentrating force (like flanking). The risk to watch at integration is rush strength against walls.

- **Assault damage overflows from a city to its garrison** (2026-10-04, the user's turn-36 assault on Tessitura). Within a stage, combined damage on a city tile brings the city's HP down first, and the excess carries over to the garrison, scaled by defense. If the garrison falls and a melee attacker took part, the city is captured that turn. Ranged overflow can kill but never captures. The defenders fight back once, as a combined counterattack split among the attackers. Replaces "No overflow within a stage", which made a garrisoned city impossible to take in the turn its HP was brought to 0.

- **Borders and city ownership are public on explored tiles** (user, 2026-10-04, Civ 6 behaviour: a city they lost still showed as theirs under fog). Tile owners and city owners update every turn on any explored tile, even out of sight. Units, city HP, garrisons, buildings and production stay fog-limited. Every player gets the same information, so the AI isn't cheating (pillar 6).

- **Population is territory** (user, 2026-10-04; for playtest 8). A new city owns its centre plus ring 1, all worked. Each population growth claims one more tile next to the city's border, the best one by the city's focus. There's no reach cap on claiming, but only tiles within 3 hexes of the city are worked; tiles claimed beyond that just block rivals. Food has a visible payoff, the hidden worked-tile rule disappears, and borders compete naturally. **Settled details (user, 2026-10-05):** the old border-growth sources (Monument family, Archive, Civic Networks, Japan’s Brood Hall) became Food and that was accepted. Claims go "nearest first, prioritizing quality": a city fills every claimable tile of its current ring before the next ring, taking the best tile by its focus within a ring (also beyond 3 hexes, where tiles are claimed but unworked); claims never lean toward rivals. **Follow-up (user, 2026-10-20):** Opening retune: the first Settler lost its 35% discount and the first growth costs 65 Food (the capital keeps its +2 Production), to hold first Settler ~T18 and second city ~T25.
- **Tile improvements, as built** (lead, within the user's decision; branch `claude/p8-improvements`): Farm (Wheat), Pasture (Cattle), Quarry (Stone) and Mine (Copper, Iron, Coal); 30 Production (Mine 40); the AI values them by yield gain over cost. **Strategic stockpile income is unchanged** (owning a known deposit is enough; the Mine only adds the tile's +1 Production), because deposits beyond the 3-hex work radius could otherwise never be improved. **Losing a tile loses its improvement, except to capture** (lead, 2026-10-06: Civ-familiar, and conquest is the core loop, so a conqueror inherits improved land; razing, release and founding a city on the tile still clear it). **Strategic supply stays as built, decided** (reconfirmed 2026-10-31): owning a known deposit is enough. Upgrades are tied to two existing techs (Intensive Cultivation for Farm and Pasture, Machine Tools for Quarry and Mine), each adding one more of the resource's main yield. Only resource tiles are improvable (no terrain-only Farm), to keep the set small. A delegation tech that auto-queues improvements is not built (follow-up, pillar 3).
- **Strategic supply needing its improvement: proposed and reversed the same day (user, 2026-10-31).** He asked for it, then, faced with the micromanagement, kept today's rule: "leave it as it is in the current game. no need to change it." Owning a known deposit supplies 2; a Mine adds +1. Don't re-propose without a delegation answer that adds no clicks and still costs something.
- **Tile improvements are built from the city's production list** (user, 2026-10-04; playtest 8, after population-is-territory). No Builder units. A city queues a Mine, Farm, Pasture and so on for a specific owned tile, and it appears on the map. Resources need their improvement to yield their bonus. Improvements upgrade automatically with tech, so there's no late-game micromanagement.
- **Goal-driven AI step 2 approved** (user, 2026-10-04; playtest 8): a shared estimated-chance-to-win score (0â10,000 integer) arbitrating campaign vs economy vs peace and discretionary spending for Hard, with persistent plans, a reserve ledger, explicit abort reasons, and calibration on held-out sim seeds (.reviews/astra-goal-driven-ai-proposal.md).
- Faction-sheet playstyle ratings are rough indicators; they don't need to be exact (user).

- **Balance waits for a competent AI** (user, 2026-10-04): while the scripted AI is weak, faction balance numbers mean little. Mechanics must work as intended, and no balance simulations run until the AI "has an IQ over 70". That will take the goal-driven AI, more user playtesting, and the Laya and LLM teacher pipeline. Tuning toward the 1.5x target starts then.

- **A city falls only when every defender sent to hold it is dead; relief units chain in, never bounce** (user, 2026-11-11, Bukhara T67: his three Phalangites killed a departing 4-HP garrison in a 0-HP city, a Mongol relief unit was also ordered in, and the engine bounced everyone so nobody held or took the city). "we should give some benefit to defenders because they control the city... keshig dies, other unit moves in to support and that other unit also gets attacked. if the keshig and all other supporting units get killed, then the city is taken. not a bounce... the game only supports one unit per tile, so there needs to be a way to reinforce the city against an attack." The owner's units ordered into the city that step are reserves: when the garrison dies (or steps out), the next one moves in and takes what is left of the assault, and so on; the city is captured that step only if all of them die and a melee attacker took part. An enemy claimant never blocks the owner's relief, and the relief never blocks a capture it did not survive. My proposal that the relief is "too late" was rejected.
- **Rival attackers on one city: a tie-breaker, not a bounce (DECIDED, user, 2026-11-11: "yes that sounds right to me. please do that."; built on `claude/city-relief-chain` with the relief chain; Lucerne at 0/200 with two empires bouncing each other every turn):** "i recommend resolving situations like this with a 'tie breaker' that takes into account 'total force applied to the space this turn', which includes archers attack and melee units attacking that space." The lead's recommendation: yes, per team, counting the unclamped damage every unit that attacked or moved on the tile would deal (ranged and melee), only a team with a melee unit ordered in can win, the loser stays where it stood, and an exact tie still bounces. (SUPERSEDED 2026-11-22, Sam, rules 51: an exact tie is a seeded draw, as on open ground; see `legatum-rules` `combat.md`.)

- **Reinforcing your own city goes first** (user, 2026-10-04, turn 107: their unit moving in to garrison was bounced and the enemy took the city). Within a movement step, a unit moving into its owner's city resolves before enemy moves into that city and becomes the garrison. The enemy's attack that step then fights it under the normal rules (city HP first, garrison at 0 HP, combined-assault overflow can still capture). Too late if the city fell in an earlier step. Owner only, not allies. One of the rare deliberate sequencing exceptions to simultaneous resolution.

- **Future direction: Generals** (user, 2026-10-04). Assign troops and an objective to a general, who runs the campaign so the player doesn't have to. It's the delegation answer to war fatigue, but only once the AI's campaign logic is good enough that "the units would just die" is no longer true. It reuses the AI campaign engine, so it comes after the goal-driven AI.
- **Settler sniping stays as a counter to greedy expansion** (user, 2026-10-04: "sniping a free settler is pretty much always worth the war... a huge swing"). The counterplay is risk-aware escorting. Recheck it for dominance once the AI protects its Settlers; until then its strength comes mostly from AI neglect.
- **Open problem: mid-game war micromanagement fatigue** (user, 2026-10-04, Byzantium game around turn 130). Managing many units in wars started to feel tedious, which strikes at the game's founding goal (a Civ-like game that isn't tedious mid-to-late). Address after playtest 8 with delegation that grows gradually (pillar 3). Ask the user which part is tedious before designing.
- **7a/7b-era AI, user's verdict:** more aggressive, gangs up on the leader, takes some cities and defends reasonably; "room for improvement". Still barely expands: most AIs had 1â3 cities by turn 130, Mongols 1 all game.

- **Natural wonders (user, 2026-10-09/10; a target player asked for them):** real, recognisable wonders (Mount Fuji, Victoria Falls and so on), about 6 per map. Each gives the owning city a flavour-matched bonus that scales, and the first discoverer gets a one-time reward. No VP for now. They're contested land worth expanding toward. Art: an ink-and-wash landmark on the map, a painted vignette on cards.
- **The second player is a target player (2026-10-09/10):** the co-op partner's first impressions drive presentation. They want pretty, covetable art, organic sound, readable borders, real history and no fantasy, an appealing faction to play, and for the game not to feel decided early.

- **Buying tiles with Gold is back, as a trial (user, 2026-11-20; reverses his 2026-10-20 deletion; decisions log; rules 46 on `claude/buy-tiles`):** the user's co-op partner told him they really like buying tiles for their cities sometimes, "so i think we need to try adding that feature back into the game." Rule as rebuilt: an explored, unowned tile touching the city's territory within 3 hexes, any ring order; 40 Gold plus 20 per tile the city has gained, times 100/125/150% by ring; uses the city's one purchase a turn; bought from a "Buy a tile" button in the city panel that opens the tile-placement mode (see `legatum-ui`; he could not find how to buy tiles in the old always-on price tags). Lead's choices, his to veto: no Code of Laws discount, and the AI does not buy tiles in the first increment. The case against, put to him: he deleted it as "too in the weeds" with 0 purchases in 24 of his games, and Gold can again buy a strategic deposit that is meant to be worth a war. Lead's reading (mine, not his words): his tapes show what he uses, not what the target player enjoys, so before deleting a Civ-familiar feature on his tapes alone, ask whether the partner would miss it. Judge the trial on the partner's play; cut it again if neither player uses it.

- **Quick removed (user, 2026-10-10):** Standard is the only pace; do not offer or display speed choices. The game goes at its natural pace, avoiding simulations that do not transfer to the normal game. Old Quick saves still load as Standard. Sims and benchmarks use Standard only.

- **A competent AI assault is a keeper (user, 2026-10-17, 8.9.2, Switzerland, T149):** "The Mongols had an actually competent attack! Great work making the AI for that. I was unprepared for such a large assault and they took one of my cities... it was not trivial to fight them off like it usually is." The AI's siege (shooters massed at range, melee held back, an army kept at the front through a long war, the city taken when the garrison is dead) is locked in as the war scenarios `war-golden-horde` and `war-golden-horde-hold`; AI changes must not lower its success (Hard 43 to 50%, check with `tools/sim/war-scenarios.sh --check`). Wanted next: that behaviour from other factions and openings, not a nerf of it.

- **Review-plan decisions (user, 2026-10-18: "I accept all of your recommendations" on the 13 Codex code reviews; details and tasks in `docs/status/review-plan-2026-10-18.md`):**
  - **One engine (D1):** local games and UI fixtures use the Rust core through WASM. Missing WASM is an error; old TypeScript-engine save formats receive a plain refusal.
  - **Public release (D2-D4, changed 2026-10-23):** as approved on 2026-10-18, the release was a curated export with fresh history, code `MIT OR Apache-2.0` and a separate assets licence; agent tooling (`.claude/`, `tools/astra`, `tools/ops`, `.progress`, `.reviews`) and the cloud infrastructure stayed out of the first release, and a short generic AGENTS.md and the rules reference as `docs/rules/` shipped instead. **Reversed by the designer on 2026-10-23** (decisions log row of the same date; `docs/open-source/readiness.md`): code is **MIT only**, original art **CC BY 4.0** (`LICENSE-ART`, audio keeps its own CC0 licences); the history is fresh from a curated tree, pushed only to the `staging` branch of the private `github.com/samcorcos/legatum` until he makes it public; and the **development layer is published too** (skills, agents, hooks, `CLAUDE.md`, `tools/cloud`, `tools/ops`, `tools/astra`, `tools/evolve`, `infra/`, plans, status, learnings), scrubbing only credentials, the AWS account ID, instance IDs, IPs, hostnames, key and profile material, tfvars and personal details: "i kind of want to keep as much of it as we can in the repo and public, but i don't want to share credentials or anything that would compromise security." Removed assets are deleted, not archived.
  - **Every missing Civ 6 system gets a recorded reason (D6):** status (deliberate, planned, scope cut) and one sentence tied to a pillar; where none was ever recorded the user is asked, not guessed at.
  - **Air (D7):** an air strike on the turn you declare war is legal, like a ground attack; rebases apply in aircraft id order, so two aircraft cannot swap between full hangars.
- **Old saves (D8):** a Quick-speed save opens at Standard with one plain notice, not converted; a save without `vp_holds` restarts victory holds (accepted, documented). The former `title_since` crown clock was removed on 2026-10-20; old values are ignored.
  - **Saves are validated on load (D9):** structure and size are checked, a broken save is refused with the field named, never silently repaired; the running game and autosave are untouched.
  - **LAN is for trusted networks (D10):** superseded 2026-11-22: LAN mode is gone; online games run in the host's browser tab and go through the relay over TLS.

- **Siege is built in from the Industrial era (user, 2026-10-28).** Industrial and later units fight walled cities as if they had Siege (every wall tier), and the Siege upgrade is no longer offered to them. Rifled artillery made masonry forts obsolete in the 1860s. Walls keep their strength and strike. The user picked this over painting gunpowder-era siege gear, after a Rifleman showed a medieval siege tower and a Field Cannon showed a bombard. Siege stays a bought upgrade through the Renaissance.
- **Training mode: tactical search, distilled (user's idea, 2026-11-07, under evaluation):** "limit the scope of what the search entails... call 'hard + search' something more like 'training'... when a city is being attacked, create a simulation of that exact combat scenario in the combat simulator, run it 1000 times... document what hard did and what the winning move was, play the winning move. I'm ok if the turns take longer when in 'training' mode." Search only the tactical layer, log where Hard deviates from the searched best move, and train Hard from those logs. Fable memo: `docs/design/training-mode/fable.md`.
  - **His corrections to the memo (2026-11-07):** (1) the scope is battles, which "often resolve within 5-20 turns", not the whole game, so the battle horizon is the evaluation. (2) Training search may see everything: "this is just for training, so there is not real concept of cheating here. but it might lead to overfitting or learning the wrong lessons that don't translate when information is limited." So the teacher search is omniscient. The guard is on the student: what Hard learns may use only what Hard sees. Measure the transfer gap (fogged Hard with the distilled lesson against the omniscient teacher). (3) Distillation is the hard part and needs a real design.
  - **Search broadly (user, 2026-11-07): "there are a finite number of moves... why are we not just testing basically all of them (and only prune the ones that are obviously bad)? battle sims are super cheap."** Never cap a search with a few hand-written candidate plans (October's cause of failure). Enumerate every legal order per unit, prune only the plainly bad, and search unit by unit until nothing improves. The joint set (about 20^8 per turn) is too large to enumerate whole.
  - **His games always train the AI (user, 2026-11-07, correcting a wrong "never train on his games" rule that a Fable memo invented): "yes my games should always be used to train the AI. it just shouldn't overfit or assume that the way that I played is the only way that the AI should play for that faction."** Use his engagements as training data, weighted alongside self-play, and hold back only his newest game or two as a test. Guard per-faction strategic diversity (pillar 10): his style is one good way, not the way.
  - **No expensive cloud training batches (user, 2026-11-07: "I don't want to spend $25 each run. that's too much").** Training-mode search runs in his local playtests: the AI seats search during his turn on his own Mac, which is free. Battles from his games are re-searched afterwards, with variants, on the Mac when it is idle (overnight, low priority). Cheap combat-lab sweeps (about $0.10) add to them. Cloud runs stay for Hard-vs-Hard balance and gates. Any cloud training run over about $5 needs his OK first.

- **Strategic resources are worth wars; obsolete units aren't worth building (user, 2026-11-07, on India fielding era-I units against his Modern army):** "thats actually fine because wars are over resources. if you see that you don't have enough, you should find a way to get more. that's happened a lot in history" and "if they don't have the resource, is it even worth building [weak] units at all or just concentrate on building up to the next era... ive never found it productive to spend my production points on [weak] units that die immediately." Keep the resource rule. The AI must value taking or settling deposits it lacks, and must stop building units that are hopelessly outclassed. Spend that Production on economy, Walls (which need no resource) and the techs and deposits that unlock real units.
  - **Deposits are deliberately not placed fairly (user, 2026-11-07, rejecting a Civ-6-style start guarantee): "deposits should not be fairly placed. I like the feeling of 'a rush to find the next era's resource'. I've found that in this iteration, I usually get the 'discover the resource' tech first because i need to know where they are so i can focus my effort there."** Don't propose guaranteed resources near starts. The AI should copy his habit: value the resource-revealing tech early in each era, then plan settling or conquest around the deposits it reveals.
  - **Each era's resource is revealed in the era it's used (user, 2026-11-07): "let's make each eras resource discovered in the era it's used. that makes it feel like more of a race."** Replaces the one-era-early reveals (Niter by Education, Coal by Metallurgy, Oil by Industrialization, Silicon by Electricity). Lead's mapping: Niter by Gunpowder, Coal by Industrialization, Oil by Combustion, Silicon by Autonomous Systems. Iron stays with Iron Working. **Landed on `claude/resource-reveal-same-era` (rules version 25).** Hard learns the reveal early in each era (genes `reveal_tech_bonus` 150, `reveal_tech_first` 1: before, it learned Industrialization 25 turns into a 28-turn era, Autonomous Systems 37 turns in). Census: best over worst fell from 2.48 to 2.1 on seed 9311003, nobody below 7.9%.

- **City placement and scouting as the designer plays them (user, 2026-11-07; full text in `docs/playtests/human-reasoning.md`):** a site is valued by its yields, by the risk from aggressive neighbours (don't settle toward one unless an army goes with it, Walls can follow fast, or that neighbour is busy in another war), and by denial (block land and resources a rival will need). Forward lookouts on hills at chokepoints are often worth more than a garrison, because 5 turns of warning lets production switch in time. The AI should reach this through valuation (risk-weighted site value, denial value, the value of information), not scripted rules.

- **"Our current strategy does not appear to be working" (user, 2026-11-07):** "My suspicion is that integrating some kind of 'system 1' model would be the right way to go with some rules around it... some decision making process that adapts overall strategy to what information it has available, and also solves tactical stuff too... i could be convinced either way." He is open to a learned policy and value model with rule guards. Fable research memo: `docs/design/ai-strategy/fable-2026-11-07.md`.

Fable's memo meant a small model trained on our own games instead. Run the cheap side experiment first ("we don't need to do a full rewrite until we know it's better").
