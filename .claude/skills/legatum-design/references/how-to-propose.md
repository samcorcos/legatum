# Design: how to propose, and the 2026-10-05 to 2026-10-31 decisions recorded with it

The proposal template and the lead's and user's decisions logged under it: building identity, border sources, AI as search, sim speed, goals, holding the target, barbarians, A2, overreach and decadence, tech boosts, caps, Alliances, legacy names, resources, Outrider, mounted units, scouting, paranoia, wonders, tiles, truces, city walls, era relaxations, raze, city automation, era objectives.

Moved verbatim from SKILL.md on 2026-11-22 (original order kept). The current rules are in SKILL.md; where this file disagrees with SKILL.md, SKILL.md wins.

## How to propose
Use a short template: broken rule, flavour-linked constraint, path to 10 VP, visible counter. State the feasibility check result from `legatum-rules`. Offer a recommendation, not a menu. When the user pushes back, update this skill (principles or rejected list) in the same turn.
- **Goal-driven AI plans (step 2, lead decision 2026-10-05, provisional until the user playtests it):** Hard picks one plan per turn by its change in win chance: Economy/Peace, Campaign, Contain or Snipe. Coalition wars that can't take a city are a separate Contain plan, priced by the VP they deny the leader (pillar 11). Its denial term is an assumption, not yet measured. Result: Hard declares about a third as many wars, and each war takes twice as many cities. Whether that feels too passive is for the user to judge (the human-playbook benchmark runs at landing).
- **A building's effect must fit its identity** (user, 2026-10-05: "how does an archive increase food? there's a cognitive mismatch"). When a rule change makes an effect obsolete, don't blanket-convert it to another yield. Give each source an effect its name and story explain, or cut the source (pillar 1).
- **Border-source rework (lead, delegated by the user, 2026-10-05):**
  - The Japan-only Brood Hall provides Food. (superseded 2026-10-25: Japan is not playable and the Brood Hall gives nothing under India or any live faction; see `legatum-factions`.)
  - Archive becomes +3 Science.
  - Public Administration makes a new city also claim its best ring-2 tile.
  - Monument and Assembly are cut.
  - The Granary is buildable from turn 1, and Soil Reclamation gives +1 Food in every city.
- **No dead buildings (lead, 2026-10-05):** a building that gives a faction nothing isn't offered to that faction. When it gates something else, drop the gate for that faction instead. Example: the Byzantium can't build the Archive (its city Science never accrues), and its Relay Uplink doesn't need one. (superseded 2026-10-10: Byzantium researches normally and builds the ordinary Archive and Library; its Science exclusions are gone.)
- **AI strategy is search, not imitation (user, 2026-10-06: "That's a great ideaâ¦ especially if we pair that with a clear goal"; Laya removed).**
  - The AI rolls candidate options forward on the deterministic engine, with the scripted AI as the base policy, from a state built only from its own view. It scores them by its win-chance estimate toward its goal and picks the best.
  - Order: big decisions, then tactical combat search, then a learned evaluation from self-play.
  - Rejected: LLM or Laya imitation (capped by the teacher; the LLM strategist only tied Hard). LLMs may still critique AI recordings.
  - Every AI change is logged on the hill-climb ledger (`docs/sim-results/ai-quality/`).
- **Search AI, measured 1v1 first (user, 2026-10-06):** start with 2-player matches before multiplayer and diplomacy dynamics; the player count changes the dynamics, and each count needs its own tuning.
  - Faction matchups and new information must drive strategy. Example: Switzerland against Mongols (more mobile, attacks early) means Rangers and Walls before Settlers. A scout sighting an army heading our way, or a neighbour being conquered, triggers an immediate re-plan.
  - The horizon is set by data (10 to the end of the game), watching for local maxima that look good short-term but lose.
  - LLMs review and challenge the extracted strategy learnings, and sims verify every claim.
- **Measure one player count at a time (user, 2026-10-06):** 1v1 first; then 3 players, with the alliance rules included (they matter more as the player count grows). 8-player and the human-like proxy are deferred: the proxy sample is small, so don't lean on it. Search-found strategies that win disproportionately are checked as possible exploits or broken rules, which make the game unfun.
- **Simulation speed is the top priority for AI and balance work (user, 2026-10-06):** "There's a big difference between three seconds per simulation and 100 simulations per second." Optimizations must be behaviour-identical (repin shows no diff); perf is tracked in `docs/sim-results/perf/ledger.csv`.
- **Tile improvements (lead, 2026-10-06):** capture keeps improvements, since the conqueror inherits improved land (Civ-familiar; conquest is a core loop). Razing, release and founding a city on the tile clear them. Strategic supply needs only a known deposit; a Mine adds only the tile's yield, because deposits beyond 3 hexes can't be improved.
- **Byzantium peace-camping exploit (lead, 2026-10-06):** in 1v1, plain Hard never fought a Byzantium camping on its land during Peace (Hard won 23%; search 52%). Fixed in the AI, not the rules: enemy units camping on our land count as a cost of staying at peace. Faction spread under plain Hard in 1v1 (Japan 70% vs Sparta 20% as side A) is recorded, not balanced, until search is the baseline.
- **Sim mode builds only what decisions need (user, 2026-10-06):** "being selective on what we build for simulation purposes versus gameplay purposes."
  - Sims skip human-facing work: labels, breakdowns, forecasts, per-item previews, event text and report formatting. The AI's view is built on demand.
  - The outcome is byte-identical (repin shows no diff).
  - The app path still builds everything for humans.
  - Simulation speed comes before search step 2.
- **AI and performance next steps (user, 2026-10-06):** do all three performance steps (profile-guided build, cross-turn caches, a lighter policy inside search rollouts) without external servers or cloud. Test the search AI against an exploiting opponent: a playable "Hard + Search" difficulty in the app, plus a 1v1 human-playbook proxy.
- **Primary AI metric: 1v1 against the human-playbook proxy (lead, delegated by the user 2026-10-06).** Plain Hard scores 24.9% against it, and search alone doesn't help (its rollouts don't model an exploiter).
  - Tune on seeds 8100001+. Report on 7100001+ and a held-out 9100001+.
  - Hard against Hard is kept as a no-regression check. The user's playtests are the final judge.
  - Work order: Hard's defence first (garrison warned cities, recapture, hunt raiders), then an exploiter model inside rollouts.
- **A leader running away is fine if the others can respond (user, 2026-10-06, after crushing an 8.2 game as Sparta at 7 VP to 0):** "if the other AIs started building walls earlier and ganged up on me, I would have had to back downâ¦ but I basically just ran through the whole board uncontested."
  - **Priorities:** (1) make the Sparta AI competent, since it should win more; (2) make the other AIs defend against it rather than nerfing Sparta (a nerf may come later).
  - **Faction-aware threat reading, as in Civ 6:** once scouting reveals that a neighbour is an aggressive faction (Babylon-style), prepare for an early rush with walls and ranged units. Next to a peaceful faction, invest in the economy instead.
  - **Coalitions** against a runaway conqueror are the catch-up mechanism (pillar 11).
- **Opening economy is forgiving, as in Civ 6 (user, 2026-10-06, Switzerland 1v1, bankrupt on turn 6 after a Ranger plus a free ruin Scout):** "in civ 6 scouts don't cost upkeep, andâ¦ players start with some gold in the bank."
  - Scouts have no upkeep and don't count toward any upkeep threshold.
  - Every empire starts with 60 Gold.
  - An early unit or a lucky ruin must never trigger a forced disband in the first turns.
- **AI strength roadmap (user deferred to the lead, 2026-10-06; intent: "AIs that are good"):** speed alone isn't the goal, because search is capped by its ~3 candidate plans.
  1. Engine speed through cross-turn incremental AI analysis, measured on a search-game bench.
  2. A wider search candidate set, so that more compute buys more strength.
  3. Large-scale automated tuning of AI genes against the proxies, with held-out validation.
  - A learned evaluator (the AlphaZero idea) comes only after these.
  - **Cloud compute (discussed 2026-10-06):** not until step 3. At that point, use AWS spot ARM (Graviton) instances started per batch, with a hard cost cap and the user's approval; switch to a flat-rate dedicated server if runs become continuous. The Mac is ARM too, and the engine uses integer maths only, so results match across machines.
- **Faction goals must be inconvenient (user, 2026-10-06, after reaching the Switzerland' 400-Gold goal on T42):** "it needs to be inconvenient, not something that I would commonly do in any game." The Switzerland goal is 1,000 Gold (superseded 2026-10-22: it is now 5,000 Gold, `goals::SWISS_HOARD_GOLD`). Judge every faction goal by this test: it should cost a real detour from ordinary good play.
- **Faction goals: a high cost, or points rivals can take (user, 2026-10-06).** Every faction goal is in theme, inconvenient, a strategic tradeoff (not a chore), free of map luck, and different in kind from the others. On top of that, it either costs a lot or lets rivals take the points away: steal them outright (the Byzantium Heir passes to his captor) or knock the faction off the goal for profit (a conquered Swiss drops below its 1,000-Gold hoard and pays the captor). Full set and history: the legatum-factions skill, "Faction goal redesign".
- **Every faction goal is worth exactly 2 VP (user rule, 2026-10-06).** Balance a goal through its threshold or cost, never its VP value.
- **Winning means holding the VP target (user, 2026-10-06):** a team wins after holding its target for 10 consecutive turns. Dropping below resets the count. Every player sees a clear countdown and an alarm the moment anyone reaches the target, "to muster action quickly". This makes a last-turn sacrifice (the Swarm, Honour the Fallen, a broadcast) never free, because rivals get 10 turns to exploit it.
- **Barbarians deny tile yields, as in Civ 6 (user, 2026-10-06):** a tile holding a barbarian camp or a barbarian unit gives its owner no yields. This is a general rule, needed for the Celts' Call the Wild.
- **Fix weak AI play in the AI, not by bending rules (user, 2026-10-07):** keep the Sparta retire level at 4 even though the AI rarely reaches it, and keep turn limits optional rather than a default stall backstop. "This is probably a skill issue for the AIs." Calibration may tune costs, but it must not make a goal trivial for humans just to make it reachable for the AI.
- **Learning AI, bounded pilot (user, 2026-10-07):** the user asked whether simulation can discover behaviours itself rather than having them hand-coded from playtests. Approved: an evolution-strategies pilot on rush defence (`claude/rl-pilot`, about $5 of cloud time), plus research into human Civ strategy and game data (`docs/research/civ-human-expertise.md`). Full AlphaStar-scale training is out of reach. Pilot result (2026-10-07, `docs/sim-results/rl-pilot/`): ES found a rush defence (capital held at T60 54% -> 83-87% vs Proxy v7 Mongols, held out) for under $1, but not sentries; not shipped, the lead decides on porting it.

- **A2 built: one unit per class in every era (2026-10-10, branch `claude/era-units`).** The user's revision of the lead's two-era-generations idea: "give every class its own unit in every era, for the flavour, provided the jumps are small. If fighting a unit that is +1 era ahead isn't suddenly '[uh oh], I might as well give up', we can have more iterations with more units." Seven eras of units in three classes plus siege: melee line (Spearman, Phalangite, Pikeman, Musketman, Rifleman, Infantry, Humanoid Robot), ranged (Archer, Composite Archer, Crossbowman, Arquebusier, Field Cannon, Artillery, Combat Drone), mounted (Horseman, Cataphract, Knight, Cuirassier, Cavalry, Tank, Autonomous Armour; 4 moves). Targets, measured with the scenario simulator at equal Production: the same class one era ahead wins about 60 to 70% of the time (never 85%+), two eras ahead 70 to 90% (ranged about 50 to 58%: unanswered fire lets an older swarm keep pace), three or more lopsided; a counter-class unit one era behind still beats what it counters, because the class bonus (+18 strength, `combat::CLASS_BONUS`, as its own forecast line) is larger than an era's step. Ranged has no bonus (range is its edge; the cavalry experiment's +27 ranged-vs-line bonus doubled melee deaths before contact). Every unit can upgrade to the newest researched unit in its line for the sum of its step Gold costs, so an army is never stranded by an era change. The class triangle holds at army level (ranged beats melee, melee beats mounted, mounted beats ranged), not for lone units in early eras. The AI picks the mix by value (`production.rs::composition_bonus`), not by rule. Air (Fighter, Bomber) is Modern only; Aztecs (no mounted class) land later and the faction rules read the class, so that is one line.
- **Overreach is a blanket tax, not a Gold tax (user, 2026-10-10):** "if we just make it gold, then only a few factions bear the brunt of the change. Let's keep strong but let's make it more of a blanket tax." One lead-scaled rate cuts Food, Production, Science and Gold in proportion (decadence: smaller families, sloth, complacency, luxury), and the Gold surcharge is pulled back. Any rubber band must weigh on every faction's economy alike, never on one currency that only some factions live on. It's being built on `claude/overreach-v3`.
- **One unit per class per era, small jumps (user, 2026-10-10):** "if fighting a unit that is +1 era ahead isn't suddenly a '[uh oh], I might as well give up'... then we can have more iterations with more units." This replaces the lead's every-other-era call. Targets at equal Production: one era ahead wins about 60â65% (never 85%+), two eras ahead about 75â85%, and a counter-class unit one era behind still roughly ties. Units upgrade for Gold so an era change never strands a mid-war army. Pacing accepted: a 220-turn median game matches Civ 6 Standard (200â250).
- **Campaign scenarios from real playtests (user, 2026-10-11):** "a scenario where there's a goal to capture these two cities with this many units... you don't necessarily need to play the whole game out... the goal is to take these two cities in the amount of time given that you know that it is achievable because I did it as a human." Train and measure the AI on short war scenarios cut from the user's games, each with a goal, a turn limit and the human result as the target, as well as on full-game benchmarks. Also teach the AI new units explicitly: feed combat-sim learnings into its valuation, and force-composition experiments (e.g. mass mounted) to learn what works ("it has no basis for understanding how to use them").
- **Decadence taxes all income and is proportional to the lead (user, 2026-10-17, after the coach found Swiss interest replaced about 80% of the Gold that decadence took, and Sparta in second paid 18% against the leader's 20.5%):** "all things that feel like income, production, etc should be taxed by decadence"; "it should be proportional to how far ahead each civ is." No income stream escapes decadence (interest, and anything else that bypasses city yields). The rate grows in proportion to each empire's own lead, so a leader twice as far ahead pays about twice the rate. This replaces the saturating `R L / (L + k)` curve, which let the runner-up pay nearly what the leader paid. Still no hard cap. Built 2026-10-17 on `claude/decadence-proportional`: rate = lead x 40 / 100 percent (`decadence-slope`; 30% ahead pays 12%, 60% pays 24%, no cap), Swiss treasury interest is cut by the same rate (the only income that bypassed the city-yield step), the old curve is behind `legacy-decadence`. Chosen over 50 (nearest the old curve's mean, but 7% of games pass a 50% rate and two pass 100%) and 60 (a stalled game at 294%); runaway conquerors can still reach 87%, raised to the lead rather than capped (`docs/sim-results/vp-v2/decadence-proportional.md`).
- **No tech boosts (user, 2026-10-17):** "I never found them useful in Civ and it was basically only ever an advantage for super experienced players who know how to game the system. For everyone else it's basically just things that happen randomly and not things that affect strategic decision making." Remove boosts entirely, and recalibrate tech costs to keep era pacing. Rejected: rewarding players for memorising hidden conditions.
- **No arbitrary caps (user, 2026-10-17):** the strategic stockpile cap of 60 is removed ("why is there a cap at all frankly?"), after the Swiss interest cap. Caps copied from Civ need a reason the player can see. Without one, remove them. Any cap that stays must be shown as a cap, never as a silent "0 per turn". Built 2026-10-17 on `claude/no-stockpile-cap`: no effect on the human-seat benchmark (seat-only -0.0 pp, opponents-only -0.2 pp, within noise), but uncapped stockpiles reach a median of 220 Iron and 290 Coal by T200 because the AI builds under 2 Troopers a game; if the stockpile should matter, Civ 6's per-turn unit upkeep is the lever (not proposed to the user yet).
- **Fighters can strike, at shorter range (user, 2026-10-17):** "let fighters strike but with more limited range." A Fighter strikes units within about 3 tiles of its city, with no city bonus (Bombers keep 5 tiles and +20 vs cities). One order per aircraft per turn, so a Fighter that strikes can't intercept that turn; one left on guard intercepts automatically. Being built on `air-ux`.

- **Alliances cost Gold again, and only defensive wars are automatic (user, 2026-10-23).**
  - Gold upkeep is back: "That was fine and limits just accepting them all." Free Alliances had produced blocs of 5–6 of 8 empires.
  - **Defensive wars:** when someone declares war on your ally, you join automatically.
  - **Offensive wars:** when your ally declares war, you're asked (a call to arms). You may decline and stay allied, with no betrayal record: "It should be possible to ally someone, they declare war on someone, and you say you don't want to join, and you still stay allied." (this 2026-10-23 rule is current; it superseded the 2026-10-18 shared-wars rule.)
- **No legacy names anywhere (user, 2026-10-23):** remove every reference to the old sci-fi factions (Japan, Celts, Switzerland, Byzantium, Mongols, Sparta, Germany, Athens) and their removed mechanics, in code identifiers too. The real names (Japan, Celts, Switzerland, Byzantium, Mongols, Sparta, Germany, Athens) are the only names. Old names survive only as load-time aliases, so saves, tapes and parameter files still load.
- **Uluru stays a natural wonder (user, 2026-10-23),** after the cultural-sensitivity question was raised before open-sourcing.
- **User answers, 2026-10-24:**
  - **No separate "Next VP" list.** The score ledger should make scoring intuitive on its own: "game scoring is a tallying of all the ways to score on a game sheet (in board games) that's really easy to see all the ways to get points."
  - **The AI must play the VP race, not only collect points:** "the way to win the game is by getting VPs, so they should not just prioritize getting their own, but also denying others."
  - **Era objectives:** one capture may count toward several objectives at once ("if that one city holds a great library (2vp) and pushes you over the top on some other objective you have"), and one city may count toward two objectives. One era still scores per turn (the R2 rule).
  - **Rules stay simple:** retaking your own city gets no special status, so there's no exception for "a city from the VP leader".
  - **Cavalry: rejected the "charge" rule.** Proposed by the user: cavalry can be attacked by melee only by a unit that **started the turn adjacent** to it. A melee unit can't move and then hit horsemen, because horses are faster. The goal is to make cavalry useful for running down archers without being "mogged" by cheap melee. He rarely builds cavalry today because cheap melee kills it too easily.
  - **Combat animations:** arrows flying, city walls firing, units moving, all simultaneous, with a settings toggle to turn them off. Work backwards from what makes an awesome promo clip, and use good web animation libraries.
- **The Future era's resource is Silicon, not Lithium (2026-10-24).** The user: "when I think of each age I think of the bronze age, the iron age, the coal age, the oil age... and the information age we're in now is really dominated by chips and there are a very limited number of silicon deposits that can be used to make chips." The lead agreed after weighing rare earths (magnets, too narrow to name an age), uranium (the atomic age, but oil defines the 20th century better) and lithium (batteries only, with alternatives). Semiconductor-grade quartz is genuinely scarce. Each era's resource, as an "age":
  - Copper: the Bronze Age;
  - Iron: the Iron Age, Classical and Medieval;
  - Niter: the gunpowder age;
  - Coal: the industrial age;
  - Oil: the oil age;
  - Silicon: the information age.
  
  The improvement becomes the Quartz Mine. User veto welcome.
- **Stop the leader is instinct (user, 2026-10-24):** "when i see that someone is close to 10 points, or especially if they're already at 10 points, I immediately rush to think 'how can I stop them from winning?' the AI should do that too." The AI should switch into stop-the-leader mode as a rival nears 10 VP, and treat it as top priority once a rival is holding.
- **Cavalry Outrider, refined (user, 2026-10-24):**
  - **No spearman exception.** "imagine you have a spearman in front of an archer. the horseman would have to go around the spearman to kill the archer, but he would end his turn next to a spearman and the spearman would kill him... the triangle applies to going 'head to head' which in this case, the spears still win just fine." Supported backlines are safe without it. Cavalry punishes unsupported archers and siege units; sacrificing horses into a defended line is a bad trade, and that's fine.
  - **Pre-Modern only,** like the triangle.
  - The lead's suggested spear exception was wrong: the adjacency at the start of the next turn already counters the snipe.
  - **Outrider is an escape, not a ban (user, 2026-10-31, correcting the build):** "a melee unit can attack a mounted unit it's not next to... but the mounted unit doesn't necessarily get attacked because it can move out of the way unless it's adjacent." Any melee unit may order the attack; a horse that moves gets away unless the attacker started beside it; a horse that stays is hit. The 10-24 build refused the order outright, which also made stationary horses untouchable.
- **Mounted units are still bad after Outrider (user, 2026-10-30):** "i noticed that horseman are just bad. i basically never buy things from that class until there are tanks. maybe we don't have such a strong rock-paper-scissors mechanic here where they get rocked by spearman... we could just make them more expensive units that have extra mobility?" The lead's diagnosis is the numbers, not the triangle. Mounted base strength is at or below the same era's melee line (Horseman 18 vs Spearman 20, Knight 32 vs Pikeman 32), and a mounted unit costs about 35% more. The flat +18 class bonus then makes it a massacre: a Horseman deals 13 damage to a Spearman and takes 67. In Civ 6 the Horseman (36) is far above the Spearman (25), and the spear's bonus only evens the fight. The lead proposes: mounted strength about 30% above the same era's melee, and the class bonus as a percentage so it bites the same in every era, both checked in the combat-lab scenario grid before changing. **Approved (user, 2026-10-31): "ok that sounds like the right fix. please fix it. similar to how civ handles it like you recommended."** Keep the triangle and fix the numbers. Don't propose dropping the spear counter again. **City penalty decided (user, 2026-10-31): "go with -33%."** A pre-Modern mounted unit charging a city loses 33% of its base strength, so taking cities stays the melee line's job (options were -33%, none or -20%).
- **Mounted against ranged under fire feels wrong (user, 2026-11-10, Hold Biel on 8.9.30; DECIDED the same day, rules version 26):** a Field Cannon (65) shooting fortified Cavalry on Hills (79 + 5 + 3 + 31 "Mounted against ranged" = 118) is forecast 3 damage. "this feels a little ridiculous a field cannon (same era as cavalry) does basically zero damage against cavalry? ... maybe we need to differentiate between the damage inflicted by a ranged attack and the damage cavalry can inflict when doing a melee attack on a ranged unit?" Lead's proposal: the mounted bonus applies only in melee (charging a ranged unit), never when shot at; Civ 6 gives cavalry no bonus against ranged at all. He approved it as the default and as an explicit exception to the rules freeze: the mounted bonus applies only in melee (a charge or meeting); a mounted unit that is the target of a shot, a Keshig's included, gets none (`combat::fight_bonus_of`). Field Cannon on open-ground Cavalry now forecasts 16 damage, not 4 (`docs/learnings.md`, 2026-11-10).
- **An LLM-played Hard mode, proposed by the user (2026-10-31):** "what if we did a play test version where we use an LLM (haiku?) to play the moves for the AIs? it could be a different hard mode... maybe it would perform better and we can use that for training. it's obviously not a long term solution." The lead advised against it for now, citing evidence. The 10-05 LLM strategist seat tied scripted Hard: it won 1 of 10 games and declared 52 wars for 1 capture, because the execution layer never carried out its plans, the same intent-to-action gap traced today. LLM unit-by-unit play would also be slow and weak at hex tactics. The lead proposed instead a "Playbook" Hard: his own playbook bots (Proxy v6/v7, scripted, which beat Hard head to head) in the AI seats. An LLM strategist stays as a later bounded experiment to measure strategy headroom once the intent-to-action fixes land. **The user rejected "Playbook Hard" (2026-10-31):** "i don't understand what 'playbook hard' even means. those are just general, reasonable strategies that you can pick to win a game. maybe we should step back and do some research on how people win games in civilization. there are many ways to do it. rush science, get an overpowered tech like flight before anyone else does, then win with that. get a huge army early and early rush and conquer. build up a huge economy and produce and get gold..." So don't frame a strategy as one player's playbook. Hard should know the general winning strategies of 4X players, pick the one that fits its faction, map and neighbours, and execute it. This restates "pick one strategy and commit" (10-09). Research into how people win Civ games was started that day.
- **AI scouting and ruins use military units too (user, 2026-10-24):** "the AI should know that you can also scout with military units. I do it all the time." Exploration and ruin collection are values any unit can pursue, not a Scout-only job.
- **Settler risk includes empires at Peace (user, 2026-10-24, turn 55):** the AI walked an unescorted Settler next to his unit at Peace. "they're so far away that declaring war on them means nothing to me... they just gave me a free settler for no reason." Price capture risk by the rival's incentive. A distant empire loses almost nothing by a surprise war, so its adjacent military is a real threat. Escort the Settler or route around.
- **The AI should be paranoid and value information (user, 2026-10-24):** "I value it a lot -- i like to know where people are... having even 5 turns of a heads up that I'm about to be attacked makes a huge difference in survivability. i can march units over to the city about to be attacked, start building walls and archers... if i see a particular army composition (if it's mostly archers, for example) i might start building cavalry. information is valuable."
  - The AI should value vision as a resource: sentries on approach routes, re-scouting stale rivals.
  - It should prepare defence on early warning.
  - It should counter what it sees.
- **Era objectives can be met by accident sometimes (user, 2026-10-24):** "I wouldn't necessarily say that none should be met by accident. if you pick any strategy you'll probably hit some by accident. it's just that in the past, pretty much all of them were hit by accident." Aim for a moderate accidental rate, not zero. No objective should be met by accident in most games, and pursuing one should clearly matter.
- **Wonders on their own tile (user proposal, 2026-10-24; open questions pending).**
  - **The proposal:** wonders and any building whose VP can be stolen (Great Works, the Yam Relay Station / Courier Hall) get built on a chosen tile, as Civ 6 does with districts and wonders. That tile gives up its yield for the wonder, giving a clear visual marker. "It's kind of cool in civilization when you conquer a city that has multiple wonders. It feels more impactful because not every city looks and feels the same."
  - **The lead's draft:**
    - any owned land tile in the 3-hex radius except the centre;
    - the tile's yield and improvement are replaced;
    - no terrain prerequisites;
    - the wonder is captured with the city;
    - the construction site stands on that tile.
  - **Decided (user, 2026-10-24): "Make points visible transferable targets."** Great Works become **held** (their +2 VP transfers to whoever holds the city). Scope: Great Works plus capturable faction-goal buildings, each built on a chosen tile per the draft above.
- **Resource reveals follow the age's metal (user, 2026-10-25):** "we have bronze working in ancient -- shouldn't that expose copper, and then we have iron working later -- shouldn't that reveal iron?"
  - **Reveals:** Bronze Working reveals Copper (no longer always visible), and Iron Working reveals Iron (no longer Bronze Working). Each reveal should be a visible moment, with the tech card listing what it reveals.
  - **Copper stays off units:** each era has one unit per class, so there's no resource-free fallback. The user thought a bronze-weapons bonus "might be more trouble than it's worth".
  - **Decided (user, 2026-10-25):** "let's just make copper a bonus resource and be done with it. keep it simple. no resource requirements in ancient era."
