## Decided 2026-10-31 (decision page and follow-ups; rows in the design doc log)
- **No rule freeze** before the AI checkpoint ("i can't promise you a rule freeze... we keep discovering more issues").
- **Simplifications in this rules round:** S1 improvements survive released tiles, S2 Great Work tiles permanent, S3 Code of Laws for existing cities, S4 allies pass through, S5 Fusion +5 Production, S7, S8, S9 direct upgrades, S10, S11, S13 opt-in garrison auto-fire. S14 is a separate UI task.
- **Rejected:** S15 supply priority (micromanagement); S16 truce-shield change ("confusing... no until I've run into this problem"); raze protection for point-holding cities ("people have destroyed great wonders of the world"), so razing them is allowed; a repeatable research VP ("it shouldn't be possible to research your way to victory"): Science after the tree becomes Gold instead (lead's pick of his options: units, HP, Gold, Food).
- **Ranged garrisons in a walled city ignore line of sight (user, 2026-11-01, 8.9.22):** "i thought we discussed that ranged units as part of the garrison would have full line of sight, just like the walls do. because you can imagine it's like they're standing on the walls." It had never been recorded. The lead limited it to cities with Walls (no walls to stand on otherwise, and the pre-Walls rush era stays as it is). Range and team visibility still apply. Garrison auto-fire (S13) already shipped in 8.9.22 as "Auto-fire from city" on the unit card.
- **Start positions: a production floor plus a faction lean (user, 2026-11-01, approved "yes that sounds right. please implement"):** he re-rolled starts for forest and production as war factions; "there's basicalyl no way to win as sparta" on a food-only plain. Start quality had summed Food and Production, so a food-only plain scored like a forest start. Now: (1) every start has a minimum of early Production within two tiles and lopsided sites score lower; (2) the fair starts are swapped between seats toward each faction's style (production for Sparta, Germany and the Aztecs; hills for Switzerland; open land for the Mongols; forest for the Celts; India keeps the most land). Fitting, never better. The AI never re-rolls, so dead starts also skewed the human-seat benchmark. **Revised 2026-11-02 on data: floor only, lean parked** (6,000 games per variant: the lean lifted no weak faction and widened the win-rate spread; `docs/sim-results/start-lean-2026-11-02.md`, lean kept at `a412c2534`). **Partly superseded 2026-11-07 (RULES_VERSION 24):** the founding territory (centre plus ring 1) is now sculpted to exactly 8 Food and 8 Production for every empire (`game-rules.md` section 7), so the floor only screens ring 2 and beyond.
- **Aircraft rebase at any distance (user, 2026-11-02):** "can we make rebase for planes go to any distance? it just makes the logistics of moving them around easier." The 10-tile limit is gone; rebasing still uses the plane's turn and hangar caps hold. The lead's case against (air power can mass anywhere in one turn) was outweighed by removing hop-by-hop busywork.
- **Alliances cost per turn only** (no signing fee). **India's distance penalty is linear corruption, 10% per hex.**
- **World wonders:** one per era, unique in the world, each empire completes at most one for its 2 VP; captured ones count beyond that ("only one civ should be able to build each wonder").
- **Era objectives, goals before mechanisms:** winnable without being primarily warlike; not winnable with zero successful wars ("tell me a civilization in history that never fought and won a war"); world domination not required, but it still wins. He likes TI4-style incrementally revealed objectives that are "achievable and not automatic. I had to change something about what I was doing in order to get them." Research: `docs/design/research/vp-scoring-boardgames-2026-10-31.md`.
- **The required war is an offensive success, not a defensive one (user, 2026-10-31, correcting the lead):** the lead argued "successful war" must include repelling an invasion so a Swiss-style turtle can win. Rejected: "Look into the history of switzerland... They had a strong military and they conquered. swiss mercenaries were legendary. they weren't pacifists. and look at all the switzerland games that I won -- I won in part by conquering." Rule: no faction is a pacifist, including Switzerland. **Not a hard gate (user, 2026-10-31):** "this shouldn't be a specific rule -- more like 'you shouldn't be able to win with zero conquest'. no full turtle victories. that's historically not a thing at all." So no "must have captured a city" check on victory; design the VP sources so zero-conquest wins don't happen, and measure the share of wins with zero captures (target about 0). Don't design any faction as a no-war turtle. (Historical record agrees: Aargau 1415, the Burgundian Wars, Ticino, Vaud 1536.)
- **Era objectives model (user, 2026-10-31):** TI4-style public cards, but "the 'cards' are the era objectives and it's not 25 turns, it's when you enter the next era and everyone competes for them... the future should be hidden until you're in that era." Three cards per era, the same for everyone, seen only on entering that era; everyone can score each card; at most one per era; 1 VP each. Cards cost something (Hold / Spend / offensive Fight); zero-conquest wins should not happen, by design rather than by a victory gate. First test: Codex task `objective-deck-1031`.
- **Objective points are flat 1 VP all game** (user, 2026-10-31): "we can keep them as 1 for the whole game because there are so many other ways to get points." No TI4-style 2 VP later stage.
- **Wonder race refund:** lost races return banked Production as 50% Gold; he accepted the trade-offs ("I think this is fine").

**Tech tree feedback (user, 2026-10-31):** working: "the resources are now meaningful. I find myself prioritizing getting the thing that discovers the resource first and moving settlers and taking cities based on that." Keep five techs per era and the era flow. Not working: placeholder techs ("the 'great library' tech just lets you make the great library and nothing else"; techs a faction can't use, like mounted techs for the Aztecs, are researched "for the sake of getting it"), and the late game, the Future era above all ("at a minimum we need to be able to upgrade the planes to something meaningful... it just doesn't quite land near the end"). Independent proposals from Astra and Fable: brief `docs/design/research/tech-tree-brief-2026-10-31.md`.

## Decided 2026-11-01 (rows in the design doc log)
- **No turn-limit backstop; a late stalemate is an AI problem** (user): "by that point in the game, people should have lots of capturable points, so a war here or there should be able to win the game." Don't propose turn limits or adjudication again; fix the AI's late capture campaigns (Codex `late-war-closer`). Follow-up the same day: "some combination of fixing the era scores and fixing AI actions during war will solve this problem. I'm open to a permanent late game way to earn [points] like in ti4" (his 2026-10-10 "Mecatol Rex for the Future" idea). He picked capitals, all game: "capturing capitals should be +1 vp for the whole game" (once per rival original capital per captor; Codex `capital-vp`). The repeatable Future project is not wanted.
- **Tech tree:** Quantum Computing can go ("drop whatever you want"). **Talk before building:** "please don't implement it until we've spoken about it." Bring him the recommendation (`docs/design/research/tech-tree-recommendation-2026-10-31.md`) and discuss; no tech tree code until he agrees.
- **Aircraft keep Oil** (user): "aircraft should require oil. its a flaw in ai gameplay, not the oil requirement." Never propose dropping a resource need because the AI handles it badly; fix the AI (Codex `air-oil-ai`).
- **Tech tree feedback on the recommendation** (user, still no building):
  - Robotics unit: "these shouldnt be exoskeletons. at that point they shoudl be autonomous killer robots of some kind." (Humanoid form was cut earlier; non-humanoid armed robots are wanted.)
  - Quantum Computing → Stealth Aircraft: "there isn't a strong connection here between quantum and stealth". Name the tech for what it gives.
  - Recursive AI: map reveal "seems fine"; free upgrades "feels odd... we need more of a rubber band, not a climax." The last tech should help the trailing empires.
- **Each era's units use that era's resource wherever possible** (user): Stealth aircraft use Silicon, "because it's a tech and requires chips."
- **Researching a tech is never a trade-off against yourself** (user, rejecting the lead's Recursive AI "leak" that sped rivals' research): "research tech shouldnt be a tradeoff like that." Rubber bands go elsewhere (diffusion, coalitions), not into a tech's own effect. Recursive AI = map reveal plus a Production boost in all your cities ("we're seeing a huge boost in worker productivity in the US from AI").
- **After the last tech the player picks what Science becomes**, empire-wide: Gold, Food or Production (replaces Gold-only). Exactly three: "let's keep it to the 3" (Veterans declined). **The tech tree pass is approved to build** ("ready to build", 2026-11-01; Codex `tech-tree-rules`).
- **Friends passing through don't force detours** (user, 2026-11-01, T51 Sparta at a mountain pass): "the other unit doesn't end there. they should both be able to go through the pass and end on an unoccupied space... this shouldnt require the unit to go all the way around." A tile a friend only passes through blocks for one movement round at most; following behind beats a long way round (Codex `path-reserve-wait`).
- **Heal (H) is back** (user, 2026-11-01): "heals the unit until it's healed then brings them back into the action queue. i find that I 'f' them and then i forget they exist." Partly reverses the 2026-10-20 fold into Sleep/Skip: a rest order that ends by itself must return the unit to the queue. Don't fold Heal into Fortify again.
- **Coalitions should deter, like EU4's (user, 2026-11-08):** "Whenever there's a runaway leader that's conquering things or taking too much territory or it seems like their military has gotten out of control, it's pretty good at containing that leader by forming a coalition... you really want to avoid this coalition from forming, because if it does, and that coalition can overwhelm you, things get messy really quickly, and you can easily lose the game." The new part beyond "fight like EU4": the threat of a coalition should shape the leader's play *before* any war (pace conquests to avoid it), and the coalition war itself must be dangerous. Research memo: `docs/design/ai-strategy/eu4-coalitions-2026-11-08.md`.
- **Coalitions come from hidden wariness, not a visible meter (user, 2026-11-08, after the EU4 memo):** "something around wariness of neighbors... It doesn't need to be visible. It just helps the AI make decisions." A visible threshold gets gamed ("the whole game is just to try to manipulate that to keep it at 69%"), so the response is smooth or probabilistic, never a hard line. Anything threatening counts: VP lead, conquest ("just because you take one or two cities doesn't mean that everyone should be terrified of you. But if you start rolling over entire civilizations, taking 3, 4, 5... and it's very clear that... you're next"), and a massive military build-up. Responses: find an ally ("an ally who's next to me or an ally who is able to open up another front"), build defences. **Biggest gap:** "coalition wars barely fight... in Europa Universalis, as soon as everyone's in a coalition, everyone just dumps all their troops in to attack immediately." Dogpiles are fine: "If you're conquering everyone, you should expect that people will fight back." Rejected: the visible Alarm meter (memo option B1). Wariness reads only public information (pillar 6); it is AI judgement, not a game rule, so pillar 5 does not apply to it.
- **Hopeless AIs fight the leader as a coalition** (user, 2026-11-01, after the 8.9.22 Aztec win): "when i make a judgment, I think 'can the combination of me and him beat the leader?' and that's especially true if it's on multiple fronts. If the Celts had pushed their advantage to my capital, I might have actually lost the game, but they inexplicably retreated." An AI's value of stopping the leader comes from the combined fronts against him, not its own solo win chance (Codex `coalition-push`).
- **A Celtic sack may burn an original capital** (user, 2026-11-01, with capital VP): "That's fine with me... sometimes you might want to do it just to deny an opponent the points." The Celts score the capital point and burn the city. **Then all cities razable** (same day, over the lead's case that a protected capital guarantees a late-game target): "no, let's make all cities razable." The original-capital exception is gone (ships in 8.9.23). Don't re-propose capital protection; the AI's late-war logic has to cope with an emptier pool. (Still decided after capital VP became held on 2026-11-22: the Celtic sack is the one exception. The Celts burn the capital and keep its point for good, because nobody can take a burned city back. Under pillar 8 the Celts' signature isn't weakened; see the Fable review batch below.)

## Open proposals: slowing the conquest snowball (not decided; the freeze was lifted 2026-11-11, so the occupation lock is being built as an off-by-default experiment)
- **Occupation garrison (user's idea, 2026-11-09):** "the unit that captures the city can't leave until peace... locking the unit in place which slows down the conquest snowball." He wants conquest to tie up the army, so each capture costs tempo. The lead's counter-case: a hard lock on the capturing unit is dodged by landing the last blow with the cheapest unit, which adds fiddly manoeuvring; and the only release besides Peace is eliminating the victim, which rewards finishing rivals off. The lead's variant: a captured city must hold a military unit until Peace with (or the elimination of) its founder, or it revolts back to the founder; any unit may be the garrison. Sits beside the 11-09 review's proposals (occupied cities yield half; capital VP held, not permanent; alliance commitment), `docs/status/fable-review-2026-11-09/`. Build as off-by-default experiments; he decides at the checkpoint.
  - **His correction (2026-11-09):** the junk-unit dodge is weaker than the lead claimed: "it's risky to walk in with junk units. They could buy a unit and it would stop the capture." And the point is army attrition: "when the mongol horde comes in with 5 units and takes a city, one of those units is now locked until peace, so the army is now only 4 units." He prefers the hard lock on the capturing unit; the lead agreed it is simpler and brakes harder than the revolt variant. He also prefers it because it adds no new mechanic (2026-11-09): "it doesn't create a new 'revolt' mechanic. It just restricts movement." The revolt variant is dropped. Release rule, his framing (2026-11-09): the unit is locked while at war with the civ the city was taken from; elimination is implicit Peace and ends the lock ("you would have to do that with a diminished army because you have all of these units stuck"). The lead agreed; build the experiment this way, keyed to the civ captured from, not the original builder. Known weak spot: against a 1-2 city civ the lock releases almost at once. No relief (his call, 2026-11-09): the unit stays stuck; if it falls, another unit may move in to hold the city "which is the thing that we currently do... but then that unit would be stuck there." So the lock belongs to the city: a military unit inside a city you captured cannot leave while at war with the civ it was taken from; it can still fortify, heal and shoot. The move forecast must warn before a unit enters. Lead's assumption, unconfirmed: civilians are not locked. Superseded question: the lock also ends when the founder is eliminated (rewards finishing rivals off), and whether the locked unit may be relieved by another.
  - **Built (2026-11-11), branch `claude/occupation-lock`, default off:** `--rules-experiment occupation-lock` (rules in `legatum-rules`, `game-rules.md`). The hard lock on the city, keyed to the civ captured from, released by Peace or that civ's elimination, no relief; disband is refused too (it would dodge the lock). Move orders out are refused with a plain reason and the tile card warns before entry or capture. The AI only avoids refused orders; it does not yet choose which unit lands the capture. Not in the app's new-game screen. He decides at the checkpoint. Extended the same day (his words: "cities should not be producing things at all. They're basically in a resistance phase until peace is declared"): the city also builds and buys nothing while the lock holds; Food, Gold and Science continue.

## Decided 2026-11-13 (rows in the design doc log)
- **Public treasuries** (user: "sure"): every empire's Gold is public, and the AI reads the true figure.
- **An Age card makes the player do something he would not otherwise do (user, 2026-11-15):** "disbanding 2 units just means i save up gold and purchase the units -- so it could hav ejus tbeen gold? it needs to be something that causes the player to take an action they otherwise wouldn't necessarily take. conquer a thing. get units to a certain level of experience." Demobilise is cut for this; a priced card is a bought card. Test every new card against it. And the VP on offer must stay in the player's head: "I often get 3/4 into the game and then think 'oh right, I'm supposed to do something this era to get points'", so the era takeover shows the open cards and the open card is on screen every turn.
- **A VP is never bought with Gold or Production alone** (lead, on his "use your judgment"): the paid World Age cards Triumphal Arch and Tribute are cut, not repriced.
- **A captured city loses half its population** (rounded down, never below 1; none when it returns to its founder). A rule for everyone, no lobby switch.
- **The occupation lock and freeze are the rule in every game;** the lobby switch is gone.
- **Capture tactics for Hard turn on "only if its winning":** a gene default flips only when it beats the baseline on its gate with no card worse.

- His words: "the tech jump to modern is too big. tanks + flight is OP. it feels like we're missing a tech era to pace things better. maybe something that's the equivalent of ww1 tech. we jump from basically the civil war up to modern tanks and infantry."
- Raised twice (2026-11-13 and 2026-11-14). The +30% Modern tech cost (rules 31) did not fix the feel: the problem is the size of the step, not when it arrives.
- Standing constraints: aircraft are "supposed to be strong", Bombers are not nerfed, Flight is not moved later inside the existing eras.
- **Approved in principle (user, 2026-11-14: "Looks good to me"):** an eighth era between Industrial and Modern (First World War technology), which takes the first tank, the first aircraft and the first modern infantry at lower strength, so Modern is one step from it, not two. Still open with him: the era's name (he doubted "Great War") and its non-military contents. Add the decisions-log row once the name is settled.
- **Game length is not an argument against it (user correction, 2026-11-14):** the lead said the era "adds roughly 30 turns to a game that already runs long"; he answered "Does it run long though? our target is average of 250 turns, and I seemingly rarely have games that exceed 150 turns." The archive agrees (`~/LegatumData/archive/index.jsonl`): his 14 finished games from 2026-11-03 to 2026-11-14 ran 116 to 188 turns, median about 147, against the 250 target; the October games ran 200 to 249. Extra turns from a new era move towards the target. Check the archive before claiming how long games run.
- **His rulings on the new era's contents (user, 2026-11-14):** the first aircraft attacks as well as scouts: "they should be able to attack too, but smaller radius and not overwhelming damage. something more historically accurate on the amount of damage they can do before ww2." - **The new era is the Machine Age, and it is a go (user, 2026-11-14):** "go with machine age". Build from `docs/design/machine-age-proposal-2026-11-14.md`, increment 1 first (playable era, placeholder art). Still open with him: Trench Infantry's Fortify bonus, and whether the rules-31 +30% Modern cost comes back off.
- **`refit_before_siege` is on by default (user, 2026-11-14):** "turn refit on", after the 1,024-game gate (+1.8 pp, nothing worse) and 35 vs 21 of 50 on After the Riflemen. Hard refits its army before assaulting a walled city.
- **Rejected: a bigger Fortify bonus for Trench Infantry (user, 2026-11-14):** "seems like an unnecessary rule addition. adds complexity for about 30 turns with little gain". A rule that only matters for one era must earn its place; Trench Infantry fortifies like any other unit. Don't re-propose era-only special rules without strong evidence.
- **The rules-31 +30% Modern tech cost comes off when the Machine Age lands (lead's call, delegated by the user 2026-11-14: "use your judgment"):** it was a stopgap to delay planes; the new era does that job.

## Decided: a calendar year beside the turn, and the game-length target (user, 2026-11-14)
His words: "what if instead of turns, we use dates like they do in Civ. it can start in the BC, then go into modern era. moves quickly in years first, then slower increments. that woudl help pace the game and help to see if our techs are roughly tracking... it would be great if flight was discovered sometime around when it was actually discovered. or tanks for the right time for tanks... it woudl be an indicator if most games get tanks in like 700ad or something. 250 turns is target number of turns, so maybe 250 turns should be roughly 'today' which is 2026." Two uses: a pacing feel for the player and a tech-pace gauge for us (year each tech is discovered in a typical game against its real year). The lead's recommendation: show the year beside the turn, never replacing it (every forecast is counted in turns, pillar 5); the year is a pure function of the turn number (display only, no rules change); anchor the curve on each era's real start year at the turn that era should begin; report discovery years in the sim output. Target game length he states here: 250 turns. **Ruling (same day): "go on the calendar, let's make it 300, but I would say 250-300 should be the target. so any game that ends between 250 and 300 is success."** The turn limit stays 300; a game that ends between turn 250 and 300 is on target (shorter is too fast, hitting the limit undecided is too slow). The first anchor table is the lead's draft (turn 250 = 2026, one year a turn after that) and is his to edit.

## Decided: two Hard genes from the 2026-11-15 fixes (user, 2026-11-15)
- **`peace_prize` stays off** ("leave peace_prize off"). The danger fix alone stopped Hard taking the T126 peace it was winning (12 of 12 runs to 0 of 12); valuing the cities it expected to take added almost nothing. Don't re-propose without new evidence.

## Decided: conquest is won by capitals (user, 2026-11-15, after a Byzantium win on 8.9.41)
His words: "can we make the "conquest" victory condition just "take all the capitals"? otherwise, the late game can get pretty tedious tracking down every city and every settler." Rule (details by the lead, rules 37, row in the design doc log): a team wins at the end of the turn in which it is the only team still holding an original capital of its own. A rival's capital has fallen when another team holds it (a held, occupation-locked capital included) or it was razed; retaking it puts that team back in. Elimination and the VP hold are unchanged. "Last team holding its own capital" (Civ 5's wording) was chosen over "hold them all" because capitals can be razed and the Celts burn what they take. The general lesson: a phase with the winner known and no decision left gets cut, not shortened. Hard defending its capital and counting capitals is a separate AI increment.

## Decided: every empire starts with its capital founded (user, 2026-11-17)
His words: "can we start with the initial city already founded instead of a settler? the reason is that I can still get an edge by re-rolling until I get a city that's in a location that I want within a turn or two, and that just feels like cheating. the goal is to have starting locations that are as similar as possible". Rule (rules 45, row in the design doc log): the capital stands on the start hex at turn 1, no starting Settler for any faction, Scout and Soldier as before. The principle to carry forward: starts should be as alike as map generation can make them, and an edge a player gets by re-rolling the map is a flaw to remove, not a skill to reward. The lead's interpretation: if re-rolling for a better capital or weaker neighbours still pays, the next lever is tighter start balancing (`mapgen::balance_founding_sites`, `start_quality`), not a new opening step. This replaces "one starting Settler" in the 2026-10 list above.

## Decided: Humble the mighty works for the leader (owner, 2026-11-22, rules 53)
- The problem: the card needed a capture from a team strictly ahead on VP, so the sole leader, or teams tied for the lead, could never score it. In a 2,000-game sim the empire that needed its last point sat on a dead card and the game ran to turn 300 with nobody winning.
- His choice of four, "Leader's card counts top rival": when no rival team is ahead of yours, a capture from the rival team with the most VP counts (every team tied for that highest counts). Everything else is unchanged: never a capital or wonder city, judged on the turn-start VP. The AI values the card with the same core function.
- Rejected with it (don't re-propose without new evidence): a default 300-turn limit (no turn limit by default stays the design; the frozen game was a card problem, not a length problem); a per-empire redraw of a card the empire cannot score (breaks the one shared card per Age race); leaving it as it was (a dead card for exactly the empire that most needs a way to close).
