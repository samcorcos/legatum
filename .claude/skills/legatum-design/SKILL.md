# Legatum design: how we think

For anything about factions, also load `legatum-factions` (the faction method, current designs and lessons).

The source of truth for decisions is the design doc's decisions log (see CLAUDE.md). This skill holds the *reasoning style* and the rejected ideas. Load `legatum-rules` too: every idea must work in this game's actual rules.

## Principles the user holds (in their own words where possible)
- **Factions are TI4, not Eclipse or Civ.** Each faction breaks one core rule and has a constraint, so it changes *what* you can do. Number tweaks feel samey and balance only by tuning.
- **Mechanics follow from the story.** Arbitrary drawbacks were rejected ("+50% until Steam feels arbitrary", "+30 per unit is just inflation", "hills make no sense for Celts"). Every rule should be obvious from who the faction is.
- **Bonuses must scale across eras.** Prefer percentages or existing progression systems (promotions) over flat numbers ("+4 isn't much by mid-game; +25% cost scales disproportionately").
- **Every mechanic must matter in this game.** Rejected: traffic tolls (no trade routes), moving cities (nobody would), contracts for Mongols (a whole new mechanic for one faction). Prefer relaxing or bending existing systems over adding systems.
- **Expansion is the core loop.** "Tall" means dense, not small. No staying-small strategy.
- **Civ 6 familiarity (pillar 2) is a strong default:** city strike only with walls; Palace +2 production; +1 sight on hills; line of sight for bows; Alexander-style heal on capture. When unsure, ask "how does Civ 6 do it, and is there a reason we differ?"
- **Simple core, real trade-offs:** as few mechanics as possible, protecting explore â expand â build â compete. Every decision should carry a trade-off that isn't obvious; dominant choices, no-brainers and dead options are design bugs (user, 2026-10-03).
- **Every tech a faction researches gives it something (user, 2026-11-22):** "there shouldn't be a tech that you get just to advance the era. every tech card that you get should have at least something. otherwise maybe just skip it? ... any time you have this kind of card is a failure mode." A tech that gives a faction nothing is skipped: the faction knows it from the start and its era needs one fewer (`data::skipped_techs`; today only Egypt's Bronze Working, since the Nile leaves Egypt no Production). Cards also never list a bonus the faction can't use (Egypt's Production-only lines are hidden). When a faction change would leave a tech empty, prefer giving it something real; a skip is the fallback, and a core test pins the list of skips.
- **Rush eras:** before Walls, units counter rushes; once most cities have Walls, rushing cities is over (user, 2026-10-03).
- **Walls are strong but beatable, as intended (user, 2026-10-07):** as Sparta in 8.4 the user took walled cities at a cost (lost at least one unit). "It was a strong deterrent though and forced me to change tactics... If they were built early enough I would have had to end my attack." Don't nerf Walls; the AI must strike before they rise, or bring a concentrated force and accept losses. **Walls-line buildings must be built with Production, never bought with Gold (user, 2026-10-18):** preparation against a likely neighbor should consume a cityâs Production, so a saved treasury alone cannot guarantee safety.
- **Bombers are meant to be very strong; Fighters are the counter (user, 2026-11-10, after a Bomber took a full-HP Castle city to 0 in one strike on the Two Cities, Two Bombers card):** "bombers should be pretty darn powerful. the counter to bombers is fighters. nobody has gotten there yet because the AIs are so bad." Don't nerf Bombers or flag their damage as a balance problem. The gap is the AI: it must use a Bomber strike plus a same-turn walk-in to capture, and build Fighters when a rival fields Bombers.
- **Aircraft should earn experience and promotions like ground units (user, 2026-11-10):** "bombers and fighters should also gain experience and have promotions and stuff." Built on `claude/aircraft-xp` (RULES_VERSION 27, 2026-11-11, the freeze was lifted): `state::Aircraft::xp`, the same automatic five levels at +10% each (`rules/promotion.rs`, one code path through the temporary map unit), XP from strikes and interceptions on the ground-unit formulas (both sides of an interception), kept through the Stealth upgrade and rebasing, level shown on the aircraft card, in the strike forecast and in the promotion event. Risk-free levelling for an unintercepted Bomber is accepted (user, 2026-11-10: "yes that's fine").
- **What an air war should feel like (user, 2026-11-10):** "it makes sense to bring in bombers with a fighter escort, and the fighters fight each other first, then the bombers come in. if the escort is wiped, the bombers are in big trouble. probably not death on the first exchange (if 1on1) but maybe 2 or if it's 2 fighters on 1 bomber then it's toast." Today's rules do not give this: there is no escort (one guarding Fighter meets the lowest-id striker, whatever its kind) and one interception kills a Bomber outright (Fighter 103 against Bomber 64 is about 143 damage on 100 HP). Lead's proposed shape, to be felt in an air-war scenario before it lands: guarding Fighters meet striking Fighters before Bombers, and a Bomber survives one interception and dies to the second. Refined the same day (user): the Bomber survives that first 1-on-1 exchange "maybe just barely. unless it's an experienced fighter, then it would take it down": a fresh Fighter leaves it at about 20 HP, a promoted Fighter kills it in one exchange.
- **No hard-coded behaviour the search can't reach (user, 2026-10-22):** "you have a bunch of hard-coded rules and you're not iterating or exploring them. That's probably not good." (The Celtic AI never attacked cities because of early returns no gene could lift; deleted 2026-10-23, with the sack priced behind genes `sack_price`, `sack_commit`, `sack_loss_percent`.) Every behaviour gate becomes a gene the climb can turn, and the AI is climbed on the box against the user's own milestones and win rate, with winners playing each other.
- **Walls may be too cheap for what they do (user, 2026-11-05: "i wonder if we think walls are too cheap? they give you a lot of benefit... how would you compare the cost of walls to the cost of a unit?").** Walls cost 60 Production (superseded 2026-11-06: now 100, next entry) against a Spearman's 80 (0.75x; Civ 6 is about 1.2x: 80 against 65) and need no upkeep, yet give city fire, +10 strength rising each era and half damage to city HP. Keep their strength (the 10-07 rule) and test the price (100 or 120). **Garrison choice is value, not a faction list (same message: "shouldn't we count this for all of them? ... if they're highly aggressive they'll focus more on troops than walls").** The AI weighs Walls against another garrison unit by defence per Production, and the unit also earns its offensive value, so aggressive factions keep troops and defensive ones lean on Walls. Measured by `walls-value-and-price` (Codex, 2026-11-05). **Overnight result (2026-11-06, `docs/sim-results/strategy-gap-1105/`):** the blunt version (Walls count as garrison for every faction, the Swiss rule without the faction test) helps every faction it reaches on its own, Sparta included (+1.7 to +5.0 pp; Athens +5.0, Sparta +3.1), and holds on held-out seeds; his "aggressive factions prefer troops" intuition did not show in the AI's own play. Landed as the shared Hard default with `army_war_extra` 1; the value-based version must beat it. **It didn't (2026-11-06, `docs/sim-results/walls-garrison-value-1106/`):** replacing the blunt rule with the value-based one helped exactly the factions his intuition named (Sparta +2.7, Switzerland +2.5) but cost Athens 2.0 and lost more cities overall (1.75 vs 1.62 a seat); on top of the blunt rule it was a wash. Kept off.
- **Walls cost 100 Production, not 60 (user, 2026-11-06: "yes increase price"), after asking whether Walls were too cheap for their HP, ranged strike and siege defence against a unit's cost.** This prices Walls, it doesn't weaken them: the strength stays and the rush window before Walls lasts longer (11-05 test: first Walls about 7 turns later, more early captures). Swiss Redoubt 120.
- **Search deeper offline (user, 2026-10-07):** "it's pretty obvious that 20 is not right if you're still doing that much worse than me." Use cheap cloud compute for deep, offline search (long horizons and plan-sequence trees) until the AI matches the human's milestones, then distil what it found into the live AI's valuation. The live AI keeps a short per-turn budget (pillar 7).
- **Rock-paper-scissors:** Rush beats greedy expansion; walls and turtling beat Rush; greedy expansion out-scales turtles. If Rush can't beat greedy expansion, look for rules that block rushes (e.g. unwalled cities shooting) before tuning AI.
- **AI adapts.** Openings are bets, not suicide pacts; AIs re-plan from what they learn about rivals (see memory "AI adapts"). AI gets harder by deciding better, never by cheating.
- **AI behaviour emerges from value scoring, not hard-coded rules.** For example, Civ AIs shoot melee units first because those capture cities; ours should reach that preference through threat scoring, not a "melee first" rule. A ranged unit in a city shoots every turn it has a target, since it takes no retaliation (playtest-6).
- **Information has strategic value (user, 2026-10-24):** the AI should be paranoid and value map visibility. Seeing an army even five turns before arrival changes survival, because it can move defenders, start Walls and ranged units, and choose counters from the observed army mix (cavalry against archers, spears against cavalry). Value fresh sightings, watch likely approaches with cheap sentries and rescout stale positions; never treat fog memory as a live combat target.
- **The user cares how Hard feels against a human.** Measure with the human-playbook benchmark (Proxy v6), not AI-vs-AI.

- **Conquest is the fastest economy (user, 2026-10-07):** "conquering these cities is the fastest way to grow my economy... in civ 6 deity, it's basically impossible to win unless you win an early war and conquer at least a few cities." AI valuation must count captured cities, their yields and population, and a rival's lost output as compounding economic growth, so a winnable early war competes with Economy on the numbers. Don't hard-code aggression.
- **Winning is zero-sum (user, 2026-10-08):** "it's not just about you building your economy (which is important) it's also a benefit to you if your rival loses economy." AI valuation must score relative position, our gains plus rivals' losses (deep search: Campaign cost rivals 9.1 Production a turn by T164), not our own economy alone.
- **Pick one strategy and commit (user, 2026-10-09):** "these strategies are mutually exclusive... if you're building an army to conquer you want to spend your cash quickly on units... you want to rush markets if you're Switzerland, but maybe not others... taking the average of all strategies is going to lead to failure. You have to pick one and go with it." AI strategies are coherent whole-game bundles chosen per game from faction, map and neighbours, then committed to. Never a blend of every behaviour. Tune each bundle separately. Committing doesn't mean being rigid (user, 2026-10-09): "that doesn't mean that you shouldn't change course if it's obviously not working. The name of the game is being flexible and adapting based on what you learn." Re-evaluate on new information and switch when the plan is clearly failing or another is clearly better; hysteresis only stops dithering on noise.
- **The mid-late game is boring, and research is the core of it (user, 2026-10-09, T170 of a Germany game):** "honestly, I'm bored. The AIs are at war with me, but they aren't really attacking me... stuck at 7 VP... the rest of the gameplay is just 'press return and wait'. And all the science at this point is useless. I just pick whatever is closest to click and there's no longer a strategy for what I'm researching."
  - **Wants a complete overhaul of research and eras, not just faster science:** "The eras should reflect human progress of some kind. In Civ you feel the eras, and when someone gets ahead it's a big deal. This whole game is basically 1 era... we don't have planes, tanks, and all the other cool stuff..." He added that this would not be enough to convince the target player to play it.
  - **The user's idea, offered tentatively:** force every tech in an era to be researched before the next era ("prevents people from rushing too hard... prevents... techs [that] just never get researched").
  - **Catch-up and predictability:** "sometimes you can tell who is going to win after about 50-100 turns and the rest of the game is just waiting." Contested titles cluster on the leader, because Gold, population, science and territory all follow from size ("maybe that's fine though... just not sure").
  - **The user's answers (2026-10-09):**
    - **Setting:** "normal human history is better, but you can have a little sci-fi flair... keeping it close to what people are familiar with makes it easier to intuitively understand." Superseded 2026-10-10 on the sci-fi flair: the whole game goes grounded in real history, no sci-fi or fantasy (see "Factions are real historical peoples" below).
    - **Length:** the 3â6 hour game stays.
    - **The pain:** "it gets boring when you're in 5th place and you're obviously going to lose and you're just waiting a few hours to get to that outcome."
    - **VP:** they like TI4's model, where victory points are things everyone can get.
  - **Unit rock-paper-scissors is missing (user, 2026-10-09):** "we have melee and ranged, but ranged units trounce land units since they generally can't get close enough before they get killed if you have a few of them. I think in Civ it's ranged beats melee, melee beats horse, horse beats ranged?" Wants a clear unit-class counter triangle that carries through the eras.
  - **The rise and fall of great powers as flavour (user, 2026-10-09):** "I wonder if there's a way to pull in some flavor from history... The Rise and Fall of the Great Powers... Dalio, Tytler, the Fourth Turning... once you become rich, you become decadent and you go into debt, and then you go bankrupt and you stop being the leader... where the leader experiences some kind of decline... only do it if it feels like there's a way to do it in a flavorful way."
    - Lead's direction: decline as visible, self-scaling choices the leader can manage, never random punishment. Candidate mechanics: imperial overstretch (holding contested VP has an upkeep that rises by era, so holding everything strains the economy), and era transitions as Golden or Dark ages (Civ 6), where Golden comes with complacency costs and Dark comes with comeback boons. A debt system is a whole new mechanic, so it goes in only if it earns its place.
  - **Decided: imperial overreach (user, 2026-10-09).** "Let's go with imperial overreach... you should be able to stay on top." Holding contested VP sources costs upkeep that rises by era. It's chosen over golden/dark ages and debt as the rise-and-fall mechanic.
  - **Competitiveness is tuned with the user, by measurement (user, 2026-10-09):** "I would bet that the person who is ahead by turn ~50 or ~100 basically just always wins... I'd love to find a way to shuffle that around where at least some percentage of the time, there is movement on who is winning... we can simulate and tune together to figure out just how much movement is required for the game to be 'fun', which is not something you can know yourself."
    - Metrics: P(the T50 leader wins), P(the T100 leader wins), lead changes per game, and the turn the result is settled.
    - Sims map knob settings to these numbers. The user picks the target by playing.
  - **Three land unit classes only (user, 2026-10-10):** "I think we just make normal melee the same thing. Historically the default line unit was a spearman. We don't need a specialized melee unit. Just 3 types." The melee line beats mounted, ranged beats melee, mounted beats ranged; siege is support. There's no separate anti-mounted unit.
  - **Validate combat with a scenario grid (user, 2026-10-10):** "running combat simulations... like chess openings. 1v1, 4v5 with different mix of units, involving a city with or without walls... make a determination if the combat makes sense." Check combat rule changes against a scenario suite with explicit, Civ-familiar expectations. It's a fast standalone Rust tactical simulator on the real core rules, with varied goals (take, defend, hold, raid, escort, relieve, retreat), wacky mixes, and a composition search. A red flag: one composition or unit type is optimal everywhere ("5 archers and 1 melee for every combat... means one type of unit is overpowered").
  - **Decided (user, 2026-10-10): the strict era gate.** "We should have the 'must research all technologies in an era before advancing to the next era'." This overrides the lead's fractional-gate recommendation. Eras are small, finishable sets of techs paced so a typical winner reaches the last era near the end of a game. Research strategy becomes sequencing within the era. No tech is dead.
  - **Distinct eras with time to breathe:** "distinct units that have enough time to develop and fight and do stuff."
  - **Era advancement is a celebrated moment:** "once you research the last tech in an era, we should have an animation, popup, takeover, video, or something that gives you a feeling of accomplishment and advancement, and something should change in the game board." Rivals see it as a public event.
  - **Cities show their era and size** (Civ style): art per era and size tier, so cities grow visually with population. "Right now our cities are abstract cubes."
  - **Era proposal answers (user, 2026-10-10)** (proposal doc (a private document, not published)):
    - **Nine eras as of rules 35, ending in a Future era**, not 1940 (Machine Age added and the 1950 to 2010 Modern era decided, both 2026-11-14). Future holds in-flight technology: military drones, humanoid robots, fusion, quantum computers, superintelligence and recursive self-improvement. "That's where I want the game to end. Not every game needs to make it there."
    - **The Future era is a living data set**, updated as real technology changes, since the game is open source ("if we made this before the war in Ukraine, we wouldn't necessarily have had drone warfare").
    - **Era length must cover a war cycle:** "enough time to build an army, attack a neighbor, and take at least one or two cities before the unit tech is out of date." Early playtests found armies arriving an era late; measure the war cycle in Standard simulations and set era length above it.
    - **Other answers:** one city art style per era, regional later; crowns plus one era objective per era ("a great idea for game pacing"); escalating Gold overreach; a team target of 20 for two players ("10 is normal, so it should just be double"); navy punted, land maps only.
  - **Late-game permanent points, a Mecatol Rex for the Future (user, 2026-10-10):** "there should be a way to get more permanent points in the late game. Potentially some 'mecatol rex' like mechanic from TI4 integrated into the future era... most games should end in industrial or modern, but if the game is stalemated somehow, there should be a way to end it in the future era that feels organic... if I'm at 8 points and I'm not going to get the crowns... a path to get two more points, even if it's hard to do."
    - The user's reaction (2026-10-10):
      - **World Seat rejected:** "too on the nose as Mecatol Rex and it only advantages civs that are based in the middle of the map."
      - **Ascension direction liked:** "probably the right direction... but I wonder if it would be better if it's one point at a time and something more complex... something like 'all cities need to participate at the same time' to make the late-game cost of focusing on 'need to get end-game points' a real focus and cost... ideally it would be something you can do more than once."
      - **Sequencing:** design it after the core (crowns, overreach, era objectives) is built and simulated: "maybe after you build this stuff though and run some simulations. We have a lot of changes all landing at once."
    - Also to research: TI4 public-objective scoring rates, as a baseline for the "about half score each era" target.
  - **Era objectives are hard (user, 2026-10-10):** "I like the idea of 'one VP available per era' because it paces the game like in TI4. The VP should be hard to get but achievable. Not every civ should be able to get it every era." Start target: about half the empires score each era's objective. TI4 baseline (`docs/research/ti4-objective-scoring.md`, 1,974 games): Stage I is scored by 70% of players, Stage II by 31%, 60% overall. Calibration band: 40â60% overall, ramping from 55â65% in early eras to 30â45% late. At least one scorer in 90% or more of eras, and every empire scoring in at most about 15% of eras.
  - **Implementation approved (user, 2026-10-10):** "I want you to land all the changes we've talked about. I only want you to hold back on the late-game VP situation until we know more. Do everything else now. I approve implementation of what we discussed." The full era overhaul is in scope, built in phases: A1 core (eras, techs, gate, diffusion, boosts, buildings, Railways); A2 units (three-class lineage, class bonuses, mounted, air); B VP v2 (crowns, overreach, era objectives, team target 20, default on); C the era-up takeover; D art (cities, units, era key art). Each phase lands in playable increments.
  - **A1 built (2026-10-10, branch claude/era-overhaul-core):** seven eras, 35 techs, strict gate, diffusion cap 50%, boosts (40% off; removed 2026-10-17, see "No tech boosts" below), historical building names, Castle/Star Fort, Railways. Start values to tune: diffusion 10% per empire (cap 50%), tech costs in `eras::TECHS` (calibrated to winners entering Classical..Modern at about T30/63/97/130/165). Details in the legatum-rules reference ("Eras and technologies"). Units are unchanged until A2; the era-up takeover is phase C (hook: `celebrate/EraUpTakeover.tsx`).
  - **Overreach must be punitive and scale with lead (user, 2026-10-10):** "I kind of think that's OK... the Roman decline, they couldn't afford the upkeep of their armies and they became decadent... overreach, decadence, and decay are the reasons for these declines, and I can see a justification for units disbanding as a result... just affecting gold seems reasonable because the mechanic is simple... it should probably scale with how far ahead they are... the gap between first and... median... trailing 30 turns... so you don't get awkwardly penalized when a civ is in the process of being conquered... The penalty should include more than just the person in first place too. It should scale to the size of empire / lead... the rubber band should keep everyone closer together than is normally the case in a Civ game."
    - **No hard cap (user, 2026-10-10):** "maybe we have 40% as the 'target cap'... but I would avoid making a rule that says 40% is the cap because that feels kind of arbitrary and if someone really gets out ahead... it costs more than 40%." The surcharge follows a smooth curve that keeps rising with lead; 40% is where a typical strong lead lands.
    - **Fallback levers if Gold decay proves insufficient or weird (user):** rising building costs, or weaker military units, for empires that overreach.
    - **Built and measured (2026-10-10, `claude/overreach-v2`):** surcharge `100 L / (L + k)` of Gold income, L = Power (trailing 30-turn total yields) over the median living empire, no cap; landed at k 45 (Strong: 60% lead pays 57%). T100 leader 64.8% to 57.6%, later last lead change, +21 turns, 5 units deserting per game; Power spread unchanged, so it contests the VP race, not the economy. Gentle/Medium (k 180/90) are inside the noise. Fallback levers (building costs, weaker units) are the next step if yields must compress.
    - **Reworked to decadence, a blanket tax (user, 2026-10-10, `claude/overreach-v3`; supersedes the Gold-only surcharge as the default):** "if we just make it gold, then only a few factions bear the brunt... let's make it more of a blanket tax. decreased production due to sloth and decadence, decreased science for the same reason... decreased population growth because people don't reproduce as much when they get wealthy... decrease them proportionately, and perhaps pull back on the gold penalty." One lead-scaled uncapped rate `R L / (L + k)` (R 45, k 45, Strong) cuts Production ("sloth"), Gold ("luxury") and Science ("complacency") of every city and slows growth ("smaller families") by the same share, starting once an empire has a full 30-turn Power window; the Gold surcharge is off (a knob). Units desert only through the normal upkeep path. Measured (512 Hard + Search games per setting): T100 leader wins 54% (v2 61%, off 68%), lead changes 11.2, top Power over median 33% (was 54%), units lost 1.6 a game (v2 4.9), game +28 turns. Medium R 30 and Gentle R 20 are knobs. Not a fix for the 1.5x faction-balance pillar: Sparta and Athens gaps remain.
  - **Working mode:** the user wants a thought partner and a solid, researched proposal. Take time; it's a significant change that will shift AI behaviour.
- **Factions are real historical peoples, not fantasy (user, 2026-10-10, after a target player's reaction):** the target player strongly disliked the fantasy component. "I still want to keep the 'bend the rules' component, but the factions need to be based in real human history." Each faction becomes a historic empire whose story matches its abilities (user's examples: Switzerland for the Switzerland, Mongolia for the Mongols, Sparta for the Sparta), named as that people. Leader art shows a representative man AND woman of that people (no named leader): "Switzerland should have a man and a woman who look like they might be from Switzerland." All flavour text and art get rewritten. The TI4-style rule-bending mechanics stay.
  - **Final mapping (user, 2026-10-10):** Switzerland = Switzerland, Mongols = Mongols, Sparta = Sparta, Germany = Germany, Japan = Japan, Byzantium = Byzantium, Celts = Celts, Athens = Athens.
  - **The whole game goes historical:** "or at least something that feels grounded in human history -- not sci-fi or fantasy... the closer we can get to real history the better." Portraits show each people in its most recognisable era. Natural wonders are real ones "my target player would recognize".
- **Coalitions must be real: commit, strike together, open fronts (user, 2026-10-17, after the coach's Swiss review):** "the only way coalitions work is if people attack all at once and open multiple fronts. So far, the coalitions have felt fake, where they declare 'war' but don't do anything. The AIs should commit to war if they're going to declare it." On targeting the leader's VP sources: "Or, at a minimum, if part of a coalition, open up another front." The user also agreed the AI should spot an economic runaway early from public signs and send its strongest army, not its nearest, and should treat war on the leader as a commitment. For the AI this means: no declaration without a force staged for a named target; coalition members time their strikes together and attack on separate fronts, valued in the plan (no hard-coded "always join"); and a member with no army in reach at least opens a front by threatening a border city, so the leader has to split its defence. The balance is the user's open question ("not sure how to balance that"). **Measured, 2026-10-18:** gating war on a staged force backfired. Wars on the leader fell from 9.6 to 3.9 a game, and the user's what-if win chance rose (23% to 38% from T126), because even token wars cost the leader (forced spending broke his reserve goal for 27 turns). Commitment now comes from pricing: the cost of a peace forfeit and the value of a joint strike or new front, not a gate on declaring. Raise this with the user, since it qualifies "commit or don't declare". Measure it on the war scenarios and the human-seat benchmark, and check that wars-without-battles drops.

- **Early rushes are meant to be final; the counter is scouting, then Walls and archers (user, 2026-10-17, after the Sparta T87 review):** asked whether one-city eliminations at T18 are the intended stakes: "It is, but mostly because when you're scouting and you see Sparta next to you, the correct reaction is, '[uh oh]' and then build walls and archers to defend yourself as soon as possible. That's the value of scouting and making sure they don't come for you." No rule softens early conquest, and Byzantium gets no special early escape (the lead's reading: Walls plus its two levies is the counter; walled Tara cost the user 4 Hoplites). The AI must scout its neighbours early and treat a visible aggressive neighbour (a rush faction such as Sparta, or a growing army near its border) as a reason to build Walls and ranged units at once, from public information, by value scoring. It must never abandon Walls already under way.

- **Rules must not need explaining (user, 2026-10-17):** "I'm always looking for ways to simplify. If rules require explaining, it's hard to make a game work." Every rule or faction break must fit one plain sentence on the faction sheet. Prefer the simpler variant even at some cost in nuance.
- **A rule break acts on the map by the faction's own hand (user, 2026-10-17):** forced peace was a veto, and war subsidies "only work in theory: they can't actually force someone to move an army." Never design a break that depends on another player choosing to act.

- **Your own land plus one ring is always visible (user, 2026-10-17, attacked by an "unseen unit" on a hill inside his borders):** "like in Civ, anything in my borders (and by default one hex beyond my borders) should be visible to me at all times." Line of sight and hills never hide a unit inside your borders. (The open question about Celtic hunters is moot: no unit is hidden since 2026-10-23.)

- **Decisions of 2026-10-18 (user, morning answers):**
  - **Allied cities:** allies may not end a turn inside each other's cities, but may move through them ("lets go with 'no' but maybe allow transit through").
  - **Coalitions must fight like EU4's** ("factions don't just declare war and chill, they pretty much always attack with whatever they have"). An AI at war always prosecutes it: armies march on the enemy, sieges and battles happen. No token wars. This qualifies the 10-18 finding that gating declarations backfired: declare freely, then always fight.

Every attacker still lands its full hit, with overkill wasted. Cities split their melee counterattack the same way; the wall strike and ranged fire are unchanged. Chosen over "focus fire resolves in order" because ordering reintroduces sequencing that simultaneous turns avoid. Effect: rewards concentrating force (like flanking). The risk to watch at integration is rush strength against walls.

- **Assault damage overflows from a city to its garrison** (2026-10-04, the user's turn-36 assault on Tessitura). Within a stage, combined damage on a city tile brings the city's HP down first, and the excess carries over to the garrison, scaled by defense. If the garrison falls and a melee attacker took part, the city is captured that turn. Ranged overflow can kill but never captures. The defenders fight back once, as a combined counterattack split among the attackers. Replaces "No overflow within a stage", which made a garrisoned city impossible to take in the turn its HP was brought to 0.

- **Borders and city ownership are public on explored tiles** (user, 2026-10-04, Civ 6 behaviour: a city they lost still showed as theirs under fog). Tile owners and city owners update every turn on any explored tile, even out of sight. Units, city HP, garrisons, buildings and production stay fog-limited. Every player gets the same information, so the AI isn't cheating (pillar 6).

- **Population is territory** (user, 2026-10-04; for playtest 8). A new city owns its centre plus ring 1, all worked. Each population growth claims one more tile next to the city's border, the best one by the city's focus. There's no reach cap on claiming, but only tiles within 3 hexes of the city are worked; tiles claimed beyond that just block rivals. Food has a visible payoff, the hidden worked-tile rule disappears, and borders compete naturally. **Settled details (user, 2026-10-05):** the old border-growth sources (Monument family, Archive, Civic Networks, Japan’s Brood Hall) became Food and that was accepted. Claims go "nearest first, prioritizing quality": a city fills every claimable tile of its current ring before the next ring, taking the best tile by its focus within a ring (also beyond 3 hexes, where tiles are claimed but unworked); claims never lean toward rivals. **Follow-up (user, 2026-10-20):** Opening retune: the first Settler lost its 35% discount and the first growth costs 65 Food (the capital keeps its +2 Production), to hold first Settler ~T18 and second city ~T25.
- **Tile improvements, as built** (lead, within the user's decision; branch `claude/p8-improvements`): Farm (Wheat), Pasture (Cattle), Quarry (Stone) and Mine (Copper, Iron, Coal); 30 Production (Mine 40); the AI values them by yield gain over cost. **Strategic stockpile income is unchanged** (owning a known deposit is enough; the Mine only adds the tile's +1 Production), because deposits beyond the 3-hex work radius could otherwise never be improved. **Losing a tile loses its improvement, except to capture** (lead, 2026-10-06: Civ-familiar, and conquest is the core loop, so a conqueror inherits improved land; razing, release and founding a city on the tile still clear it). **Strategic supply stays as built, decided** (reconfirmed 2026-10-31): owning a known deposit is enough. ## Rejected ideas (don't re-propose without new evidence)
- A fungible production pool that carries over to any item (too exploitable; the user rejected it).
- The map-reveal ruin reward ("cartographer's cache"): it felt useless, so it was removed on 2026-10-03. Ruin rewards should be things you feel.
- Removing tech diffusion: it's a rubber band that keeps games competitive (pillar 11). Keep it, and make it visible in research forecasts.
- A fixed number of turns for a hunter's reveal (not flavourful).
- Making Walls available earlier to counter rushes (2026-10-03). The counter to a rush is units: a ranged garrison or two puts up a serious fight. Walls are what end the rush era. Instead: the city absorbs damage before its garrison (Civ 6).
- Traffic tolls; moving cities; Mongols contracts; hit-and-run (needs two-step orders; impossible with simultaneous turns and one order per unit).
- Starting with 3 Settlers (breaks balance; the user chose one).
- The National Academy to rescue Tall (Tall is dropped instead).
- A "+50% unit cost until Steam era" Germany drawback; a flat +30 unit cost.
- Skip-resistance as the Sparta bonus ("weak, flavourless").
- Hard v2 full-game openings as the app's Hard (easier and stalls); the hand-off version is opt-in only.

- Suggesting VP routes to the player ("fastest route", "buy 2 Soldiers to win", near-victory prompts). User, 2026-10-06: "I don't need the suggested way to get more VPs, I should be able to figure that out myself." Show the sources, their rules and their ownership; the player plans.

- Hard era cutovers like Civ 7's Ages (user, 2026-10-09: "I don't want hard era cutovers like civ 7... I do not want that"). No global age transition that resets or converts empires, swaps civs, or ends an era for everyone at once. Eras are per empire and continuous, Civ 6 style: you enter the next era through your own research. Era-staged VP and overreach costs key off each empire's own era or the world's progress, without a reset.

- Fantasy and alien factions (vault goblins, the insectoid Waxen Synod, elves): rejected 2026-10-10 by the designer and a target player. Use real historical peoples.

- A central contested landmark worth VP ("World Seat", Mecatol Rex clone): rejected 2026-10-10. It's too on the nose and favours central starts.

## How to propose
Use a short template: broken rule, flavour-linked constraint, path to 10 VP, visible counter. State the feasibility check result from `legatum-rules`. Offer a recommendation, not a menu. When the user pushes back, update this skill (principles or rejected list) in the same turn.
- **Goal-driven AI plans (step 2, lead decision 2026-10-05, provisional until the user playtests it):** Hard picks one plan per turn by its change in win chance: Economy/Peace, Campaign, Contain or Snipe. Coalition wars that can't take a city are a separate Contain plan, priced by the VP they deny the leader (pillar 11). Its denial term is an assumption, not yet measured. Result: Hard declares about a third as many wars, and each war takes twice as many cities. Whether that feels too passive is for the user to judge (the human-playbook benchmark runs at landing).

## Decided: conquest is won by capitals (user, 2026-11-15, after a Byzantium win on 8.9.41)
His words: "can we make the "conquest" victory condition just "take all the capitals"? otherwise, the late game can get pretty tedious tracking down every city and every settler." Rule (details by the lead, rules 37, row in the design doc log): a team wins at the end of the turn in which it is the only team still holding an original capital of its own. A rival's capital has fallen when another team holds it (a held, occupation-locked capital included) or it was razed; retaking it puts that team back in. Elimination and the VP hold are unchanged. "Last team holding its own capital" (Civ 5's wording) was chosen over "hold them all" because capitals can be razed and the Celts burn what they take. The general lesson: a phase with the winner known and no decision left gets cut, not shortened. Hard defending its capital and counting capitals is a separate AI increment.

## Decided: every empire starts with its capital founded (user, 2026-11-17)
His words: "can we start with the initial city already founded instead of a settler? the reason is that I can still get an edge by re-rolling until I get a city that's in a location that I want within a turn or two, and that just feels like cheating. the goal is to have starting locations that are as similar as possible". Rule (rules 45, row in the design doc log): the capital stands on the start hex at turn 1, no starting Settler for any faction, Scout and Soldier as before. The principle to carry forward: starts should be as alike as map generation can make them, and an edge a player gets by re-rolling the map is a flaw to remove, not a skill to reward. The lead's interpretation: if re-rolling for a better capital or weaker neighbours still pays, the next lever is tighter start balancing (`mapgen::balance_founding_sites`, `start_quality`), not a new opening step. This replaces "one starting Settler" in the 2026-10 list above.

## Decided: a unit slipping into a besieged city takes the volley first (user, 2026-11-21, Geneva T67)
- His order of events when a defender slips into a city: "1) slip into garrison (with support) 2) ranged attack 3) melee attack". A shot at a fallen, empty city waits for whoever comes in and hits them before the melee (rules 47; `legatum-rules` §4 "Held shots at a fallen city"). His framing: "if anyone was going in to support the city's defense, they should have been hit by my city attacks"; arriving after the volley was "a rules hack".
- A charge whose target dies to a shot before movement goes on against the next enemy on that tile (his turn-75 Settler that "got away"); an order never ends silently.
