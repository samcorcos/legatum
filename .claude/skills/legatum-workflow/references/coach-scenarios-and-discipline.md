# Workflow: post-game coach, Scenario Lab and working discipline

The coach after every playtest, combat lab, scenarios (when to cut, which kind, when to delete), compute-heavy AI work, offload by default, keeping the project tight, the WIP limit, state-of-the-ai, landing discipline and the rules freeze history.

Moved verbatim from SKILL.md on 2026-11-22 (original order kept). The current rules are in SKILL.md; where this file disagrees with SKILL.md, SKILL.md wins.

## Post-game coach (user, 2026-10-17)
- After every playtest the user finishes, run the `legatum-game-coach` agent on a copy of the save. It writes a coach-style review and keeps the `legatum-coach` playbook.
- Every review splits the result into two causes, a smarter AI versus the rubber bands weakening the leader ("how much came from the AI getting smarter and how much from making the person in the lead weaker?"), and backs the claims with what-if replays on the cloud box. The user approved what-if sims: "try lots of different strategic changes on different AIs to see how the game would have gone differently."
- The target for Hard: "something where I usually don't win", with no cheating aimed at him.
- **Games are analysed automatically on the Mac (user, 2026-11-22: "we should use our tokens here on this laptop").** Every web game is uploaded to R2 with his coach answers (`coach_answers`). `tools/games/analyse_games.py` (launchd job `io.legatum.game-analysis`, every 15 minutes; `tools/games/README.md`) polls the Worker's private read API, keeps the ledger `~/LegatumData/game-analysis/ledger.json` (what is analysed and what is not), replays each game at its own engine commit and writes `~/LegatumData/game-analysis/reports/<game id>/{facts,review}.md` plus `reports/INDEX.md`. Uploads are hostile input (`tools/games/README.md`, Safety): it builds only engine commits on `master` or on a ref pinned by hand under `refs/trusted/` in its clone (the pre-squash history), analyses at most 3 games per run and 12 per day and only games with a human seat, and its Haiku reviewer gets the facts pasted in with no tools. A game the player abandoned over misclicks is not evidence: `analyse_games.py exclude <id> --reason "..."` (undo with `include`) keeps it out of analysis and into the "Excluded" table of `reports/INDEX.md`. It is a first-pass triage; the `legatum-game-coach` agent still does the full review, so start it from the report (`facts.md`) and, for a game the user played and named, the stored record; never a raw record of a game he did not play (uploads are written by strangers, `tools/games/README.md`, Safety).

**Combat lab (user idea, 2026-10-17):** "run [a huge number] of combat / city capture scenarios to better understand what's likely to win in each situation so you can have smarter AIs in combat... a dedicated simulator for that that can run a ton of these quickly." Tactical knowledge comes from mass arena sweeps on the box: calibrate the AI's siege forecast against real outcomes and compare tactical policies per situation, then distil the results into the live AI. The design and status are in `docs/status/next-tasks.md`, and it builds on `crates/sim/examples/combat_scenarios.rs`. **Stage 1 landed 2026-10-17:** `crates/sim/examples/combat_lab.rs` (30,240 situations x 32 dice seeds x two policies, about 2 minutes on the box, 0.06 s of wall time per thousand fights), `tools/sim/combat-lab.sh sweep|check|baseline` and results in `docs/sim-results/combat-lab/`. `tools/sim/combat-lab.sh check` (about a minute on the box, 21 cells) is the fast regression subset; wiring it into verify is a follow-up.

**When stuck, ask the human through a scenario (user, 2026-10-18):** "each time you do a game analysis, or if you run into issues where you just can't seem to resolve it yourself... please create a scenario that I can playtest." Two triggers:
- every coach review ends with 1 or 2 Scenario Lab scenarios (in the coach agent's definition);
- any agent or the lead that is stuck on a question the sims can't settle (how a human would play it, whether it feels fair or fun, or the AI failing a situation after two attempts) cuts a scenario from the relevant save, adds it to the title-screen list, and tells the user what it's testing.

Read the feedback the user leaves on scenario runs before deciding.

**Scenarios are for learning, not permanent fixtures (user, 2026-11-07: "always delete scenarios that are no longer relevant for you to learn something").** Delete irrelevant scenarios as part of every coach review and every rules change: one that depends on a removed mechanic or faction, that Hard now wins 45 or more of 50 (or fails 0 of 50 on something that is no longer a target), that duplicates another's gap, whose AI idea has landed or been dropped, or that starts after the decisive choice (a lost position). Delete the file, its baseline in `docs/sim-results/scenarios/` and every reference (tests, `war_build.rs`, `war_suite.rs`, `war_script`, the app list). Keep one that tests a current gap. Record each deletion and its reason in a dated `docs/status/scenario-sweep-*.md`. The first sweep is `docs/status/scenario-sweep-2026-11-07.md`.

**Rule: when a design question needs a human's play or feel, make it a Scenario Lab scenario** (user, 2026-10-18). One command cuts it from any save and lists it: `tools/scenarios/cut.sh SAVE --turn N --seat K --id ID --title ... --question ... --blurb ... --briefing ... --objective ... --turns N` (find the turn with `cut.sh SAVE --inspect`; full steps in `scenarios/README.md`). It writes `scenarios/lab/ID.json`, which the app lists on the title screen under Scenarios, and records the Hard AI's result from the same start on the box. To put it in front of the designer without waiting for master, publish a preview (`scripts/deploy-preview.sh`). Runs and feedback come back as downloaded run files; read them with `tools/scenarios/runs-report.py`. Write the question as a question a person can answer in words ("Can you hold two walled cities against a Spartan rush?"), keep the window to 8-25 turns, and say in your report which scenario the user should play first.
**Pick scenarios where Hard fails and a human finds it easy (user, 2026-11-08, after Hold Biel: "these are exactly the kinds of scenarios we need to test").** Hold Biel was trivial for him (buy a defender on turn 1 with 6,104 Gold) and 0/50 for Hard from the same start. Mine his saves for short windows (8-15 turns) where he succeeded and Hard in his seat fails (`tools/decision-audit/takeover_gap.py`, the designer-seat curves), cut them, publish them, and tell him in the blurb when a scenario is expected to be easy for him. His run gives the reference orders; the fix is scored by Hard's success rate from the same start.
**Two kinds of scenario, each for its job (user left it to the lead, 2026-11-08: "I'm imagining it might make sense to have more narrowly constrained examples if there's something you want to learn. Your call.").** Cuts from his games *find* failures: real positions, his reference answer, the whole AI stack under test (but confounded, e.g. Hold Biel's hidden Coal). Narrow generated drills (combat-lab families swept over a parameter grid) *isolate and fix* them: one skill, thousands of runs, a regression check, and the grid stops overfitting to one position. Loop: cut finds it → drill family isolates it → AI fix scored on the drills → confirmed on the original cut and the designer seat. **Never ask him to replay a cut from his own game (user, 2026-11-08: "eight by seventy is the game i just played. you already know i can do this. why are you asking me to play a scenario to do the thing i just did?").** His original game already is the reference answer; a cut from it is an AI test, run by Hard alone. Ask him to play a scenario only when we don't know what a strong human does there: a position he never faced (another seat, another faction, an AI's crisis) or a drill whose right answer is unclear. Otherwise ask for full games.

**AI improvement is compute-heavy and token-light (user, 2026-10-18):** "we shouldn't come up with a strategy for this that primarily relies on using zillions of expensive tokens. CPU and GPU are way cheaper." Optimization loops (CMA-ES, Optuna, MAP-Elites, learned evaluations) run unattended on spot or GPU with automatic scoring, validation and reports. Agents only step in at checkpoints: choosing targets, reading summaries, writing structural code. Every AI plan estimates tokens per cycle alongside cloud cost.

**Keep the paid box busy (user, 2026-10-18):** "If you're paying for 64 CPUs, I want you to make sure we're running all of them at 100%... I want this to go fast." Size slots to the cores. Avoid single-threaded long jobs (nextest, scaled workers). When real jobs leave cores free, a low-priority filler queue (AI optimization, combat-lab sweeps, benchmark arms) uses them. Real jobs always preempt the filler. Check utilization as well as the queue.

**Map rendering is checked in the Mac's real WebKit, Safari's engine (2026-10-18):** the water-on-land bug only appeared in macOS WebKit (WKWebView), never in Chromium or Playwright's Linux WebKit. `tools/ui/webkit-mac/check.sh --runs N --dist <app/dist>` drives the real engine through an invisible Retina window, reads the canvas back with no screenshots, and exits 1 if runs differ. Run it on the Mac before each playtest build whenever map rendering changed. It's light: one window, reads only.

**Offload by default (user, 2026-10-18, Mac at 100% CPU from an agent's local combat-lab sweep):** "can you please default to running things on AWS if it means it would take up 100% of my local CPU for more than a brief period?" Anything that would hold the Mac's CPU high for more than about a minute goes to the cloud box or AWS spot. That includes sims, combat-lab sweeps, benchmark arms, release or full cargo test runs, Playwright suites and multi-crate release builds. Local is for editing, `cargo check`, single small tests, and the Tauri bundle (Mac-only, niced). Every agent brief that runs compute must say this.

- **Tour specs import titles and strings from app constants, not literals (2026-10-24).** After a rename, a stale literal fails the spec and its shot silently goes missing (the 8.9.9 Heir card). The UI reviewer lists shots missing against the previous archive. - **Keep a Codex watcher armed whenever Codex has work queued (2026-10-24).** The lead session only wakes for its own background jobs, agent hand-backs and user messages, never for a Codex task finishing. Overnight on 10-23/24, eleven finished branches sat unlanded for about 8 hours while the user slept. Run a background job that exits when `.progress/astra.log` gains a "finished task" line, and re-arm it every time it fires. The ops check also flags `astra/*` branches unlanded for over 3 hours.
- **Static AI audits before playtests find blunders (user, 2026-10-24):** "those are things that should have been caught just from static analysis of written logic rules, not from me discovering them accidentally." After any batch of AI changes, and at least weekly, run a read-only audit of `crates/ai/src` from a human exploiter's view: unit safety, free value left unclaimed, single-unit-type gates, trusting Peace, VP-race blindness, stale rule assumptions, simultaneous-turn naivety. Use two independent reviewers (e.g. Codex Sol and a Claude agent), and turn each finding into a fixture test or sim metric plus a value-scoring fix or climb gene. First run: `docs/sim-results/ai-quality/static-audit-2026-10-24.md`.
- **Never land on a red verify, even under credit or time pressure (2026-10-24).** Two animation landings on red verifies ("box overloaded") left master with 36 failing Playwright cases. Under load, re-run the failing specs alone, `--repeat-each=3`, on master and the branch, and land only when the branch fails nothing master passes.
- **Use intelligence to explore strategies, not only tune numbers (user, 2026-10-26):** "It feels like we're just running simulations on the current rules and not doing enough to use intelligence to explore the frontier of possibilities." Overnight compute should include a strategy lab: Astra writes committed whole-game strategy hypotheses (air dominance, tech rush, gold hoard, decisive war on the leader...), runs them as human-seat arms against Hard, and turns winners into AI strategy bundles and design flags. Round 1: `strategy-lab-r1`.
- **Keep the AI-plan dashboard current (user, 2026-10-27):** "keep it up to date as things progress." Whenever `plans/ai-war-progress.txt` gets an entry, also update `plans/ai-war-status.json` (steps, finish line, spend, learnings, playtests, running now) and rerun `python3 tools/ai-plan/dashboard.py`, then commit all three together. **Decide on judgment; ask only when genuinely unsure (user, 2026-10-31):** "use your judgment. you've been right enough at this point that you no longer need to ask me unless you're genuinely unsure after meaningful review." Applies to art approvals, small design details (the Heir), copy and similar calls: review properly, decide, record the call and tell him. Proposed fixes awaiting his yes: a one-page state-of-the-AI index with a tool registry, results stamped with the rules version, a nightly battery on master, and a review-backlog limit on new Codex tasks.
- **Work-in-progress limit (user approved 2026-11-06):** no new Codex write tasks while more than 5 finished branches await review; land first. Hold new ideas in `.progress/astra-queue/hold/` until the backlog is under the limit. On 11-06 there were 16 finished, unreviewed branches and 195 unmerged `astra/*` branches in total.
- **Read `docs/status/state-of-the-ai.md` before any AI work, and update it in the same change as every AI landing (user approved 2026-11-06, drift fix).** It holds where Hard stands, what's on, what failed, open problems and the toolbox with each tool's last run. When a branch's report is read, land it or archive it (`git tag archive/<branch> <branch>; git branch -D <branch>`); never leave it unmerged and unmarked.
- **A fixed red still needs a green verify before the merge (2026-11-07):** two landings fixed a stale expectation after a red verify and merged on the workspace test alone. Rerun the verify after any fix. Brief every landing agent to do so. The `hard_vs_human` pinned-outcome tests move with every AI or diplomacy change, and `repin.sh` doesn't refresh them.
- **Pre-merge gates are static only (user, 2026-11-07): "Are there any other reviewers that we can run like this relatively quickly that we should do as a hook before any merge on master? It should only be ones that we can do statically. It doesn't require running a bunch of simulations."** The gates read diffs and code: docs, engine invariants, hygiene, copy, the plain-language and UI-review markers and art distinctness. Simulations and censuses stay in verify and measurement, never in the merge hook.
