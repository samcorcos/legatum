# Legatum UI preferences

Pillars 5 (no hidden math) and 9 (the next action is always obvious) from CLAUDE.md govern everything here.

## Recurring feedback, as rules
- **Question every requirement on every screen (user, 2026-11-22: "Yes, question every requirement").** Clear is not enough: each screen and map piece is reviewed for what can be removed or merged without losing a decision, and before each release the whole game is checked for one icon, one word, one colour and one component per concept. The UI reviewer reports both (simplicity findings, consistency table); art quality itself belongs to `legatum-art-reviewer`.
- **Escape never throws away something he has to answer; whatever it hides, the turn report (L) brings back (user, 2026-11-21, Coach questions):** "sometimes I press escape and accidentally close out coaches notes. is there a way to find that?" and "it would be nice if they were in the game log ("L") so i can click on it if i accidentally dismiss it." The coach card grabbed focus when it appeared, so an Escape meant for the map skipped the question for good. Now Esc only hides the card behind a "Coach question" button in the same spot (draft kept); skipping takes the Skip button; the latest turn report lists the question with Answer (waiting) or Answer now (skipped). Apply to any card that wants a reply: Esc hides, an explicit button discards, and L can find it again.
  - A floating reopen button and the turn report never show together (at 1000x600 the button covered the report's header): while the report lists the item, the button hides (UI review, 2026-11-21).
  - A report button that reopens a card closes the report first, so the card never covers the report's own Close.
  - A component that restyles a `.btn.quiet` border needs at least `.btn.quiet.x` specificity, or `.btn.quiet { border-color: transparent }` silently wins and the button reads as a label.
- **Every faction sheet has the same shape (user, 2026-11-15, 8.9.59):** "the left side should be the + and the right side the -, but we have some that are inline and some that are overlapping"; "the symmetry is wrong. they should all feel the same in symmetry." Benefits stand in a left column and costs in a right column on every sheet (`.fs-pairs` with two `.fs-col`), never full-width rows on one faction and pairs on another. This replaces the 2026-10-17 rule of setting a cost beside the benefit it pays for. Every faction shows at least one cost, and every faction should have a special unit and a special building so the card row is the same everywhere (Byzantium, the Celts and the Aztecs have no building yet; Germany's cost is 1 Food for each Production building in a city).
- **Every military unit can auto-explore with E (user, 2026-11-14, 8.9.34):** "all military units should be able to 'e' to auto explore." Auto-explore is not a Scout-only order. A Scout still leads with it as its primary; other military units keep Fortify as primary and show Auto-explore (E) beside it. Civilians do not auto-explore.
- **A long list of cards has type-to-filter, and shows nothing that can't be used (user, 2026-11-14, Scenario Lab in 8.9.34):** "can we add a quick type to filter feature here? i can't even find the scenarios because there are so many" and "anything that can't be played should be deleted". The Lab has a filter box that takes any letter typed on the list (title, people, question, blurb; Esc clears it, Return opens the first match). Scenario files made for an older rules version are removed from his folder at install, never left as greyed-out "Cannot be played" cards.
- **A unit kept out of its line's fold says why on its card (user, 2026-11-15, Athens 8.9.47):** "these two units are presented when isn't Peltast on the same track as Arquebusier?" `unitLines` keeps an older unit as its own card when the newer one loses a capability (the Peltast's 3 moves against the Arquebusier's 2), but both cards read "Strikes from 2 tiles away without retaliation", so it looked like a bug. The older card's line leads with what it still does better ("3 moves; the Arquebusier has 2").
- **Two settings never share a name (user, 2026-11-15):** he opened 8.9.47 expecting "Training questions" to be the "Training" AI level next to Hard ("i thought this would be the same as 'training mode' next to hard"). The why-did-you-do-that setting is renamed "Coach questions" in the next build (approved: "you can rename it in the next version"). Before naming a new setting, grep the lobby for the word.
- **A scoring option that is available is announced, every time it becomes available (user, 2026-11-11, Athens game: the second Triumphal Arch):** "i didnt know it was there. would have gotten it earlier." He queued it 13 turns late. Anything that scores VP and can be bought or built again must show as available in This Turn and the race panel the turn it opens, not only in a build list.
- **The map never moves under a mouse that is aiming (user, 2026-11-11):** "i might click on a unit, then right click to tell it to move, but right as i click, the screen re-centers on the unit, which causes my move location click to be the wrong location." The delayed layout nudge that reveals a selected unit or city under the left panel (420 ms after the selection, `MapCanvas.tsx`, the reveal effect) is cancelled by a pointer press, a wheel turn, a key press, or a mouse move of more than 8 px after the selection; a still mouse still gets the reveal. Any new delayed or automatic camera move must follow the same rule. A camera move the player asked for (Return to the next unit, Go to city, a minimap click) goes through the focus request and starts at once (380 ms ease), never after a delay, and this cancel does not touch it.
- **Build lists are dense; no line holds one short item (user, 2026-10-28, city panel in 8.9.15.1):** "the density here is too low... the what you get (like +2 food) and the 'more' on separate lines leaves way too much blank space... requires too much scrolling." A build card's yield and its More toggle share one line, the card has no stretched empty rows, and Buy with its price sits on one line. Judge every list by cards per screen at 1000x600 and flag a line that carries one short token.
- **Buying a tile starts from one obvious button, never from tags that are always on the map (user, 2026-11-20, on the tile buying removed 2026-10-20):** "the challenge last time was that hte UI was so cluttered and confusing i couldnt figure out how to buy the tiles even if i wanted to." The old version put a price tag on every buyable tile whenever a city was selected, plus a row with an info tooltip in the panel. This replaces the 2026-10 rule "a selected city shows prices on buyable tiles". Rebuilt (rules 46, `claude/buy-tiles`): a button-like "Buy a tile · from N Gold" in the city panel beside the other purchases; it opens the same focused placement mode as a tile-chosen build (next entry), where buyable tiles are lit with their prices and a click buys; outside that mode the map shows nothing about tile buying except the queued tile's mark. Lead's reading (mine, not his words): a rarely used action needs a findable entry point more than a faster one, and an overlay that is always on reads as clutter, not as an invitation.
- **Rules from the 8.9.73 "Buy a tile" review (UI reviewer's proposals, accepted by the lead 2026-11-21; not the user's words):** the first build put the button in the picker's "Units" row, which is 302 px below the fold at 1000x600 in any city that is building (`uirev` shot B1), the case he complained about.
  - A control that has to be findable is shot in a city that is building, at 1000x600 and scroll 0. The tour's city fixtures were all idle, and an idle city auto-scrolls to the picker and hides the problem.
  - "Buy a tile" lives under "Tiles N worked · M owned" in the territory group when the city is building, as a full-width row of normal button height; in an idle city, where the panel opens on the picker, it stays in the picker's head. One instance, never both.
  - A disabled reason never sits inside a letter-spaced caps button. The label stays ("Buy a tile") and the reason goes beside it in sentence case, free to wrap; reasons from core are mapped to a short phrase and an unmapped one wraps.
  - In a focused placement mode: first-game hints and toasts are suppressed; a click on a candidate that cannot be chosen is refused in place (the mode stays open, the reason shows on the tile card); the card states the resource being spent, because the top bar is dimmed; the hovered candidate's chip is drawn above the hover wash.
  - A new purchase control gets tour shots of each disabled state and of the "another purchase is queued" state.
  - Two purchases that share a slot refuse each other the same way ("Cancel the Settler purchase first"); neither silently replaces the other.
- **A tile-chosen build is placed on the map, like Civ's districts (user, 2026-10-29, the Yam Relay Station):** "why are there so many of these? isn't there just one relay?" then "you pick the thing you want to build, then you select the tile in the UI (with valid tiles being highlighted somehow). that's how it works in civ for district placement". One card per building (Yam Relay Station, each Great Work), never a card or row per tile. Its Build, Queue or Buy opens placement (a focused mode since 2026-11-07, see "Wonders are always visible"): every eligible tile is highlighted on the map, the hovered tile's card says what it gives up, a click places it, Cancel, Escape or a click elsewhere cancels. Once placed, the card names its tile and its cost ("Plains, 3 tiles W", "gives up +1 Food, +1 Production"). A short list of tiles under the card was tried and rejected. A placement shows three marks (UI review, 2026-10-29): highlighted candidates bold enough (gilt, `render.ts::draw`) to read over territory tint, the hovered tile's cost in its tile card ("gives up", the same words as the card), and a lasting mark on the chosen tile once placed, distinct from any border (a gilt double ring). Highlighted tiles sit under city banners and unit tokens; only the hovered one is drawn above them. A placement card's instruction line wraps, never truncates (8.9.17 UI review).
- **Territory must be readable at a glance (a target player, 2026-10-09):** "having a hard time telling whose territory is whose. The colors... might be a little bit too pale." The user: "very elegant and beautiful, but it might be too subtle." Borders need Civ-level clarity (a strong seat-coloured line plus a visible territory tint), with neighbours distinguishable for colour-blind players, and without losing the atlas look. **Built (2026-10-09, branch `claude/borders-legible`; sheet `docs/design/borders/borders-variants.png`):** territory borders are variant C, "ownership at a glance": from the boundary inward an ink edge, a bold seat-colour line (about 3.4 world px, never under 2.6 CSS px), a band fading over a third of a hex, and a visible tint of 0.18 over the whole territory. Each empire paints only its own side of a shared boundary, so neighbours sit ink against ink and never overpaint, and a boundary with another empire gets a heavier ink rule. **Colours are per faction, not per seat (user, 2026-11-11: "countries have similar colors across games... e.g. sparta as red"):** `FACTION_PALETTE` in `theme.ts` gives each faction one colour in every game, the player's own empire included: Sparta crimson, Athens azure, Byzantium violet, Celts jade, Mongols amber, India rose, Aztecs lime, Germany steel, Switzerland deep teal. `playerColor(id)`, `mapPlayerColor`, `mapBorderColor` resolve the seat's faction from the game on screen (the store calls `setSeatFactions`), so call sites keep passing a player id; where there is no game yet (lobby, join screen, scenario cards) use `factionColor(faction)`. If two seats share a faction (the lobby allows it), the later one wears the colour of a faction that is not playing (`colorFactions`). "You" is told by words and the You marker, never by colour. Barbarians keep brick red. Border colours come from `ATLAS.seat.border[faction]` (`mapBorderColor`), not `mapPlayerColor`: the same hue families with the lightness staggered so every pair of the nine stays at least 10 CIEDE2000 apart for normal, protan, deutan and tritan sight (`seatColors.test.ts`; the toned-down map colours had azure and violet at 2 under deutan). The per-seat dash patterns are not used by the new looks. A (line and thin band only) and B (Civ-style two-tone, tint 0.20) stay selectable with `?borders=a|b|c|current` (remembered under `legatum.dev.borders`, or `legatumMap.setBorderVariant(v)`) until the user picks. The Borders lens keeps its 0.3 cap; this tint is the always-on territory, not a lens. Counters, banners and roots still use `mapPlayerColor`, so they are not yet CVD-checked. Tour fixture: `src/test/bordersFixture.ts`, shots `e2e/borders-variants.spec.ts`.

- **Sound effects are organic and in-world, never robotic (a target player, 2026-10-09):** they like the music, but the click, Return and move-order sounds are "too techy and robotic... not in theme for what the units are actually doing." Effects come from physical sounds matched to the action (footsteps for moves, a closing ledger for ending a turn, cloth or leather for selection), never digital blips or beeps.

- **The sound on frequent clicks is still disliked (a target player, via the user, 2026-11-20):** the user's co-op partner hates "the sound that the game makes when i click on things like queuing production, moving units, etc." Found: one sample, `orders/commit.m4a` (three footsteps, 0.77 s), plays for every unit move and every production, research and goal order (`editOrders(..., 'commit')`). Lead's reading, not yet confirmed by the partner: it is too long and too frequent, and footsteps are wrong for queuing a building. Rules to carry forward: a sound heard dozens of times a turn is under 0.3 s and quiet; one sample never serves unrelated actions; the partner chooses frequent sounds by ear from an audition page (a private document) because the lead cannot hear them. **The partner's choice (2026-11-20, "for choosing research, let's go with page. and swish for movement"):** a page turn (0.2 s) for production and research choices (feel event `queue`), a cloak swish (0.3 s) for unit orders (`commit`), both at -24 LUFS; shipped in 8.9.73. Replacing the footsteps confirmed they were the sound meant; loudness was not mentioned, so it stays as auditioned.

- **Resources and map features must look desirable (a target player, 2026-10-09, first look at the game):** they miss "how pretty the resources are in Civilization... even in strategic mode... it makes you almost want to get them. Ours are... very simple icons with no real art." They also loved Civ's natural wonders: "things that look pretty on the strategic map that give you bonuses." Map art for things you want (resources, wonders) is painterly and covetable, not abstract glyphs, while staying legible at both zooms.

- **Faction and rules text is plain language: benefit and cost, never riddles (user, 2026-10-09, on the Byzantium and Germany sheet lines):** "Some of the language in these things is super confusing... what is the cost and what is the benefit? Are some just general flavor text?" Every strength line says what you get, with the number. Every price line says what it costs or blocks as a concrete game effect. No idioms ("free lunch") or flavour in rules lines; flavour belongs only in lore.

- **Every faction goal is discoverable, with visible progress (user, 2026-10-08, Switzerland):** "It's entirely unclear to me how to get the +2 VP special thing just for this faction. Where do I find that?" Passive goals (like the 1,000 Gold hoard) need their rule and progress in the race panel, the objectives sheet and This Turn, never only a bare token.

- **Borders and cities are live through fog (user, 2026-10-08):** "Even when there is fog, we should still show borders... it should even show new cities that are built, etc. That's how Civ does it." On explored tiles, territory and city ownership are always current. Never show a stale owner, banner or HP bar on a fogged city.
- **A rival closing on victory is never a surprise (user, 2026-11-11, Sparta game in 8.9.31):** "the celts just got 10vp and i was surprised. there should probably be a warning of some kind once someone gets to 8 or something." The hold alarm at the target is too late to be the first notice. When any team first comes within 2 VP of its target (8 of 10), every player gets a warning: who, their VP and target, what scored it, and what would stop them, with Show on map and a route to the race panel. It fires once per team per crossing (again only after it drops below and returns), leads the turn report, and never fires for a team already in its hold. Pillar 4.

- **Each faction has a preferred colour, the same in every game (user, 2026-11-11):** "it would be nice if the factions had 'preferred colors' like they do in civ so that countries have similar colors across games. it just makes it easier to tell who people are. e.g. sparta as red". Colour follows the faction, not the seat: borders, tokens, banners, crests, race rows and the minimap all use the faction's colour, including for the player's own empire. The colour comes from who the faction is (Sparta red). The border palette still has to pass the colour-blind distance test for every pair of factions.
- **No "coalition" in the UI (user, 2026-11-11):** "the 'coalition' thing doesn't need to be an explicit mechanic... you can always just declare war on them. maybe we simplify?" then "yes put the coalition ui change in 8.9.31.1". The coalition flag, its "Alliances signed against" list and the coalition alarm are removed. A runaway is shown with plain facts ("The Celts are 2 VP from winning. At war with them: Sparta, India.") and the actions: Declare war, Offer Alliance to an empire already at war with them, Show what holds their VP. The AI still gangs up on the leader; it just has no player-facing name.

- **A city under attack is never a surprise (user, 2026-10-08, playtest Switzerland T59):** "This was the first turn I realized this city was even under attack. I could have easily defended it, but I just had no idea it was happening... I shouldn't be surprised to see that one of my cities is about to get taken over." Make it unmissable:
  - a top-priority This Turn item with the city, its HP, the attackers and a capture forecast, plus a go-to button;
  - an alarmed city banner on the map;
  - the first line of the turn report;
  - an early warning when an enemy army approaches a city at war.
  - **Built (2026-10-08, `app/src/siege.ts` is the one place the logic lives):**
    - **This Turn:** `SiegeAlerts.tsx` is the first thing in the dock body. Each card has the city, `HP/200` on a bar with last turn's loss hatched and signed ("−110 HP last turn"), who can strike it next turn with the damage they do at average rolls, a one-line capture forecast ("Falls next turn unless reinforced", "Holds about 2 turns unless reinforced", with the math in its tooltip), and a "Go to city" button. It fits at 1000x600 at scroll 0 (the screen tour's `expectDockWhole` checks it).
    - **One primary:** a city an enemy can strike next turn that you have not opened this turn is a `siege` pending decision (`turnFlow.ts`, `store.siegeSeen`). It outranks every routine decision, the turn's call to action becomes "City under attack · <city> · falls next turn", Return goes there, and ending the turn asks first. Opening the city answers it; the card and map alarm stay while the siege lasts.
    - **Map:** `map/siegeMark.ts`. A wide numbered HP bar (`200/200`), a siege badge (crossed swords on a red disc) and a double ring (white halo under red) that pulses gently; steady under reduced motion. Zoomed out it is a ring, a badge and a short bar on the seal. Colour is never alone (badge, digits, bar length). The app has no colour-blind setting.
    - **Report:** `Under attack` is its own first category: "Waypost Seven was attacked: 200 → 120 HP by 3 Soldiers", red, with Show on map and the strike-by-strike details. HP before comes from the view before the turn (one stage's battles all report the HP left after the whole stage).
    - **Early warning:** two or more units of one empire at war with you within 3 tiles of a city (not beside it) give an orange "Army closing on <city>" card. It is not a decision and does not alarm the banner.
    - **Review rules (UI reviewer, 2026-10-08):** an alarmed banner is drawn above unit tokens (three adjacent attackers covered its name and production label); an alarm names the attacking empire ("3 Soldiers from Byzantium · Rival", the report line too); every map alarm has an off-screen route, so the minimap rings the city (red for an attack, amber dashed for an approaching army); a forecast line carrying a pillar-5 number is at least 12 px; tour shots of animated states wait out the damage floaters and park the mouse. Left open: no edge arrow toward an off-screen alarmed city, and the Combat section's "held against a combined assault" line still reads oddly beside the Under attack line.
    - **A loaded game has no turn report,** so the card says "Took damage last turn" from `CityDetail.attacked_last_turn` instead of a number.

- **Announcements and claims (UI review of World Ages, 2026-11-11):**
  - A toast or banner never draws over a takeover, moment card, modal or the open turn report, and never outlives the turn it announces. An announcement about the player's own action is folded into that action's celebration.
  - A banner with a cost column is shot at 1000x600 with the race panel open; nowrap cost chips must not squeeze the text column below about 180 px.
  - A claim that spends units or Gold names exactly what is spent and any crown or VP it costs before it commits; afterwards the surface shows "Queued · Undo".
  - When an objective becomes claimable, its button appears on the always-visible race row and as a This Turn item, not only inside the ledger.
  - A world-level reveal (a new Age) gets a report line so it can be reopened.
  - A one-off building never reuses another building's thumbnail in the same list.
  - Rule text that depends on a game option is branched everywhere it appears (Guide, ledger header, report lines); grep for the old sentence.
- **Faction sheets show the art we have (user, 2026-11-15, Choose your empire on Germany):** "we have art availbale that we're not showing, like for units." A faction's special unit and special building each get their painted art on the sheet, never a text row with a generic icon; any new faction art is wired into the sheet in the same change.
- **A faction sheet shows the painted art of its special unit and special building; each rule once; goal in one block (user, 2026-11-15, Sparta: "what ever happened to the screen redesign"):** the unit and each live building are painted cards under the trait rows (a faction with no building shows one card, with the goal beside it); the goal block states the goal, its VP and the action and key, and replaces "How you win"; "How you lose" appears only when it is not a price already on the sheet (the opening resource tip was removed on 2026-11-21, see below). A new faction's sheet must still lay out with no goal, no counter or placeholder art.
- **One unmistakable primary action per screen;** anything clickable looks clickable before hover. Acting on a decision card closes it.
- **A unit has two idle buttons** (user, 2026-10-20; the five orders Hold, Sleep, Skip, Heal and Wake were folded into two, from his own use of Hold 221, Skip 265, Heal 98 times in 24 games): military units say Fortify, civilians say Wait, and both use F; Skip this turn uses Space. Fortify or Wait is the primary on a unit's card unless the unit leads with Auto-explore (Scout) or Found City (Settler). Any new order replaces it, including Skip this turn. A unit status says "Fortifying" or "Waiting" until woken. A waiting ranged unit does not fire. Keep the internal `SleepUnit` and `StandingOrder::Sleep` names for save and tape compatibility.
- **Heal (H) is back beside Fortify (user, 2026-11-01):** "heals the unit until it's healed then brings them back into the action queue. i find that I 'f' them and then i forget they exist." A damaged unit's row is Fortify, Heal, Skip this turn (Heal is hidden at full HP; where nothing heals it is off with its reason under the row). Its tooltip gives the rate where the unit stands and the turns to full (pillar 5). A healing unit wears the `mark-heal` badge and says "Healing"; when it wakes healed it returns to the Return queue labelled "healed to full, needs orders" and the report says so. A rest order that ends by itself must put the unit back in the queue; don't fold Heal into Fortify again.
- **A unit a rule holds in place shows it on the map and the card before you try to move it (user, 2026-11-11, occupation lock in 8.9.31):** "there needs to be some kind of UI indicator that the unit is locked, otherwise it just feels like a bug." A status word on the card and a refusal at the click are not enough. The unit's map token wears a lock mark (icon, not colour alone), the card leads with the lock and its way out ("Locked in <city> until Peace with <people>"), and hovering the token says the same. Applies to any future rule that pins a unit. **A locked unit also stays out of the action queue (user, same day):** "maybe the locked unit just isn't even activatable... they don't go into the action queue. they're just automatically fortified and you can't take actions with them." It is fortified automatically and never asks for orders. Exception he accepted ("makes sense to me"): it stays clickable for what the lock still allows (upgrade, a ranged shot), and a ranged occupier with a target in range still comes up, so the lock never silently costs a shot.
- **A garrison released from a held city comes back to the queue (user, 2026-11-15):** "when cities go from 'held' during an attack to 'mine' after peace or total victory, the units that are fortified there should go back to attention so they're in queue. i often forget they exist." The occupation lock fortifies those units for him, so when Peace or the rival's elimination lifts it, each one asks for orders once ("<city> is secure, needs orders") in the Return queue and the report. Same principle as Heal: anything the game put to rest for him must hand itself back when the reason ends.
- **Keyboard first:** Return confirms or advances (including closing overlays and "what happened"); Esc closes; accept and decline have keys. Show shortcuts on buttons.
- **Explain "why not".** Disabled or invalid actions say why in plain words, with how to fix it (e.g. a blocked ranged shot: "No line of sight: the Forest at X blocks the shot; fire from a hill or move adjacent"). Never a silent bounce or a stuck "needs orders" loop.
- **Locked upgrades nest under the building they follow; they don't take a full card until they can be built.** Keep the upgrade visible as a compact, actionable row, and preserve the full explanation on hover.
- **Costs are loud:** a cost (e.g. an Alliance's Gold fee and upkeep) must be visibly a cost, in red, never looking like a free lunch.
- **Neutral "nothing yet" lines are muted ink, never green** (8.9.4 review: "No discounts yet" on research cards). Green means a real gain.
- **A defeat line's rank handles ties** ("finished tied for 2nd, 1 VP short"; the 8.9.4 reveal said "2nd" while tied at 9).
- **Good versus bad at a glance:** events and logs are colour-coded from the viewer's perspective (red bad, green good, neutral otherwise), with an icon or sign too, never colour alone; HP changes as signed numbers.
- **Big moments get a full interstitial (Civ 6 style):** a declaration of war on you must be impossible to miss. Use a full-screen moment before anything else, with the declarer's crest, what it means now and "Show on map"; a report line isn't enough (playtest 6: the user noticed Sparta's war several turns late). Lighter versions suit other major events: an ally's war, a broken treaty, a lost city.
- **Entering an era is a celebrated takeover, and the board changes with it** (user, 2026-10-10: "a cool animation, popup, takeover"; "something should change in the game board"). **Built (phase C, branch `claude/era-takeover`; `celebrate/EraUpTakeover.tsx`, `eraTakeover.css`, `eraArt.ts`):**
  - Your own `EraEntered` holds a full-screen takeover at the start of your turn: the era's key-art painting full-bleed (Ken Burns drift under a warm night wash), a gilt numeral seal, "The Classical Era" with a letter-spaced reveal, gold motes, then three parchment panels: **New this era** (the units and buildings its technologies open for your faction, icons, "+N more" past 8), **Era objectives** (the three, "any one scores 1 VP") and **Your cities** (the previous era's painted city beside the new one at your largest city's size). The footer says the strict gate in one line ("5 to go") and holds the one primary: "Choose research" when nothing is queued, else "Continue", with Return; Esc is Later.
  - The whole sequence is under 3 s and every part of it is skippable at once: Return, Esc or the button close it at any moment. It is local to your screen, never touches an order or the turn, never blocks a teammate (pillar 7), waits behind an open dialog and returns, and under reduced motion the finished scene stands with no animation. Rivals still get only the banner and the report line.
  - The sting is the organic japan (`fanfare.legendary`, via `soundHooksForMoments`), never a synthesized blip.
  - Key-art JPEG derivatives (1280 and 1920 wide, quality 84) load through `srcset`; the PNG masters stay in the docs.
  - **Review rules (UI reviewer, 2026-10-10):** text over a painting gets its own local dark scrim and a tight shadow and is checked on the brightest eras (Renaissance, Future, Classical), at least 12 px, with gilt only on rules and never straight on sky or stone; the primary on a takeover is the highest-contrast control (filled gilt, ink text, a `drop-shadow` glow, because the global notched `.btn.primary` clips box-shadows) and shots include its unfocused state; entrance animation applies to panels, never to the buttons, which are on screen within 0.4 s; the era-up's turn-report line has "Show card" like an unlock card (`reopenEra` in the store); a panel with 0-2 rows fills with something true (the era's technology names) instead of standing blank; a faction ornament drawn on a painted city is drawn in sprite space on the roofs, never a flat vector at the hex centre.

- **Marks that share a cell never overlap** (map-art polish review, 2026-10-11): an improvement glyph and a resource medallion get 2–3 px of clear space (or stack with a gap), checked at both window sizes. A letter-glyph fallback for a painted icon ("I 14" for Iron) carries a tooltip with the full name.

**Map bonuses are visible and explained (user, 2026-10-11, playtest 8.8.1: "Grand canyon and I'm sure other wonders break the yield view and probably the other lenses... Are there bonuses?... I can't see what they are"):**
- **Map art never hides yields or lens overlays; every map bonus is visible on the tile and explained on hover.** Painted map art (natural wonders, and anything like them) is drawn right after the terrain, before improvements, lens tint, yield icons, resource medallions, borders, units and banners, kept to about 2.5 hex radii wide (about 1.4 hex widths; it sits under every mark, so size can never hide one, but a wide thin landmark must still show at least a hex wide and 0.8 hex tall), and its alpha drops to about half under every lens. A bonus the art stands for gets a badge on its own tile in every lens ("+25% [yield icon]" for a wonder: a parchment pill in the hex's upper half at least 10 px of text, narrow enough to stay inside the hex and off its border line so it never lands on a neighbour's yield icons or the owner's border, a coloured pip on the far-zoom mark; a yield is told apart by its icon shape, not hue alone), above the lens tint and below units and banners.
- **Tile tooltips lead with the most valuable thing on the tile: wonder, then resource, then terrain.** The lead block is the painted art (wonder vignette 56 px, resource medallion 46 px), its name and its kind (Natural wonder, Strategic resource, Bonus resource). Under it, short separate benefit lines ("+1 ⚙ with a Mine", "+2 Iron per turn inside your borders", "The city that owns this tile gets +25% Science"), then the status as the next action ("Unclaimed: bring it inside your borders to gain +2 Iron per turn", "Held by your city Kyoto: +3 Science each turn", the open discovery reward), and only then the terrain name, yields, move cost and defence as the secondary block. A tile with neither keeps the terrain as its title. No run-on resource sentence. A tooltip states each number once (the benefit line and the status line never repeat it; the status is only the next action), and red is for bad: a resource's kind label is ink, never red.
- **A bonus is named wherever its number lands:** the owning city's yield breakdown has the wonder as its own row (painting, "Grand Canyon +25%", amount and icon), the city panel lists it in one line above the economy, the Science hover has a "Natural wonders" row instead of lumping it into "Faction bonus", the Settle card names unclaimed wonders in reach with their bonus, and the Guide lists all eight with bonus and reward. ## Game text: plain language

Every word a player reads (sheets, cards, the Guide, tooltips, breakdowns, report lines, refusals) follows this guide (user, 2026-10-17: "'per complete pair' is not normal language... is there a rule... to make sure we use normal language"). The cause of the bad wording was agents translating code literally (`floor(pop / 2)` became "per complete pair"). Write what the player gets, not how the code computes it.

- **Tone:** a good board-game rulebook, or Civ's Civilopedia at its clearest. Plain, warm, exact.
- **The card says the rule; the Guide says the fine print (user, 2026-11-21, on the Celts sheet line "Barbarians never attack the Celts, all game. No camp, scout or raider touches your units, cities or Settlers, and the warband camps you raise raid your rivals only."):** "a typical board game ... would tell you the simple explanation and then in the rules, there would be a much more detailed explanation in the rule book. e.g. this one might say 'Barbarians don't attack the Celts' and that's about it. Then all the nuance of other things (can i attack them? can i take camps? when i attack them, does it still do damage?) get handled in the rule book." A sheet row, card, tooltip, tip, notice or report line is one short sentence (about 15 words, two at most) that a player can act on. Exceptions, what it doesn't cover, interactions and edge cases go in the Guide (`GuideContent.tsx`; a faction sheet row's longer `detail` is what the Guide prints). Drop emphasis that adds no rule ("all game", "always"). The headline must still be true: for every faction that sees it ("up to level 5" is false for Sparta), in every era it applies to (Mongols build Walls from the Medieval era), and everywhere a shared string is reused (a unit's rule on a tech card must still name the unit). `legatum-plain-language-reviewer` checks this on every PR that changes player text.
- **A card's way to the rulebook (UI review, 2026-11-21):** a faction sheet ends with "Full rules in How to Play", which opens the Guide at that faction's own rules. A link-style button is at least 13px in the accent colour with a `:focus-visible` ring, so it reads as clickable before hover. A deep link into a modal scrolls only the modal's content pane, never the dialog shell: the title and Close stay in view.
- **A number the player can change is never stated as fixed (user, 2026-11-22, on the title screen's "You against five AI empires": "it could be more or less than that"):** say the range ("1 to 7 AI empires", built from the constants) or show the live count; "five" is only true as "five by default".
- **Numbers first, in the player's terms:** "+1 Gold for every 2 population", not "per complete pair of population". "Up to 100 HP", not "(max 100)". "At least 1 move", not "(minimum 1)".
- **No mechanics-speak or code terms,** unless the game teaches the word: floor, rounded down, complete, held (for Gold: say "in your treasury"), accrue, tick, live or permanent VP source, base (strength, cost), adjusted, multiplier, stage, view, excepted, retain, provided that, mutually exclusive, Σ, max/min, i.e./e.g. A rounding rule is never worth a clause: state the number or say "about".
- **In-theme words for mechanics:** interest (Swiss Gold from the treasury), tribute (bought peace), levy (Byzantine armies), the agoge, a vow (Sakoku). Lore may be colourful; rules lines are plain.
- **Every rule fits one plain sentence** (user, `legatum-design`). If a rule needs a paragraph of exceptions to say, flag it as a design smell rather than writing the paragraph.
- **One idea per sentence,** at most about 30 words, no more than one semicolon. Active voice, second person ("you", "your cities"). No stacked qualifiers ("each military unit beyond your first two, Scouts excepted, per turn"): split them into sentences.
- **Say what it means for the player and when it matters,** not how it is implemented. "Your units deal half damage to city HP", not "half damage, rounded down after the attack roll and any siege bonus".
- **A live goal says how it is lost** in a sentence: "You lose the VP while your treasury is below 1,000 Gold", never a "Live:" tag.
- **Text shown for another empire is third person** ("its land"), and shared rule text built by one function never says "you" for a rival.
- **The same term everywhere** for the same thing. The glossary below is canonical; its synonyms are lint failures.
- **A drawback sits beside the ability it pays for** (user, 2026-10-17, Germany's sheet: unrelated rows side by side read as related). A faction sheet row pairs a strength with the price that offsets it; a strength with no matching price stands alone across the row (`FactionPair.price` is optional). A "drawback" that only restates its ability is not a drawback: cut it.
- **A faction sheet row is the rule, not its fine print (user, 2026-11-05, Swiss sheet: "i think we can simplify this"):** one idea per card ("Only Switzerland can buy a world wonder" (superseded 2026-11-06: Swiss wonder buying is cut and nobody buys wonders); "Military unit upkeep is triple: 3 Gold a turn instead of 1"). Leave out exemptions every empire shares (Scouts pay no upkeep for anyone), consequences the player can work out ("can finish a contested wonder immediately", "can delay the reserve goal"), and one example item's price standing in for a class (a Great Library price when every era has its own wonder). Two unrelated drawbacks are two cards, not one paragraph.
- **Compare a replacement with what it replaces in the same breath:** "Ruhr Foundry: +6 Production (a Forge gives +3)", never a bare "+3" that leaves the player asking whether it is on top.
- **No standing explainer notes** that restate what each line already says (a "A live goal gives VP only while..." note under goals that each say how they are lost). Say it once, where it applies.
- **Every form of a template reads well:** check the one-item and many-item forms ("moved 1 tile, now 1 tile E of Kyoto" as well as "moved 3 tiles, now ..."), the at-Peace and at-War forms, and your own and a rival's.
- **A note under a breakdown uses the breakdown's own split** (a growth note saying "30 + 16 per population" over a table of "Population growth + Game pace" is two stories).
