# UI: process, and per-screen rules as built

Verification process, tour rules, Sakoku, held goals, painted units, Byzantium screens, the 8.8 and Quick-speed reviews, pickers, structured text, the research screen, strength chips, tile tooltips, era objectives, the Settle lens, unlock hovercards, unique-piece comparison, overlays and lenses, the Cities overview, the race panel, aircraft, faction sheet text, the Keshig, Siege review rules.

Moved verbatim from SKILL.md on 2026-11-22 (original order kept). The current rules are in SKILL.md; where this file disagrees with SKILL.md, SKILL.md wins.

## Process
- Text added inside the forecast card (`.forecast` is overflow-hidden with a capped height) joins the short-window trimming rule beside `.forecast-note`, or is checked by hand at 1000x600 (6.3 review: the walls/city-fire math sat at zero margin).
- The user's laptop window is about 1000x600 CSS pixels: treat it as the primary desktop size, not an edge case.
- Highlighted states (current research, selection) must stay legible: never dark text on a dark fill.
Verify UI changes with Playwright screenshots at the three sizes and attach them to the report. When the user gives UI feedback, add the lesson here in the same turn if it generalises.
- The screen tour shoots every gallery option (all 8 factions plus Random) at both sizes, parks the mouse before every shot (8.3: a tooltip leaked into shot 77), and its fixtures agree with what the screen says (8.3: "most VP" over a table listing You last).
- A fix the review asks for in the tour spec (new shots, mouse parks) comes back as a patch and lands with the release; otherwise the next tour silently loses it (8.3b: the 04c sheets and the shot-77 mouse park were lost this way).
- Timing-dependent UI tests use Playwright's paused clock (`clock.install` plus `pauseAt`), never real-time sweeps, because release machines run at load 100+. Tour shot names are stable ids (faction id, not visible text). Great Works rules prose belongs in a tooltip; the Works themselves are build cards ("Locked items look like the things you build").
- **Sakoku, Japan's public vow (2026-10-10, `claude/sakoku`):** one action both ways. The race panel's goal row says "Close the country" or "Reopen the country" (G) and carries the meter ("N of 30 turns closed", "Not closed yet · 0 of 30 turns"); a long state label never goes in the dashed-crest slot of the race row (it covered the empire's name), so labels there stay under about 26 characters. The confirm lists exactly what closing blocks as a four-row table (War, Alliances, Tech diffusion with the Science it costs now, Foreign units), the table is never height-capped (`goal-rows tall`: a cut row defeats "exactly what it blocks"), and it must fit 1000x600 with its primary in view. The countdown is public: a `closed` chip (torii mark and "12/30", "holding") sits beside the empire's tokens in every race row (`ClosedChip`), the goal card's facts repeat it, and `SakokuClosed`/`SakokuReopened` are report lines for everyone. The map draws a closed country's border as a palisade (ink rule with inward ticks, gilt once the VP holds). The vow's refusals name it ("X has closed the country (Sakoku): ...") wherever a war, Alliance or move is refused.
- **Held goals (Byzantium's Heir, 2026-10-07):** the race token sits in the holder's row with the goal's own crest and the card says who holds it; the unit panel states whose Heir he is, who holds him and what a capture does (at Peace: declare War first); disbanding him asks first, in place of the Disband row, and states the forfeited VP. A built-once building that counts toward a goal (the Engine) stays in the build list as a greyed "Built" card carrying the goal's count.
- **Held goals, generalised for the Courier Hall (2026-10-07):** a held goal's race token wears the item's own icon (Heir, Hall), not the faction crest; its card says who holds it and, when the city or unit is on your map, where (`heldAt`; "in view" or "last seen"), with a "Show on map" button on the objectives-sheet slip as well as the token click. A Hall hand-over is one report line from the new holder's event, a premium moment for each participant (the plain city-captured card steps aside for it), and the open road shows as a note under a unit standing on the holder's land and in the Peace line of the diplomacy panel ("their land is open to you"). The Hall's build card says what it scores, what it opens and that it is one per empire; a standing Hall reads Built, one lost to a rival says retake the city.
- Tour fixtures must be consistent with the rules: staged VP and hold states must be ones the rules can produce, so a reviewer can judge whether the text is true.
- Render-only tour staging updates every number the HUD shows (yields, VP) along with the card it stages. A calibration commit that changes a goal number also updates the screen tour's assertions.

- **Units are painted (A2, 2026-10-10):** the map draws the painted miniature (`assets/units`, `unitArt.ts`) on a base washed in the owner's colour at normal zoom and the class roundel (silhouette in ink, owner disc wash and a firm owner ring) at far zoom (`PIP_HALF` is 8); the unit panel badge and unit build cards use the 64 px miniature (`ItemArt`), faction pieces use their own figure where one exists (Oathbearer, Wayfinder). The unit panel has a Class stat whose tooltip states the triangle; the class bonus is a named line in every forecast breakdown (pillar 5). Unit plurals go through `pluralUnit` (Spearmen, Infantry, Combat Drones).
- **Bought peace (Byzantium's tribute, 2026-10-10):** the primary action on an enemy's diplomacy row reads "Pay tribute: N Gold · peace for 10 turns" with the price breakdown in its tooltip and under it, greyed with the server's reason when unaffordable; both sides see "Tribute peace: N turns left (war possible from turn T)" and who paid; every number comes from `RULES.treaty.tribute` and the quote (`app/src/tribute.ts`).
- **Thin levies (Byzantium, 2026-10-10):** the cap shows as "Armed 5/5" in the top bar (red and the word "full" at the cap), under each city's Units heading, and on a rival Byzantium's diplomacy row; unit items past the cap are greyed with the engine's "Levies full: N of M". Hire an army (a priced "Hire against" action and its offer card) was retired with Call to Arms on 2026-10-18.
- **Bought peace review rules (2026-10-11, `claude/bought-peace`):** an action block inside the narrow diplomacy panel stretches to the panel (`align-items: stretch`, `min-width: 0`) and its buttons wrap (`white-space: normal`); a button sized to its content clipped "Pay tribute" and its price math at 1000x600, so a panel action gets a sideways-overflow check (`scrollWidth <= clientWidth`) in its spec (`e2e/bought-peace-shots.spec.ts` does this for every shot). A faction's headline action on an empire row renders before the definition boilerplate so it is in view when the row opens. One word for a mechanic on every surface: a priced call is a "Hire" in the action, the sent-offer card, the report line, the not-now reason and the seat bar, never "call to arms" (`offerLabel` in `diplomacy.ts`); a hire recipient's primary names the pay ("Accept: fight for 90 Gold"; the card above names the war, since a long empire title would overflow the button). An action that is silently absent gets a "Not now" reason when its sibling has one (a closed Japan: "can't be called or hired into a war"). A rival's levy cap is on its open diplomacy row only, not on the race row. Left open: "Levies full: N of M" still doesn't say how to fix it in the production row (the top-bar tooltip does).

**From the 8.8 release review (2026-10-11):**
- **Remove a mode, then grep the whole UI.** When a mode or setting goes away (Quick speed), search every player-facing string for it. Dead "(… at Standard)" text survived in faction sheets, the Guide and treaties.
- **Decision cards keep every choice above the fold** at 1000x600 (Accept and Decline both visible without scrolling).
- **Era transitions must look different at display size.** Before/after thumbnails that read the same defeat the point.
- **Tour fixtures use shipping names.** Old fictional empire and city names in fixtures hide real text bugs.

**From the Quick-speed removal review (2026-10-11):**
- **One source per number.** A price on an icon chip and the same price in prose come from the same value (`pieceStats`, and the strip-from-prose step depends on the strings matching). The data tables hold the real prices: there is no game-speed scaling step (removed 2026-10-21), so never multiply a table price in the UI. No card shows two prices for one item. Base figures are never player-facing.
- **When a mode or option is removed, grep player copy** for its qualifiers ("at Standard", "base", "on Quick") and for engine breakdown labels named after it ("Game speed", "Standard costs"); use one plain label in every tooltip. Open: core still emits "Standard costs" as a breakdown row (growth, research, production, buy quotes) while the unit upgrade tooltip relabels it "Fixed cost multiplier ×2"; pick one label and emit it from core.
- **Never write "base" in player copy** for prices or deltas: show the full Standard value ("20 more Production").
- Tour gap: tooltip shots for the growth, tile-price and research cost breakdowns.


**Pickers select on click (user, 2026-10-11, faction picker):** "The click is the selection, and then you close the window when you're done. No hover-over preview." Clicking a list item makes it the current choice at once and shows its details, hovering changes nothing, and there's no "Play as …" or confirm button. Closing keeps the choice.

**Build UI text from structured data; never strip prose with regexes (user, 2026-10-11, the Celts' piece card read "152 ⚙,."; "I've noticed artifacts like this in a few places").** A card or line is composed from fields (numbers, requirement, rule sentences), not made by deleting phrases from a description and patching the punctuation afterwards; a description that is pure stats renders no text at all. A unique piece lives in `UNIQUE_PIECE` (`data.ts`): the sheet, the Guide prose, the unlock sentence and the card all read it. The artifact guard (`ui/textArtifacts.test.tsx`, `.screens.test.tsx`, the detector in `test/textArtifacts.ts`, and the same check on every screen-tour shot's visible text) must pass: no `,.` `, .` `;.` `:.` `..` ` ,` ` .` `,,` `()` `( )`, doubled spaces, `undefined`, `NaN`, `null`, an empty `· ·`, a leading or trailing separator. A new surface that prints prose from data is added to the guard in the same change.

**Research screen as built is liked (user, 2026-10-11):** "this is an interesting way to do the tech tree. I'm into it". That means the era cards, Research now / Queue, and collapsed future eras (the boost block was removed 2026-10-17). Keep the structure, and make its unlocks covetable (legatum-art).
- **A struck "listed" price is in the same units as the price beside it (UI review, 2026-10-17):** the research cards struck the unscaled base (19) next to the Standard price (30), so a discount looked like a rise. Scale it with `scaleCost`; `era-research.spec.ts` asserts struck > paid. Removing the boost block also left the shorter cards with blank space above their buttons; that reads as a consistent footer, not a defect.

**Tech cards: click researches, shift+click queues; queued cards show their position, not the button; selection never shifts layout (user, 2026-10-11, 8.8.1 playtest: "everything gets pushed down in a way that is jarring"; built in `claude/research-click`, `app/src/ui/EraCards.tsx`, `ResearchQueue.tsx`, `TechPanel.tsx`, `flyChip.ts`, `queueHint.ts`):**
- The whole card is the target (Civ style): a click researches it now (the same as the "Research now" button: it goes first, the rest keep their order), Shift+click appends it to the queue (the same as "+ Queue"); Enter and Shift+Enter do the same from a focused card. Clicks on a button, the Details link or the cost breakdown keep their own meaning. The buttons stay for touch and discoverability and name the shortcut in their tooltips. The card title is plain text now: Details is the link at the card's foot.
- States: the researching card has no Research now button, it says "Researching · N turns" with a progress meter. A queued card shows a "Queued #N" tag pinned to the card's top-right corner (out of the flow, over the card's reserved top edge) with a small remove x, and keeps one action, Research now; it has no "+ Queue". Available cards keep Research now plus "+ Queue".
- **Nothing moves under the pointer:** the queue strip always shows one slot per technology that can still be queued; queued techs fill their slots and the rest are dashed "Empty" placeholders, so the strip's height is constant. The head row has a reserved height. Card states reuse the same footer height and border-compensating padding. `e2e/research-click.spec.ts` clicks a card, Shift+clicks two more and asserts the queue order, the tags, the missing "+ Queue" and unchanged bounding boxes of all five cards and the strip.
- **Feedback instead of movement (about 0.4 s):** the picked card glows (box-shadow only), a chip with the tech's name flies from the card to its slot in the strip (position: fixed, removed afterwards) and the slot flashes as it lands. Reduced motion: no chip and no glow, the states still change at once.
- **First-time hint:** the first free queue slot reads "Shift+click a technology to queue it next." until the player has queued once (any queueing path), remembered under `legatum.research-queue-hint.v1` with an in-memory fallback when storage is blocked.
- Rule behind it: a rewrite that drops an established interaction is a regression. List a screen's interactions before rewriting it and pin each with a Playwright test.


**Strength before you attack (user, 2026-10-11, decided from the era-distinct mocks):** with a military unit selected, each enemy it could attack this turn shows a chip with how much stronger or weaker it is in that fight (red rising triangle and number, green falling, even mark; far zoom: the mark only). Enemies it can't reach get no chip: their strength is in the forecast and the tile hover, which names the unit's era. The attack forecast's first line is the era line ("Phalangite, one era newer: 26 vs your 22. It outclasses you."), after any severe aftermath warning. Its numbers are the forecast's own totals. The map chips count the base strength, promotion, wounds and class bonus, but not terrain or fortify (`matchupStrength` in `map/unitEra.ts`).

**Tile tooltips lead with what matters most on the tile (user, 2026-10-11):** "The iron is the most important part here, so we should move that to the top and make the icon bigger." The order is: a natural wonder or resource first, with its large painted medallion, then its benefits as short separate lines, then the next action (improve it, claim it), and the terrain and its yields last.

**Era objectives must be unmissable (user, 2026-10-11):** "it was entirely unclear to me that there were VP goals in the first era... it doesn't emphasize enough how important they are, given that this is how you actually win the game." Keep the open Age's card with live progress in the HUD at all times (rules 50: it is a World Age card; before any Age begins the HUD says when the first one does, with no preview of cards). Frame them as how you win.
- **Built (2026-10-11, branch `claude/era-objectives-hud`; `eraObjectives.ts`, `ui/EraObjectives.tsx`, `ui/eraObjectives.css`):** one `ObjectiveRows` list is drawn by every surface, so a rule is worded and counted the same everywhere.
  - **Race panel:** an ink "How you win" band with your VP and the target (10, 20 for a team), a line of the sources behind it ("Eras 1/6 · Goal 0/2 · Work 0/2", tooltips give the rules), then the Age in focus (rules 50: "Score 1 VP in the Classical Age · never expires" over its one card; the older wording "any one of three" and the three-line list are gone), one line with the progress as the fill behind the line and "3/4" at the right (a met one says Met, a scored one Scored). The family is an icon here (Build, Expand, Fight are words in the takeover and the sheet, and an icon with the word in the research header). Every objective counts only what you gain after you enter the era and after your last era score (R1, 2026-10-21), so an era you have not entered shows 0 and each count's tooltip says so; the objectives sheet has a one-line note under "Era objectives" (`data-testid=era-count-rule`) and the Guide's "Era objectives" section says it in full.
  - **Ancient:** previews Classical ("Next era: 1 VP in Classical") and says "No objective in the Ancient era, by design. Finish its 5 technologies (0 known) to open these." Once the current era is scored the block says so and previews the next era. An earlier era you entered and left unscored is a link to the sheet ("Classical is still open to score").
  - **Takeover:** the objectives are the first and widest panel, gilt-framed and titled "Score 1 VP this era" with a +1 VP seal, your progress on each and the line "This is how you win".
  - **Research screen:** above the technologies, this era's objectives with progress beside the next era's preview (the first era says it has none).
  - **Report:** "Close to the Classical era's VP: Own 4 cities (3 of 4, 1 to go)" the turn an objective first gets within one (or a tenth) of its target; the scored card adds "N of 6 era VP scored".
  - **Fit at 1000x600:** the race panel is zero-sum with the dock, so the block is compact (about 135 px open, 170 px in a preview) and, on windows 700 px tall or less, the "Nobody yet" row steps aside . Re-check the race rows (yours and the leader's whole) when this block grows.


- **Compact "How you win" (8.9.2, `claude/ui-892-fixes`; `eraHudOpen.ts`, `Glance` in `EraObjectives.tsx`):** the 8.9.1 block crowded the race panel (2 rows + "6 more" on an 8-empire first turn). It is now compact by default: the ink band (How you win, your VP, a bordered chevron) plus two fixed lines, the era in focus with what it scores ("Next: 1 VP in Classical", "1 VP in Classical") and its objective nearest done with progress as the fill behind it; a click on the band's button or the line opens the full block, remembered per viewer (`legatum.era-hud-open.v1`). `fit()` in `VpTrack.tsx` lets the crowns strip and "Nobody yet" step aside (`data-aside`) only when the rows cannot all show, so 8 empires show 6 rows at 1000x600 and all 8 at 1440x900 (what 8.9 showed); opened by choice it tries the crowns first, then drops the next era and notes (levels 1 and 2), and takes the try with the most whole rows. Rules: a compact summary keeps the stake ("1 VP", "any one of three") and a bare progress bar is not a summary; a fit ladder picks the try that shows the most whole list rows, never the first that reaches a minimum; specs about crowns, tokens and the Nobody row set `legatum.dev.no-era-hud` so the block does not compete for room (`era-objectives.spec.ts` and `era-hud-compact.spec.ts` cover it).
- **Research screen at 1000x600 (8.9.2):** at max-height 700 px a card's name, art, cost and both buttons are in view together at scroll 0 on turn 1 (the longest head), mid-era (with the objectives header) and with a queue (`e2e/research-fold.spec.ts`): the head is a grid with the Science note spanning below it, the first-era objectives header is one line, an open Age's box (`EraObjectivesResearch`) is one gilt-framed box: its 46 px era seal (hollow while the Age is open, a gilt disc once scored; 85% size at this height) beside "<Age> Age: score 1 VP" ("<Age> Age: 1 VP scored" when won), the card's rows with family icon, word, progress bar and count, and, right-aligned on the heading row, "The next Age begins when any empire enters the <era> era." (there is no separate "Next" column; it is absent in the last era), the queue strip is one 38 px row per slot (number, name over arrival, then the three 24 px controls: the layout audit's minimum target, so never shrink a control below 24 px to buy room) and cards are tighter. Forecast numbers in a short-height override stay at 12 px or more.

**The Settle lens shows every legal site on an absolute scale (user, 2026-10-11):** "The UI for the settler view should show *all* valid places to settle, and it should indicate which ones are better based on some objective scale... not relative to each other (though the 'best', 'second', 'third' indicator is helpful as a relative spot)." Use fixed quality bands, the same on every map, plus the relative top-3 markers. Legality must come from the core rule, never a TS copy. **Built (2026-10-11, branch `claude/settle-lens-absolute`; core `settlement.rs`, `app/src/map/siteBands.ts`, `settleAdvice.ts`, `SettlementCard.tsx`, `drawLens.ts`, `e2e/settle-lens.spec.ts`):**
- **Why legal tiles were missing:** the lens listed the AI planner's sites, and the planner drops any legal site a visible foreign border might put beside an unseen city (`border_city_risks`), which in fog hid wide strips of legal land. The lens now lists every `known_legal_site` tile (the founding rule), flags the risky ones with a dashed rim and a hover warning, and never recommends them. A test pins "tinted set = legal set = `check_found_city` ok". The reason for any other explored land tile comes from core's `site_blocker` (the tile card's note: "Cannot settle here: Too close to Kyoto (1 hex away); cities need 3 tiles between them", "Foreign territory (Sparta)"), not from a TS check.
- **The scale:** five bands, Poor under 120, Fair 120-159, Good 160-199, Great 200-249, Exceptional 250 or more, of the site's absolute quality (Food ×4 + Production ×3 + Gold ×2 over the work radius, plus Iron/Coal, wonders, minus foreign land; no Settler, travel or threat in it). The floors are `SITE_BAND_FLOORS` in core, calibrated on 309,829 legal sites of 480 generated maps (about 18/21/30/23/8%); the app reads them from `rules.generated.json`. The top-three markers keep the relative, travel-adjusted score: a Best spot can be a Poor site when the good land is risky or far, and the hover says both.
- **Colour (redone 2026-10-17: single-hue green, no dots, plain hover):** one emerald hue (about 150°, bluer than the olive terrain greens) from a faint tint to nearly opaque: `--settle-poor` `#17a05e` at 26% cover, then 50, 66, 77 and 84% of progressively deeper greens (`#0e8a4c`, `#097238`, `#06552c`, `#033820`), so "darker green = better". Each band also darkens whatever is under it by more than the last, so lightness carries the order over every terrain and for protan, deutan, tritan and greyscale (`siteBands.test.ts`: CIEDE2000 between neighbours and to bare ground over the six land terrains, at the full and the faded wash; Poor is a faint tint by design). The legend swatch is the band colour at its cover over parchment (`color-mix` from the same `--settle-*` and `--settle-*-a` tokens the canvas reads). No dots, no numbers on the scale: five joined swatches with the band names under them, "Better places to found a city are darker green. Hover a tile for why.", then "Top three for this Settler, marked on the map" over the Best spot, #2 and #3 buttons, and a "Dashed edge: an unseen city may block" key when any site is risky. Marks that must read on deep green: the top-three ring is ink with a paper edge outside it, and the risky site's dashed rim is ink on a paper underlay. Band ranges stay in the swatch tooltips.
- **Hover (plain, 2026-10-17):** one sentence first, built from the site's data (`siteVerdict`): "Great spot for a city: plenty of Food and Production nearby, Wheat 3 tiles away and the terrain gives units here +3 defence." ("plenty", "some" and "little" start at 20/10 Food and 15/7 Production within reach; the two nearest resources are named, the rest go on an "Also nearby" line). Then the key numbers in plain words ("Nearby land: +23 Food, +36 Production", wonders with their bonus, "3 tiles nearby belong to another empire and add nothing", "4 tiles nearby are not explored yet, so this spot may be better than it looks", the unseen-city warning), then the relative line ("Best spot · 3 turns away"). No points, weights, "outer ring" or quality numbers on the hover: the click-through card ("Place for a city", never raw coordinates) has the same lead and a closed "Details" toggle holding the quality as a sum of its parts (weights, outer ring at half, points), the fixed scale and the travel-adjusted score for this Settler. Copy follows "Game text: plain language".
- **Review rules to keep:** a lens that says "every X" is driven by the rule that defines X, not by a planner's filter (mark the extra cases instead of dropping them); a band's colour never carries the meaning alone; a hover for an untinted tile says why in one clause naming the empire or city.
- **Review rules from the green redo (UI review, 2026-10-17):** a lens legend also says what blank means ("No tint: not a site"); a single-hue lens over same-hue terrain needs a second cue for its faintest band (a firm rim, 0.7+ alpha) and is checked against every terrain, not only bare ground; a per-tile risk mark that can cover a third of the lit tiles (the dashed unseen-city rim) is drawn only at near zoom, or it becomes the texture; the deepest band covers at most about 85% so terrain and resource icons stay legible; a verdict head never contradicts its own warning ("Exceptional spot for a city (unconfirmed)"); route lines name the distance plainly ("Best spot · 3 turns away"), never a bare "within N turns". Left open: with Details open at 1000x600 the click-through card is height-capped and the "Preview move to this site" button scrolls below the fold.

- **A select-on-click list has three distinct states** (picker review, 2026-10-11): a light hover tint, a chosen state with more than a fill (accent bar, ring, bold name, a check) and a focus ring. A hover that matches the choice reads as two choices. A dialog with no confirm button closes on Return, Esc, its Close button and a backdrop click, and its Tab wrap counts only tabbable stops (exclude `tabindex="-1"` roving items); after removing a button, sweep Tab, Return and Esc (one Tab then Return must still close).
**Unlock hovers sell the unlock: big art, what it's for, what it beats, how it compares (user, 2026-10-11, on a Classical tech card: "The hover-over for units and stuff in the tech tree does not give me enough information. I have no idea what these units are and they don't look cool... it should give me the feeling of 'ooh, that's cool, I want that'". Built in `claude/research-click`: `app/src/ui/UnlockHover.tsx`, `unlockCardModel.ts` (the data, tested without a DOM), `unlockLore.ts` (history and why-lines), `unlockCard.css`, hero art in `assets/unlocks/`):**
- Every unlock row on a tech card (unit, building, effect) has a hovercard on hover (after about 160 ms), on keyboard focus (Tab to the row; Enter or Space pins it) and on a tap (pinned, with a Close button; a mouse click on the row still researches the tech). Escape closes the card first, not the research screen (it shares the one-tip-at-a-time slot in `Tip.tsx`, and the row carries `data-enter-activates` so the global Return handler leaves it alone).
- **Units:** the large painted miniature (about 150 px, the A2 art), name, class, era, one line of real history, the class triangle in plain words with the numbers ("Holds off mounted units (+8 strength against them). Ranged units shoot it down.", Beats and Weak to chips), the stats compared with the unit it upgrades from (Strength 20 → 26, +30%, moves, cost, range), the upgrade price from that unit with both miniatures and an arrow, and the build and buy prices. Siege and air units show their rule line.
- **Buildings and improvements:** a large image slot (painted art from `assets/unlocks/<icon-name>.png` when it exists, the engraved glyph in the same frame otherwise), the history line, "What it gives" in icon amounts, "Why build it" in one plain sentence, what it upgrades, the population it needs, build and buy prices.
- **Effects:** a real mark, never a dashed placeholder circle; an upgrade effect draws both miniatures with an arrow on the card row and big in the hovercard; every effect says in one sentence what it does for you.
- **Layout:** the hovercard sits beside the tech card (its right, else its left) and is centred on the hovered row, so it never covers the card or the text it describes; it stays inside the window and fits at 1000x600 (a unit card is about 400 px tall). A hover card is never touched by the pointer (`pointer-events: none`); a pinned one is.
- All prose (history, why-lines) lives in `unlockLore.ts` with one entry per unit and building kind, a test guards every kind, and the numbers come only from the rules data and the view's unlock forecast. `e2e/unlock-hover.spec.ts` checks content, no overlap with the tech card, fit at both sizes, keyboard and touch.
- **UI review rules for the research screen (2026-10-11):** a card's state change must not change the card row's height (every state renders the same fixed-height `when` line, one line, and the same fixed-height footer; the spec measures the cards and the strip after each pick); placeholder slots never make a strip taller than one row at 1000x600 (it scrolls sideways) and the first row of real targets, with their buttons, stays above the fold at scroll 0; a programmatic focus ring is not the selection mark (focus is a soft accent outline, selection a solid ink one); a pending mark always carries the word "pending"; a quiet button still has a hairline border so it looks clickable; a hovercard for an unlock needs real painted art in its image slot (the glyph fallback is only a stand-in until the unlock-art set lands) and a "Buy: not for sale" line when it cannot be bought.

**A unique piece always says how it differs from what it replaces (user, 2026-10-17):** "it's not clear to me why this is better than what it replaces. does it have more strength? something else?"
