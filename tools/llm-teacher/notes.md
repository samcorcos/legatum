How this player (the designer, who beats the AI easily with every faction) reasons. These are his own recorded reasons.
1. First thing on meeting a neighbour: check its faction and read the threat. Peaceful factions will not rush you; aggressive ones (Sparta, Mongols) will.
2. Keep the opening flexible until the neighbourhood is scouted; plan research as a queue that serves the plan.
3. Open land and no visible threat means expand hard: as many Settlers as possible, minimal military, a Gold reserve to buy Walls and a defender if surprised.
4. Do not settle toward an aggressive neighbour: the city is likely taken, the neighbour gets a city and a reason to declare, and it then threatens every other city. Settle away, even far away. Exceptions: with a whole army there, while that neighbour is busy in another war, or once the city can be defended.
5. Contested land between two empires is a race: take the contested sites first, safe ones behind you later. Denying a rival land you or they will need is worth a risky city.
6. Information is worth a unit. Put scouts and lookouts on hills at passes and chokepoints instead of garrisons; five turns of warning lets you build what actually defends.
7. Escort Settlers (spare Scouts will do); snipe an unescorted rival Settler.
8. Conquest is the fastest economy: a captured city's yield and territory compound, and the rival's loss counts for you. An early winnable war beats pure economy when the math says so.
9. Pick the war target by its visible defence: garrison, army, Walls. Strike the empty or weakly held city; the army stands two or three tiles out before declaring.
10. Strike before Walls go up. A walled city is a costly target, not off limits: concentrate force, shoot the walls down from range, ride in at 0 HP; abort if Walls finish early and the prize is small.
11. A small veteran army (5 or 6 units) is enough. Keep veterans alive, retreat to save experience, and upgrade rather than replace when you have the Gold.
12. Walls first in every new city, even tiny frontier towns: they are buffer cities that delay armies and hold territory. Width is the defence; peripheral towns are expendable, core cities are not.
13. In a capital whose fall ends the empire: Walls, then walled buffer cities, a permanent garrison, then a mobile reserve.
14. Hold the Gold hoard as an emergency defence, and spend it the turn a threat appears; spend on the decisive war when it is the plan.
15. Do not join other people's wars. An Alliance can make you a defender in two wars at once; he lost a Swiss game to exactly that. Check the offerer's situation (is its city at 0 HP?) before accepting.
16. Choose your wars; near a win, close the fronts you do not need and keep only the one you are using. Accept Peace with one enemy to fight the next.
17. Read a rival conqueror by what it is doing (cities taken, empires eliminated), not by its VP. A leader needs to be contested early, before its army arrives.
18. Research the tech that reveals the next era's resource first, then aim settling and armies at the deposits.
19. Time scoring on purpose: do not score big early or a coalition forms; convert a lead late.
20. Scouting is part of the game; most of his losses came from what he never saw, not from a choice he weighed.
