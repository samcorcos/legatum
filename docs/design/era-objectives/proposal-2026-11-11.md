# Era objectives: redesign proposal

2026-11-11. Design thinking only; no code, rules or skills changed. Written for the designer's request of 2026-11-11 ("remember the goals and use fable 5.2 to think deeply about our options") and his follow-up the same day: "take a look at TI4 and how they do it with incrementally revealed cards. because you don't know what the era goal is before you're in the era, you can't game it."

So the TI4 public-objective model is the anchor. The settled parts are not reopened here: three public cards per era, the same for everyone, hidden until the era, everyone can score, at most one per era, flat 1 VP, permanent, cards cost something (Hold / Spend / offensive Fight), no hard conquest gate, no secret objectives, no resource objectives.

Sources: `legatum-design`, `legatum-factions`, `legatum-rules` (game-rules.md §6, §9), `docs/learnings.md`, `docs/research/ti4-objective-scoring.md`, `docs/design/research/vp-scoring-boardgames-2026-10-31.md`, `docs/sim-results/objectives/deck-2026-11-01.md`, the revival report on branch `astra/revive-era-objectives`, `.reviews/astra-era-objectives-ideas.md`, `docs/playtests/human-reasoning.md`, `legatum-coach`. The design doc connector returned only the tab list, so I relied on the skills that mirror its decisions log. TI4 rule details below are from memory, as in the repo's own TI4 note; the scoring rates are from that note's data.

## 1. What the system is for

Era objectives pace the VP race. Every era puts a fresh, public point on the table that costs something, so each stretch of the game has a decision ("do I pay for this one?") and the race stays readable (pillars 4, 11). His words, 2026-10-10: "one VP available per era... paces the game like in TI4. The VP should be hard to get but achievable. Not every civ should be able to get it every era." And 2026-10-31: "achievable and not automatic. I had to change something about what I was doing in order to get them."

## 2. What is on master

- Default (`objectives=era`): eras 2–7 each show three objectives (Build, Expand, Fight) drawn by seed. Meet any one for 1 permanent VP. Only gains since era entry and since your last era score count; one era scores per turn. Unscored eras stay open for the rest of the game.
- Opt-in (`objectives=deck`, built 2026-10-31, off by default): three cards per era (one Hold, one Spend, one Fight), hidden until you enter that era yourself. Hold = current holdings with at least one new since entry. Spend = pay printed Gold (70/170/270/400/580/800 (last raised from 300 on 2026-11-11)). Fight = capture from another empire. Still open forever.
- Era points are six of the ten VP needed. Peaceful ceiling is 6 era + 2 goal + 2 wonder = exactly 10.

## 3. What went wrong with each version

| --- | --- | --- |
| Level counts (2026-10-10) | Linear chains; met by playing normally; one event scored several eras | His 10-20 quote ("3 universities and the next era it's 5... get all the points by accident"); Sparta scored eras 3, 4, 5 in one turn from one recapture |
| Gains pool (on master) | Still mostly accidental; pursuit barely matters; old eras score late from ordinary growth | Learnings 10-20: AI pursuit on vs off 5,670 vs 5,673 objectives; 26 of 50 objectives met by over 15% who never aimed. Seek-0 accidental rate 61/50/42/33/30/20% for eras 2–7. His Celts scored Market x9 155 turns after entering era 2; Japan scored Modern and Future the turn he entered them; Celts 8.9.25 "scored all five eras he entered with the cheapest card on offer". Winners hold 5.4 of 6 |
| 12-kind hidden pool (revived 10-31) | See 3.1 | Narrow gate pass, then shelved the same day |
| Deck v1 (opt-in) | Hold cards work inside 25 turns but not over a whole game; Fight cards score from wars already under way; Spend and Fight are fixed per era, so only the Hold card is a surprise | Hold seek-0 vs seek-1 inside 25 turns: 1–28% vs 29–78%; but era 2 points with seeking off rose 61% to 93%. **What the record does not say.** On 2026-10-31 he chose "Revive all three", then the same day said era objectives need "a serious redesign" and "i do think we need to redo it somehow". The record has no sentence saying what he disliked that day. I have not guessed; it is question 1.

### 3.1 Why the 12-kind pool failed its gate

Two gates, two different stories:

- The first gate (`gate-audit2`) failed at -5.0 pp and thin-war 56% to 4%, but it tested a bundle (objectives plus Silicon plus Outrider). It does not isolate objectives.
- The revival alone: Hard in his seat won 21.97% to 20.26% (-1.71 pp, interval [-3.61, +0.24]), a pass on "no clear harm", narrowly. Human-seat captures fell 2.43 to 2.00 while objective points rose 2.48 to 2.94. Thin-war went 32/64 to 28/64, both under the floor.

The gate measures whether the AI still wins from his seat. It fell because the AI spent effort on cards instead of the captures that actually win, and because five of its card estimates were wrong: partial progress priced as a full point; "a good site exists" treated as "reachable" for the spatial kinds (Frontier, Far city); a fixed effort for the Monument; individual instead of team VP for Topple; progress assumed to survive scoring another era.

What a pool needs to avoid a repeat:

1. Every card must be forecastable from quantities the AI already forecasts in its own view: Gold, production, a capture plan. No spatial or map-dependent kinds.
2. A card is judged by its pursuit gap (seek 0 vs seek 1 inside the window) and by his tapes, not by the Hard-in-his-seat win rate. That gate tests the AI, and the AI is still weak.
3. A card must not pay conquest a second time. Capture already pays through the capital point, the economy crown and captured wonders.

## 4. How TI4 does it, and what transfers

TI4: ten public cards sit face down (five Stage I at 1 VP, then five Stage II at 2 VP). Two Stage I start face up. At the end of every round one more is revealed, to everyone at once. In the status phase each player may score one public objective (plus one secret). A card stays scoreable for the rest of the game, any number of players can score it, and points are permanent. Games last about five rounds, so a card has at most four scoring chances. Data: a Stage I card is scored by 70% of players, Stage II by 31%; cards revealed later are scored less (4.7 scorers falling to 3.2) because the window is shorter.

| TI4 feature | Transfers? | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Public, same cards for all | Yes | Settled 10-31 |
| Hidden until revealed | Yes | Settled 10-22 |
| One scored per round | Yes | "At most one per era", and one era per turn |
| Nonexclusive, permanent | Yes | Settled |
| Revealed to everyone at the same moment | **Open** | TI4 has a round clock. Legatum has none: each empire enters an era the turn it finishes that era's fifth technology |
| Card open for the rest of the game | **No** | Harmless over five rounds. Over 200 turns, growth completes any Hold card eventually. This is the measured failure of both master and deck v1 |
| Stage II at 2 VP | No | He chose flat 1 VP |
| Secret objectives | No | Ruled out |
| Many currencies (trade goods, influence, planets, tech colours, fleets) | Partly | Legatum is lean (§9: no trade, culture, religion, districts). See 5.2 |

### 4.1 Who triggers the reveal

Two clean choices. A hybrid (everyone sees the cards when the first empire enters, but you can only score after entering yourself) is worse than both: the laggard knows the cards early and either pre-builds or holds builds back, which is exactly his "holding back on building libraries" complaint.

| | Reveal on your own entry (deck v1) | World Age (new) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Trigger | You finish the previous era's last technology | The first empire to enter the era starts the Age for everyone |
| Laggard | Sees the cards late; with expiry, sees fewer Ages before the game ends | Sees them the same turn; pays with a smaller economy |
| Tech leader | Head start on every card, and more cards in total | Chooses the moment, nothing else |
| Snowball or rubber band | Snowball: science already pays through Most Advanced and stronger units; this pays it again in VP chances | Mild rubber band: chances arrive on one clock regardless of tech |
| Can it be gamed | No | No. Nobody sees a card before the Age starts |
| Race visible (pillar 4) | No. He cannot see the cards a rival ahead of him is working on | Yes. One set of cards, one countdown |
| Denial by AI and by him | Blind against anyone in a later era, which is where it matters | Works from the public view |
| Co-op with a partner | Each partner sees different cards at different times | Both see the same cards the same turn |

His words fit the first reading most literally ("the future should be hidden until you're in that era"). The same sentence also says "everyone competes for them", which only happens on a shared clock. Civ 6 players already know a world era with a countdown (Rise and Fall), so pillar 2 favours the World Age too.

## 5. The options

### Option A. Era cards, revealed on your own entry (deck v1, repaired)

**Rule.** When you enter an era you see its three cards. You have 30 turns to complete one for 1 permanent VP. Only what you do after the reveal counts. After 30 turns the cards close.

Repairs over deck v1: the 30-turn window; the card pool of 5.2; no fixed Spend and Fight per era.

- **Example.** T63, he enters Medieval. Cards: "Pay 170 Gold", "Build the Triumphal Arch (120 Production, no yield)", "Capture a city from an empire ahead of you on VP". He has 90 Gold and wants Knights. He queues the Arch in his second city, delaying its Castle 9 turns, and scores at T74. Byzantium enters at T81 and sees the same cards then.
- **Pillars.** Serves 1 (window removes open-forever bookkeeping), 3, 5, 7, 8, 10. Strains 4 (cards of empires ahead are invisible), 11 (tech leader gets more Ages), 6 only in the sense that AI denial is blind.
- **AI.** Revealed card: existing `costed_objective_effort` plus a completes-before-expiry probability; pick the best of three against the no-card line. Unknown card: needs a term in research timing (entering sooner reveals a card sooner). About 4 hours of agent time on top of what exists.
- **Snowball / pillar 11.** Worse than B, for the reasons in 4.1.
- **Dominant-strategy risk.** Tech rush gains extra VP chances on top of everything else it gives.
- **UI.** Reveal modal, card strip in "How you win" with a countdown per era; mostly built.
- **Build.** About 6 hours wall-clock across two agent lanes plus reviewers; playable 2026-11-12.
- **Strongest case against.** It keeps the snowball and the invisibility, and it is the version already measured to shorten games and favour the T100 leader.

### Option B. World Ages (recommended)

**Rule as the player reads it.** "When the first empire reaches a new era, a new Age begins for the whole world and three cards are revealed to everyone. For the next 30 turns any empire may complete one of them for 1 permanent VP. Only what you do after the reveal counts. When the Age closes, the cards are gone."

Everything settled on 10-31 holds. Two changes: the reveal is on a world clock, and cards expire. An empire still in an earlier era can score; it simply cannot use buildings or units it has not researched.

- **Example.** T58: the Mongols finish Classical. Banner for everyone: "The Medieval Age has begun. 30 turns." Cards: "Tribute: pay 170 Gold", "Triumphal Arch: 120 Production, no yield", "Humble the mighty: capture a city from an empire ahead of you on VP". He is still two technologies short of Medieval, has 110 Gold and is saving for Pikemen against Sparta. Sparta is ahead of him on VP, so the Fight card lines up with a war he was weighing but against a harder target than the Aztec town he had picked. He chooses the Arch in his capital, losing a Market for 8 turns. T66 he scores. The VP track shows who has scored this Age and who has 22 turns left. The Mongols paid Gold on T59; India never scores it and that point is gone for India.
- **Pillars served.** 1: cuts open-forever eras and the per-empire reveal state. 2: Civ 6's world era and countdown. 3: one decision every 30 or so turns. 4: one public set of cards, one clock, scorers shown on the VP track. 5: every card prints its price and a forecast ("Arch: 8 turns here, delays Market"). 7: no scoring order needed. 9: the reveal is one screen with three choices and "skip" as the fourth. 10: three lanes, and a tech rush no longer buys extra chances. 11: laggards get every Age; expiry means missed points stay missed, so winners hold fewer than 5.4 of 6 and need crowns or captures to close.
- **Pillars strained.** 8: factions meet the same cards (Swiss Gold cards are cheap in effort but fight their own 5,000 Gold goal, which is a fair tension). 10: Gold may become the rote answer (see case against).
- **AI, without cheating (pillar 6).** The view carries only revealed Ages; deck v1 already stops the view at what the seat may see, and a view-leak test should pin it. *Revealed card:* for each card, cost in its own currency converted through the existing valuations (Gold through the purchase it displaces, production through the build it delays, a capture through the war forecast), times the chance of finishing before the Age closes, against the win-chance value of +1 permanent VP from `vp_race`. Take the best if it beats the no-card line. *Unknown card:* the pool is public, like the TI4 deck. The AI holds a prior: "an Age will start within about N turns (public: every empire's era and tech count), and my historical chance of affording one of its cards is p." That prior feeds exactly one thing, the option value of unspent Gold. It needs no research-timing term, because entering an era no longer reveals cards to you alone. *Denial:* all cards are public, so `rival_objective_denial` works from the view against any empire, including one ahead in tech.
- **Snowball.** Removes the science double-pay. Does not fix conquest paying several times; it only stops paying it a further time (5.2 rule 5).
- **Dominant-strategy risk.** Hoard Gold before every Age. Held down by pool variety (a Gold card in about half the Ages) and by Gold being what he uses for units.
- **UI.** Age banner and reveal screen; world countdown in the top bar; three cards in "How you win" with price, forecast and progress; "scored this Age" marks on the VP track. The reveal screen is new; the rest extends deck v1's panel.
- **Build.** About 8–10 hours wall-clock: rules (window, world trigger, pool swap) and AI (expiry probability, Gold option value) in two parallel lanes about 5 hours, UI lane about 4 hours, a seek-0/seek-1 card check on the cloud about 1 hour, docs and UI reviewers about 2 hours. Playable 2026-11-12 or 11-13. The real bottleneck is his games: three at 3–4 hours each.
- **Strongest case against.** (1) The pool is thin. Legatum has about five things a player can spend, so by his third game he has seen every card; the surprise is only which three and when. (2) With today's AI the shared race may not feel contested, and he will score most Ages regardless. (3) Expiry lowers the peaceful ceiling below 10 for most empires. He wants that ("no full turtle victories"), but learnings 2026-11-01 show games stalling at 8–9 VP when nobody can close; expired points make that worse until the AI can win wars. (4) It departs from the literal reading of his sentence about being "in that era". (5) The first entrant can sit one technology short and choose when the Age starts; small, but it is a timing edge for the tech leader.

### Option C. Cut era objectives

**Rule.** No era points. VP come from crowns (3, live), the faction goal (2), a world wonder (2, held) and captured capitals (1 each). Target drops to about 6 solo, 12 for a pair (Astra's figure).

- **Example.** T63, he enters Medieval. Nothing happens on the VP track. He is at 3 of 6; the next point is a crown or a capital.
- **Pillars.** Serves 1 and 3. Strains 11 and 4 badly: no fresh points arrive on a clock, and learnings already show stalls where crowns flip at the margin. Strains 10: with half the remaining points coming from capture, war is the answer.
- **AI.** Easiest: delete the objective genes. About 3 hours including the target change and UI.
- **Snowball.** Worst of the four; what remains is live or capturable, and the largest empire holds most of it.
- **Strongest case against.** It removes the one thing he called "a great idea for game pacing", and the T170 "press return and wait" stretch gets longer, not shorter. Keep it as the fallback if two more card designs fail the "would you miss it?" test.

### Option D. Rival of the Age (my own; not in the record)

**Rule.** When an Age begins, each empire is given a rival: the empire directly above it on the VP track (the leader's rival is everyone). One measure is revealed, for example "cities gained" or "Gold spent on buildings". Over 30 turns, out-gain your rival on that measure and you score 1 VP. Your rival sees it and can answer.

- **Example.** T58, Medieval begins, measure "cities gained". He is third; his rival is Sparta in second. Sparta gains one city by T80. He needs two: he founds one and buys a Settler for the second on T84. Sparta, chased by him and chasing the Mongols, has to decide whether to spend on a third.
- **Pillars.** Serves 11 (thresholds scale with the opponent; the leader has the hardest card), 10 (every point has a counter), 4. No percentile calibration, which matters while the AI is weak. Strains 5 (the target moves until the last turn), 3 (watching a rival's count), 9.
- **AI.** Hardest: it must forecast a rival's gains, not only its own. About 10–12 hours.
- **Dominant-strategy risk.** Sniping on the last turn; kingmaking by a rival with nothing to lose.
- **Strongest case against.** When he leads, his rivals are weak AIs and out-gaining them happens by accident, which is the original complaint. It is also a contest, and the research ruled out exclusive races as the main source. Not recommended now; worth a second look once Hard matches him.

### 5.2 The card pool (applies to A and B)

**The test for a card.** A card goes in the pool only if all of these hold:

1. **A flow, not a stock.** It counts only what happens after the reveal, and nothing you already own helps or hurts. That removes both pre-banking and holding back. Deck v1's "current holdings plus one new" fails this; so does any "have N buildings" card once an empire is developed.
2. **A named price in something you want for another purpose.** The player can say what he gave up.
3. **Pursuit gap.** Inside the 30-turn window, under 15% of empires that ignore it complete it and over 40% of empires that pursue it do (deck v1's own calibration rule). Checked a second way for free: replay his 8.9.x tapes and count how many window cards he would have completed without knowing they existed.
4. **Forecastable** by the AI from its own view (3.1).
5. **Not paid twice.** Capturing a capital or a wonder city never counts toward a Fight card.
6. **Not map luck, not a chore** (the `legatum-factions` tests for goals apply).

**What this game can actually charge** (checked against game-rules.md):

| Lane | Card kind | Exists in the rules? | Detour? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Spend | Tribute: pay printed Gold | Yes (`ClaimObjective`) | Yes; Gold is his unit reserve. Bankable before the reveal, which is "save then burn", his own model for goals |
| Spend | Monument: a Production-only project with no yield | Needs one new buildable per era; wonders already show "cannot be bought" works | Yes; he accepted the Monument sacrifice on 10-20 "but don't overdo it", so at most one per Age |
| Spend | Demobilise: disband N current-era units | `DisbandUnit` exists | Yes; costs army strength and the army crown. Same kind as Sparta's goal, so keep it rare |
| Hold | N cities gain a building line after the reveal | Yes | Only early. Era 2 Library/Market cards measured 1% vs 75%. Fails rule 1 later, when a developed empire has nowhere left to build it |
| Hold | Own N improved strategic deposits | Yes | Measured 14% vs 42%; borderline, and partly map luck |
| Fight | Capture a city from an empire ahead of you on VP (the leader: from the runner-up) | Yes, the VP snapshot exists | Yes; he picks targets by weakest defence, and this names a stronger one |
| Fight | Capture a walled city (a higher wall tier in later Ages) | Wall tiers exist | Yes, same reason |
| Expand | Gain N cities by founding or capture | Yes | Only from the Renaissance on, when free land is gone; before that it is normal play |

That is five real currencies: Gold, Production, units, hard captures, new cities. The pool is honest but small, about 15–18 cards across six Ages. Each Age draws three from different lanes, with no fixed Spend or Fight per era, so the Gold card appears in roughly half the Ages. Cut from deck v1: the eight Hold variants that missed calibration (Walls at 28% passive, the late Bank and University cards), any Fight card met by any capture, and capitals as a Fight condition.

## 6. Recommendation

**Option B, World Ages**, built on the deck that already exists.

Why: it is his TI4 model with the two parts that did not transfer repaired. TI4's shared reveal comes from a round clock Legatum lacks; the World Age restores it. TI4's open-ended cards are safe over five rounds and not over 200 turns; the 30-turn window restores that too, and it is the direct answer to the deck's own finding that a card rarely met in 25 turns is still met by accident over a whole game.

**Skateboard (one build, playable 2026-11-12 or 11-13).** Deck v1 plus three changes behind the existing experiment switch: (1) the Age starts for everyone when the first empire enters the era; (2) cards close after 30 turns; (3) the pool is cut to what passes the test today without new art or new buildables: Tribute, the four Hold cards that met calibration (era 2 Library and Market, era 4 University, era 5 deposits), counted from the reveal, the two hard-target Fight cards, Gain N cities from the Renaissance on. The AI gets the expiry probability only. The UI gets the banner, the countdown and the existing card panel. Monument, Demobilise and the Gold option value come in the next increment, if the first one earns it.

**How we will know within three playtests.** From each tape, per Age: did he score, which card, how many turns after the reveal, and what he delayed. After each game one question: "which Ages did you change your plan for, and which did you not notice?"

- Works: in at least two of three games he names two or more Ages where he changed plan; he misses at least one Age per game; nothing scores on the reveal turn; no stretch longer than one Age with nothing to aim at.
- Fails: he scores every Age with the same card, or cannot say what any of them cost him. Then the pool is the problem, not the reveal, and the next step is Monument and Demobilise; if those also fail, Option C.

No win-rate claim is made or needed. The only sim is the seek-0/seek-1 card check, which tests whether cards function.

## 7. Questions only he can answer

1. **What exactly bothered you on 10-31 when you asked for the full redesign?** The record does not say. My suggested answer: "I scored them without changing my plan and mostly did not notice them." If it was something else (too many, too warlike, too fiddly), the recommendation may change.
2. **Should a new Age reveal its cards to everyone at once, when the first empire gets there, or to each empire when it arrives?** Suggested: everyone at once. It is the TI4 moment, it stops science paying twice, and you and your co-op partner see the same cards on the same turn.
3. **Should cards expire?** Thirty turns, then that Age's point is gone for whoever missed it. Suggested: yes. It is what makes "not every civ should be able to get it every era" true.
4. **Is saving Gold ahead of an Age gaming it, or a good decision?** Suggested: a good decision ("save then burn"), provided the Gold card shows up in only about half the Ages.
5. **Should a Fight card ever be completed by a war you would have fought anyway?** Suggested: no. Only harder targets count (an empire ahead of you, a walled city), and capitals and wonder cities never count, because they already pay.
