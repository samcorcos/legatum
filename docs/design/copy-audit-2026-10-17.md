# Copy audit, 2026-10-17

Every player-facing string was checked against the plain-language guide ("Game text: plain language" in `.claude/skills/legatum-ui/SKILL.md`) and rewritten where it broke it. Words only: no rule changed.

**Why** (user, 2026-10-17): the Swiss sheet said "+1 Gold per complete pair of population... Every 100 Gold held adds +1 Gold per turn, up to +5." "'per complete pair' is not normal language... is there a rule or something we can add to make sure we use normal language?" Agents had translated code literally (`floor(pop / 2)` became "per complete pair"), and nothing enforced a standard.

**Enforcement now:** the copy lint (`app/src/test/copyRules.ts` is the one data file of banned phrases, glossary synonyms and sentence limits). `app/src/ui/textArtifacts.test.tsx` runs it with the artifact guard over the rendered corpus in `app/src/test/playerText.tsx` (production, technology, faction, goal, Guide, victory, diplomacy and treaty text, and every turn-report line of a 60-turn mock game). `app/src/ui/copySource.test.ts` runs its phrase rules over string literals in `app/src` and `crates/core/src`. The UI reviewer reviews copy on every screen, and agent briefs that write player text must cite the guide (`legatum-workflow`).

## Scale
- Rendered data strings (sheets, goals, tooltips, production, technology, rules): 176 distinct changes, table 1.
- Strings in components, report lines, refusals and core labels: 79 edits, table 2.
- The Guide: about 293 sentences rewritten as about 500 shorter ones, in 101 paragraph-level rows, table 3.

## Left to other agents
- Switzerland's two abilities (the population Gold and the interest lines, and the "Buys cost 175% of full adjusted Production" tail of its tooltip): the `swiss-interest` agent. The lint fails on them until that lands.
- Unique-piece cards' "versus what it replaces" comparisons: the `piece-compare` agent.
- The Settle lens hover, legend and its core breakdown labels (`settlement.rs`, "15 × N hexes beyond 6"): the `settle-green` agent.

## Worst offenders
1. Switzerland: "per complete pair of population", "Every 100 Gold held adds", "175% of its adjusted Production price" (with `swiss-interest`).
2. The Mongol tooltip: "Settlers retain 2 moves... provided they can reach a free landing. Moving and firing remain mutually exclusive. Active Market-family cities at least 8 hexes from the owned capital".
3. Germany's goal: one 47-word sentence behind a "Live:" tag.
4. The turn report: "Bo's Scout moved 2 hexes to 2 tiles NE of Lugano" (two words for one unit, in one line).
5. The Guide said Sakoku needs "60 turns closed"; the rule is 30 (the number was doubled as if it were a price). Fixed.
6. The Agoge refusal said "Spartan regiments don't heal", untrue since they rest for 2 HP a turn.
7. The Chivalry blurb said "War Shrine for Sparta": an internal faction id and a retired building name.
8. Athens' counter read "Their Markets give 2 less Gold than normal. Use your Gold for units and buildings": not a counter.
9. Cost breakdowns showed "Base cost" and "Standard costs" rows (a removed speed mode); the unit upgrade tooltip relabelled the same row "Fixed cost multiplier ×2".
10. "Σ strength × HP", "(max 5 per turn)", "(minimum 1)", "Scouts excepted" (20 places), "hexes" (66 places in data alone).

## Possible design smells (rules that are hard to say plainly)
- **Switzerland's price card lists a discount.** Buying at 175% is cheaper than everyone else's 200%, yet it sits on the "price" side. The real price is that a Great Work costs over 1,000 Gold, more than the goal's reserve; the sheet now says so.
- **The Mongol Market bonus is on the price side.** "+2 Gold only at 8 or more tiles from the capital" is a conditional bonus, not a cost.
- **Athens' Lyceum price is not a price.** It costs 140 Production, less than the Library's 160.
- **Japan's two percentage systems** measure from different points (other cities within 5 tiles; the capital beyond 4 tiles) on different yields (Food and Production; all four). Each needs its own sentence.
- **Byzantine repeat prices add, they don't double:** "+100% per earlier payment" had to become "the second costs 200%, the third 300%".
- **Germany's goal has three exceptions** (a new or captured city breaks it, a lost city doesn't, unless you fall below four).
- **Cost breakdowns show a hidden base figure plus a "Game pace" row** (the opening ramp and the ×2). A player never sees the base; showing every row at full scale would remove the row.
- **Great Work rules are eight separate edge rules** (one per empire, one at a time, resistance, switching, lost requirements, capture, finality, publicity).
- From the Guide pass: crowns plus decadence take about nine sentences; fighter interception follows aircraft build order, which the player doesn't control; border claims tie-break on axial coordinates; Alliance upkeep has a minimum that only matters with no cities.
- Left unchanged: the expansion-coalition experiment's reason text (`diplomacy/coalition.rs`, off by default) is a formula with two semicolons.

## Later in the same pass
- **Switzerland** (after `swiss-interest` landed): its toll and interest lines are theirs ("Each city earns +1 Gold for every 2 population. Every 100 Gold in your treasury earns +1 Gold a turn in interest."). This pass reworded the tooltip's tail "Buys cost 175% of full adjusted Production, including Great Works." to "Buying anything costs Gold equal to 175% of its Production cost (other empires pay 200%), Great Works included."
- **Germany** (user, 2026-10-17): "The Ruhr Foundry replaces the Workshop and gives +3 Production" became "Ruhr Foundry: +3 Production (a Workshop gives +2)"; "New building families cost 90% Production" became "Your first building of each kind costs 10% less Production (upgrades and Great Works cost the usual price)" ("building family" is now banned); the Industry project reads "A city can work on an Industry project instead of building: each turn it turns half of its Production into Gold, or a quarter into Science"; its "While it runs, the city cannot produce a unit or building" drawback is gone (it restated the ability), and the real drawback ("Heavy machines and supply trains: every unit, Settlers too, has 1 less move") now sits beside the building discount it pays for.
- **Faction sheet rows now pair a strength with the price that offsets it**, and a strength with no price spans the row. Rows changed: Switzerland's Great Work row (price: a Great Work costs 1,050 Gold, more than the 1,000 Gold reserve); Japan's Food row stands alone and Sakoku is its own row with its limits as the price; the Celts' territory damage now pairs with razing and the +3 ranged archers with the weak melee line; the Mongol Outrider and Market bonus stand alone (the bonus was listed as a price); Athens' Lyceum stands alone (its "price" was cheaper than the Library).
- **From the UI review:** one-step moves read "moved 1 tile, now 1 tile E of Kyoto"; the "A live goal gives VP only while..." notes on the sheet and in the Guide are gone (each goal says how it is lost); the growth tooltip, tile-price breakdown, tribute breakdown ("to the nearest 5") and tribute note are plain; Guide wording for crowns, decadence, aircraft and Spartan Settlers; British spelling is a lint rule.

- **Copy that landed on master during the pass (2026-10-18 merge):** the Keshig, the Hoplite phalanx, the Imperial Scriptorium, Fighter strikes and the air wing, proportional decadence, the stockpile, the post-game report, the Cities overview and where-to-build. Fixed in the merge: "hexes" in the Keshig and resource Guide text and the bow-range refusal; "techs rivals know" (Scriptorium card and breakdown label); "Victory points" in the post-game chart; "at war" in air wing notes; "the walls" (air lore, Cities overview hint); "defense" in forecasts and the unit panel (British spelling); "eaten by the citizens"; "Base 20" in the strength tooltip; the Mongol tooltip's "a unit moves or fires, not both", which the Keshig made untrue; long sentences in the new Fighter, Bomber, Keshig, decadence and crown paragraphs. Branches not yet on master (Japan's 3-tile spacing, the Celtic Sack, territory sight, Germany's slow abroad) meet the lint when they land.
