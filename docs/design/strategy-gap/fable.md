# Why the AI keeps missing strategies the designer finds obvious (Fable, 2026-11-05)

Answer to `brief.md`. Sources: every coach review in `docs/playtests/reviews/`, the decision diffs and ledger, `human-reasoning.md`, the coach, design and factions skills, `docs/learnings.md`, `docs/sim-results/ai-quality/` and `crates/ai/src`. Each root cause names the code that encodes it.

The short version: the AI is a value scorer whose biggest decisions are not scored. Army size, garrisons, site reach, target lists and plan horizons are quotas, gates and constants written by us from a Civ player's intuitions. The gene climb tunes numbers inside those boxes and cannot leave them. The evaluator it climbs against has no term for the things he plays for (time bought, depth, option value of Gold, a tempo window). And every census and benchmark is Hard against Hard, so nothing in the loop ever plays the strategies Hard cannot play. He is the only exploiter in the league. That is why we find them one at a time, after he plays.

## 1. Catalogue: what he found, what it was worth, why the AI missed it

Root-cause classes, refined from the brief: **Q** a fixed quota or minimum where a valued choice belongs; **U** uniform per-city treatment where cities differ in value; **T** the valuation omits a whole effect (time bought, denial, depth, tempo, option value); **H** horizon too short; **K** a shared default the faction's kit makes wrong; **X** an execution bug in a plan the AI already chose; **B** a hard ban or hard-coded target list; **I** information the AI could read but does not; **P** a plan term that never becomes an order; **M** the measurement cannot see it (benchmark blind).

Tally: eleven of twenty are quotas, bans, bugs or plan terms that never became orders (Q, B, X, P); seven are missing value terms or a short horizon (T, H); two are information the view already holds (I). The learnings entry of 2026-11-05 is right for the measured ones: the gains came from a quota or a bug, not from his literal moves (Walls first alone +0.5, more Settlers −1.1, the Diwan −0.35, territory towns −0.6). But that is partly because the only levers we can toggle *are* quotas and bugs; his valuation ideas (depth, time, tempo) have no gene to turn.

## 2. The meta-answer: why one at a time, after he plays

**1. The climb can only move what a gene expresses, and the structure is outside the genes.** `optimization-program.md` §4 already names this: the early army is a formula (`uncapped_army`), production gates return before values are compared (`choose_item_inner`), target bans and quotas are constants. CMA-ES over 34 war genes found "expansion, not his rush" and wandered within five generations; the war genes "wandered without effect" because no setting reaches a T20 capture. That is the textbook local optimum of parameter tuning: the optimum of the parameterised family is not the optimum of the game. A quota is a frozen answer to a question the valuation should ask each turn ("how many troops does this city need, given Walls, position and who can reach it?"). Every one of the five playbook wins this week was the same discovery: delete the frozen answer and let the value decide (#1, #2, #3, #5, #6).

**2. The evaluator is missing the terms he plays for.** `winchance::WEIGHTS` scores nine features: VP progress, and shares of cities, population, economy, army, science, territory, plus runway. Army share has weight 2,480, so the evaluator *rewards* a standing army; the 10-30 decision audit noted "`winchance` credits army size" when his lean Japan read −5.9 pp while his cities were +0.63. There is no term for turns bought, defensive depth, Gold held as an option, upkeep saved, a tech lead measured in eras against the nearest rival, or the output a rival loses while its army walks past walled towns. A plan that trades output for time (buffer towns), or holds 800 Gold at T50, scores zero or negative under this function, so search, the climb and the plan layer all agree it is bad. Berliner's point from 1973 still stands: a fixed-depth searcher with a leaf evaluation that lacks a term cannot discover the strategy that term pays for; it will "create diversions" instead ([Horizon effect](https://wikipedia.com/wiki/Horizon_effect)).

**3. The horizon is ten turns and the decisive effects land at fifteen to thirty.** `plan.rs::plan_horizon()` returns 10; `search::Config` rolls 20 turns at 4 players and 10 at 5+, with 2 samples. A Settler pays after the horizon (the diff's `settler_routes` row is −6.23 VP *inside* its window while `settler_founding` is +5.08 at game end); Peace with the Mongols at T130 freed an army that reached 8 VP at T147; the Bomber war decided in 5–8 turns but needed Flight 17 turns earlier. The same horizon sits in our measurement tools: a one-turn swap with Hard continuing is read at T+20 to T+50, which is why "he founds, Hard waits" reads positive and "his Settler routes" negative in the same table.

**4. Every census and benchmark is Hard against Hard, so the league has one member.** AI defence was tuned against AI attackers: the same defenders that fell to him in 4–7 turns held 30/30 against a forced AI Sparta (8.9.2). The human-seat benchmark's Hard seat "rarely snipes Settlers, buys a decisive Bomber or holds a Celtic goal, so a fix against his tactic reads identical or within noise" (coach skill, 11-03). The Engineer genes read +2.9 pp against a Hard seat and 30→0/30 against his walled, empty line. AlphaStar's league exists for exactly this: main exploiters "play only against the current main agents to identify potential exploits", league exploiters hunt "systemic weaknesses of the entire league", and both are periodically reset to keep finding specialist strategies ([Vinyals et al. 2019](https://deepmind.google/blog/alphastar-grandmaster-level-in-starcraft-ii-using-multi-agent-reinforcement-learning/)). We have no exploiter but him. Self-play with one policy settles into whatever that policy can already do, and a strategy space with rock-paper-scissors in it (pillar 10) is non-transitive by design, so a single champion cannot cover it ([Balduzzi et al. 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1901.08106)).

**5. The coach loop is reactive and ports moves, not valuations.** A review replays one game, swaps his orders against Hard's, and ports what differs. Behaviour cloning from n = 1 fails for the stated reason (`optimization-program.md` §1: AlphaStar used about a million replays). What transfers is the *principle* behind the move: "width is my defence", "a walled town needs no troop", "Gold is an army I haven't bought yet". Those are value terms, and the review has nowhere to put a value term except a new gene gated to the faction he just played. So each faction gets its own playbook flag (`swiss_playbook`, `aztecs_playbook`, `celts_playbook`, `india_playbook`), and the shared quota that caused all four stays at 5.

**6. The AI has no whole-game bundle.** He picks one plan and commits; the plan layer has Economy, Peace, Campaign, Contain, Snipe, FieldStrike and Hold (`plan.rs::PlanKind`), re-chosen each review point, with production and staging driven only partly by the chosen plan (review #1 in the program doc). "Pick one and commit" has no object in the code to commit to.

Strong humans in Civ 6 Deity play the same way he does: expand before the AI claims the land, keep a minimal ranged defence, take unwalled cities only and stop when walls go up, and win the tech tempo race ([Civfanatics threads](https://forums.civfanatics.com/threads/lets-talk-deity-dominant-strategies-openings-etc.619959), [early-war advice](https://forums.civfanatics.com/threads/early-wars-without-uu.621953/latest)). Those are valuations of time and softness, and the Deity AI loses to them for the same reason ours does: it defends by quota and attacks by threshold.

## 3. Still-hidden strategies

Predicted from the same root causes, with the code that hides each. Ordered by how sure I am and how much it would pay him.

1. **Peace-cycle the AI's war quota.** `uncapped_army` wants `cities + 5` only while a Campaign or Contain is on; sign Peace and the quota falls to `cities + army_hard_extra` (2), so the AI stops training and lets units go (Sparta deserts at Peace). Redeclare after `PEACE_TRUCE_TURNS`: the AI re-arms from scratch each cycle while he keeps his army. `peace_redeclare` prices *our* redeclaration, not his. Class Q, H. Any faction; cheapest for Sparta and the Mongols.
2. **Bait the door to drain the AI's Gold.** `door.rs::plan_door_defense` buys, per Gold, the strongest unit at any own city a visible enemy field unit can strike within `DOOR_TURNS` (3), one purchase per city per turn, and `door_military` strikes only at 115% expected damage with ≤30% loss. Two full-HP mounted units walked along a rich AI's border each turn force a purchase a turn and no counterattack; upgrades and Settlers stop (the standing-chest result: door buys crowd out upgrades 5.7 → 1.5). Class T (no feint term), Q. Mongols.
3. **Fog cavalry on interior cities.** With `war_garrison` and `hollow_march`, `military.rs::garrison_cities` leaves any city with `threat_exposure` 0 and no enemy unit within 5 hexes ungarrisoned; `threat.rs::exposure_to` counts only units within `NEAR_RADIUS` and reachable. A 5-move Cavalry (Mongols 6) staged 7 hexes out in fog arrives and captures in one turn. The 8.9.4 storm seat lost its empty capital 30/30 to AI Sparta; the AI's own interiors are now empty by gene. Class U, I.
4. **Arrive inside the Walls deadline.** `threat.rs::walls_beat_arrival` assumes an unseen army arrives in "rival city distance / 2 + 3" turns and builds a shooter first when Walls would lose that race. A human who stages out of sight and covers 7 hexes in two turns beats both the shooter and the Walls; a human who stays visible at 12 hexes keeps the AI on Walls and Archers forever (the 8.9.4 Athens Granary-and-Settler pattern in reverse). Class Q.
5. **Stealth wide: stay below the runaway thresholds, then score eight points in twenty turns.** `conquest_runaway_percent` 45, `contain_leader` and `runaway_signs` key on the strict VP leader and public riches (a crown, a live goal, a Power lead); `leader_race` scales by the leader's own win chance `p`, so a 2-VP empire with 15 cities is worth nothing to contain. He already half-plays this (8.9.15: nobody above 3 VP until T158; 8.9.25 Celts: he delayed the Wild "so people wouldn't gang up on me"). The full form: spend Gold to avoid Largest Economy, keep the army below Strongest Army, enter eras second, bank cheapest cards, then close on a captured Great Work plus the faction goal. Class T, H.
6. **The era-tempo window below Flight.** The Bomber is the visible case of a general rule: strength grows 30% an era and a unit two eras ahead wins at equal Production; the AI spends the era resource on its key building during war (Niter into Banks, 8.9.22) and `modernization_upgrade` is gated, while `research.rs::tech_benefit_value` has no term for the public era gap to the nearest rival. Riflemen and Field Cannons at T100–T120 against Musketmen are the same free war as Bombers at T150, earlier and cheaper, and nothing in the AI races to close it. Class T, K.
7. **Supply starvation.** Settle or capture the one Iron or Coal deposit within a rival's reach: `supply_value.rs::capture_value` prices our gain, but no defender term in `exposure_to` or `plan_threat_defense` weighs a deposit city higher, and an empire without the current era's resource fields last era's army (Germany held none all game in 8.9.22). Class U, T. Strongest against Germany and the Celts.
8. **Pull AI Sparta's army onto an expedition, then take its capital.** `sparta_valued_expedition` is 1 now, so check that the legacy value-0 "forced expedition" (`plan.rs` `sparta_force_campaign`, `march_ignores_second_war` 1) no longer fires; in 8.9.25 it held Sparta's army 49 turns at a far Celtic town while he took Sellasia with two archers. If the gene closed it, the next form is the same with `campaign_march_patience` 12: a visible soft city at 12 hexes keeps a Spartan army marching for a dozen turns. Class B, H.
9. **Take the AI's own VP objects, which it does not garrison.** `garrison_cities` ranks Hall, Diwan, wonder and capital cities first, but a Great Work city, the Heir's city and a Celtic camp ring get no defender beyond the quota, and `hoard_denial_value` is the only VP-denial term rivals price. Five games in a row his closing VP was a captured Great Work; AI Celts now hold the Wild with unguarded camps a human clears in one move. Class P, U.
10. **Alliance as a shield.** Allies fight wars together and an Alliance is signed by value (`alliance_security`, `alliance_opportunity`); the AIs refuse each other over 60 Gold but accept a rich human. Allied to the strongest army, every rival that declares on him is at war with it, and `second_front_value` already discounts a target whose allies are strong. Class T (the human's side of the alliance is not priced as a shield).

## 4. A discovery method that runs here, ranked by expected yield per cost

Rule for all of them: an AI-only census decides a faction gene; the human-seat benchmark only checks rivals are not weaker; a win against a Hard seat is not evidence against a human strategy.
