# Making the AI actually good: options and costs (2026-10-27)

Written for the designer after his 10-27 ask: think hard about what the game is, what the results and tapes say, what the research says, and what each path would cost. Everything below is checked against the repo's own numbers (file paths in brackets) or linked to a source.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

Yes, this AI can get good enough to beat you most of the time, without cheating, but not by more tuning and not by one big training run. The evidence says the gap is almost entirely in **fighting wars**: the AI picks reasonable goals and prices them sensibly, then cannot turn a goal into an army that arrives together, fights as one, keeps its best units in the fight, uses the units and aircraft of its era, and finishes a siege or a leader. Your own strategy-lab plan, when the AI executes it, already beats today's Hard by 7–11 points, and an oracle that picks the best of eight AI variants per game would win 66% in your seat instead of 28%, so better *decisions at the operational level* are worth a lot; more rollouts, more genes and perfect information are worth almost nothing (measured). The research agrees: shipped 4X AIs are weak at war because nobody built an operational layer (Firaxis, Freeciv and Soren Johnson all say so), while full-game deep RL on Civ-like games has failed in public (CivRealm) and AlphaStar-scale training is out of reach. What works at our scale is **search over a small set of executable operations on the real engine, tactical lookahead at engagements, and small learned models trained offline with PyTorch and shipped as integer weights**; our own $0.88 evolution pilot found a rush defence (54% → 85% capitals held) that no gene could, which is proof the method fits this engine. Realistic expectation: a Hard that beats you about half the time by January with 3–4 months of focused work (Codex doing most of the implementation, roughly 150–400 box-hours, $150–$600 of AWS, and about 2–4 weeks of Claude judgement time); a Hard you "usually can't beat" is plausible after that, but not promised, because every gate we have is noisy (±3 pp per 1,024 games) and the final judge is a handful of your games.

## What we know (verified against the docs)

- **You win about 76% of finished games (13/17; 8/10 on 4-player Small)**, and the recent ones (Sparta, Athens, Japan, Switzerland on 8.9.10–8.9.12) all end the same way: "the AIs don't know how to fight wars" [`docs/playtests/human-reasoning.md`]. Note: the 8.9.12 Chur game ran every AI at **Normal** because of a lobby bug (fixed 10-27), so that one tape overstates Hard's weakness [`docs/learnings.md` 2026-10-26].
- **Hard in your seat on the eight fixed maps wins 32–39%** depending on dice and engine version; the paired noise on 1,024 games is about ±3 pp [`docs/sim-results/ai-quality/headroom-decomposition.md`, learnings 2026-10-26 gate noise].
- **The failures are operational, not evaluative.** Sparta game: Japan lost 53 combat units to 3, 37 of them to one Spartan shooter that was never attacked in melee; troops were "counted as a force before they could arrive together" [`astra/analyze-sparta-game-1025`]. Athens game: Switzerland staged an obsolete army at your border for the whole war while a stale Mongol war pinned its one modern gun at home; bombers flattened a city nobody walked into; Germany's capture valuation counted a crown you couldn't lose and ignored its own live goal [`astra/analyze-athens-game-1025`]. Japan game: all four AIs reached Flight, none built a Fighter, Germany kept interrupting Bomber builds [`astra/analyze-japan-air-war-1026`]. Twelve Hard games: 14 solo attacks into city fire, 91 parked-army turns, 129 turns at war with 300+ Gold unspent [learnings 2026-10-26].
- **Tuning has run out.** CMA-ES banked +3 to +8 pp for four factions in round 1, then 12 of 12 round 3/4 candidates failed; Celts, Japan, Mongols and Sparta have plateaued [`climb-program/README.md`]. The 10-18 and 10-21 climbs found the same thing: genes buy Settlers, never a rush, because "no gene sets six Hoplites before anything else" and no gene gets an army to a city in time [`optimization-program.md` §3–4].
- **Search is capped by its choices, not its information.** Hard+Search gains +4.5 points in 1v1, but the omniscient upper bound adds only +0.1 over fogged search; more samples, longer horizons, rival-policy variants and opening search all failed to add anything [learnings 2026-10-06]. The move-level tactical skateboard chose differently from the script in 36% of engagements and was +0.4 (noise) because its 3-turn material score "values keeping units alive over taking cities".
- **Learning works here when the action space is small and the decision is local.** The 2026-10-07 evolution-strategies pilot (150 integer weights, 15 observable features, five unit orders and four city orders) raised capitals held at T60 from 54% to 83–87% against the Proxy v7 Mongols, held out, for $0.88 of spot compute [`docs/sim-results/rl-pilot/README.md`]. The distilled macro model (ridge regression on 64-sample rollouts) only agreed with the oracle 57–60% of the time and was not a plan replacement [`docs/sim-results/distilled-macro/README.md`].
- **Your plan transfers to the AI when executed.** Strategy lab: the Japan air-and-capture bundle is +10.7 pp [+6.2, +15.3] (r4), replicated at +7.3/+6.0 (r5) and +6.6 (r6); Fighter corridors +3.0; "save then buy" as implemented *loses* (−7.7 to −9.5 pp) because the AI buys at the wrong moment, not because saving is wrong [`astra/strategy-lab-r6`]. Army modernization (value units against the enemies they will meet) is +2.2 pp and on.
- **Engine speed.** A full 4-player Small game is about 7.6 core-seconds on the Graviton box (about 30,000 games per box-hour); an 8-player Standard game is about 1.2 CPU-seconds on the laptop [learnings, `perf/ledger.csv`]. A search decision (3 candidates × 20 turns × 4 samples) costs about 73 ms native in 1v1; late 8-empire turns cost 22 ms native for all seven AIs' planning plus resolution [`perf/turn-latency.md`]. The app's Hard is plain `ScriptedBrain` (no search) and runs in WASM, which is slower and single-threaded; spot servers are about $0.72/hour, on-demand $2.3–2.6/hour; October's AWS spend is already close to the monthly cap.

## What the research says, and what it means for us

### Shipped 4X AIs

- **Soren Johnson (Civ IV, Old World), "Playing to Lose" (GDC 2008)** and his 2023 Donkeyspace interview: Civ AI sits between "good" and "fun"; multi-sided games need target choice and diplomacy that two-sided games don't; and "rushing the human is both hard for AI programmers to execute and would also be a bad experience… so has been avoided intentionally" [designer-notes.com/playing-to-lose-ai-and-civilization-gdc-2008, designer-notes.com/donkeyspace-ai-interview]. *For us:* the industry never solved operational war AI; it chose not to. Our pillar 6 (harder by deciding better) means we have to.
- **Civilization V/VI/VII, Humankind, Endless Legend, Total War, Polytopia:** difficulty comes from bonuses, and the strategic layer is weighted utility ("flavors", "agendas") with scripted tactics; the Civ V tactical AI was rewritten by the community because the shipped one attacked piecemeal [github.com/LoneGazebo/Community-Patch-DLL]. No primary Firaxis, Amplitude, Creative Assembly or Midjiwan source on *how* their war AI works was found; the public record is reviews and mods. *For us:* nothing to copy; our value-scoring architecture is already at the shipped state of the art, and that state of the art is exactly what you are beating.
- **Freeciv's own AI document** says it is "widely recognized as being as good as or better military-wise" than comparable games and "still too easy for experienced players"; it is want-values plus amortised profit estimates for military operations [raw.githubusercontent.com/freeciv/freeciv/main/doc/README.AI]. **Unciv** automates the same way [github.com/yairm210/Unciv …/logic/automation]. **AI War (Arcen)** gets its reputation from emergent per-unit utility with a shared "danger map", explicitly not planning [gamedeveloper.com/blogs/designing-emergent-ai-part-1-an-introduction]. **Stellaris 3.0** rebuilt its AI around an explicit economic plan [forum.paradoxplaza.com, dev diary 203]. *For us:* AI War's danger map and Stellaris' explicit plan are the two ideas worth borrowing; both are cheap.

### Research systems

- **AlphaStar** (StarCraft II, Nature 2019) and **OpenAI Five** (Dota 2) reached top-human play with imitation from about a million human games plus a self-play league with exploiters, on thousands of TPUs/GPUs for weeks [nature.com/articles/s41586-019-1724-z; arxiv.org/abs/1912.06680]. *For us:* the recipe (human-initialised, league, exploiters) is right; the scale is not available and not needed for a 300-turn game with 6–8 scripted opponents. We can run the league idea with parameter files and small nets.
- **CICERO** (Diplomacy, Science 2022) combined a language model for talk with a *planning* core (piKL-regularised search over simultaneous moves); the no-press predecessor used best-response policy iteration for "a large combinatorial action space and simultaneous moves" [ai.meta.com/research/cicero; arxiv.org/abs/2006.04635]. *For us:* Diplomacy is the closest cousin (7 players, simultaneous orders, one order per unit). Its lesson: search with a learned prior, over a sampled set of joint moves, not a full tree.
- **DeepNash** (Stratego) learned a near-Nash policy for a huge imperfect-information game with model-free RL and no search [arxiv.org/abs/2206.15378]; **ReBeL** (poker) and the **Bayesian Action Decoder** (Hanabi) show public-belief search for hidden information [arxiv.org/abs/2007.13544; arxiv.org/abs/1811.01458]. *For us:* fog is not our bottleneck (omniscient search adds +0.1), so belief machinery is a later refinement; our determinization from the fogged view is already adequate.
- **RTS search literature (Buro's group, Ontañón):** Puppet Search picks among scripted behaviours with exposed choice points and searches over those [skatgame.net/mburo/ps/aiide15-puppet.pdf]; combining a learned strategic selector with tactical search beat either alone in microRTS [arxiv.org/abs/1709.03480]; naive sampling / combinatorial bandits handle multi-unit action spaces [arxiv.org/abs/1710.04805]; NTBEA tunes noisy game parameters [arxiv.org/abs/1802.05991]. *For us:* this is our architecture, named: scripts as the executor, search over the few choices that matter (commit now, wait for arrivals, withdraw, bomb-then-capture), learned evaluation at the leaves.
- **MicroRTS competitions:** scripted bots won five years running; the first RL winner (2024) bootstrapped with behaviour cloning, fine-tuned by map, and trained in about 60 GPU-hours on one machine [arxiv.org/abs/2402.08112; arxiv.org/abs/2105.13807]. *For us:* RL for a bounded tactical controller is affordable; RL for the whole game is not.
- **CivRealm (Freeciv learning environment, ICLR 2024):** "both RL- and LLM-based agents struggle to make substantial progress in the full game"; the hierarchical LLM agent "achieve[s] only moderate economic expansion… while failing to defend against pirate invasions" [arxiv.org/abs/2401.10568]. Branavan's Civ II work reached 78% wins only with MCTS plus a learned evaluation that read the manual [arxiv.org/abs/1401.5390]. *For us:* no full-game RL, no live LLM. Use the LLMs where they already help: reading tapes, writing fixtures and candidate strategies, auditing code.
- **Entity-based policies** (transformers over units, as in the Lux AI Kaggle seasons and the entity-neural-network project) make multi-unit control learnable without a fixed action grid [kaggle.com/competitions/lux-ai-season-2; github.com/entity-neural-network/incubator]. *For us:* the natural model shape if we ever learn a tactical policy; small enough to quantise into Rust.
- **Tactical combat on hex maps:** influence and danger maps (Game AI Pro 2, ch. 30, "Modular Tactical Influence Maps") for safe staging and arrival routes [gameaipro.com]; combat simulation with focus-fire scripts and short lookahead (Churchill & Buro's portfolio search) for engagements; synchronising arrivals is a scheduling problem the RTS literature solves with explicit rendezvous points and "wait for the slowest" rules, which our planner does not have.

## Six strategies

Each one: what it fixes, why it might fail, the first increment that proves it (skateboard), and cost. "Box-hours" are hours of the whole 64-core box; a spot server is about $0.72/hour and on-demand about $2.3–2.6/hour. Live cost is per AI seat per turn on your laptop, native; multiply by about 2–3 for WASM in the browser build until measured.

### 1. An operational planner that owns the war (fix the architecture)

Today a campaign is a target and a value; the units, the route, the arrival turn, the funding and the home-defence cost live in other passes that disagree with it (the garrison pass that pinned Switzerland's gun ran before siege planning; staging became a destination at Chur). Make one **operation record**: objective, committed roster (ground and air), rendezvous tile and turn, required force at arrival, purchase commitments and deadline, home-defence allocation. Every turn it must **commit, gather, or withdraw**, and tactical refusals return a reason the operation can act on. Search 3–6 alternatives (commit now, wait for unit X, release garrison Y, buy Z then attack, bomb and capture together, switch objective) by rolling each forward on the engine, as Hard+Search already does for plans.

- **Fixes:** piecemeal arrivals (Sparta game), indefinite staging (Chur, Athens game), stale reservations, air builds dropped mid-way, bombers without a capture force, saving with no named purchase, the leader-stop that fires with no executable target.
- **Why it might fail:** the executor still refuses every promising plan; the 20-turn horizon still prefers delaying losses to taking cities; more candidates blow the live budget in 8-player lobbies; the staging-ring fix already showed that a forecast that doesn't match executable orders breaks a working siege (Aarau 38/64 → 0/64).
- **Skateboard:** one known enemy city, one front, three executable choices (commit, gather, withdraw), scored on six save-derived positions (Swiss T180/T210/T220, German T214/T219, Sparta-game Japan T142) with recorded and adaptive replies. Pass: better capture-by-deadline and fewer losses than the local fixes already on branches, war suite unchanged (Golden Horde ≥ 40/64).
- **Cost:** 3–5 weeks calendar. Codex 3–5 lane-weeks (Sol), Claude 0.5–1 week (design of the record, reviews, go/no-go). 40–120 box-hours ($30–90 spot, $100–300 on-demand). Live: +5–40 ms per seat per turn with a search, under 1 ms otherwise; budget the whole lobby at p95 < 1 s on your laptop and measure in WASM.
- **This is the backbone.** Every other strategy either feeds it (evaluation, candidates) or sits under it (tactics). Astra's 10-26 proposal (`ai-architecture-2026-10-26.md`) is this, with a two-week plan; I agree with it and would keep its "A first" order.

### 2. Tactical engagement search (make unit movement smart)

Unit orders in a fight are one-shot scripts today. Add an **engagement controller**: when our units are within reach of an enemy group or city, group them, build a danger map (what each enemy can hit this turn, including city fire and the split counterattack), and choose among a small portfolio per group: focus fire on the target the forecast says dies this turn, kill the exposed shooter first (the Spartan unit 40 case), screen the shooters with melee, pull the wounded out of reach, hold until the slowest unit arrives, or assault together. Resolve each option on 2–4 determinized states for 1–3 turns on the real engine and score it by **captures and kills, not material** (the 10-06 skateboard's lesson). Arrival sync comes free: "hold until the slowest arrives" is a portfolio entry, and the danger map keeps staging tiles out of city range.

- **Fixes:** solo attacks into city fire (14 in 12 games), feeding units into one shooter's arc, never attacking the unit that is killing you, parking in range, melee not finishing a 0-HP city, Fighters not covering the front (the same controller can own interception and rebase).
- **Why it might fail:** simultaneous resolution means the enemy's move is unknown; with scripted Hard as the sampled enemy the search learns to beat scripted Hard, not you (your kiting as the Mongols is exactly the move it would not expect). Portfolio search is only as good as the portfolio. Live cost scales with engagements per turn.
- **Skateboard:** the 10-06 `x` option already exists (four order sets, 3-turn material). Replace the score with captures and kills, add "kill the exposed shooter", "screen", "wait for arrivals" and the danger map, and measure on the Sparta-game Japan fixture (53:3) and the combat lab's 30,000 situations. Pass: exchange ratio and capture-by-deadline improve on held-out engagements; Golden Horde unchanged.
- **Cost:** 2–4 weeks. Codex 2–4 lane-weeks, Claude 0.3–0.5 week. 20–60 box-hours ($15–45 spot). Live: 2–4 states × 1–3 turns × 5–8 options per engagement; keep it to ≤ 30 ms per engagement native, cap engagements per turn, and run the rest scripted. In WASM this is the one place we may need a smaller budget (fewer samples) than native.

### 3. A learned evaluator for the searches (PyTorch offline, integers live)

Search leaves and candidate ranking use a hand-fitted win-chance formula from 10-04 rules. Train a small net (about 100 features → 64 → 32 → win chance plus auxiliaries: capture by deadline, losses, hold survival) on 2–10 million positions from 100,000–250,000 games played by a **mixture** of Hard, proxy attackers, strategy-lab bundles and climb champions; quantise to int8/int32 and run it in Rust and WASM. Refit automatically after rule changes.

- **Fixes:** the 20-turn horizon's blind spot (delaying losses beats winning), save-versus-spend trade-offs, veterans versus replacements, and the accounting bugs a fixed formula keeps acquiring (the crown that couldn't be lost).
- **Why it might fail:** it learns weak-policy outcomes (Hard versus Hard) and is then exploited by the search; correlated positions from the same game look like millions of examples; a better predictor does not always pick better actions (the distilled macro model agreed with the oracle only 57–60%). It cannot fix candidates that are never offered (no Fighter in the list) or units pinned by an earlier pass.
- **Skateboard:** linear baseline plus one small net on 20,000 games; test it on *ranking* campaign-versus-refit-versus-economy continuations and operation alternatives from strategy 1's fixtures, not on prediction error. Scale only if regret drops on held-out continuations.
- **Cost:** 2–3 weeks after strategy 1 exists. Codex 2–3 lane-weeks (data export, training script, quantised inference, refit pipeline), Claude 0.3 week. 10–30 box-hours for games ($10–25 spot) plus minutes of GPU on the Mac or a $1–3 cloud GPU hour; refits about $5–10 each. Live: a 100×64×32 integer MLP is microseconds; the cost is the search that calls it.

### 4. Learned controllers by evolution or RL, with a league (the rush-defence pilot, scaled)

Where strategies 1 and 2 define a small decision with observable inputs (which portfolio entry per engagement; commit/gather/withdraw; what to build when a threat appears), train the policy instead of writing it: evolution strategies or PPO in PyTorch over the Rust engine, millions of short games cut from saves and sim states, opponents drawn from a pool (Hard, proxies, past champions, exploiters trained to beat the current Hard from your seat). Ship as integer weights behind a gene. This is the AlphaStar league without the neural scale.

- **Fixes:** behaviours no one thought to script (the pilot found "ranged units while small, Walls and units when enemies are near, call units home at war" by itself); it also gives us counters to your plan (the league's exploiters will find them) and a measure of pillar 10 diversity.
- **Why it might fail:** the pilot also showed what it does *not* find (sentries never emerged); reward design is the hard part (short-horizon rewards made climbs buy Settlers that lost games); the training opponents define the ceiling; a policy that beats proxies may still lose to you. Each new decision interface is engineering, and the Rust↔PyTorch bridge (feature export, action masks, batch rollouts) is a one-time cost. Full-game RL stays off the table (CivRealm).
- **Skateboard:** port the 10-07 rush-defence policy to today's engine behind a gene and gate it (it was never shipped); then train the engagement portfolio chooser from strategy 2 on combat-lab arenas with ES. Pass: beats the scripted chooser on held-out arenas and does not break the war suite.
- **Cost:** 4–6 weeks for the bridge plus two controllers. Codex 4–6 lane-weeks, Claude 0.5–1 week (reward and interface design; this is where judgement matters). 100–300 box-hours for games ($70–220 spot; more if on-demand) plus GPU hours in the low tens (ES needs no GPU). Live: integer policy, microseconds.

### 5. Strategy bundles from your tapes and the lab (commit to a plan, then adapt)

Your "pick one strategy and commit" is already how the strategy lab works: whole-game bundles (research order, build priorities, treasury target, force requirement, trigger to attack) measured in your seat. Extract decision episodes from your 24+ saves (when you armed, settled, declared, saved, bought, switched), keep only what you could see at the time, add the lab's confirmed winners, and train a small **selector** that picks a bundle per game from faction, map, neighbours and observed threats, with explicit abandon conditions. MAP-Elites keeps the best genes per style so Hard has several committed bundles per faction (pillar 10).

- **Fixes:** the blend-of-everything AI; late or absent air transitions; Switzerland that never hoards past the goal; the leader-stop that comes with no plan; it is also the fastest way to make Hard *feel* like a human opponent.
- **Why it might fail:** bundles copy what beat today's weak AI; Hard v2's league openings were "easier for a strong player" when shipped; your tapes are a few dozen games, enough for priors and start states, far too few to clone play (AlphaStar used about a million); "save then buy" showed a bundle can lose on timing even when the idea is right. Without strategy 1, a bundle still cannot fight.
- **Skateboard:** one faction (Japan), two bundles (air-and-capture, early conquest), a selector on faction/map/neighbour features, compared with each fixed bundle and the opening draw on unseen maps. Pass: beats fixed selection on held-out maps; opponents-only benchmark ≤ −2 pp.
- **Cost:** 2–3 weeks. Codex 2–3 lane-weeks (extraction, selector, MAP-Elites archive on the existing climber), Claude 0.3 week, 1–2 hours of your time judging ambiguous episodes. 20–60 box-hours ($15–45 spot). Live: a table lookup.

### 6. LLM-guided strategy at design time (not in the game)

Keep Claude and Codex where they already pay: reading tapes and traces, finding the mechanism behind a loss, writing fixtures from your saves, proposing candidate operations and bundles as code, red-teaming the AI from your seat, and auditing VP accounting. Never in the live game, never per simulated game (pillar 7 and the budgets). The 10-25/10-26 analyses are this strategy working: three tapes, three root causes, six off-by-default fixes with fixtures.

- **Fixes:** the "what is it actually doing wrong" question, fast; candidate generation for strategies 1 and 5.
- **Why it might fail:** it does not fix anything on its own (every finding still needs code and a gate), and tokens are the scarce budget: Codex's weekly window was used up on 10-27 and the Claude session limit has been hit with 15 agents running. The failed Laya teacher pipeline (10-06) is the warning against LLM-in-the-loop.
- **Skateboard:** already running. Keep each tape review to one Sol task plus one Claude review; cap at 2–3 reviews a week.
- **Cost:** 0.3–1.5M tokens per structural finding-plus-fix (the measured range); about one Sol task and one Opus review per tape. $0 compute. Live: none.

### Not recommended

- **More CMA-ES rounds on the current genes:** plateaued, 12/12 failed; only worth it again after strategies 1–2 add knobs.
- **Full-game deep RL or an LLM playing live:** CivRealm's result, our Laya result, the per-turn budget, and the token budget all say no.
- **Deeper or wider plan search as it stands:** horizon, samples, openings and omniscience all measured at zero or negative; the choice set is the limit.

## Recommended roadmap

Order: 1 → 2 → 3, with 5 feeding candidates from week 2 and 6 running throughout; 4 only after 1–3 show transfer. Every step is behind a gene, lands only through the three gates, and gets a playtest build you can react to.

|---|---|---|---|

Operational skateboard (strategy 1) | 3–5 | Hard that commits, gathers or withdraws; keeps its gun in the fight; bombs *and* captures; saves for a named purchase | War suite: ≥ +15 pp capture-by-deadline on the new cases, Golden Horde ≥ 40/64; human-seat benchmark opponents-only ≤ −3 pp, seat-only ≥ +3 pp; two of your games where a war actually hurt |
| 2. Engagement search (strategy 2) | 2–4 | Armies that focus fire, kill the shooter, screen, wait for stragglers, stay out of city range until they strike together | Exchange ratio on held-out engagements; the 53:3 fixture; p95 whole-lobby turn under 1 s on your laptop in WASM |

**Total cost range** (steps 0–3 and 5; step 4 adds the upper end):
- Calendar: 3–4 months to the checkpoint, 4–6 months with step 4.
- Codex: 10–17 lane-weeks of Sol (mechanical parts on Luna), i.e. one lane busy most of the time, two lanes in weeks 2–8.
- Claude: 2–4 weeks of Opus-level judgement spread across design, reviews and go/no-go calls; in tokens, roughly 10–20 structural changes × 0.3–1.5M each.
- Compute: 150–400 box-hours (up to 700 with step 4). At spot prices $110–300 ($500 with step 4); at the box's on-demand rate $350–900 ($1,600 with step 4). Spread over 3–6 months this fits the monthly cap only if the climb program's spot use is paused or capped during the big batches.
- Live: plain Hard stays under 1 ms per seat per turn when nothing is contested; an operation search costs 5–40 ms and an engagement search ≤ 30 ms, native; target p95 < 1 s for the whole lobby on your laptop, measured in WASM before each release.

**What "good enough to beat you" would look like at the checkpoint:** Hard in your seat wins 50%+ on the eight maps (from 32–39%), the war suite's attacker scenarios match your own results in at least half the cases (today 0–3% on most), and you lose two of four fresh games where the losses come from wars you could see coming and could not stop.

## Where this could still go wrong

- **The gates are noisy and few.** ±3 pp on 1,024 games means every real step needs 2–4k paired games and a frozen control; your playtests are the final judge and there are only a few a week. Expect at least one step to "pass" the sims and feel no different to you.
- **Fixing war may expose the economy.** You out-expand the AI (you had about 1.5× the best AI's economy at T100 on 8.9.2); once the AI fights well, expansion and VP conversion become the next gap. Strategy 5 covers part of it; the climb's city targets cover the rest.
- **Rubber bands blur the measurement.** Decadence and crowns already move win rates by 20–85% of the effect in several reviews; keep the bands-off arm in every gate so we know the AI got smarter rather than you got taxed.
- **Rules keep moving.** Every rule change invalidates fitted evaluators and some fixtures; the refit pipeline in strategy 3 and save-derived fixtures are the hedge, but a quiet rules month during steps 1–2 would help a lot.
- **The budget is tokens, not CPU.** CPU for everything above is a few hundred dollars; the limit is Codex's weekly window and Claude's session limit. The plan above assumes one to two Codex lanes and about four Claude agents at a time, as you asked on 10-17.

## Questions for you

1. **Order.** Do you agree with wars first (operations, then engagements), with the economy and expansion gap deferred to after the checkpoint? Or do you want expansion fixed in parallel because you also out-settle the AI?
2. **Live budget.** Is p95 under one second for the whole lobby's AI turn on your laptop acceptable, or should plain Hard stay effectively instant and the searched behaviours be a separate "Hard+" setting?
3. **Rules freeze.** Can we hold rule changes that touch combat, air, siege and VP for the 6–8 weeks of steps 1–2, so the fixtures and gates stay valid? Cosmetic and UI changes would continue.
4. **Your time.** Can we count on two fresh playtests at Hard per step (about 6–8 games over three months), plus one to two hours judging ambiguous decision episodes from your tapes for strategy 5?
5. **Spend.** Are you fine with the compute range above ($110–300 spot, up to $500 with the league) if it means pausing the CMA-ES climb's spot use during the big batches, so the month stays under the cap?
6. **Checkpoint rule.** If at week 10–12 Hard beats you in the war suite and the benchmark but you still win your playtests comfortably, do we stop and diagnose (my recommendation) or fund step 4 anyway?
7. **Difficulty labels.** Once Hard really fights, do you want today's Hard kept as "Normal" so your target player has a step down, or should the new behaviour replace every level with the gap set by budget rather than by cheating?
