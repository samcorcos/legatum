# Making the AI good: diagnosis, options and a path (Fable, 2026-11-07)

Decision memo for the designer, written against master `adb0c918a` (RULES_VERSION 21). Every internal number names its file; paths are from the repo root. External claims carry a link in section 9. Dates are wall-clock with agents, from Sat 2026-11-07.

## 0. One page

**Where we are.** Hard playing your seat wins 23% on the eight benchmark maps against a 50% target (`docs/status/state-of-the-ai.md` §1). You are 8 and 0 since 10-27, Sparta today with five or six soldiers was your easiest win, and the same AI Sparta is third-worst in AI-only play. About 1,500 AI commits since 10-01 and 315 ledger rows have bought roughly +7 points on the benchmark, most of it from two blunt rules (Walls as garrison, Settler stalking).

**Why it hasn't worked: five root causes, not a list of missing behaviours.**

1. **We optimised the wrong loop.** The gates are Hard against Hard (the census) and a one-bit win on eight maps with about ±3 pp of noise. Both measure how Hard does against Hard. Your edge is measured elsewhere: the decision diff over 24 of your games finds +8.9 VP in army routes and targets, +5.8 in founding sooner, +5.2 in garrisons and escorts (`docs/playtests/diffs/patterns-2026-11.md`). The value net proved it: +18% better calibrated than the hand formula, zero regret against Hard rivals, because plan choice "is not where Hard loses" to Hard (`docs/sim-results/value-net/README.md`). It is where it loses to you.
2. **Genes tune numbers on fixed code paths; they cannot add a behaviour.** CMA-ES: 12 of 12 candidates failed. Ninety-two gates became genes with no gain. Each coach review becomes two or three one-off genes that pass the gate and then never fire in your next game (`legatum-learn-from-designer` memory; `state-of-the-ai.md` §3).
3. **Intent never becomes action.** One plan layer prices a Campaign; a dozen passes (`military.rs`, `city_assault.rs`, `threat.rs`, `production.rs`, `civilians.rs`) execute independently and disagree. Campaigns end `insufficient_army` in two turns; four AIs at War with you hit your cities five times in 144 turns while 111 city-turns stood empty beside their units (`docs/playtests/reviews/2026-11-07-8.9.26-switzerland-win.md`).
4. **The AI only ever meets itself.** Rollouts, gates, climbs and playbooks all model the rival as scripted Hard. Your small veteran army wins because every defence was tuned against AI attackers (`legatum-coach` pattern table, "AI defence is tuned against AI attackers").
5. **The ground moves.** Twenty-one `RULES_VERSION` bumps in eight days; every fixture, ablation and label predates one. Nothing can be learned on a surface that changes daily.

**Your hunch, straight.** "System 1 with rules around it" is half right. A learned model that reads the fogged view and prices positions is the one thing that has worked here twice (the value net's calibration, the ES defence at $0.88). A learned *actor* that issues unit orders from a few dozen of your games plus self-play is the thing that has failed everywhere it was tried at our scale (CivRealm, DORA's self-play equilibria, our own ES policy losing full games). And "rules around it" is the hard-coded gating you rejected on 10-22. The version that fits our evidence: **system 1 is the evaluator and the move prior; system 2 is search over operations on the real engine; the engine is the only actor.** Pluribus beat top humans in six-player poker with eight days on 64 cores and under $150 of compute because it searched instead of scaling. That is our budget class.

**Recommendation.** Stop measuring against Hard. Build one loop, expert iteration over *operations*, measured in your seat on your tapes:

- **Skateboard (Tue 11-10 to Fri 11-13):** an offline operation search (the teacher) that, at Hard's plan review points on three of your tapes, enumerates whole operations copied from your own decision categories (march everything within reach on city X and strike when assembled; found now; escort or snipe; buy X then strike; sentry the pass), rolls each 30 turns forward on the Mac, and logs where it beats Hard's own plan. Kill if it beats Hard in fewer than 30% of your decisive windows: then the candidate set is still the cap, and we rethink the representation before spending more.
- **Scooter (Sat 11-14 to Wed 11-18):** the operations become live plan candidates, priced by a value net refit on the teacher's outcomes (the pipeline exists, 111 s per fit), behind one gene; measured as the **designer-seat score**, the area under Hard's takeover curve on held-out tapes.
- **Bicycle (Thu 11-19 to Tue 11-24):** round two with the new Hard as rollout policy and as rivals, your faction playbooks as league exploiters, then a playtest build. **Checkpoint Fri 11-27:** you lose one of three, or the score reaches 40%.

**Stop:** the census as a strength gate, one-gene coach fixes, CMA-ES rounds, strategy-lab rounds, rule changes while a round is in flight, and the stale toolbox.

## 1. What the evidence says, honestly

The 10-27 memo (`docs/design/research/ai-strategy-options-2026-10-27.md`) diagnosed "the gap is almost entirely in fighting wars" and recommended an operational planner, engagement search, a learned evaluator and strategy bundles. Eleven days later: the planner (W1) gave +3.5 pp on the war suite against a +15 mark and is off; the value net is calibrated and neutral; the tactical search gained +0.4 pp; the bundles became per-faction playbooks worth +1 to +5 pp of AI-only win share (`plans/ai-war.md`; `state-of-the-ai.md` §3). The diagnosis was right about the symptom and wrong about the cause. Each piece was built as another hand-written layer, tuned by genes, gated against Hard. The same method produced the same result.

### 1.2 Root cause 1: we optimised against the wrong opponent and with the wrong instrument

Every gate is Hard against Hard. The 8-map human-seat gate puts Hard in your seat against Hard rivals; the census is eight Hards; CMA-ES climbed against Hard; the search rolled out against Hard; the playbooks are measured by AI-only win share. The value-net regret study is the clean proof: on 3,069 positions the better estimate changed 17.8% of plan picks and saved 0.04 pp, and in 299 of 547 disagreements both picks lost 24 of 24 because "the seat loses either way" (`value-net/README.md`, Decision regret). Against Hard, Hard's plan choice doesn't matter. Against you it is the whole game: Hard in your Swiss seat wins 13 of 48 from T2 and 45 of 48 from T80 (`2026-11-07-8.9.26-switzerland-win.md`), so everything that decides the game happens in turns 2 to 80, in decisions the census never scores.

The instrument compounds it. One bit per game, eight maps, 256 salts: the paired noise is about ±3 pp, and per-map effects of +33 pp and −5 pp average to "no effect" (`headroom-decomposition.md`; ideas #11, #12). Policies that differ in 36% of decisions come out as +0.4 pp. We have been reading a thermometer with three-degree ticks to find half-degree changes.

### 1.3 Root cause 2: genes are scalars on fixed paths

A gene is a number in a formula someone already wrote. CMA-ES can move the number; it cannot write "six Hoplites before anything else, march on T18" if no path produces that (`optimization-program.md` §3–4 via the 10-27 memo). The Celts never attacked cities for a week because of early returns no gene could lift (design skill, 10-22). The stage-1 conversion of 92 gates to genes was the right instinct and produced nothing climbable, because the expressiveness is in the code, not the constants. Every new behaviour is a Codex branch, a gate, and a ledger row, and 10 to 15 of them a week have landed about one point each.

### 1.4 Root cause 3: one plan, many hands

`plan.rs` (5,062 lines) picks one objective and prices it in win-chance hundredths. Then `military.rs`, `city_assault.rs`, `threat.rs`, `production.rs`, `civilians.rs`, `siege.rs`, `door.rs`, `hold.rs` and friends each decide their own slice with their own quotas, and the plan has no hand on any of them. The failure signatures are all the same failure: "a forecast-feasible campaign can still never begin" (learnings 2026-10-05); campaigns "ended `insufficient_army`" in two to five turns in every review since 10-18; the Swiss army staged obsolete at your border for a whole war; India's "supply" war on Biel was right and 45 turns late because the plan sat on Peace while the quota built Spearmen (`2026-11-07-8.9.26-switzerland-win.md` §2). W1 tried to fix this with an operation record and gained +3.5 pp, because the record still asked the same executors to carry it out, and they refused as before.

### 1.5 Root cause 4: the opponent model is a mirror

Hard's defence is sized against the armies Hard builds. The coach's standing pattern "AI defence is tuned against AI attackers" has the proof: defenders that fell to you in four to seven turns survived to T87 in 30 of 30 runs when an AI Sparta was forced into the same wars (coach skill). Your 8.9.26 Sparta won with five or six veterans because no AI expects a small veteran force to keep coming; your India won with five units for twenty cities because no AI punishes width. The proxies (v6, v7, v8) were the attempt to model you; they were "too weak to settle which playbook wins" (learnings 2026-10-07) and were dropped. Nothing in the loop today plays like you.

### 1.6 Root cause 5: the rules moved 21 times in eight days

`RULES_VERSION` went 7 to 21 between 10-31 and 11-07. The ablation table, the combat lab, the war suite, the deep-search headroom and the era duels are all marked STALE (`state-of-the-ai.md` §5). Labels for a learned model carry `rules_version`; six bumps in one week invalidate a round before it finishes (`learning-loop.md` §2). Half the AI work this week was repinning tapes and re-measuring controls. This is a process cause, and it is yours to fix: a freeze on combat, siege, Walls and VP rules for the length of a round, or no round can finish.

### 1.7 What did work, and why

- **The ES defence policy** (150 integer weights, 15 fogged features, nine order choices) took "capital held at T60" from 54% to 87% held out, for $0.88, and found "ranged units while small, Walls when enemies seen, units home at war" with no hand-written rule (learnings 2026-10-07). It then lost full games because its objective was T60 survival and it overrode orders for the whole game. The lesson is not "learning fails"; it is that **objective and representation are the design work, and compute is free.**
- **The value net** is +18% Brier skill where the hand formula is −34% (worse than a uniform guess, overconfident at every phase before T200). Inference is 7 µs in integer Rust from a JSON weights file; the fit is 111 s on the Mac. The pipeline is built and deterministic.
- **The decision diff** is the one measurement that reads your edge directly: +8.85 VP army routes and targets, +5.81 founding sooner, +5.23 garrisons and escorts, +3.9 spending the hoard, with intervals, over 24 hash-exact replays (`patterns-2026-11.md`).
- **The takeover curve** (Hard in your seat from T2, T40, T80 on your own tape) is the one number that says *when* the game was decided and by how much: Swiss 8.9.26 13/48 → 30/48 → 45/48; India 8.9.25 8% from T16, 56% from T22. It is cheap (48 reseeded runs), reads in your language, and nobody has used it as a target.

### 2.1 (a) Restructure the scripted AI

The strongest case for it: this is what every shipped 4X did, it needs no data, and it is readable. Better BTS AI reached version 1.00 with a full victory-strategy framework and effective follow-up invasions; the Community Patch rewrote Civ 5's tactical layer; Old World's AI is written by the designer and a Civ IV AI programmer. HTN planners (Guerrilla's Killzone and Horizon) and utility systems (Dave Mark's IAUS) are the standard tools.

The case against, from our own evidence: we have done this five times. The plan layer *is* a utility system (every candidate priced in one currency, hysteresis, explicit abandon reasons); the operation record *is* a shallow HTN; the playbooks *are* strategy bundles. Each was hand-written, gated against Hard, and gained a point or three. Vox Populi's own forum thread reports the tactical AI "regressed" between releases despite years of work. Soren Johnson's view is that rushing the human was "avoided intentionally" in Civ because it is hard to execute and unpleasant; the industry's ceiling here is a design choice, not an achievement, and it is exactly the ceiling you are beating. Restructuring alone is option (a) again with better names.

What survives from (a) into the recommendation: the layering (strategy, operations, tactics) is the right *vocabulary* for candidates; the executors stay as the actor.

### 2.2 (b) A learned system-1 policy with rules around it

See section 3.

### 2.3 (c) System 1 plus system 2

The evaluator and move prior are learned; the decision is search over candidates on the real engine; the search's outputs train the next evaluator (expert iteration). The strongest evidence that this is our budget class is Pluribus: a six-player imperfect-information game, top human players, eight days of training on a 64-core server, under $150 of compute, because the strength came from search at decision time, not from a huge policy. KataGo cut AlphaZero's compute about fifty-fold with better training targets, not more hardware. CICERO is the nearest cousin in shape (seven players, simultaneous orders): a planning engine with a learned value and a learned prior regularised toward human play. Expert Iteration is the name of the loop.

Our own evidence agrees: the search gained against Hard whenever its candidates were real (+4.5 pp with 12 plan composites) and gained nothing when they weren't (four hand-picked tactical order sets, +0.4). The learned value is already better calibrated than anything hand-written. The two failures were the candidate set and the rollout policy, both fixable, and both named as the first kill criterion below.

### 2.4 (d) LLM in the loop

Offline, it is what has produced every root-cause finding since 10-25 and this memo; keep it there. Online it fails three constraints at once: pillar 7 (seconds per decision, not microseconds), determinism (same seed, same game) and budget (tokens are the scarce resource; Codex hit 100% on 10-27). The research record for LLM agents in full strategy games is uniformly weak at long horizons: CivRealm's LLM baseline, Digital Player's CivAgent on Unciv, SC2Arena and DSGBench in 2025. The Laya teacher pipeline (removed 10-06) is our own warning: 73% escalation, findings that described the briefing rather than the game (learnings 2026-10-05).

### 2.5 (e) The hybrid

Everything below is (c) with (a)'s vocabulary, (b)'s evaluator, and (d) kept at design time.

## 3. Your hunch: "system 1 with rules around it"

**The strongest case for it, in our setting.**

1. The two learned things we built beat the hand-written versions by wide margins on their own test: the value net's calibration (+18% against −34%) and the ES defence (54% → 87%). Both read only the fogged `PlayerView`, so they adapt "to what information it has available" by construction.
2. Inference is already deterministic integer arithmetic from a JSON file, 7 µs a call. No ONNX runtime, no floats, no PyO3; training reads exported rows in PyTorch and writes integers back. The Mac fits a 1,217-parameter net in under two minutes.
3. Self-play is free here in a way it is not for StarCraft: 45,000 whole games a Mac-hour. The compute argument against learning does not apply.
4. Your tapes are rich, hash-exact, replayable and come with your stated reasons (`human-reasoning.md`). No Civ-like has that data (learnings 2026-10-07, "No usable public human game data").
5. A single learned function replaces dozens of quotas, each of which is a hand-fitted constant that drifts with every rules change. India's "army tally counts an obsolete Spearman as a tenth of a unit, so build another Spearman" loop is exactly the kind of bug a fitted value does not have.

**The strongest case against it.**

1. **A learned actor at the unit-order level needs data we don't have and can't make.** AlphaStar started from about a million human replays before a 44-day league on TPUs; OpenAI Five consumed 45,000 years of self-play. CivRealm built the AlphaStar-style hierarchical policy for Freeciv and reports that it "struggles to make substantial progress" in the full game, growing score by unit production while "neglecting early city expansion". You have about 30 tapes and 3 to 10 a week; behaviour cloning from that compounds errors the moment the policy leaves your trajectories (Ross et al.'s DAgger result), and your style is one of several good ones, as you said yourself.
2. **Self-play learns to beat itself.** Bakhtin et al.'s DORA reached superhuman play in two-player Diplomacy from scratch, then in the seven-player game found equilibria "incompatible with agents bootstrapped from human data": a DORA agent among six human-like agents underperformed. Our search already did this in miniature: it beat Hard and was flat against the human proxies because its rollouts "don't foresee the proxy's assaults" (learnings 2026-10-07). A system-1 policy trained on Hard self-play would be a stronger Hard-beater and no better against you.
3. **Reward and representation are hand design, and they are the hard part.** The ES policy that won its narrow test lost full games by 5.8 pp even when gated to its training horizon (learnings 2026-10-07 `es-port`). Whoever writes the objective writes the behaviour; a bad objective in a learned system is a worse bug than a bad constant, because it is spread over every weight.
4. **"Rules around it" is the thing you rejected.** On 10-22 you said "you have a bunch of hard-coded rules and you're not iterating or exploring them." Guards around a learned policy become the behaviour whenever the policy is uncertain, and nobody can climb them either. The 10-06 Laya memo and our decision-trace work exist because when Hard does something dumb in your game we need to know *why*; a weight has no `--decision-trace`.
5. **The readable half is the valuable half.** What you want is coherent whole-game strategies that commit and adapt. A value function over the fogged view, with operations as candidates and search choosing among them, gives exactly that: the commitment is the operation, the adaptation is re-pricing it each turn as the view changes, and the record of why is the candidate list with its prices. A policy gives you the move and no reason.

**Verdict.** Keep the learned part as **system 1 = value and move prior**, not the actor. Put a **system 2 = search over operations on the real engine** above it, offline as the teacher and, later, live in a worker while you plan (pillar 7 gives us seconds, not milliseconds). Keep the **engine's scripted executors as the only actor**, so every order is legal, deterministic and traceable. Train the value on the teacher's outcomes and test it on your tapes. That is your hunch with the actor and the evaluator swapped, and it is what the evidence supports.

Where I would still push: your addendum to the training memo says "the teacher may be omniscient". For a *tactical* teacher that is fine. For an *operations* teacher it is dangerous, because the operation to choose depends on what is seen (you settled Biel and Bern before Niter was visible; a teacher that knows the Niter is there would teach the AI to settle for reasons it cannot have). Label from determinized views at the operations level; omniscience only to score regret afterwards. `search/determinize.rs` does this in 0.8 ms.

## 4. The path

One loop. Everything in it already exists in some form; the work is wiring, a new candidate generator and a new metric.

```
your tapes ──► takeover windows ──► TEACHER: operation search on the engine (offline, Mac, nightly)
                                         │  logs: candidates, prices, outcome, regret of Hard's plan
                                         ▼
                              STUDENT: value net refit (minutes) + operations as live candidates
                                         │  gene on; deterministic integer weights
                                         ▼
                      designer-seat score on held-out tapes (primary) ─► 8-map gate (secondary)
                                         │
                                         ▼
                    next round: new Hard is the rollout policy and the rivals; playbooks as exploiters
```

### 4.1 Increment 0: the instrument (Sun 11-08 to Mon 11-09)

- **Designer-seat score.** For each of your last six finished tapes, run Hard in your seat from T2, T20, T40, T60 and T80 with 48 reseeded dice each (the coach's what-if runner does this today). The score is the mean win share across start turns, a single number per tape that falls as Hard gets better earlier. Today: Swiss 8.9.26 about 0.6, Sparta 8.9.26 to be measured. Three tapes are the training set (Sparta 8.9.26, Swiss 8.9.26, India 8.9.25 won); three are held out and never searched (Mongols 8.9.25, Celts 8.9.25, Swiss 8.9.24). Cost: 6 × 5 × 48 = 1,440 games, about 30 minutes on the Mac.
- **Freeze.** Combat, siege, Walls and VP rules frozen until the checkpoint; one frozen control commit for every number. This needs your yes.
- **Bottleneck:** one Sonnet agent and one Mac run. **Done when** `docs/status/designer-seat.md` shows six curves with their commit and rules version.

### 4.2 Increment 1, the skateboard: the operations teacher (Tue 11-10 to Fri 11-13)

Build `crates/sim/examples/operation_search.rs` on the pattern of `value_regret.rs`: at each of Hard's plan review points inside your decisive windows (the diff reports name them: Sparta T18 to T30, India T13 to T22 and T148 to T152, Swiss T30 to T40), enumerate **operations**, not genes, generated from the categories where your edge is measured:

| Operation template | From your pattern | Executor it drives |
|---|---|---|
| March every military unit within N tiles on enemy city X; strike when the forecast says assembled | army routes and targets (+8.85 VP) | the Campaign plan with a forced roster, `city_assault` |
| Found now at the best legal site within two moves; or route to the contested site first | founding sooner (+5.81); "contested land is a race" (`human-reasoning.md` 10-08) | `plan_settler` |
| Escort this Settler with the nearest unit; or snipe that exposed rival Settler | garrisons and escorts (+5.23); India T13–T17 +35 pp | `settler_escort`, Snipe |
| Buy X now, then strike or hold | spending the hoard (+3.9); Swiss T140 Bomber +2.38 VP | `decisive_buy` |
| Post a sentry on the pass toward the rush-capable neighbour | "information is worth a unit" (11-07) | the sentry gene |
| Refuse this Peace; or close this front and keep the other | Peace priced at 0; "close fronts you don't need" (11-07) | `wants_peace` |

Each operation is forced for its duration on 16 determinized states built from the seat's own view, played 30 turns with Hard everywhere else, and scored by the eventual outcome of the full game (reseeded, as `value_regret` does). Log every candidate's price, the outcome, and Hard's own plan's regret. Twelve operations × 16 states × 30 turns is about 30 CPU-seconds a window, two seconds of wall time on 16 cores; a night covers thousands of windows.

- **Go/no-go, written now:** in at least 30% of your decisive windows the best operation beats Hard's own plan by 10 pp or more of win share, with the interval clear of zero. **If it fails twice, stop:** the candidate set is still the cap, as in October, and the next move is a wider generator (per-unit coordinate descent as in the training addendum, lifted to operations), not a fit.

## 5. Stop doing

1. **Using the AI-only census as a strength gate.** It measures Hard against Hard. Keep it for the faction ratio (pillar 8) once a fortnight, with intervals.
2. **One-gene coach fixes.** Each review's findings go into the operation templates and the window set, not into a new gene. The diff and the takeover curve are the review's outputs.
3. **CMA-ES rounds and strategy-lab rounds** on the current representation. Both plateaued; both measured against Hard.
4. **Rule changes while a round is in flight.** Twenty-one bumps in eight days is the single largest cost to AI progress this week. Batch rules changes to the checkpoint.
5. **Publishing a headline benchmark number without a frozen control.** The 16.2% and 23.0% are not comparable; say so until a rerun on one commit exists.
6. **Maintaining the stale toolbox.** Archive the combat lab, era duels, deep search, blunder audit and the climb daemon until a round needs one; delete `proxy_v4` to `proxy_v8` (about 8,000 lines) once the playbooks are the league exploiters.
7. **Building search that ships before it beats Hard in a lab.** The 10-06 result; the training memo says this already.
8. **Running an LLM inside the game loop, or re-proposing a Laya-style teacher.**

## 6. How to measure so progress is visible and doesn't drift

- **One page, one number.** `docs/status/designer-seat.md`: the held-out designer-seat score per tape and overall, as a chart with one point per landing, stamped with commit and `RULES_VERSION`. A portrait PNG goes to you after every landing (`legatum-show-on-mobile`). Nothing lands without a point on this chart.
- **Three secondary rows,** each with its interval: the 8-map gate (seat-only, rivals-only), the teacher's regret rate on the fixed window set (must fall round over round), and your real results (wins, losses, the turn the game was decided by the takeover curve).
- **Held-out means held out.** The three test tapes are never searched, never diffed into templates, never used to tune. Each new game you finish goes into the training set only after its coach review, and the oldest training tape rotates into the test set, so the test set tracks your current play.
- **Frozen controls.** Every number on the page has a control run on the same commit and rules version; the nightly (`tools/sim/nightly.sh`) reruns the designer-seat score on master and marks drift.
- **Kill criteria are written before the run** (sections 4.2 to 4.5) and are kept in `plans/ai-war.prd.json`. A step that misses its number gets a diagnosis entry in `docs/learnings.md`, not a quiet retry.
- **Overfitting guards on your games:** the held-out tapes; the census ratio as a secondary gate (a gain only Sparta can use widens it); the playbooks as exploiters so the AI meets more than one human style; and your own standing rule, "don't assume the way I played is the only way".

## 7. What I am not sure about

- Whether 30 turns is the right teacher horizon for operations. Founding pays after the horizon (the diff's Settler-route rows are negative inside it, positive after). The fix if the skateboard shows it: score at the horizon with the refit value, as the search does, and check a sample against full games.
- Whether a value over the existing 20 facts can price an operation well enough, or whether it needs operation-specific inputs (distance to target, units assembled, Walls at target). I expect the latter; the first fit will say.
- Whether the executors will carry a forced operation faithfully. W1's +3.5 pp says partly. The teacher measures exactly this: an operation that scores well in rollouts and fails live is an executor bug with a fixture attached, which is the useful kind.
