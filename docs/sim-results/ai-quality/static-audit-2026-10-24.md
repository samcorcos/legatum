# Static audit of the Hard AI, 2026-10-24

A read-through of `crates/ai/src` as a player trying to beat Hard, after the designer found two blunders by accident (a ruin left unclaimed inside the AI's borders; an unescorted Settler walked next to his unit at Peace). His standard: "those are things that should have been caught just from static analysis of written logic rules." This is that analysis. No code was changed and no sims were run: every finding cites the lines, says how a human exploits it, how sure I am it really happens, what test would catch it, and what the value-scoring fix is.

What the human faces: `ScriptedBrain` at Hard with `SearchConfig::in_app` (`crates/ai/src/search/mod.rs:167-175`: 3 plan candidates, horizon 20 on maps of 4 players or fewer, 2 determinizations, fitted win-chance weights, no tactics search). Every pass below runs for that brain. Several genes that address known problems exist but default to off (`peace_retaliation`, `settler_safety`, `leader_commit`, `war_pressure`, `war_garrison`, `peace_scout_danger`), so the shipped Hard is the legacy behaviour where those are concerned.

Already in flight, noted but not re-analysed: Codex `fix-ai-ignores-ruins` (finding 16), `fix-ai-naked-settler` (finding 17), `ws-ai-vp-race` (stop-the-leader, holds; touches findings 2, 8, 21), `ws-ai-factions-revive` (Celts, Japan; touches 12).

## The root cause behind half the list

`Ctx::build` defines "enemy" as "at War with us" (`crates/ai/src/analysis.rs:149-152`, `hostile[p] = view.at_war(me, p)`). `enemy_units`, `enemy_cities`, `danger`, `settler_danger`, `threat`, every exposure key, every retreat, every garrison quota and every target list derive from that set. A rival at Peace is `neutral_at`: an obstacle, never a threat. Only two places look past it: `prepare_hard_defense` (`hard_defense.rs:72-208`) adds rivals we could legally declare on to the Settler danger map and to city threat when two or more of them stand within five hexes, and `threat.rs` scores rivals from public conquest records. In a game where a surprise war takes effect before ranged fire and movement (`crates/core/src/rules/mod.rs:271`), and a distant empire loses nothing by declaring, treating Peace as safety is the single assumption a human exploits most. Findings 1, 4, 6, 7, 9, 10, 11 and 14 are all faces of it.

## Top 15, ranked by expected impact on Hard against a human

### 1. The tactical layer is blind to armies at Peace

- **Where:** `crates/ai/src/analysis.rs:149-163` (`hostile`, `enemy_units`), `:189-212` (`danger` only from hostile units); `crates/ai/src/tactics.rs:583-611` (`compute_exposure_key` reads `enemy_cities` and `enemy_exposure_key`), `:950-962` (`avoid_free_damage` retreats only from that exposure), `:271-280` (`can_defend_at`); `crates/ai/src/military.rs:3159-3214` (`rest` parks idle units on any tile within 2 of a city); `crates/ai/src/hard_defense.rs:122-143` (Peace rivals enter the Settler map only).
- **The exploit:** at Peace, walk a stack of Archers and Spearmen up to the AI's field army or its unwalled city. Nothing in the AI moves: its units rest on flat open ground beside yours, its garrison choice ignores you, its wounded heal in your range. Declare. Your shots and charges land in the same resolution; the AI's orders for that turn were planned as if you were scenery. You get one free turn of damage and one free turn of movement in every war you start, and in this game one turn is often the city.
- **Confidence:** certain that the code behaves this way; high that it costs games (the designer's "surprise war takes effect immediately" is exactly this; coach rows "Campaign flip-flops leave home empty", "Declares on a stale picture" are the AI-vs-AI shadow of it).
- **Test:** fixture (`crate::fixture::ViewBuilder`): an own Archer on Grassland two hexes from an own city, a rival Spearman and Archer at Peace adjacent to it, a legal `DeclareWar` in `diplomacy.actions`. Expect the Archer to end the turn in the city or out of their reach. Sim metric: in the human-seat benchmark, own units lost or damaged on the turn a rival declares on the AI, per declaration; threshold half of today's.
- **Fix (value scoring):** make `enemy_exposure_key` include every military unit of a team we could be at war with this turn, weighted by `P(declares)`: a gene `peace_exposure_percent` (0 keeps today), scaled up by the rival's `declared_wars`, its being our top or watched threat, its army standing nearer our cities than its own, and down for an ally or a guaranteed Peace. Tile choice then prefers the city tile, hills and distance from the stack at no cost when there is no threat, and the same code handles war. Yes, a climb gene: it trades mobility for safety and the arena can find the slope.

### 2. A fully walled leader is immune to a second front

- **Where:** `crates/ai/src/threat.rs:806-816` (`soft` = recently captured by the threat, or visible, unwalled and damaged; with `soft == 0`, no allies at war and temperament under 25, `second_front_value` is `None`), `:761` (ratio under 80 is `None`), `:792-805` (only its cities within 12 hexes count); `crates/ai/src/diplomacy.rs:790-799` (a Contain war on the top threat needs that value); `crates/ai/src/military.rs:2798-2824` (`winnable` thresholds; the leader bonus only lowers the bar by 20).
- **The exploit:** build Walls everywhere (the designer already does) and never leave a city damaged. No AI can name a "soft" city, so the conqueror-containment path never declares unless someone else already fights you. Your cities being hard is read as a reason not to fight you, when a leader who is hard everywhere is exactly who must be fought now.
- **Confidence:** high. Coach rows "Leaders aren't checked", "Nobody contests the hold", "The strongest AI is busy elsewhere" all describe it. `ws-ai-vp-race` is adding hold-breaking; this gate sits underneath it and will still veto most declarations.
- **Test:** fixture: we and one ally are at war with nobody, the top threat (score 90) holds 70% of its target, three walled full-HP cities 8 hexes from ours, our side's strength 1.2x its. Expect `second_front_value` to be `Some(>= 0)`. Sim metric: war-scenario "leader at 70% with every city walled", fraction of AIs that declare within 20 turns; threshold 50%.
- **Fix:** replace the `soft` veto with a priced term: the siege forecast against its weakest city with the Siege units we could buy (`siege_buy.rs` already prices this), plus the VP denied (`leader_race` already exists in `contain_candidates`). A walled city then costs more, it does not forbid. Gene: `second_front_soft_required` (1 today, 0 priced).

### 3. Peace is free for the human and expensive for the AI

- **Where:** `crates/ai/src/plan.rs:1726-1791` (the quiet plan is worth 0; the `peace_retaliation` term that would charge it for the army at the gate is behind a gene defaulting to 0, `params.rs:324`); `plan.rs:734-756` and `:612-627` (`peace_credibility` 300 and "broken peace" charge only the AI's own re-declaration); `crates/ai/src/diplomacy.rs:1400-1423` (a Peace offer scores `10 + 2*age ... + 100 when a campaign just withdrew`), `:1850-1853` (`wants_peace` is true for every team except the one plan target); `crates/core/src/diplomacy.rs:72` (`GUARANTEE_TURNS` 3).
- **The exploit:** repel the AI's first assault (its campaign withdraws, +100 on your Peace offer), offer Peace, heal and reposition under a 3-turn guarantee, re-declare on turn 4. The AI, having signed, now pays 300 to re-declare on you for 30 turns and treats the signed peace as a reason to send its army elsewhere. You pay nothing. Repeat every time a war goes badly. The designer did this to Japan in 8.8.1 and the Mongols in 8.9.2 (peace T169, war again T172).
- **Confidence:** high that the asymmetry exists; the designer's record says it's used.
- **Test:** sim metric over the benchmark: wars re-declared by the rival within 10 turns of a Peace the AI accepted, per accepted Peace; and the AI's win rate in those games. Fixture: enemy army of 3 within 5 hexes of our damaged city, their Peace offer, our plan Contain on them; expect the offer refused unless `endangered`.
- **Fix:** price the rival's own re-declaration: `P(they re-declare within horizon)` from their public record (wars they declared, wars on us, their army still at our border after the offer) times the retaliation term, charged to the Peace candidate and to the Peace score. Turn `peace_retaliation` on by default once that term exists, and drop the flat +100 on withdrawal for a priced "army needs to heal" term. Genes: `rival_redeclare_percent`, and `peace_retaliation` 1.

### 4. Six hexes is safe: the threat radii are shorter than a two-turn march

- **Where:** `crates/ai/src/hard_defense.rs:172-195` (Peace rivals count toward a city only within 5 hexes and only two or more of one owner), `crates/ai/src/development.rs:122-130` (`exposed_city`: enemy unit within 5, enemy city within 8), `crates/ai/src/diplomacy.rs:1626-1644` (`endangered` within 5), `crates/ai/src/military.rs:1770-1777` (garrisons recruited from within 5), `crates/ai/src/opponents.rs:13-14, 146-150, 175-186` (`ASSESSMENT_INTERVAL` 5 turns, `BORDER_RADIUS` 5), `crates/ai/src/threat.rs:664-669` (`NEAR_RADIUS` 6).
- **The exploit:** stage at 6 or 7 hexes. Spearmen move 2 (3 on a road, Mongol +1), so the stack is two turns from the city and no counter runs: no city threat, no `exposed_city`, no `endangered`, the opponent model refreshes only every fifth turn, and `aggressive_neighbour` needs two units within 6. Declare and arrive before the first Walls or shooter can be bought. Every radius is a literal a human learns in one game.
- **Confidence:** high. Coach rows "No reaction to Sparta next door until the army arrives" and "AI defence is tuned against AI attackers" (defenders that survive AI attackers fall to him in 4 to 7 turns).
- **Test:** fixture: three rival Spearmen at Peace 7 hexes from our unwalled city, legal declaration, no other threat. Expect a garrison move or a Walls/shooter production switch this turn. Sim metric: war scenario "stage at seven, declare, march": cities taken within 6 turns of the declaration; threshold the AI-attacker rate.
- **Fix:** measure threat in turns to arrive (`threat_reaches_this_turn` already walks real paths; extend it to N turns with roads), not hexes, and let the arrival time scale the response (two turns out: garrison and start Walls; one turn out: buy). Reassess the opponent model every turn for any rival with an army within two turns' march. Genes: `threat_horizon_turns` (default 1 to keep today's behaviour, climb to 2 or 3), and `assessment_interval` as a gene.

### 5. Search rollouts put the human's unseen army at home

- **Where:** `crates/ai/src/search/determinize.rs:1-17` (module doc: "unseen rival units: remembered sightings stay where they were last seen, and the rest of its public army title is filled with its known unit kinds, garrisoned at its cities"; "rival technology is ours"; "rival cities hold no buildings beyond the Walls and Castles we've seen").
- **The exploit:** mass in fog (anything beyond sight 2 of the AI's units and one ring outside its borders). Every plan the AI rolls out has your army sitting in your cities, so Campaign candidates against you look cheap and your threat to its cities looks small. Combined with 4, the first the AI's plan sees of your army is the turn it declares.
- **Confidence:** medium-high: the sampling rule is explicit; how much the fitted plan layer moves on it is not measured. The in-app config uses 2 determinizations, so the error is systematic, not averaged away.
- **Test:** fixture through `determinize`: a rival with 8 public army points, 2 seen at its city, that declared a war last turn. Count sampled units placed within 4 hexes of our nearest city; expect more than 0. Sim: the war scenarios with the attacker's approach in fog, AI capture-forecast error (forecast odds against realised).
- **Fix:** distribute the unseen share by a prior over where it would be: toward our border for a rival at War with us, our top threat or one that has declared wars; toward its own front for one fighting someone else; at home otherwise. Weight by its last-seen headings. Gene: `fog_army_forward_percent`.

### 6. Hard dropped routine Settler escorts and the replacement reads only visible threats

- **Where:** `crates/ai/src/military.rs:182-184` (`escort_settlers` runs only when `!hard_defense()`), `crates/ai/src/hard_defense.rs:403-409` (Hard runs `guard_exposed_settlers`, and with `settler_safety` 0, `params.rs:263`, the older `guard_exposed_settlers_duel`, `:521-594`), `:544-546` (escort only when `settler_trip_risk >= 40` from visible units and remembered sightings), `crates/ai/src/civilians.rs:168-208` (the legacy risk: Peace units 50, within `max_moves + 1`), `params.rs:266` (`peace_scout_danger` 0: a Peace Scout marks nothing).
- **The exploit:** a Scout (3 moves, 60 Production, attacks in melee: `crates/core/src/data.rs:245, 550-552`) in fog two turns from the AI's Settler route. The Settler walks alone because nothing visible is near; by the time the Scout is seen it is adjacent, and `legal_declaration` plus a move takes it. The designer captured three Settlers in one game and lost none.
- **Confidence:** high. `fix-ai-naked-settler` is pricing the Peace weight; this is the structural half: escort value never considers what fog hides.
- **Test:** fixture: a Settler ordered 7 hexes to a site, with the three tiles beside the route unexplored and a rival border 4 hexes away, no visible unit; a free Spearman at home. Expect the Spearman ordered to the Settler or the Settler to wait one turn. Sim metric (blunder audit class "Exposed Settler", extended to Peace captors): Settlers captured per 100 trained; threshold half of today.
- **Fix:** escort value = Settler value (a city) x P(an unseen raider reaches the route), where P grows with unexplored or unseen tiles within 3 of the route, known rival borders within 4, the rival's faction (Mongols, Sparta) and its declared wars; escort cost = the unit's upkeep turns and the defence it leaves. Make `settler_safety` and `peace_scout_danger` genes the climb actually varies (today they pin the legacy path). New gene: `fog_escort_percent`.

### 7. A quiet human never becomes a threat

- **Where:** `crates/ai/src/threat.rs:543-582` (score = conquest from captures, eliminations and declared wars, plus momentum from city growth, times proximity, plus a border-army term capped at 20 + 5, plus the VP race from 30% or 40% of the target), `:21` (`TOP_THREAT` 60), `:657-680` (`threat_exposure` needs a top threat), `:936-949` (`plan_threat_defense` runs only for exposed cities), `crates/ai/src/production.rs:2045-2064` (Walls worth 20 in a quiet city, 100 near a border), `crates/ai/src/research.rs:377-387` (Masonry gets its 80 only for an `aggressive_neighbour`).
- **The exploit:** take no city, declare no war, stay under 30% of the VP target, and build. An army twice the AI's at its border scores at most 25. You are never the top threat, so the AI builds no Walls (20 points), researches Masonry late, funds no `threat_pressure` army and prices no second front. Then one war, to the capital. The 8.9.4 Sparta game is this: the AIs saw him at T4 and armed at T16.
- **Confidence:** high for the scoring; the human-seat benchmark proxies are not quiet, so the sims under-measure it.
- **Test:** fixture: a rival at Peace with twice our public army, no wars, a city 8 hexes away; expect `watched_exposure` true for our nearest city and Walls or Masonry valued above a Granary. Sim: war scenario "quiet build-up then strike at T40" (the designer's Sparta opening without the T18 declaration), city loss rate.
- **Fix:** add an army term to the threat score that is not capped: `(their public army - ours) / ours` scaled by proximity in turns, and growth in their Strongest Army value; let `watched_exposure` and the Walls value read it. Genes: `army_threat_weight`, `army_threat_cap`.

### 8. Title logic still prices Most Populous and Most Territory, and nothing defends or attacks Most Advanced

- **Where:** `crates/ai/src/hold.rs:107-170` (`capture_title_losses` documents and computes Most Populous and Most Territory; Strongest Army and Most Advanced return `false` at `:161`), `crates/ai/src/diplomacy.rs:1663-1695` (`capture_flips_title` matches only Most Populous and Most Territory, so under VP v2 it is always false and the "title" sort key in declarations never fires), `crates/ai/src/victory.rs:489, 528`, `crates/ai/src/goal_actions.rs:139-140`; the live crowns are `crates/core/src/scoring.rs:14-20` (`Title::CROWNS`: Largest Economy, Strongest Army, Most Advanced). `crates/ai/src/research.rs:88-116` has no term for a rival's hold resting on Most Advanced.
- **The exploit:** hold your VP target with Most Advanced by one technology (the designer did, 8.9.2: "ties keep the incumbent"). No AI prices taking it by research or by buying Science, the capture-denial maths looks for titles that no longer exist, and the only crown a capture can break is Largest Economy. The hold completes.
- **Confidence:** certain for the stale code; high that it matters in the endgame.
- **Test:** fixture: a rival counting down at exactly its target holding Most Advanced by one tech, our next tech 3 turns away. Expect `plan_research` to put the cheapest available tech first and `plan_victory` to consider buying it. Unit test: `capture_flips_title` on a VP v2 view with a Largest Economy holder returns true where the share math says so.
- **Fix:** rewrite the title helpers against `Title::CROWNS`; add a research-denial term: value of a tech = its ordinary value + `denial_priority(holder) * (title_gap(MostAdvanced) == 1)`; price Science purchases the same way. Not a climb gene; a correctness fix with one weight (`advanced_denial_weight`).

### 9. No first strike on an army massed at Peace

- **Where:** `crates/ai/src/plan.rs:1794-1893` (`plan_candidates`: quiet, Campaign on a city, Contain on a team, Snipe on a Settler; nothing prices attacking a field army), `crates/ai/src/opportunity.rs:188-258` (`choose_strike` evaluates cities only), `crates/ai/src/siege.rs:264-330` (`raid_field_army` runs only inside a war the plan already fights).
- **The exploit:** the mirror of 1: park your stack beside the AI's city at Peace for as many turns as you need to bring the rest up. The AI will never declare on you to hit the stack with local superiority, even when its three Spearmen could kill your two Archers on open ground. A human in its seat would.
- **Confidence:** high. This is the Civ 6 "your army on my border is a declaration" instinct, absent.
- **Test:** fixture: two rival Archers at Peace 3 hexes from our city on Grassland, our three Spearmen adjacent to them, legal declaration, their nearest city 12 hexes away. Expect a `DeclareWar` plus moves onto them. Sim: war scenario "siege staging at Peace", enemy units killed before the human's declaration.
- **Fix:** a `FieldStrike` candidate: expected kills (the existing melee forecast) x the threat those units pose to our cities (`enemy_threat`) minus retaliation and the new-war terms; offered only when they stand within two turns of our city and we have local superiority. Gene: `field_strike` (0 off).

### 10. An Alliance with the human switches off defence against him and prices away nothing he gets

- **Where:** `crates/ai/src/development.rs:152-169` (an allied neighbour is never `aggressive_neighbour`, so Walls fall to 20), `crates/ai/src/threat.rs:903-910` (`walls_beat_arrival` ignores allied armies), `crates/ai/src/civilians.rs:115, 151, 228` (allied units are no Settler risk), `crates/ai/src/diplomacy.rs:1349-1399` (the Alliance score counts his army as "security" and never the vision we hand him, his passage into our land, or that he leaves free after the 10-turn window: `ALLIANCE_PROTECTED_TURNS`, rules section 5), `:548-582` (we leave only when it is free and a listed reason holds), `ally_offer_progress` 60 (gates.md: we accept him up to 60% of his target).
- **The exploit:** ally every AI early (60 Gold each): shared vision of their whole empires, passage through their land, their Walls value drops to 20 against you, and their Settler escorts ignore your units. After ten turns, leave for free, declare the next turn, with your army already at their unwalled cities. The coach's "Sparta allied with the eventual winner" (8.9.7) is the AI side of it. Whether a unit standing inside former allied land is expelled when the Alliance ends is not covered by the rules reference: verify in `crates/core/src/rules/movement.rs` before pricing the passage term.
- **Confidence:** high for the defence suppression and the free exit; medium for the vision term's weight.
- **Test:** fixture: an allied rival with twice our army 6 hexes from our unwalled city, inside its free-exit window. Expect Walls valued at least as a border city (100 + military). Sim: human proxy that allies then declares after the window; city loss rate.
- **Fix:** Alliance value minus `exposure to his army x his aggression x (1 - exit cost)`, plus a readiness floor toward any allied neighbour whose army outweighs ours (Walls value never below the border value). Genes: `ally_army_discount_percent`, `ally_vision_cost`.

### 11. Scouts are excluded from every threat sum, and Scouts capture

- **Where:** `crates/core/src/data.rs:245` (Scout: 60 Production, 3 moves, strength 5, military), `:550-552` (`attacks_in_melee`: ranged 0, so a Scout captures a 0-HP city and takes civilians); AI exclusions: `crates/ai/src/analysis.rs:220-231` ("Scouts are too weak to count"), `crates/ai/src/hard_defense.rs:130-135, 164, 177, 224, 235`, `crates/ai/src/military.rs:294` (`seize_open_city`: an enemy city with only a Scout beside it is "open"), `crates/ai/src/campaign.rs:127`, `crates/ai/src/plan.rs:511`.
- **The exploit:** three Scouts for 180 Production are the fastest thing in the Ancient era. Shoot a city to 0 HP from range, walk a Scout in: the AI counted no threat from it, kept no garrison for it, and `reserve_front_guards` never fired. Also the Settler captures of finding 6.
- **Confidence:** certain for the exclusions; medium for how often a human uses Scouts as captors (the Settler case is common; the city case needs the shooter first).
- **Test:** fixture: our city at 0 HP, no garrison, an enemy Scout 3 hexes away at war, a free Spearman 2 hexes away. Expect the Spearman moved into the city. Unit test: `enemy_threat` of a Scout adjacent to an ungarrisoned 0-HP city equals the city's stake.
- **Fix:** stop excluding by kind; weight every unit by `threat_power` (a Scout's strength 5 is already small) and count any `charges` unit for the capture stake (`tactics.rs:150-166` already does for `enemy_threat`; the quotas and radii should read the same function).

### 12. Germany is gated out of war

- **Where:** stage-1 genes with Germany's defaults (`docs/sim-results/ai-quality/climb-program/gates.md`): `conquest_enabled` 0 (`params.rs:203`), `siege_exempt` 1 (`crates/ai/src/siege.rs:19-28`), `campaign_contain_only` 1 (`crates/ai/src/military.rs:2689`), `scout_front` 0, `border_raid` 1, `strike_eagerness` -20, `second_front_temperament` -10.
- **The exploit:** settle next to AI Germany. It never nominates a conquest city, never sieges, never scouts your cities, and declares only a containment war. Its 21% to 24% arena win rate (climb README) says the gates cost it, and the designer went 0 for 2 as Germany, so the human-seat targets are unreliable here.
- **Confidence:** high that the gates hold; medium that lifting them helps (Germany's slow machines were the stated reason; the 8.6 Germany map measurement was 30% without the siege and 2% with it, under an older AI).
- **Test:** climb round with Germany's `conquest_enabled`, `siege_exempt` and `campaign_contain_only` in the gene set; the arena gate decides. Also a war scenario "Germany attacker" (today it has none).
- **Fix:** these are already genes; the fix is to climb them rather than keep the faction defaults pinned. The slow-machine objection belongs in the siege forecast (one fewer move is a cost), not in a veto.

### 13. Defence stops at Walls and scales only with what is visible now

- **Where:** `crates/ai/src/threat.rs:1011-1013` (`if c.walls { continue }`: nothing more is bought for an exposed walled city), `crates/ai/src/production.rs:1221` (the defence branch runs only `&& !c.walls`), `crates/ai/src/military.rs:1515-1548` (`defend_cities` gathers `defend_margin_percent` of the visible attackers' weight and counts `city_power / 2` as defenders), `crates/ai/src/production.rs:2486` (emergency production only when visible threat exceeds `local_defense`).
- **The exploit:** bring Siege, or just more units than the AI sees at once (finding 5), to a walled city with one garrison. Once walled, the AI treats the city as finished and builds Granaries behind a 1-unit garrison; coach row "Defence doesn't scale with the army at the door, and answers melee with Archers". The designer takes walled cities with phalanx Hoplites in three turns.
- **Confidence:** high.
- **Test:** fixture: our walled city, one Archer garrison, six enemy units including a Siege unit within 4 hexes at war, 300 Gold. Expect a purchased melee defender or a second shooter. Sim metric: walled cities lost per siege by human proxies, against AI attackers.
- **Fix:** `defense_risk` (`purchase_defense.rs:252`) already forecasts whether a city falls; make the emergency purchase and the production choice read the forecast loss x `city_keep_value` for walled cities too, with the walls as a term in the forecast, not a terminal state. Gene: `walled_defense_percent` (0 keeps today's `continue`).

### 14. The AI throws away its own opening volley on the turn it declares

- **Where:** `crates/ai/src/scripted.rs:382-398` (`finish_logged`: every `RangedAttack` on a target not at War in this turn's view becomes `SkipUnit`, "ranged fire is validated against the original war state"); but the core has validated ranged and air orders against the same-turn surprise war since 2026-10-03 (`crates/core/src/rules/mod.rs:63-116`, commit e3b4b851d). `crates/ai/src/military.rs:544-557` (`press_strike` does order the shots with the declaration), `:558-562` (its melee waits for `teams_at_war`, so the declaration turn has no charge either).
- **The exploit:** none needed: every war the AI starts gives the defender a free turn. The human's surprise war lands shots and charges in the same resolution; the AI's lands nothing, moves into position, and the defender buys a shooter.
- **Confidence:** certain. This is a rule that changed under the AI.
- **Test:** unit test on `finish_logged`: a view at Peace with a legal declaration and a shooter in range of the target's unit; expect the `RangedAttack` kept. Sim: war scenarios, damage dealt on the declaration turn by AI attackers; threshold above 0.
- **Fix:** delete the filter (or keep it only for targets of teams the orders do not declare on), and let the melee branch at `:561` accept `declared` as `press_strike` does at `:515`. Not a gene.

### 15. The commitment genes exist and are off

- **Where:** `crates/ai/src/params.rs:337` (`leader_commit` 0), `:346` (`war_pressure` 0), `:345` (`war_garrison` 0), `:324` (`peace_retaliation` 0), `:263` (`settler_safety` 0), `:266` (`peace_scout_danger` 0). Each was built for a coach row ("Coalition wars without the army", "Peace priced at 0", "Settlers left unescorted") and left at 0 after a benchmark read.
- **The exploit:** the behaviours those rows describe are still the shipped behaviour.
- **Confidence:** certain that they are off; the benchmark deltas that left them off were small and noisy (the gene descriptions quote them), so the loss from leaving them off is unmeasured against a human who plays to the gap.
- **Test:** the climb program with these six in the gene set, gated as usual; and the war scenarios, which are where they should show.
- **Fix:** none in code; a decision for the climb director to include them in round 2's gene set rather than the stage-1 literals alone.

## Lower-ranked findings

16. **Ruins are a Scout's job only** (`crates/ai/src/exploration.rs:25-29`; `crates/ai/src/civilians.rs:911-917`; `crates/ai/src/military.rs:74` strips Scouts from the army and no military pass values a ruin; `crates/ai/src/analysis.rs:425-441` does put known ruins in the frontier, so the Scout at least knows). Codex `fix-ai-ignores-ruins` is on it. The fix should be a value term in `rest` and `garrison_cities` (a ruin within reach of an idle unit in safe land is 40 Gold or 25 Science for one move), not a Scout rule; and a `ruin_bonus` that scales with the reward, not a flat frontier discount.
