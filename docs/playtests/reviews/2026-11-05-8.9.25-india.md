## Coach's verdict

You won on T177, 15 VP to 3, 3, 2 and 1. It was not decided early: at T100 you had 3 VP to the Celts' 3, and the Mongols led you 8 to 5 at T147 with a 74% win chance on their own estimate. Hard playing your seat from T148 loses to the Mongols 57 of 64 times. What won it was a whole-game plan the AI does not play. You founded 22 cities (AI India founds 7.5 a game), walled each one first, and ran the whole empire on five military units from T60 to T150. You lost 2 units (one a Scout) and no city all game, while the Mongols and Byzantium were at War with you for 89 turns. You let corruption take 7 cities to zero output because they still counted as territory for three era objectives. You never built the Imperial Diwan. Then, first to Flight by 17 turns, you declared on the VP leader at T149 with Bombers and took its Great Works, Byzantium's Heir and the Mongol capital.

Your "India almost feels overpowered" and AI India's 8.7% are both true. The gap between them is mostly AI execution, plus a real drawback. AI India expands fine: it has the most cities at T100 of any faction. But it garrisons like everyone else, with a troop per city, an extra guard for every two linked cities and a 250% defensive margin, and pays Gold upkeep for all of them. India's remittance rule leaves it little Gold to pay with, so it trains 29 military units, upgrades 6, loses 3.6 cities a game (twice any other faction) and is eliminated in 17% of seats. With the same 8+ cities at T100 it wins 30%; other factions win 58%. Making Walls an Indian city's garrison, as you played it and as the Swiss playbook already does, takes AI India from 8.7% to 13.1% (held-out seed +3.3 points). Setting India's own extra guards to the shared values as well takes it to 14.7% (held-out seed 14.8%). That is the `india_playbook` gene in §4d. Rivals singling India out is not the cause (switching that targeting off changes nothing). The drawbacks are real: with full remittance AI India would win 17.6%, and without corruption 15.2%. You show they are playable, so treat them as design, not a bug.

The rest of his plan is already what AI India does (opening, research order, the linked core, Revenue Offices) or measured null or worse for the AI (territory towns past corruption, more Settlers, Walls first alone, upgrades first, the Diwan, free campaigns). It is the same lesson as the Swiss, Celtic and Aztec playbooks: the lever was a shared quota India's kit makes wrong, not his visible moves.

**Why the quota is wrong for India in particular.** The shared rule wants a troop per city (Hard: cities + 1 at Peace, cities + 5 at War), and India adds one per two linked cities and a 250% defensive margin. Every troop costs Gold upkeep. India's remittance rule leaves a provincial city with no Gold until it has an Office and half after, so the AI's troops eat the Gold that would upgrade them. K2 shows the size of that: with full remittance, AI India's upgrades double and it wins 17.6%. He paid 445 Gold of upkeep in 177 turns with five units behind Walls and spent the Gold on upgrades and one Bomber.

## 5. Was the win fair?

Skill the AI can learn, with one rules question.
- **Skill:** the expansion engine, the lean walled garrison, the upgrades, the timing of the one war and the choice of VP objects are all things Hard could do from its own view.
- **Rules question for you (not an AI fix):** a city at 100% corruption (14+ hexes from the capital) produces nothing but still counts as a city and as territory for the "Gain N cities" and "Gain N tiles" objectives. Seven such cities gave you most of three era VP. Is a corruption-dead town meant to score like a real one? Options that keep India's identity: a city's tiles count toward territory objectives only while it yields anything (drawback-side), or leave it as India's reward for spreading out.
- **Luck:** the start was ordinary for India (the widest-land rule placed you), and two neighbours declared on you at T41, which cost you nothing. The Celts' three-front collapse handed the Mongols their run. It nearly cost you the game.

## 5a. Smarter AI or a weaker leader?

- **Rubber bands:** decadence is off in 8.9.25 (slope 0) and there is no crown upkeep. Tech diffusion is the only band. Your techs saved each AI 534 to 594 Science (about 9% of its tech bill, one or two techs); you got 182 Science (2%).
- **Verdict:** about 0% bands, 100% AI decisions. Diffusion slightly helped the AIs and did not slow you. The AI-decision side is all in §4 and §4d: the Mongols' dead war and later Peace, no air defence, and AI India's own play in his seat.

## 6. Recommendations

Ranked by expected effect on how often he wins with any faction and on AI India's win share.

1. **Turn on `india_playbook` (§4d) for Hard India.** Mechanism: an Indian city's Walls are its garrison (level 1) and India's extra network guard and 250% defend margin go to the shared values (level 2); level 3 takes AI India from 8.7% to 14.7% of seats (+6.0 pp). Measure: AI-only census, India seats (paired), then the human-seat gate (seat-only and rivals-only; no benchmark map seats India, so say so).
2. **Never sign Peace with the strict VP leader near its target while at War with it.** `diplomacy.rs::wants_peace_why`: when `against` is the strict VP leader at 60% or more of its target, Peace needs the war to be lost, not just expensive. India-only it gave +7.3 pp [+1.8, +12.8] in his seat from T130 to T148 (192 paired runs) but nothing AI-only (−0.05 pp [−0.48, +0.37], with eliminations +2 points), so ship it only for the leader-race case (at War with the strict leader, it at 60%+ of its target, our own win chance above its) and gate it on his seat. It is the same pattern as 8.9.4 (the Mongols' Peace with Byzantium) and 8.9.15 (Peace offered during his hold). Gate: his T148 seat, the benchmark both ways.
3. **Answer the air stage from public information.** (a) `decisive_buy.rs::could_intercept`: a rival is a Fighter threat when it has flown, intercepted, or built hangars in view, not merely by being in the Modern era. Switching the era proxy off alone changed nothing in his seat (Hard India still prefers land campaigns), so this is a correctness fix, not a lever. (b) Defenders: when a rival at War, or the public VP leader, has Flight, a Fighter in each Great Work or capital city in Bomber range is worth the VP it protects (`air.rs`, `production.rs`). Ninth game with no Fighter before his Bombers landed. Measure: the war suite, the human-seat benchmark rivals-only.
4. **Rivals: no late war on a Flight-first leader you cannot answer in the air** (from §5b: a forced war at T148 is his best case). Fold into rec 3 (b): the war value of a Contain or Campaign on a rival with Flight subtracts the VP its Bombers can take from our Great Work and capital cities in range.
5. **Rules question for him:** do corruption-dead cities count for territory and city objectives (§5)?
