It ran 150 turns, which is the middle of your recent wins (T116 to T181), and it took 33 minutes of active play because you played fast: 13 seconds a turn, 143 automation orders and never more than 6 military units. ## 5. Was the win fair?

Mostly skill the AI can learn, helped by a good draw of neighbours:
- **Skill.** Hard in your seat from your own position wins 1 in 48 at T80 and 34 in 48 at T120, and from T130 wins at a median T188 against your T150. What you did in between (expansion at War, a growing treasury, units a generation ahead, refusing every peace and alliance that would have tied you down) is all learnable from the AI's own information.
- **The neighbours.** You drew the weakest AI empire next door (the Celts, who never attacked a city) while the strongest one (the Mongols) fought three other empires. That is luck, but the Mongols' choice of victims was the AI's own: the trace shows a war on you was its best plan on 23 turns.
- **No rules hole.** The Reserve worked as designed: capturing almost any of your cities during T141-T143 would have taken more than the 113 to 454 Gold above 5,000 you then held (the capture bounty is your treasury times the city's share of your population), and Lugano or Lugdunum would each have cost you 2 VP. Nobody tried.

## 5a. Smarter AI or a weaker leader?

There was almost nothing to split this time:
- **Decadence:** off in ordinary games since 2026-10-31.
- **Crowns:** gone since 2026-11-11.
- **Tech diffusion,** the one band left: your techs saved each AI 244 to 339 Science (6% to 12% of its research bill; Athens 46% in all, mostly from others). You got 339 from them (6% of your 5,636). At the AIs' 21 to 80 Science a turn that is 4 to 14 turns of research each, spread over 150 turns.

**Verdict:** about 5% of the game's closeness came from the rubber bands and about 95% from AI decisions and your play, with the uncertainty mostly in how to value diffusion (no knob turns it off). The closeness up to T120 was real: the Mongols were winning without you. What made it end at T150 rather than T200 was AI decisions, above all the Mongols never turning on you.

## 6. Coach's recommendations (ranked by expected effect on how often you win on Hard)

1. **Let the leader war through the one-front filter** (new idea #58). *Mechanism:* in `plan.rs::choose_plan` the `reachable_wars` retain removes a Contain whenever the AI ran a campaign against anyone else in the last 15 turns (`recent_other_campaign`, `runaway.rs::FRONT_TURNS`), is at War elsewhere without a funded forecast (`campaign_against`: "no second front"), or fails `runaway.rs::reachable_war_force`. A serial conqueror like these Mongols is never free of all three, so the leader is never attacked. Change: when a Contain or Campaign on the team that ranks as the top threat is the top-valued candidate by a margin (say 1.5× the best other plan) for N consecutive turns, (a) let it replace the current campaign (seek Peace with the current victim, which `diplomacy.rs` already prices) instead of being filtered, and (b) judge reach by march turns to the nearest known city (≤ 6), not units within 8 tiles today. *Gates:* fires on the Mongols at T134-T140 of this game (trace: Contain top on 7 consecutive turns, 121-405); scenario `mongols-turn-on-the-banker` (Hard 0/50 today); paired benchmark rivals-only, plus the dead-war count the gene was shipped to cut (Germany 8.9.43), so the fix does not bring back wars without an army. *Measured here:* Mongols with the whole gene off cut your recorded orders' win by T150 from 48 to 25 of 48 at T135.
2. **Don't count the Reserve as an army** (part of #58). `runaway.rs::reachable_war_force` adds `prospective` defenders bought from public Gold (558 to 819 here). For a Swiss leader whose Reserve is held or within one turn, only Gold above `SWISS_HOARD_GOLD` is spendable without losing 2 VP; count only that. Pure valuation, public information. *Gate:* the same trace turns plus the Swiss-target war fixtures in the war suite.
3. **Contest the hold with the city that breaks it** (extends #44). During a rival's hold, rank removable VP sources by whether losing them drops the holder below target: the Celts' committed operation went for Emain Macha (11 → 10, still holding) instead of Lugdunum (Hagia Sophia, 11 → 9) four tiles from Tara; and on T141-T143 any Swiss city captured would have pulled the Reserve under 5,000. Code: the "contest the hold" term in `plan.rs` (about line 2547) and target choice in `campaign.rs::choose_conquest_city` / `hold.rs`. *Gate:* fires at T141-T147 for the Celts; benchmark.
4. **Buy the strike, not the guard** (extends #56). His T130 purchase of two Cavalry for the Emain Macha strike against Hard's one Rifleman in Lucerne: **+31.2 pp [+11.8, +50.7]** (to T240, 48 runs); his army orders T126-T130 +24.8 pp. Code: `production::plan_buy` / `decisive_buy.rs` (a capture this turn or next of a capital or Great Work city should outrank a garrison buy in a city nobody threatens). *Gate:* fires at T130; window diff; benchmark seat-only.
5. **Cap the supply term** (idea #57, not built): it was 614 of the 1,157 that put the Mongols' T141 Aztec campaign above their war on you (602).
6. **Hard Switzerland banks at War** (extends #50, §4d).
