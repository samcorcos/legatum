## Coach's verdict

You won on T181, 11 VP to the Celts' 8. It was the closest finish in two weeks, and for the first time an AI broke one of your holds: the Celts took the Strongest Army crown on T170 and dropped you from 10 to 9.

**Your read on the Celts was right, and the numbers back it.** They closed their two old wars (Athens T104, Germany T125) and declared on you on T130, the same turn as Switzerland. From then on they made all 19 attacks on your cities after T66 and sacked five of your towns. Without their war, Hard playing your seat from T125 wins about 22 turns sooner (median T173 against T195). From T115 it wins 31 of 32 instead of 24.

**But it was too little to beat you, for three reasons:**
- **The Celts lost sight of you.** After burning Chalco (T138) and Tlatelolco (T148), the only Aztec towns they had ever seen, their planner knew no Aztec city at all. A war it can't aim was worth −260, so for 17 turns (T149–T165) their plan was "Peace with the Aztecs". That window was exactly when your army was down south eating Switzerland.
- **Athens never saw an Aztec city in 181 turns.** It had the map's second-biggest army (2,152) and named you its top threat from T125 to the end, yet its war on you was worth exactly 0 every turn.
- **The rest were armies in name only.** Switzerland, Germany and Byzantium were at War with you for 36, 55 and 98 turns. Between them they killed 2 of your units and never hit one of your cities. Their armies were Spearmen and Archers in the Industrial era, because their era resources went into Banks and Forges, or they had none.

**What decided it:** eliminating Sparta (T122–T124) while Switzerland was at Peace with you. Hard in your seat wins 24 of 32 from T115 and 32 of 32 from T125.

**What would most likely have beaten you:**
- **The old decadence rule (removed on 10-31).** It is the biggest single lever: Hard in your seat from T115 falls from 24 to 13 of 32.
- **Among AI decisions:** all five rivals at War with you from T100, never making Peace. That cuts Hard in your seat from 18 to 12 of 32, and your own recorded orders stop winning.

- **Decadence.** In this game it was the largest single brake on you by far: from T115, Hard in your seat 24 → 13 of 32 with it on. Since 10-31 nothing slows a leader except the AIs' decisions, and those were worth about 7 points here. This is reported for pillar 11, not as a recommendation to bring it back.
- **Should a hopeless AI fight the leader?** Rec 2 makes a 1-VP Athens attack you to stop your win even though it can't win. Human players do that; a strict "maximize my own win" AI does not.
- **The Celtic sack.** With today's AI the Celts fight well, but the rule that burns every captured city cost them this game. Keeping the cities from T125 makes them win 7 of 32. Faction balance gets one line only: you decided the sack stays.
