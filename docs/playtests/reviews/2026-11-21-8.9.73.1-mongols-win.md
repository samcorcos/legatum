# Mongols on Hard, playtest 8.9.73.1 (rules 46): won T109, 10 VP to 1

**Verdict.** This was the fastest win in the playtest record: 10 VP on T100, held to T109, in 37 minutes of play. Pillar 11 failed outright. You won with a small empire (3 Settlers trained, one taken) and a horse army that was 2 to 4 times anyone else's from T40. Two AI failures made it easy:
- the Swiss never explored, so they never settled, and their one city held the Pyramids;
- a four-empire coalition was at war with you for 25 turns and never attacked one of your cities.

## 2. Turning points
1. **T7: the Swiss Scout goes on sentry beside your capital and stays there until T63.** The Swiss never sent another unit exploring, so they never saw a city site and never trained a Settler. Their settler gate read `sites_ok: false` every turn. They stayed at one city for 71 turns and spent the time on the Pyramids (T48).
2. **T40 to T61: your army grew at Peace while nobody armed.** The Swiss threat log read "army 231 near us" on T61. Their plan stayed Economy for the whole game (they never changed plan once), they had 219 Gold unspent, and they bought one Soldier after you declared.
3. **T71: Geneva.** One siege gave you 3 VP (the capital and the Pyramids), the Library era objective and an elimination.
4. **T84 to T109: the coalition that never fought.** Four empires were at War with you, with about 50 military units between them against your 6. In 25 turns they made 24 attacks on your units: the Celts 17, Germany 5, Egypt 1, Byzantium 0. Your units made 87 attacks. They lost about 35 units and attacked none of your cities. On T100 to T105, Geneva (3 VP, no Walls, no garrison) and Bukhara (Hagia Sophia, 2 VP, walled, no garrison) had coalition units within 1 to 4 hexes, and capturing either one would have broken your 10-of-10 hold.

## 3. Your edge
- **A small empire with a big army.** You trained 3 Settlers and built 7 Cataphracts or Keshigs by T56. You had 5 cities at T50; the AIs had 3 to 5.
- **Targets with points in them.** Your first victim was the one-city empire that held a Great Work. Your second was a capital. Five of your 10 VP came from two captures.
- **Few units, used on the front.** You held 7 cities with 6 military units during the hold, and 4 of the 7 cities had no unit inside.

## 4. The AI empires
- **Switzerland, F.** Its strategy was effectively none: 0 Settlers, no plan change in 71 turns, and the only Scout parked on sentry duty (`civilians.rs::post_sentry`; you are a rush-capable neighbour by your public rules).
- **Egypt, B then D.** It expanded best (9 cities) and sniped Settlers twice. It joined the coalition by Alliance with 11 to 16 units and made 1 attack. Its plan layer gated every Campaign on your VP cities with "funded forecast unavailable". Its open-city strike saw Geneva and Bukhara as "not currently visible", then as "walls still fire".
- **Germany, C.** It declared first (T16, a Settler snipe), then declared on you on T84 ("exposed leader city" +380) and made 5 attacks.
- **The Celts, C.** It was the only empire that took a city back (Bibracte T87), then massed 8 to 11 Soldiers around walled Bibracte during the hold.
- **Byzantium, D.** It joined by Alliance and made no attacks.

## 5. Was it fair?
Yes. No rules hole was involved beyond the two siege-order issues, which have their own fix. The win came from AI decisions the AI can learn to avoid: never exploring, not arming against a visible army at Peace, and a coalition that would not commit to a siege.

Your own recorded orders cannot measure an opening change. Once the Swiss Scout moves differently, your T15 Settler's order no longer applies and your seat collapses to one city. That is why the table uses Hard in your seat.

**Not fixed, diagnosed (the next stream): the coalition does not contest a hold.** From your T96 position, your own orders win by T110 in 8 of 8 runs on the newest AI (the Mac's 43de37f59, with leader war and the siege clock), and also under each of seven existing settings: `min_seat_war_odds` 5 and 0, `march_turns` 10 and 12, `coalition_own_read`, `late_capture_value` 200, `siege_stance` 12, and wider open-city radii. The causes:
- the plan layer has no funded siege forecast for any of your VP cities;
- the open-city strike never sees Geneva, and refuses walled Bukhara while its walls still fire;
- `contest.rs` (gene `contest_hold`) removes only Celtic camps. It leaves VP cities to the campaign valuations, and those never fire.

Proposed fix: extend the contest pass to a VP city whose capture takes the holder below target, with one joint force drawn from the coalition's units already in reach.
