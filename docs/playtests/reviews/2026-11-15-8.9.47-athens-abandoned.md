# Athens on Hard, Playtest 8.9.47 (rules 39): abandoned at turn 89

Cities and army strength by turn (archived outside the repository)

**Coach's verdict.** Sparta picked you fairly: on its own view your second city, Athens, was the nearest unwalled rival city it could reach (7 hexes), and Germany's capital came first until Germany walled it. You lost Athens on T40 because you walked its only garrison out on the turn three Hoplites stormed it; kept in, the city survives that turn in 64 of 64 replays. The war did not pay for Sparta. It razed the city instead of keeping it, sat on one city until T66, and is 5th of 6 on cities, army and Science at T89. Played on by Hard to T300, Sparta wins 1 of 24 games and you are eliminated in 21. That is a kamikaze with a fair target: mutual ruin, not a competitive rush. Hard in your seat from T30 does no better (it loses a city in 36 of 36 runs), because Hard does not use the new supported defence either. You quit at T89, one turn after the Celts declared and razed the city you had just refounded, not at about T65.

Evidence: the archived tape `web-1794773458810-…` (the backup `autosave.legatum` is a new game at turn 1). It replays at `playtest-8.9.47` with 0 hash mismatches and 0 AI-order mismatches (1,684 AI seat-turns). Seats: you (Athens), the Aztecs, Switzerland, the Celts, Sparta and Germany, all Hard. Recorded play time: 13.5 minutes for 89 turns in one session.

## 1. Why Sparta picked you at T36

The war is declared in Sparta's T36 orders, so you saw it on T37. Sparta's plan trace for T32–T36 (`plan.rs::choose_plan` and `conquest.rs::choose_conquest_city`, logged by a throwaway env-gated `eprintln!`):

| Turn | Conquest nominees (value / cost; lower cost per value wins) | Plan |
|---|---|---|
| T23–T31 | Germany's capital Essen (path 10, ratio 64–90) and your Athens (path 9, ratio 66–116) | Economy |
| T32 | Essen 340/218 = **64**; Athens 321/326 = 101 | **Campaign on Germany (Essen)**; declares on Germany |
| T33 | Essen is dropped (walled, and no forecast says the siege takes it); Athens 381/232 = 60 | Switches to Campaign on you: value 618 |
| T36 | Athens 595/232 = **38** (the only nominee) | Campaign v1,158, so it declares |

The T36 value terms: capture at 30% +1,047, Athenian technology +130, early campaign window +80, era deficit −40, second front −37 (it was already at war with Germany), aggressor risk −18, failed siege −4. The only alternative on offer was Peace with Germany at 0.

**On the merits:**
- **Nearest:** yes. Athens was 7 hexes from Sellasia, the nearest rival city; the next were Essen at 9 and your capital Laurion and Tara at 11.
- **Weakest army:** no. Yours was second (75 to Sparta's 89; Germany 41, the Aztecs 55, the Swiss and Celts 21). But the walled cities (Essen, Tara, Berlin, Zug, Chalco) are skipped before T120 unless a forecast takes them, and Athens was the only unwalled city in reach with a Spearman or two around it.
- **Best prize:** your city was pop 4, the biggest within 11 hexes, and your faction's drawback adds a free technology for its first captor (+130).

**Does any term depend on the seat being human?** No. I replayed the game with every seat flagged as an AI in each AI's view. Sparta's T33 switch and T36 declaration are identical. Only seven diplomatic-offer timings changed:
- three were Germany's Alliance offers (T41–T43);
- three were Sparta's Peace offers (T60–T62), which came on T63 instead.

They move because of `diplomacy.rs::offer_slot_open`, which lets a human team receive one new AI offer per turn. It is a pacing rule, not a target term. One human-dependent term does exist elsewhere: `civilians.rs` uses an aggression of 15 for a human owner when it prices Peace after a Settler capture. It did not act in this war.

**What Sparta lost:**

This expansion failure predates the war: your Scouts and Spearmen stood 2–4 tiles from Sellasia from T15. It is untraced; the likely cause is the threatened gate on the Settler.

**Played on to T300** from his last turn (T89), with every seat Hard including yours (24 runs, dice reseeded; `seat_run`):
- **Winners:** Germany 10, the Celts 7, the Aztecs 6, Sparta 1.

**Verdict: mutual ruin.** By his own test, the target was fair, but Sparta did not come out able to compete. It spent its opening on a war it could not convert: it razed instead of keeping, then fought Germany for 57 turns and the Celts for 14 with one city. This is the existing "AI Sparta can't convert a rush into cities" pattern, now with a second cause: a one-city Sparta that never expands.

## 3. The Aztecs

**Did they declare?** Not in his game; their plan was Economy from T25 to T89. In the 24 continuations they declared on you in 11. Seven of those came within 20 turns (T93, T93, T93, T94, T99, T101, T109), including the run on the recorded dice (T93). They took a city by T109 in only 1 of 24 runs (6 cities over all 24 games).

**Their valuation at T89:**
- **The plan:** Contain the Celts +35 ("leader's race" +211, "war footing" −100, "attrition" −76) against Economy 0. There is no plan against you.
- **Conquest nominees:** your Piraeus was excluded by `conquest.rs::assessed_conquest_allowed`. Before T120 (`adaptation::early`) a walled city above a third of its HP is a target only when a campaign forecast says the siege takes it. Piraeus was walled, at 200 HP, held by one Composite Archer, 5 hexes from the nearest Aztec city.
- **Laurion is not among their known cities at all.** Your walled capital, **empty**, is 8 hexes away, and the Aztecs never scouted it.

**The term that stops them:** the early-walls gate with no forecast, behind a picture that is missing your capital.

**A valuation error?** Partly:
- Against Piraeus alone the gate is roughly right in outcome. Their army at T89 was 6 Spearmen, 2 Crossbowmen, a Pikeman and an Archer, with no Siege, and in the runs where they declared they almost never took a city quickly.
- The real gap is intel. A neighbour with an army of 327 never looked at the empty walled capital 8 hexes away.
- You were right that you were prey; the predator was another empire. The Celts (army 348) declared on T87 and razed your new Athens on T88, valuing your city at +158 for the Athenian technology. In the continuations Germany, not the Aztecs, finishes you.

## 4. Why you lost the war

**Armies:**
- **You:** 3 military units at T30–T36 (a Horseman and two Spearmen), 4 by T37, 5 at the peak (T46–T48); army strength 75–93 against Sparta's 85–99.
- **Your Gold:** 210–222, then 164–173 after a 60-Gold Phalangite upgrade on T37. You bought nothing.
- **Sparta:** three Hoplites (phalanx +5 next to each other, level 1) at Athens, two more behind, a Ranger from T44, and 17–37 Gold all war.

**The fights:**
1. **T37–T39:** your Horseman and Spearman attacked Hoplites in the open. They dealt 21–24 a hit and took 30–51 back.
2. **T40, the decisive turn:** your Spearman #35 was Athens' garrison at full HP. You ordered it out to (6,14). The city, at 116 HP, fell to three Hoplite hits that turn.
   - **Replay:** I replayed T40 with the Spearman kept in (a sleep order, 64 dice seeds; `coach_t40`). Athens is still yours after T40 in **64 of 64**, and still yours through T42 in 64 of 64. Sparta's T41–T42 orders are its taped ones, so read T41–T42 as an upper bound.
3. **T48:** you put Athens to 0 HP with an Archer and three melee hits, but its Hoplite garrison lived, so there was no capture. Sparta razed it the next turn.
4. **T50–T53:** you lost five units fighting beside the ruins (2 kills to 5 losses).

**Hard in your seat from T30, T34 and T36** (12 runs each, to T100):
- it loses a city in **36 of 36** runs and has 2 cities at T64 in 32 (3 in the other four), as you did;
- in the six traced runs Athens falls on T39–T41;
- its Gold buys tile improvements (a Quarry, a Farm, a Mine) and one or two Phalangite upgrades, never a unit;
- it garrisons Athens with a Horseman (T34 run) or a wounded Phalangite, with its other units 2–3 tiles away and none stepping in as supporters;
- it signs Peace with Sparta around T74–T86 (one run in 12 accepts the T48 offer), so your refusal on T49 matched Hard.

**Your first coach question:** "Hard would have bought on 8 of them (about 1,020 Gold)" cannot be true.
- **The arithmetic:** the window rule (`why.rs::window`) adds up what a shadow Hard would buy each turn from *your unspent treasury*. With 164–173 Gold every turn, it counts about 127 a turn eight times. Real Hard has 164 + roughly 2 a turn to spend in that window, about 185 Gold: one Spearman at most (the Aztecs paid 180 for one on T36).
- **The purchases:** played for real, Hard spends the window on improvements and upgrades, not on the war.

## 5. Supported defence (rules 39)

**It decided nothing in this war, because it never happened.** The game logged no `CityReinforced` event, and no unit moved into an attacked city. The one turn it could have mattered was T40, and there you stepped the garrison out instead.

**What it would have done, counted:** with Spearman #35 kept in and Spearman #46 (one tile away) moving in as a supporter, the three Hoplites took 19 and 18 counter-damage instead of 8 and 8. Adding Phalangite #3 as a second supporter made it 28 and 28. The city holds 64 of 64 either way, and Sparta's storm costs it about three times the HP.

**The AI does not use it at all:** no file in `crates/ai` mentions supporters. Hard in your seat left its two Phalangites 2–3 tiles from the attacked city.

## 6. Coach-question quality

The game asked two questions in 89 turns, both in the answers file. The save wrapper's `why.asked` keys for this game are gone: the backup and live autosaves both hold later games.

**T46 (window, "no purchases in 10 war turns"): wrong.**
- The premise "a city of yours has been threatened and you bought nothing" was fair.
- The numbers were not: it summed per-turn shadow purchases from one unspent treasury (1,020 Gold against about 185 available).
- It did not check what Hard actually buys. Played out, that is improvements and upgrades.
- **Fix:** simulate the shadow's spending against a running treasury, and show what Hard would have bought (unit kinds), not only Gold.

**T64 (war_peace, "Hard would not have made peace"): wrong.** It is the known bug, already fixed on the playtest line: Hard's own plan that turn was Peace with Sparta.

**The good questions it missed:**
- **T40:** "you moved your only garrison out of Athens with three Hoplites adjacent; why?" This was the decisive move, and no category covers unit moves.
- **T37–T39:** attacking phalanx Hoplites in the open at 22–26 strength against 30–35.

Your T49 refusal of Sparta's Peace was correctly not asked: Hard refused it too.

## Ranked proposals for Hard (for the lead; nothing implemented)

1. **Supported defence in the AI** (the largest effect here, and the reason Hard in his seat loses the same city 36 of 36):
   - When an enemy melee army is adjacent to an own city, keep the garrison in.
   - Step adjacent non-ranged units in as supporters; prefer the unit with the best soak as garrison, never a Horseman against Hoplites.
   - Where: `crates/ai/src/hard_defense.rs` and the garrison and movement passes in `tactics.rs`.
   - Measure: the T40 position from this game as a fixture (the city holds 64 of 64 when the garrison stays; Hard's own play from T36 should hold Athens to T45), plus the war-scenario suite and the `supported-defence-hold` Lab card baseline.
2. **Fix the window question's arithmetic and content** (`crates/ai/src/why.rs::window` and `record`).
   - Carry a running shadow treasury across the window.
   - Report the item kinds.
   - Do not ask when Hard's purchases are improvements.
   - Add a "garrison left a city under assault" question (a unit-move category), the one question this game needed.
3. **A one-city Sparta must expand or not declare.**
   - Sparta declared a second war at T36 with one city and a Settler-less build since T1, and razed its only capture.
   - Make the Campaign pay for "keep the city", and price a war with 1 city against expansion: an expansion-deficit term in `price_campaign`, or Sparta's Settler gate in `production.rs`.
   - Measure: Sparta's AI-only win share and cities at T60, against the census.
4. **Scout the neighbour you could take.**
   - The Aztecs (army 327) never saw his empty capital 8 hexes away. A Hard empire with a big idle army should refresh intel on the nearest rival cities (`intel.rs`) before the conquest gate prices them.
   - This is the "blind to the leader" pattern, extended to the weakest neighbour.
   - Fair: it is exploration, not map-hack.
5. **Design question for the designer, not an AI fix.** The early-walls gate (`adaptation::early`, T120) makes every walled city off-limits without a forecast, so a ruined two-city Athens with walls was safe from the Aztecs for 30+ turns. That fits pillar 11 and is arguably right. Keep it unless he wants punished collapses earlier.
