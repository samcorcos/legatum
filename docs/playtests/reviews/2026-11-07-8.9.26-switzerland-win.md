**What the AIs never did:** in 144 turns they hit your cities 5 times (all India, on Biel, T132–T134) and took one city, which you took back. Your cities stood empty with 2+ enemy units within 6 tiles for **111 city-turns while at War**, and only Biel was punished. At T135 your treasury was **5,065 Gold, 65 above the Reserve's 5,000**: one more Swiss city lost in the next turns would have cost you 2 VP and the hold.

**India's Ancient army was resource starvation plus an AI loop.** India was not behind in technology (first into the Renaissance, T85; Modern at T139, 9 turns after you). It had **no Niter for the whole Renaissance era**: you claimed both Niter deposits near it (Biel T60, Bern T69); the Celts, Athens and the Aztecs took the others. Every Renaissance and later land unit needs its era's resource, and its one Iron went to two Forges, so the only units it could start were Spearmen, Archers and Horsemen. Then the AI's army quota discounts obsolete units to a fraction of a unit (`modernization_value`: a Spearman counts as about a tenth of a unit against Riflemen), so the quota never filled and India kept building the same obsolete units: **31 Ancient units after T85; 23 of its 24 military units at T130 were Ancient, with 22 Gold of upkeep leaving it 1 Gold a turn.** When India finally went to War for Niter (T130, "supply" 7,560 of a 9,738 plan value) it took Biel in 5 turns. That war was right and 45 turns late.

**Was it fair?** Mostly skill the AI can learn, on a good map. Hard from T2 wins 27% in your seat, about 1.6 times the fair share, while AI Switzerland wins about half its fair share in the census (6.8% a seat in 8-empire games), so this start was kind to Switzerland. The rest was your economy (12 cities, 3 soldiers, interest) against AIs that never attacked it. No exploit or rule hole decided it; one rule question (the resource-starved empire, §2) and one rule detail (your recapture of Biel scored "Capture 2 enemy cities", worth your 11th VP, which you didn't need) are below.

**Smarter AI or weaker leader:** 0% rubber bands (decadence off, no crown upkeep; diffusion a wash: your techs saved each AI 210–271 Science, 5–7% of its bill, and you got 742). All of it was AI decisions.

## 2. India's Ancient army (your question)

**What India built.** After entering the Renaissance (T85): 16 Spearmen (one bought), 10 Archers and 5 Horsemen, then 4 Cavalry once it had Coal (T126 on) and one Crossbowman (T130). Its only upgrades were early (Cataphract T44, Phalangite T69, Knight T84); none after T84. At T115 it had 14 Ancient units of 15, at T130 23 of 24.

**Its technology.** Not the cause. India entered the Renaissance first of all six (T85), the Industrial era T111 (you T109) and the Modern era T139 (you T130); it was the most advanced AI all game (25 techs at the end, you 28).

**Its strategic resources** (deposits inside its borders, from the `ResourcesRevealed` and `SupplyShortage` events and the final map):

| Resource (era) | What India had | Who held the nearest deposits |
|---|---|---|
| Iron (Classical, Medieval) | 1 deposit at Allahabad, improved: 3 supply, used by Forges in Ajmer (T94) and Lahore (T121) and one old unit | |
| **Niter (Renaissance)** | **none from T81 (reveal) to T135** | **you**: Biel (T60, 2 tiles from Varanasi) and Bern (T69); the Celts (Lugdunum, next to Jaunpur); Athens (Laurion); the Aztecs (two near Fatehpur Sikri) |
| Coal (Industrial) | 1 deposit at Jaunpur from T97: 2–3 supply, hence the Cavalry | |
| Oil (Modern) | 2 deposits from T133–T134 | |

So from T85 every Musketman, Arquebusier and Cuirassier, and every upgrade into them, was blocked; Medieval units needed spare Iron its Forges were using. The rule is in `.claude/skills/legatum-rules/references/game-rules.md` §3 (`resources::unit_need`, `upgrade_need`): every land unit from the Classical era on uses one of its era's resource, and only Spearmen, Archers and Horsemen need nothing. Upgrades need the final unit's resource too, so nothing could be modernised.

**Why it kept building them: an AI loop, not just starvation.** `crates/ai/src/production.rs` drops every option with a `resource_blocker`, so the Ancient units were the only units on India's list. That alone would only mean "build fewer, older units". The loop is the army tally under `modernization_value` (default 100, `production.rs` around line 2680; `modernization.rs::modernization_percent`): each existing unit counts as its squared strength against the enemy units it sees, so a Spearman next to Riflemen counts as roughly 0.1 of a unit. The quota (`desired_army`) is therefore never met, the only buildable answer is another Spearman, which also counts as 0.1, and India keeps paying for them: upkeep 5 Gold at T85, 22 at T130, net income 1 Gold a turn. `composition::obsolete` never marks a melee-line unit obsolete ("the better one needs Iron"), and its Archer and Horseman check is only applied when `modernization_value` is 0. About 3,000 Production went into these units after T85, about 430 Production for each of its seven cities.

**What a resource-starved empire can do in Legatum.** Settle or capture a deposit, and nothing else: there is no resource trade, stockpile or market (rules §9), Byzantine Companies are Byzantium only, and an upgrade needs the target's resource. So yes, **without a deposit an empire is stuck with era-I land units forever**, and Industrial siege built into units never reaches it. India's one real fix was right: on T130 its plan valued the war on you for Biel at 9,738, of which "supply" was 7,560, and it took Biel in 5 turns (T135). The supply term had fired before (T103 against Athens, 2,640, dropped as infeasible in 2 turns); it was never pointed at the Niter next door while India sat at Peace with you T105–T129. In Civ 6, by contrast, a resource-starved civ still has resource-free lines (Pikeman, Crossbowman and later Field Cannon in most rule sets), so it falls behind but never back to Warriors.

This is a **design question for you**, not an AI fix: you want the "rush to find the next era's resource" (2026-11-07) and unfair deposits, and that is what made India's army useless. Options, strongest case against each first:
1. **Leave it.** Against: a loser of the resource rush is not just weaker, it is out of the war game for 50+ turns, and that read as "the AIs are bad" to you, not "India lost the Niter race".
2. **Let an empire start a unit it lacks the resource for; it is born idle** (the existing unsupplied rule, −25% strength, "No Niter supply" line). A Musketman at −25% (33) is still worth 1.7 Spearmen. Against: it weakens resources as a reason for war, which you asked for on 10-19; a −40% or −50% born-idle penalty keeps more of the pull.
3. **A resource-free levy line per era** (Civ 6's Pikeman model). Against: one more unit per era to explain, against pillar 1.

My lean is (2) at a steeper penalty, but your call.
