## 4e. The counter: how a rival beats a Mongol army played your way

What hurt you in this game, and what a rival should do:
1. **Have an army before T40.** Every AI had 1 to 3 units when your 5 riders arrived. Three Spearmen around a city (melee beats mounted, +40%) would have made each charge into a city cost you a Keshig. Your Keshigs avoid this by shooting, but someone still has to ride in to take the city.
2. **Walls before the Mongols reach the Medieval era.** Walls halve every attack without Siege, and the Mongols cannot buy Siege until the Medieval era (your T56). Walled Hamburg took 10 hits from three units, unwalled Dyrrachium 6.
3. **Sally against Keshigs at range 2.** A shooting Keshig stands two tiles out with no terrain defence (a Mongol drawback). A Spearman or Pikeman that steps out and charges it gets +40% and full damage. Archers are the wrong answer: a Keshig defends as mounted against arrows and gets the mounted bonus.
4. **Hit his empty cities.** At T75 and T80 you had 3 to 4 cities with no defender, enemies within 6 tiles, and no Walls before T63. The Mongols cannot build Walls before the Medieval era. A rival at War should take an empty Mongol town while the horde is ten tiles away (the `byzantium-empty-horde` scenario).
5. **The strongest neighbour must commit.** The Celts' 12 military units, including 3 Cataphracts, were the only force that could stop you. They stayed on an Economy plan for 83 turns.
6. **Don't go broke.** India's remittance left it with 4 Gold and no army: it disbanded its last unit on T52, three turns before you declared.

The rival decision diff agrees: India joining Sparta's war on T44 (−37.8 pp for you) and one more Byzantine Spearman on T35 (−14.8 pp) were the rivals' best single choices.

Keep the rule, teach the AI to play it and to counter it, then re-ablate. If the horde AI then wins well above its share, price the Keshig (Production or a second Iron); don't cut its bow (pillar 8).
