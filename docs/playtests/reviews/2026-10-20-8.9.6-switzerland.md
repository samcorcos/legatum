## Coach's verdict

Japan was the biggest empire all game, but it was never as far ahead on points as it looked. It had:
- 7 cities at T50, when nobody else had more than 2;
- the largest population from T40 (90 at T200 against 55 for the next);
- the strongest army by T200.

On VP it had 2 at T150 to your 3. It led on VP only from T159 to T219, and peaked at 9 for four turns (T199–T202).

**Why it didn't convert is mostly the rubber band.** Japan's lead in Power (it counts Food, and Japan's whole faction is a Food and Production network) put its decadence at:
- 55 to 63% around T50–T75;
- 47% at T100;
- 41% at T150;
- 35% at T200.

In all, Japan lost 39% of everything it made: 13,657 Production, 2,015 Gold and 3,598 Science. You lost 10%.

Switch decadence off and Japan wins nearly every time:
- 30 of 30 from T100, by about T195, with its own strategy;
- 24 to 29 of 30 from T150;
- 28 of 30 even from T196.

None of the "different strategies" I gave Japan under the bands made it win more than 4 of 30. Those were: push its closest VP path, contain you, close the country earlier, and the two combined. The one exception: containing you from T150, on your own orders, won Japan 11 of 30.

So your hunch is half right: Japan should have won, and it would have if the band didn't eat 40% of its economy for 190 turns. It wasn't a better strategy that it lacked. It lacked:
- **a closer:** at 9 VP it was 2 tiles of territory, or 3 Factories, short of 10;
- **a defended vow:** it refused Athens's peace offer at T192, then lost Sakoku when Athens, Germany and Sparta marched in, and Sparta took Himeji at T203.

**The Mongol attack at T179 was smart on purpose, but not because you were winning.** Their plan targeted Lugano while Sparta held it (T176). When you took it first, the target was gone (T177). They switched to a Byzantine city, found it infeasible, and re-aimed at Lugano in your hands (T179). Their siege forecast saw what was public:
- **the city:** a freshly captured city at 0 HP;
- **your garrison:** a Rifleman down to 36 HP and a Field Cannon at 25;
- **the prize:** a capture bounty from your treasury (316 Gold).

They took it at T181. Their threat log never named you the leader, so it was opportunism by value scoring, which is what we want to keep.

## 4. Why Japan didn't convert (the AI's mistakes, and the band)
**The band (rules, not AI):**
- **How decadence works:** it is 0.45 × Japan's lead over the mean Power. Power counts Food, Production, Gold and Science, and Japan's faction rule adds Food and Production, so its own signature keeps the lead high.
- **What it cost:** 39% of all output over 244 turns, against your 10%.
- **What the VP were:** with the bands off, Japan wins with three crowns (Largest Economy in 30 of 30 games, Most Advanced 25–29, Strongest Army 27–30), on top of the eras and its Great Work. With the bands on, it rarely holds any crown, because net yields set Largest Economy and Most Advanced.
- **No cap:** the decadence rule has no cap. You rejected one (`legatum-rules`, overreach v3), so a 60% rate is the rule working as written.

**Strategic AI errors:**
- **One point short, no closer** (the 8.9.4 Byzantium pattern again). At T199–T202 Japan sat at 9 with these open:
  - Industrial: 141 of 143 tiles, and 0 of 3 Factories;
  - Future (not yet entered): 11 of 12 cities;
  - two crowns held by others.

  Taking any one Swiss or Athenian city would have added the tiles; Japan had the strongest army. `crowns.rs::objective_effort` doesn't look for a capture that completes a territory count.
- **A vow it couldn't hold:** Japan closed at T165, which the value scoring allows at 55% of the game. The vow scored only for T198–T201. The countdown invited every rival's denial (`sakoku_denial_value` adds 100 to a Japanese city's capture value), and Japan refused Athens's peace offer at T192, six turns before the vow would score. It then asked for peace 6 times (T192–T206) and got it only after the vow was lost.
- **Weak defence while closed:** Sparta, with an army of 145, took Himeji from Japan (398) at T203.
- **Wars on the wrong rival:** Japan's six campaigns from T80 were all against Athens, most ending `insufficient_army` or "abandons a declared war". It never marched on you, although you led on VP at T150 with 4 military units and 2,657 Gold.

**Tactical:** from T135 Japan opened 5 campaigns on Athenian cities at 30–44% capture odds. Four ended within 1–4 turns; the fifth took Laurion at T195.

**What the other AIs did right (keep):** the denial war on a closed Japan near the target (Athens, Germany, Sparta, T190–T200). That's the "contest the hold" behaviour the playbook asked for, applied to a vow.

## 6. Recommendations (ranked by effect on how often you win on Hard)
1. **Rule question for you, not an AI fix: should a Power lead with no VP lead be taxed at 35–63% for 190 turns?** Japan paid 39% of its output while holding 2–4 VP; turning decadence off from any turn T100–T196 makes Japan win 24–30 of 30. A cap was rejected, so the options are elsewhere:
   - **(a)** measure Power without Food, so Japan's network isn't taxed for being Japan's network;
   - **(b)** scale the rate by the empire's VP standing as well as its Power;
   - **(c)** keep it. Then Japan's faction needs a different VP path that doesn't run through crowns.
2. **Convert a lead: a closer for an empire one point short** (extends ideas #26).
   - **The change:** rank every open objective by turns to finish, including a capture that completes a territory or city count. Japan was 2 tiles short with the strongest army, and any Swiss or Athenian city would have given them.
   - **Where:** `crates/ai/src/crowns.rs::objective_effort`, `chosen_objective`, `endgame_push`; `conquest.rs` target value with the VP as its prize.
   - **Measure:** Scenario Lab `japan-big-empire` (Hard +1 VP median in 30 turns) and `byzantium-last-point` (Hard 14%); the human-seat benchmark opponents-only.
3. **Hold the vow you close.**
   - **(a) Accept peace while the vow is the closing VP.** A peace treaty turns a rival's units into non-intruders. Japan refused Athens's peace six turns before scoring.
   - **(b) Price the expected denial wars when deciding to close.** Rivals' `sakoku_denial_value` is public logic.
   - **(c) Garrison the border cities for the countdown.**
   - **Where:** `crates/ai/src/goal_sakoku.rs` (close/reopen value), the diplomacy peace response.
   - **Measure:** a T190 branch where Japan accepts peace, plus `japan-big-empire`.
4. **Contain the VP leader earlier when we out-arm it.** Let `contain_leader_percent` fall with the public army ratio. You led at 3 VP with 4 military units and 2,657 Gold, and Japan had an army of 220 against your 71. Evidence: T150 on your orders, 19 → 0 of 30.
   - **Where:** `plan.rs::contain_candidates`.
   - **Measure:** the human-seat benchmark opponents-only, and this game's T150 branches.
   - **Risk:** it crowned Athens more often than Japan.
5. **Keep the Mongol behaviour** (section 5c) and add the war scenario.
6. **For the lead:** `scenarios/lab/hold-athens.json` and `hold-therapne.json` still wait on "walls halve damage", which landed in 8.9.6.
