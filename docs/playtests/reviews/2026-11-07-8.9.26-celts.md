Playtest 8.9.26 (`playtest-8.9.26`, engine `514dbb8fe`, rules version 19). Six empires on a 48x32 map, every AI on Hard: Byzantium, Athens, Switzerland, Sparta, Germany. You won on turn 135 with 13 VP; Athens and Byzantium had 4. 25 active minutes in one session (0.1 minutes a turn to the Medieval era, 0.3 in the Industrial and Modern eras). ## Coach's verdict

You were right that it was easier than Sparta, and both of your readings hold in part. The AI made it easy, and the Celtic kit made it cheap.

The AI side: you founded 16 towns and kept 3 or 4 military units for almost the whole game. Four AIs declared war on you and only Sparta ever hit a city: five hits on one colony, which it took on T91. For 406 city-turns one of your towns had no defender while an enemy military unit stood within six tiles, and nobody came. Hard playing your seat from turn 2 wins this map 34 times in 48, about three times the Celts' AI-only rate, so the rivals were soft before you made a move.

The kit side: barbarians never attack you, so you didn't need garrisons. Your unit upkeep stayed at 1 to 4 Gold a turn while the AIs paid 4 to 22, and your treasury went from 354 Gold at T50 to 3,257 at T120. After you called the Wild on T88, the camps around your towns raided your neighbours instead. Sparta made 93 attacks on barbarians and 14 on you; Germany made 67 on barbarians and 4 on you. In your last war, barbarians killed six German units around the two Riflemen and one Bomber that razed five German cities in eleven turns.

The sack did nothing for you. All 2,520 Gold of it came after your only purchase, and you finished with 6,075 Gold unspent.

The single move that decided the most was the Settler you took from Germany on T17. It founded Gergovia, your Great Work city. Hard taking your seat at T10 loses 48 of 48, mostly to Germany; from T20 it wins 39 of 48.

## 6. Recommendations, as operations and plan gaps

Under the 11-07 strategy reset, these are operation templates for the operation search (`docs/design/ai-strategy/fable-2026-11-07.md` §4.2) and their decisive windows on this tape, not one-gene fixes.

1. **Operation "strike the empty city".**
   - **What it does:** an empire at War with the leader marches every military unit within 8 tiles on his nearest city that has no military unit within 2 and his field army more than 6 tiles away, and strikes when assembled.
   - **Executor:** Campaign with a forced roster, `city_assault`.
   - **Windows on this tape:**
     - Germany T89–T120 on Gergovia, Bibracte or Colony 10, with 4–6 German units within 6 tiles at T100;
     - Sparta T92–T131 on Emain Macha or Tara, with 3–4 Spartan units within 6 tiles;
     - Byzantium T45+.
   - **The same window in the other wins today:** Celts T85 on Tlatelolco (Sparta), and the Swiss game's 111 empty city-turns.
   - **Scenarios:** `germany-take-gergovia` and `sparta-second-celtic-city` (Hard 0/50 each).
2. **Operation "contest the Settler".**
   - **What it does:** a Settler in contact with a rival's military unit is escorted home or held in a city; a rival's Settler in reach is sniped.
   - **Executors:** `settler_escort`, Snipe.
   - **Windows:** Germany T16–T17 (it walked the Settler it had just captured next to your units), and your own seat at T17 (Hard in your seat from T10 doesn't snipe and loses 48/48).
   - **The same window in earlier games:** Swiss 8.9.24 T41, India 8.9.25 T13–T17.
3. **Operation "clear the leader's camps".**
   - **What it does:** when the leader holds or is within 2 VP of the target and the Wild is one of his sources, units within reach clear his camps first.
   - **Executor:** `contest_hold` and `wild_camp_approach` as an operation, not a priority tweak.
   - **Window:** T122–T134. Spartan units stood within 3 tiles of 2–3 of his camps every turn to T132, and Sparta had already cleared two camps on T102 and T114 while fighting raiders.
4. **Plan gap: Hard Celts plays his playbook (extends idea #45-style playbooks; one committed bundle, not three genes).** Light garrisons behind a barbarian screen, the Wild called once the cities have camp sites (not at 70% of the game), Settler theft, and a single bought war against the neighbour whose army is busy with barbarians. In the reset this is a league exploiter seat (`fable-2026-11-07.md` §4.4) and a candidate bundle. Measure it by AI-only Celts share on and off, and by Hard in his seat on this tape from T2 (34/48).
5. **Plan gap: air defence when the leader has Flight.** "Buy or build a Fighter, and pull the field army into walled cities, when a rival Bomber is in range." Germany had 49 Gold and a field army in the open when your Bomber arrived. This is a plan for the turn the leader reaches Flight, not a T122 purchase. Same pattern as India 8.9.25 and the Swiss win (idea #46).
6. **Metric note for the lead.** This tape is easy for Hard (87.9% at the control). Swapping it in as the held-out Celts tape lifts the held-out score from 29.0% to 57.4% at the same commit. Rerun the control on one commit after the swap, and keep the T10 cell (0/48 on master) as a sensitivity check: it moves on whether Hard takes the T17 Settler.

**Design flag (for you):** barbarian peace now does three jobs in a human's hands: no garrisons, a border screen, and a raiding force against the neighbours once the Wild is called. Meanwhile the drawback (no keeping cities) never touched you, because you won by settling. In AI hands the Celts are still below the even share (9.4–11.7%), so this is not a balance emergency, and pillar 8 says not to weaken the strength. Options, if you want the drawback to bite the way you play:
- (a) keep as is, since the AI needs the help;
- (b) a Celtic-story drawback on width: barbarians spare the Celts only near their own camps or towns founded before the Wild;
- (c) let rivals hunt the Wild's camps for more than the normal reward, so the Celtic land invites raids.

Measure any of them as AI-only Celts share plus your next Celts game.
