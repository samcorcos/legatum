# Coach review: Athens on 8.9.6, won at T234

Save: `~/LegatumRuns/bug-saves/p896-athens-win-1019-1800.legatum` (autosave, turn 234; 8 players, Hard, seed 1261515010563583..., engine `632868622` = `playtest-8.9.6`). Replayed at the tag on the box with 0 hash mismatches over 233 turns. Chart: `2026-10-20-8.9.6-athens.png`.

## Coach's verdict

You were right that it was easy, and partly right about why. From turn 120 your position was already a winning one: Hard playing your seat from there won 30 of 30 games. Three things made it easy.

- **Nobody could take a walled city.** 8.9.6 made Walls halve every attack on a city's HP unless a Military Engineer stands with the attacker. The AI's Engineer genes ship switched off, and in 233 turns no AI built a single Engineer. Every one of your 8 cities had Walls or a Castle, and at turn 190 most had no garrison at all. The AIs' campaigns against you ran at 3 to 10% capture odds and gave up within two turns. Sparta's two captures were Piraeus, which you had just retaken (no Walls, 40 HP, no garrison), and Marathon, walled but empty, which took it until T223 to grind down.
- **No AI snowballed.** Unlike your 8.9.5 loss, there was no Wall-less neighbour to eat. Sparta eliminated Japan at turn 23, then sat on 2 cities until about turn 180, while Khanbaliq changed hands 9 times between Sparta, Switzerland and the Mongols. The Celts, who got closest (7 VP), can't keep cities and never declared on you.
- **You converted your points this time.** You sat on 5 VP from T166 to T209, the same plateau as in 8.9.5. Then you took 6 VP in 18 turns:
  - Castles in 3 cities;
  - 29 kills, from 65 Bomber strikes;
  - Mills in 5 cities;
  - Share the Learning as the closer at T224, which started your hold;
  - Factories in 3 cities.

**Did 8.9.6 weaken the AI?** Yes, in one measurable way: the AI treats a walled city as nearly off limits. Switching on just one Engineer gene from your T200 position (`walls_as_cost`, "a wall is a price to pay, not a reason to stay away") turned your own orders from 30 wins in 30 into 0 in 30. You lost 4.9 cities instead of 2.0, and Switzerland won every game. Two smaller 8.9.6 changes didn't matter here:
- Walls can no longer be bought. That hits the AI's defence, and you took only one city all game.
- The bomber fixes helped you, not the AI.

The rubber bands cost you a fifth of your output but didn't change who won.

## 3. Your edge (what you did that the AI doesn't)
- **Expansion:** 4 cities at T50, 5 at T100, 8 at T150 and 9 at T198. The best AI had 3, 4, 7 and 6.
- **Walls everywhere, a thin army.** You had Walls or a Castle in all 8 cities, and Castles in 3 also scored the Medieval objective. Your army strength was 51 at T150 and 103 at T200, against 285 to 390 for Sparta, Germany and the Celts. Behind 8.9.6's Walls, that was enough.
- **The Bomber as a VP engine:** 65 air strikes from T184 and 60 kills in the game. The Future objective "Destroy 29 enemy military units" fell at T214. This was the lesson from 8.9.5, and the bomber fixes delivered it.
- **Share the Learning as the closer:** one order at T224 took you from 8 to 10. In 8.9.5 you never used it.
- **Gold:** one purchase in 233 turns (an Archer at T44), and you finished on 958 Gold. The hoard did nothing for you. It didn't cost you either, because nobody could reach it.
- **Diplomacy:** you refused 12 peace offers and declared war twice (Switzerland at T17, Sparta at T187). Japan and the Celts never fought you.

## 4. The AI's mistakes
- **Strategic: walls are a ban, not a price (every AI).** With `walls_as_cost` off, the siege forecast and `assessed_campaign_allowed` treat a walled city as unwinnable. With `engineer_for_walls` off, nobody builds the counter. Four AIs at War with you for up to 75 turns took two cities, both with no garrison.
- **Strategic: the Celts.** The strongest army at T200 (390) and the closest challenger on VP (7). Your top threat from T159 ("VP race +37"), yet the Celts never declared on you and ran 0 campaigns all game: the known "war plan the army can't fight" pattern.
- **Strategic: Switzerland's war without an army.** At War with you from T159 to the end with 6 Classical units at T190 (3 Phalangites, 2 Spearmen, a Composite Archer), while knowing Metallurgy and holding 602 Gold. Its plan switched 20 times from T159, between Contain, Peace and campaigns.
- **Tactical: Sparta's captures were the right targets.** Piraeus with no Walls, 40 HP and no garrison four turns after your recapture, and Marathon, walled and empty. But it switched campaign and plan 10 times from T188, and peace kept breaking the war.
- **Nothing cheating-adjacent was needed or tried.** Letting the AI see your garrisons through fog would be cheating and is rejected. Everything below uses public information.

## 5. Was the win fair?
Mostly skill plus an AI blind spot, with some luck in the neighbours.
- **Skill:** you expanded faster, led Science all game, and converted VP with the closer you learned in 8.9.5.
- **The blind spot:** the rules give the attacker the answer to Walls (the Military Engineer), and the shipped AI doesn't use it. That's an AI gap, not a rules hole.
- **Luck:** Japan died at T23, and the Celts are a faction that can't keep cities.

## 6. Recommendations (ranked by effect on how often you win on Hard)
1. **Walls are a cost, not a ban: put `walls_as_cost` (and `engineer_for_walls`) back on, measured against a walled seat.**

   - **Where:** `crates/ai/src/params.rs` (gene defaults); `engineers.rs`; `campaign.rs` (the walled-target pricing near line 571); `city_assault.rs::add_engineer_cover`; `assessed_campaign_allowed` and the `WallsBeforeArrival` campaign end.
   - **Measure:** the human-seat benchmark can't see it with a Hard seat. Add a seat that walls every city and keeps a thin army (proxy v4 with Walls first, or the tape seat on this game's T187/T200 branches). Also run the Scenario Lab `swiss-walls-athens` baseline (Hard 0/50) and the war suite's walled scenarios (Keystone and Anvil, 0% at the landing).
