# Playtest 8.9.24, Switzerland: an easy win, and why

Save: `~/LegatumRuns/bug-saves/swiss-win-8924-20261102/autosave.legatum` (engine `a8463a971`, tag `playtest-8.9.24`, 5 players: Switzerland, Germany, Byzantium, Mongols, Sparta, Hard AIs, 48x32 map, replay 160/160 turns, 0 hash mismatches). Chart: `2026-11-02-8.9.24-swiss.png` (his curves against Hard Switzerland from turn 1).

## Coach's verdict

You won on turn 161, 12 VP to 4 (Germany), in 40 active minutes (0.25 minutes a turn, one session, about 520 orders). It was easy because of two moments and one gap, and the Swiss kit played a supporting part.

1. **Turn 41 (the Settler).** A Soldier shadowed a Mongol Settler for three turns, you declared War and took it on the spot, and it became Biel (T44). That left the Mongols on 3 cities until T80 and you on 4 at T50. Hard in your seat from T40 declares the same War but ends T50 with 3 cities to the Mongols' 4, and wins 4% of 48 runs. From T42, with the Settler already yours, it wins 46%.
2. **Turns 58 to 61 (the raid).** The Mongols hit unwalled Lugano six times in four turns and left it at 30 of 200 HP. Two bought Composite Archers (T58, T59) killed the Soldier and Phalangite that would have walked in. Take away either purchase and Lugano falls by T75 in 32 of 32 runs (control 0 of 32). The Walls you credit did their job afterwards (Lugano's from T63 and Thun's from T40 ended two later Mongol campaigns, T66 and T74, as "walls before arrival"); the siege itself was beaten by Gold.
3. **The gap.** From T50 your Production was 1.5 to 2 times any AI's (T100: 165 against 51 to 102) while every AI sat on 4 to 6 cities with 7 to 13 military units, and the AIs never attacked a core Swiss city after T61. By T90 Hard in your seat wins 94 to 97%, though the VP track still read 3 to 4 for Germany.

"Is it the Swiss?" Partly the kit, mostly not. The AI plays the Swiss badly (11.9% in 5-seat AI-only games, even share 20%; Hard from turn 1 on your map wins 0 of 32 and is eliminated in 56% of runs), while you win with every faction. The kit multiplies a lead: take it away and your T90 position drops from 94% to 16%. "I used my gold hoard to buy armies" is half right: you made 7 purchases all game (2 archers, 2 Assault Troopers, an Arquebusier, a Bomber, a Tank), 2 to 5 military units at any time, and ended with 7,387 Gold. The decisive buy was one Bomber (T140) right after Flight (T139): nobody else had Flight.

Reading: the tolls and the interest are where the faction's strength lives (the AI Swiss loses two thirds of its wins without tolls); the Redoubt is neutral for the AI and worth about 20 points at T90 only because he builds them early. Together they take his T90 position from a near-certain win to a long shot. But Hard with the full kit wins 0 of 32 from turn 1, and he wins every faction he plays (the Aztecs twice, the Mongols, Germany, Athens, Sparta, the Celts and four Swiss games in the last two weeks). The Swiss are not especially good; the AI Swiss (6.4 cities at the end of AI-only games against his 22) is especially bad, and the kit is a multiplier a good player collects.

## 4. When was it decided (pillar 11)

By strength, between T42 and T90: Hard's win chance from his position goes 4% (T40), 46% (T42), 59% (T50), 84% (T70), 94 to 97% (T90). ## 5. Did the new genes show up?

- **Rival expansion (`colony_compound`):** AI cities 3/3/3/2 at T50 (his 4), 6/6/4/4 at T100 (his 9), none above 8 all game, Germany flat at 6 from T90 to the end. With the three expansion genes and `coalition_push` off for every AI (Hard seat from T1, 32 runs each) the rivals' T100 cities are the same: Germany 4 against 4, Byzantium 5 against 5, Mongols 8 against 7.5, Sparta 6 against 6. No visible effect on this map.
- **Escorted Settlers (`settler_escort`, `settler_stalk`):** the Mongol Settler walked three turns along his border, two hexes from Thun, with one Soldier in each Mongol city, and was taken (T41); a second fell at T153. No AI took any of his 16 Settlers, and no stalk was seen. The gene did not fire when it mattered, because the War arrived the same turn.
- **Coalition (`coalition_push`):** it fired. Germany and Byzantium declared the same turn (T107; plan Contain +411 and +221, "second front: busy elsewhere"), Germany signed an Alliance with the Mongols at T109, "coalition city prize" (+92 to +245) and "joint strike" (+42) priced their campaigns, and every AI had named him top threat by T79 (Mongols T62). Sparta never joined (25 tiles away, +65 rising to +261). **But the coalition did 19 hits in 160 turns: Mongols 9 (six on Lugano T58 to T61), Byzantium 5 (St. Gallen, T147 to T150), Germany 5 (Sarai, T152 to T155).** Byzantium shot St. Gallen to 0 HP at T148 and again at T150 and nobody walked in; Germany took Sarai (a Mongol town he had captured) at T155 and lost it T157. No core Swiss city was touched after T61. The rivals did not back off; they declared and never marched.

## 6. His pattern: Walls, then Gold at the decisive moment

What he built: 14 Alpine Redoubts (Thun T40, Winterthur T45, Biel T50, Lugano only T63), 16 Settlers, 12 military units in 160 turns (the AIs trained 15 to 30 each). What he bought (7 orders): archers T58 and T59, two Assault Troopers T113 (six turns after the T107 declarations), an Arquebusier T122, a Bomber T140, a Tank T157. The treasury never fell below about 400 after T26 and ended at 7,387; interest paid +61 a turn at T150.

Why the AI did not punish it:

1. **The one real punishment was one unit short.** The Mongols brought 6 attackers in four turns, one or two a turn (damage 20, 20, 22, 44, 33, 31). Their army strength at T50 was 93 against his 32, and the capital had no Walls. A force that arrived together on T58 would have taken it, with 810 Gold of treasury share.
2. **After T66 nobody could see the prize.** Germany's planner knew Winterthur (seen T112) and Mongol towns, never Lugano, Thun or Biel; Byzantium knew three Mongol-border towns. The capital with 1,300 to 7,400 Gold was never in any campaign after T66. Their campaigns went at his newly captured Mongol towns (Almaliq twice, Sarai, St. Gallen), which carry a huge "supply" term (+1,036 to +5,760) in the plan's value.
3. **The AIs hold no reserve.** All four AI treasuries stayed between 4 and 240 Gold from T30 to the end (one spike of 581), so they cannot answer a raid or a siege with purchases; Byzantium did hire 15 mercenary companies and still never marched on him.
4. **The air stage was uncontested for the ninth game running.** He had Flight at T139 (AIs in Industrialization T139 to T153). Nineteen bomber strikes T141 to T159, five on cities (about 200 damage to a 200 HP city, ignoring Walls), 14 on units. Without Flight the walled AI cities and units could not answer.

What a rival would have to do: (a) concentrate and arrive together, or wait; (b) know where the capital is and price the treasury as one purchased defender per city per turn; (c) march an army that exists (Germany had 9 to 13 units, strength 388 at T100 and 1,638 at T150 against his 171 and 1,522, and fought once between T25 and T125); (d) hold its own Gold; (e) race to Flight. What does not help: turtling. Forcing a defence floor (Walls, 3 shooters, 2 melee) on all four AIs from T50 raises his win rate to 100% (rivals' T100 cities fall from 5 to 6 down to 3 to 4); the same floor on the Mongols alone, to 78 to 84% from 59 to 66%.

## 8. Tactics and battles

- **Exchange:** he killed 52 military units (33 Mongol, 12 German, 7 Byzantine) and lost 7, all to the Mongols: about 7 to 1. Damage dealt to the Mongols 7,203, taken 1,482.
- **Focus fire:** his Lugano defence put archer shots and city fire on the same unit each turn (T59 Ranger, T60 and T61 Soldier and Phalangite killed). Mongol shots spread: 20, 20, 22, 44, 33, 31 across 6 attacks, 4 turns, 30 HP left.
- **Lone attacks and stragglers:** the Mongol raid, as above. Germany's Sarai siege was the best AI siege in the game: five hits in four turns (74, 1, 62, 90, 75), city taken T155; it was recaptured in two turns because Germany left it one wounded unit.
- **Wounded units left in place:** St. Gallen sat at 0 HP T148 to T150 with Byzantium's army adjacent and no melee.
- **Walls and upgrades:** German and Byzantine cities were walled (5 to 6 of 6, 6 to 7 of 8) and held no counter to air. His units: Infantry, Field Cannon, Tank, 2 Bombers against Soldiers, Arquebusiers and Assault Troopers.
- **Rejected ideas:** none are cheating. A "defend more" change for the AIs makes it easier for him (section 6).

## 9. Fair? Bands or decisions?

- **Rubber bands:** decadence is off (since 10-31) and there is no crown upkeep, so the bands cost him 0%. Tech diffusion: from techs he knew, each AI got 250 to 355 Science of discount (7 to 8% of its tech bill for Germany and Byzantium, 8 to 9% for Sparta and the Mongols; about 10 to 17 turns of its Science at T100 rates, 1 to 1.5 techs); he got 331 from theirs (5%). **Split: about 0% from the bands, about 100% from the AI's decisions and his play**, with an honest uncertainty of a few percent for diffusion.
- **Smarter AI:** the opening is where it loses (0 of 32 from T1, 4% from T40). The AI-smartness benchmark (opponents-only genes) was not run in this review; the three new genes did not move rival expansion here (section 5).
- **Skill or exploit?** Skill the AI can learn: the Settler snipe, the hoard as an emergency army, Flight first plus one bought Bomber. No rules hole beyond the standing ones (air ignores Walls; a captured Great Work scores for the captor, the tenth game running).

## 10. Recommendations, ranked

1. **Land the strike you start (the T58 raid; extends ideas #37a, #24d).** Do not open fire on a city until the force that takes it is in range in the same turn; price the public treasury as one bought defender per city per turn and the capital's missing Walls as a window that closes. Evidence: six attacks T58 to T61, capital 30/200 HP, two purchases decided the game. Code: `crates/ai/src/campaign.rs` (staging and arrival), `military.rs` siege staging, `plan.rs` capture-at term. Measure: Scenario Lab `swiss-strongbox` (Hard Mongols 6%, 3/50), the war-scenario suite, and the T55 tape branch with the purchases removed.
2. **Make the Settler contest work both ways (extends #34, #24; the 8.9.23 Germany finding, again).** (a) Escort or hold a Settler that walks within two hexes of a foreign city at Peace; (b) when the Hard seat declares to snipe a Settler (`settler_stalk`, `declare_and_strike`), the capture must land: from T40 Hard declares at T41 in 48 of 48 runs and takes nothing, so trace why (a Soldier leaving the only garrison?). Code: `hard_defense.rs` (`settler_escort`), `plan.rs::settler_stalk` (~line 2620), `military.rs`. Measure: branches `h40`, `h42`, `h44` (4%, 46%, 38%) and the Hard city count at T50 (3 against his 4); the human-seat benchmark, seat-only and opponents-only.
3. **A declared war must march, and see the capital (extends #38, #39, #40).** A war opened by the second-front rule needs a named target in staging within a few turns or is re-priced; the nearest scout keeps the leader's capital in the planner's view; cap the "supply" term that sent Germany at a one-turn-old Mongol town at +5,760. Evidence: Germany 1 battle T25 to T125, hits only T147 to T155, `city_seen` never listing Lugano. Code: `plan.rs` (campaign terms around line 1530), `campaign.rs` end reasons, scouting. Measure: Scenario Lab `german-second-front` (Hard 0%), the war-scenario suite; do not expect the Hard-seat benchmark to move (88 to 100% with forced coalitions).

Design or rules questions for the user (no recommendation to change): (a) the AI wants a reserve: should every AI hold some Gold for emergencies, as he does with 800 at T50? The AIs sat between 4 and 240 all game. (b) Air ignores Walls and no AI built a Fighter (the ninth game): answer by Fighters, not by rules? (c) One line on faction balance: the Swiss tolls (+1 Gold per 2 population) carry the AI Swiss from 4.1% to 11.9%; the Redoubt is neutral for the AI. No pillar 8 option is named; the gap is the AI's play.
