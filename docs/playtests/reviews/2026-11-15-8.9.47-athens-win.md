# Athens on Hard, Playtest 8.9.47 (rules 39): won T160, 13 VP to 6

VP, cities, army and the takeover curve (archived outside the repository)

**Coach's verdict.** This map was soft: Hard in your seat from turn 2 already wins 9 of 24 games. What made it certain was how you fought the Celtic war from T80 to T100. Hard taking your seat at T80 wins 11 of 24; at T100 it wins 20 of 24. In those twenty turns Hard would have:
- stopped expanding (it founds 1 city where you founded 3, and builds no Settlers or Walls);
- turned your cities into military production;
- bought ten cheap old units;
- attacked half as often as you did.

You kept settling behind Walls, bought one Cuirassier, refused Peace, and took the war to Lugdunum.

The rivals' worst error was the Celts. A Hard rule that won't declare on a city it hasn't seen recently kept them at Economy for 38 turns, next to an empire showing one military unit. When they finally declared, they arrived too late and too small.

Your verdict on the coach questions was right. In 160 turns the question generator found only 11 candidates in the whole game, none before T87. At the decisive turns it found nothing to ask, because Hard agreed with you on *whether* to act and differed only on *what* to build and buy, and no question category covers that.

## 2. The questions the coach card should have asked

The generator (`crates/ai/src/why.rs`) replayed exactly on your tape. It found 11 candidates in the whole game: the 10 you were asked and one more at T160. **There was never a second candidate to rank against.** So the failure is not ranking. The categories miss the turns where you and Hard disagreed:
- **T85–T90:** Hard agreed with you on arming. On T85 its shadow also started a Cuirassier in Athens. On T90 both of you bought, so the purchase rule (which needs one side to buy nothing) stayed silent, although the *items* differed completely.
- **T104, T114, T124:** Hard also refused the Celtic Peace, so there was no war-and-peace question.
- **T80–T100:** settling, Walls and attacks differed on most turns, but only the founding tile has a category.

**The questions it should have asked:**

| T104 | "The Celts offer Peace after razing Marathon. Why keep fighting?" | Hard agreed here; ask only when the shadow's own win-chance estimate (`winchance.rs`) moves a lot either way. |
| T119 | "Lugdunum, the Celtic capital, is yours. Do you hold it, raze it, or push on?" | A capital capture changes its VP standings and their win-chance estimate. |

**The ten that were asked:**
- **T87 Settler site:** worth asking (section 4.2); your answer was the right valuation.
- **T147 no war on India:** worth asking; it found a Hard error. It named the empire by team number and gave no front.
- **T143 Tank:** borderline; Hard's "buy nothing" was wrong.
- **Noise:**
  - T129, T138, T155 and T158 were automated cities (section 5).
  - T100 (window) adds up 10 turns of shadow purchases from one unspent treasury, the bug found in the last review.
  - T101 and T131 asked you to justify not buying what Hard picked badly (section 4.3).

**What the generator needs to rank yours first:**
- skip automated cities;
- score each candidate by the shadow's own estimated change in win chance (or plan-value terms), not by a fixed number per category: today a 17.7x production ratio scores 88,550 and a Settler site scores 33,700;
- compare *what* was bought and built, not only *whether*;
- name the city and the tile in every question.

## 4. Why Hard lost

**4.1 The Celts waited 38 turns: the exact gate.**
- **What they saw:** from T50 to T87 their only plan candidate was Economy, with 8–9 units against your visible 1 (strength 5). Their conquest nominee, chosen by `conquest.rs::choose_conquest_city`, was your Eleusis: 6 tiles away, the best value-for-cost of any city they knew (41–56).
- **Why it never became a war:** they last saw Eleusis on T44. From T61 to T88, `plan.rs::campaign_candidates` dropped it every turn as stale (picture 17 to 44 turns old). The rule is gene `stale_intel` (15 turns) in `campaign.rs::stale_city`.
- **What it blocked:** a stale nominee shuts out every other target, including visible Marathon (value-for-cost 51–70). No unit went to look; the gene's own description says Scouts revisit first.
- **The way in:** they declared on T89, the turn after the nominee switched to Marathon.
- **At War:** campaigns kept dropping for "no forecast" (79 drops). The plan flip-flopped between Campaign and Peace on T92, T94, T103, T107 and T113, and you took nine of their cities.

**4.2 Hard's site value ignores armies of empires at Peace (T87).**
- Hard's best site for your Settler was (19,22): 2 tiles from a Celtic Horseman and 4 from Celts Colony 7. The Celts declared two turns later.
- `civilians.rs::enemy_army_near` and the other site blocks read `ctx.threat_units`, which lists only empires at War.
- Your reason ("found something far away from them") was the better valuation.

**4.3 Purchases ignore era and what is coming (T101, T131).** Every defensive purchase pass picks by `purchase_combat_strength` per Gold. That picks the Composite Archer against mounted units (T101) and Ancient Spearmen in era 6 (T131). Tags from the purchase-pass trace over the whole game, every AI seat:

| Pass | Spearmen bought by AIs | When |
|---|---|---|
| `door` (door defence) | 5 | Celts T91, T115, T125, T153; Byzantium T125 |
| `ready_defenders` | 3 | Byzantium T128, T129, T158 |
| `emergency` | 2 | Byzantium T54, Celts T120 |
| `frontline` | 1 | Switzerland T124 |
| `second_troop` | 1 | Switzerland T26 |

The T131 Spearman in your seat came from `door`, not `second_troop_buy`. The named-fund change only closes this if it reaches these four passes.

**4.4 Contain carries no second-front cost (T147).** `plan.rs::contain_candidates` never calls `new_war_terms`, so a fifth war on India cost Hard nothing. Your "I can't afford another front" is the right valuation.

**4.5 A Hard crash.** `opportunity.rs::forecast_siege_rounds` reads `force[0]` with an empty force (index out of bounds). It killed the decision diff in a branch near T80. The same code is on master.

**4.6 The coalition was a phony war.**
- **Mongols:** Contain from T120 with the largest AI army (28 units, strength 2,893), but only 1 attack on you in 40 turns.
- **Switzerland:** joined on T137 through Byzantium's call to arms with 15 units, and never attacked.
- **India:** named you its top threat on T120, but its plan stayed Economy all game.
- **Byzantium:** its campaign value came mostly from the known `supply` term bug (2,904 and 5,808). Both campaigns ended for "insufficient army".

**Tactics.** You made 106 attacks (57 on Byzantium, 41 on the Celts, 8 on the Mongols) and lost one military unit all game. The Celts attacked you 22 times, Byzantium 15, the Mongols once, Switzerland never.

## 5. City automation against Hard

Every city you automated used the **Science** focus. In that focus, `production.rs::automated_choice`:
- drops military units;
- ranks any Science building first.

The question compared that with Hard with *no* focus, which picks units while at War. There is one leak: the military filter uses `composition::is_combat`, which excludes the Air class, so a Biplane passes as a Science-focus pick.

| Turn, city | Automation | Hard | Which was right |
|---|---|---|---|
| T129, Athens Colony 8 | Pasture | Landship | Neutral: a frontier town far from every front |
| T138, Athens | Biplane | Trench Infantry | Likely the Biplane: you upgraded two Biplanes to Bombers on T151–T152 and flew 16 strikes |
| T155, Phaleron; T158, Eleusis | Inference Core | Infantry | Harmless: you were holding to win |

**The fixes:**
- the question generator should skip a city with a `SetCityAutomation` order or `build_automation` set (or compare against Hard with the same focus);
- the Science and Gold focus should also exclude Air units.

## 7. New proposals for the lead

The fixes already queued are not repeated here: stale nominee and empty-force guard, armies at Peace in site reads, Contain second-front cost, shared purchase scorer, supported defence in Hard, and the question generator. New:

1. **Hard stops expanding at War.**
   - **The evidence:** in T80–T100 Hard in your seat built 0 Settlers and founded 1 city against your 3 Settlers and 3 foundings. Its cities went to 13 military units against your 4, and it reaches 8 cities at T100 against your 9. The same gap holds from T2 (4.5 cities at T100).
   - **Where to look:** the Settler gates at War in `production.rs` (the Settler's score and its `SettlerGates`), and `civilians.rs::choose_site_scored`'s War routing (`settler_danger`, enemy war tiles).
   - **The rule to test:** keep a Settler pipeline in cities that are not under threat, while the army is above the local forecast.
   - **Measure:** Hard-seat cities at T100 and seat wins from this game's T80 position (11 of 24 now), plus the war-scenario suite.
2. **Ask "what", not only "whether".** Two new categories:
   - purchases of very different era or class at a similar Gold total (T90);
   - Settlers trained or founded at War where Hard would stop (T92–T96).
3. **The stale-nominee fix needs an outcome check on this game's states.** From the T60 state with `stale_intel` off for the Celts only, does the Celtic declaration move from T89 to the T60s? Does your seat still win from T80? This states the counter for his wide, thinly defended opening.
