Save: `~/LegatumRuns/bug-saves/p897-celts-win-1022-2028.legatum` (engine `5637d8115` = `playtest-8.9.7`). Replayed at the tag on the box: 249 turns, 0 hash mismatches. Chart: `2026-10-22-8.9.7-celts.png`. Rivals: Japan, Germany (eliminated by Sparta on T21) and Sparta, all Hard.

You said: "I just played a game as the celts and crushed everyone. I'm not sure the advantage or disadvantage of the celts made much of a difference. I played a pretty standard game." And: what do you do differently from the AI Celts, who win 2.4% of balanced games?

## Coach's verdict

You were right on both counts. The Celtic rules hardly touched your first 200 turns, and the AI Celts lose for an AI reason, not a rules reason.
- **You won with standard good play.** You settled 12 cities, walled all of them, kept a small army, and finished build objectives from old eras. Then you picked one war, against the VP rival, at the right moment.
- **The faction only showed up at the end.** Celtic land wounded invaders for 132 HP in the whole game, because the AIs mostly stayed out. Your hidden archers fired 42 times and killed 11 units. The sacks mattered: from T214 to T232 you burned 5 Japanese cities, the capital among them, and carried off 1,813 Gold. That took Japan from 11 cities to 5 and gave you the Most Advanced crown.
- **The AI Celts never do that last part.** Their code forbids them from attacking cities at all. In 2,048 balanced games they sacked 10 cities in total (you sacked 6 in one game). That ban is the main reason they win 2.4%.
- **Lifting the ban, the rules kept:**
  - in balanced games the AI Celts win 5.2%;
  - in your seat from your turn-50 position, Hard Celts win 20 of 40 instead of 4 of 40.
- **Removing every Celtic rule (no hiding, no sack, no −2 melee, no land damage, no hunter changes):** with today's AI the Celts win 2.0%, the same as with the rules. For an AI that never attacks, the strengths and the weaknesses both do nothing.
- **The game was decided between T190 and T206.** That was when Sparta allied with you against Japan and Japan's army bled from 490 to 119 against your walls. If Japan and Sparta had both gone to War with you at T190 and refused peace, your own orders lose all 40 replays (with a caveat on replay fragility, section 5b). By T206 the same coalition still beats your orders 40 of 40, but in the real game nobody formed it.
- **The rubber bands were small here.** You lost 6.6% of your output to decadence and Japan 9.6%. My split: about 20% bands, 80% AI decisions.

The five behaviours that explain the gap, in order of size:
1. **You went to war with the VP rival, at the moment its army was down, and sacked its cities. The AI Celts are forbidden from attacking cities.**
   - Every city-attack path in the AI returns early for the Celts: city assaults, campaigns, open-city strikes, rush sieges, containment wars and second fronts. This is a hard-coded "never", not a priced choice.
   - Lifting it is the biggest single change we found (section 6, item 1).
2. **You kept settling after T100.**
   - You trained 11 Settlers. Six of your cities were founded after T100, so you reached 12, while Hard in your seat stops at about 8.
   - The Celts can't grow by conquest, since a sack burns the city. So every extra city has to be settled, and the AI's settler valuation doesn't know that: AI Celts peak at 4.7 cities in balanced games, while winners average 9.1.
3. **You built for old eras' objectives as your city count grew.**
   - Market ×9 and Walls ×11 needed 9 and 11 cities, and Bank ×7 and Forge ×7 needed seven of each. You scored all four.
   - The AI prunes any era objective more than 80 turns of effort away (`objective_horizon`) and judges effort on the cities it has now. With 4 cities, "9 Markets" never looks reachable.
4. **You walled every city early and kept a small army.**
   - Walls went into the queue from T11. Your army sat at 135 strength from T70 to T115, and you lost no city to two Japanese wars and two Spartan ones.
   - Sparta's campaigns on you ended within 1–3 turns as "infeasible" or "insufficient army" (T117, T151, T237). Your cities had 200 HP behind Castles and Star Forts.
   - The Walls also paid a VP (Walls ×11).
5. **You raised the Warbands once, after your last city, and never lost it.**
   - The goal is live: a new city, or a cleared camp, breaks it.
   - Hard in your seat holds it at the end in only about half its games, and in 18–33% from your T100 and T150 positions, because it raises the camps on a timer and keeps settling or losing camps afterwards.

Your diplomacy was simple and it worked:
- you accepted every early peace (T43, T73);
- you offered Sparta peace once (T134) and took its peace after razing Essen (T171–T172); in between you turned down five proposals (Japan's Alliance offers against Sparta and Sparta's peace, T109–T164);
- you allied with Sparta against Japan while Japan was at War with you (T193–T203).

What did not matter much:
- **Gold.** You sat on 1,500–4,700 Gold from T150 to T210 and bought 12 items in the whole game. The Bombers at T211 were the purchase that counted.
- **Hunters and homeland.** Their combined effect was small, because the AIs rarely entered your land.

## 5. Was your win fair?

Mostly skill the AI can learn, with one piece of luck:
- **Skill (learnable):**
  - settle past T100 because your faction can't grow by conquest;
  - build into old eras' objectives as you grow;
  - wall everything;
  - pick one war against the VP rival when its army is spent.
- **Luck:** your two rivals fought each other for 220 turns, and Germany vanished at T21.
  - With reseeded dice from T1, Hard Celts in your seat meet a Sparta that eats Japan instead (Japan has 1.3 cities at T50 in those runs, against 2 in yours), and Sparta wins 29 of 40.
  - Once your T50 position is fixed, with Japan alive, the same AI wins 4 of 40, and with attacks allowed 20 of 40.
- **No rules hole.** The Celtic rules are neutral for the AI and paid off for you only in the last 40 turns.
- **The AI "blind spot" you exploited** is the AI's own no-attack gate. A second blind spot is allying with the leader.

## 6. Coach's recommendations for the AI

Ranked by expected effect on how often you win on Hard:

1. **Let the Celtic AI attack cities by value: lift the hard-coded gates and price a sack.**
   - **Mechanism:** a sack is worth the Gold it carries, plus the VP it removes from the victim (crowns it topples, the victim's count objectives), plus the victim's lost output. That is the same value scoring every other faction uses, with "keep the city" replaced by "burn it".
   - **Code:** remove the `self.faction == Faction::Celts` early returns in:
     - `city_assault.rs::coordinate_city_assaults_at`;
     - `military.rs`: `campaign`, `campaign_target_for`, `seize_open_city`, `rush_siege`, `occupy_rush_city`, `open_front`, the target list;
     - `plan.rs`: `campaign_candidates`, `contain_war`, the Contain march and `peace_would_be_broken`;
     - `campaign.rs`: `reconcile_campaign`, `adopt_started_siege`;
     - `victory.rs::conquest_victory_value`;
     - `threat.rs::second_front_temperament`, which is `None` for Celts.
   - Then make `opportunity.rs::sack_gold_value` and the capture value see the sack's VP effect instead of a kept city's value.
   - **Measured on a throwaway branch (all gates lifted, no new valuation):**
     - balanced 8-empire win share 2.4% → **5.2%** (2,048 games; Sparta 10.4 → 12.6%, Japan 3.1 → 2.9%, Byzantium 29.2 → 28.1%);
     - your seat from T50 / T100 / T150: **4 / 2 / 2 of 40 → 20 / 18 / 18 of 40**.
   - **Measure:**
     - the human-seat benchmark seat-only (the 8.4 Celts map) and opponents-only;
     - the war-scenario suite;
     - a new Celtic war scenario (`celtic-woods`, below, from the other side).
2. **Settle as a faction that can't conquer: value Settlers past T100 by the objectives the extra cities unlock.**
   - **Mechanism:** a Settler's value should include the era objectives it brings within reach. Examples: "Have X in N cities" and "Own N cities" for every open era, old ones included.
   - Judge objective effort with the cities the empire is on course to have, not the cities it has now.
   - **Code:** `crowns.rs::objective_effort`, `objective_horizon` (prunes anything more than 80 turns away), `production.rs` settler value and `settlement.rs`.
   - Gene-only versions failed in this game: `settler_priority` 2000 and the objective genes in your seat from T1 left it at 0–1 of 40 and the same 7.8 cities. It needs the valuation, not a weight.
   - **Measure:** cities at T150/T200 and era VP in balanced games (AI Celts 4.0 cities at T150, 1.05 era VP), and the benchmark seat-only.
3. **Don't ally with the leader; commit to containing it; value a capture from the VP leader as the VP it scores.**
   - **Mechanism:**
     - an Alliance with the VP leader, or a public runaway, is priced by the VP it lets the leader keep;
     - a capture from the VP leader is worth the open era objective it completes;
     - a Contain war on the leader doesn't end at value 0 one turn later.
   - **Code:** `diplomacy.rs` (alliance and peace value), `plan.rs::contain_candidates`, `victory.rs::objective_capture_value`, `conquest.rs`. This merges with ideas #17 and #27.
   - **Measured:** the T190 branches, 37 → 0 of 40.
   - **Measure:** the benchmark opponents-only, the T180–T206 branches of this review (`~/LegatumRuns/coach-tools/coach-897-celts/`) and the war suite.

**Design or rule questions for you, not AI fixes:**
- **Is Raise the Warbands a free 2 VP?** The AI reaches it in 98% of balanced games, always at T210, which is the faction method's "freebie" warning (a goal met in 98% of games isn't a choice). It didn't help the AI Celts win, though.
- **The Celtic city names run out.** Your 7th to 12th cities were called "Celts Colony 7" to "Celts Colony 12". The Celts, Germany, the Mongols, Sparta and Athens have six names each (Switzerland, Japan and Byzantium have 20). The Celtic list needs more real Gaulish and Irish names (for example Avaricum, Cenabum, Durocortorum, Vesontio, Dun Ailinne, Cruachan). This is a content fix, not a rule question.
- **Your feeling that the faction didn't matter is right for this AI, and it's worth a thought.** The Celtic strengths (land damage, hidden archers) only work when rivals invade. Here the AIs mostly didn't, so the faction played like no faction until you started sacking. If you want the Celts to feel Celtic earlier, that's a design question about what draws rivals into Celtic land, not an AI fix.

## Notes for the record

- **A method trap: reseeding the dice in a branch also redraws every era's objectives.**
  - The runner's `reseed` overwrites `setup.seed`, and `objectives.rs` derives each era's objectives from it. So reseeded Hard-seat branches from T50 or later play with different objectives from yours (e.g. "Capture 2 enemy cities" instead of "Market ×9").
  - Paired comparisons within a salt are still fair, but absolute levels aren't your game. Earlier reviews' reseeded branches have the same caveat.
  - Fix for the tool: reseed a separate dice stream, not the setup seed.
- **Rule-arm replays drift:** with a Celtic rule switched off, the warm-up replay of your orders diverges (40 to 210 hash mismatches), so rule arms are read from T1 and T50 and from balanced games, not from your later states.
