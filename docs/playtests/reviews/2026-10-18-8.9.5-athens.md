# Coach review: Athens on 8.9.5, lost to Sparta at T230

Save: `~/LegatumRuns/bug-saves/p895-athens-loss-1927.legatum` (8 players, Hard, Standard, seed 6641729794..., engine `64ad962f9` = `playtest-8.9.5`). Replayed at the tag on the box with 0 hash mismatches over 229 turns. Chart: `2026-10-18-8.9.5-athens.png`.

## Coach's verdict

This was a fair loss, and a good one. Sparta didn't beat you on the battlefield: you never lost a city. It beat you on points. From turn 150 you sat on 5 VP for 80 turns, holding 1,000 to 1,900 Gold and 2 military units. Meanwhile Sparta ate the Mongols, who can't build Walls, and turned the conquest into 6 VP. One recapture at T146 scored three eras at once, and the Mongols' elimination at T211 scored two more. Your points were there to take:

- **Share the Learning**, your one-order faction goal, worth 2 VP.
- **Banks in 6 cities**, which scores two eras at once.
- **9 more tiles of territory**, which also scores two eras.
- **Destroying units** with the Bomber, which was the right tool and the one that broke.

From your exact T150 position, Hard in your seat won 16 of 30 (with the dice reseeded).

- **How:** it built an army (about 440 strength by T200 against your 105), took about 2 Japanese cities, shared the Learning (21 of 30 games) and scored the Renaissance, Industrial and Future era objectives.
- **What it did to Sparta:** with Athens in the scramble for the same border cities, Sparta took 4 cities by T211 instead of 10, and the Mongols survived in 77% of games.
- **The timing:** from T170 the same seat won 8 of 30.

Walls and garrisons alone, on your own orders, changed nothing (0 of 30), and neither did the coalition against Sparta from T170 or later. The rubber bands mattered somewhat: switched off from T150 they took Hard-in-your-seat from 16 to 22 of 30. The bigger share was decisions.

So two things are worth learning here. For you: by T160, an idle army and a full treasury were your weak point. For the game: the "How you win" panel didn't show you any of these routes.

**Is it the UI?** Mostly, yes. The panel at 8.9.5 (`app/src/ui/EraObjectives.tsx`) has these problems:

**Proposed UI change: a "Next VP" list in How you win** (for the lead and `legatum-ui`). Up to four rows, ranked by how close each one is:
- Each row is a VP you can still take, with its cost and its counter, for example:
  - "Share the Learning: +2 VP now. One order: each rival gains 3 technologies."
  - "Banks in 6 cities: +2 VP (Renaissance and Modern). You have 0. Buy one for N Gold."
  - "9 more tiles: +2 VP (Renaissance and Industrial). Next tile N Gold."
  - "Take Largest Economy from Sparta: +1 VP, and it breaks their hold. 43 behind."
- Show every open era's objectives, not only the current era's.
- Mark an objective that counts in more than one era with "×2".
- Give each row its action (Buy, Share, Show on map), so the next action is obvious (pillar 9). Prices and counters come from data the view already has (pillar 5).

## 6. Recommendations (ranked by effect on how often you win on Hard)
1. **Deny the leader its next VP: fight for the prey, not only the leader.**
   - **The pattern:** Sparta's last 4 VP came from public, countable things: 12 cities for two eras, and the Great Work.
   - **What the others did:** Japan, next to the same border cities, fought Sparta over Nagoya and signed peace four times. The Celts and Switzerland never joined.
   - **The evidence:** in the branches, a seat that took those border cities itself (Hard Athens) cut Sparta's captures from 9.7 to 4.1 and let the Mongols live.
   - **The change:** value capturing or defending a city that would complete the leader's objective (public objective progress: "Own 12 cities" at 11) as VP denial. This is the same shape as `victory.rs::hoard_denial_value`, applied to every objective count. Price peace with the leader by the VP it lets the leader take.
   - **Learnable from public information,** so no cheating.
   - **Measure:** the human-seat benchmark opponents-only, plus this game's T150 and T170 branches (Sparta's win rate with the AIs' genes on).
2. **Convert open objectives into VP (the AI's points economy).** Merge into ideas #26.
   - **The gap:** even Hard Athens took 50–150 turns to finish from T150.
   - **The change:** rank every open era's objectives by VP per cost: one Bank type counting for Renaissance and Modern, tiles bought toward a territory count, kills by an air unit. Play the one-shot faction goal (Share the Learning) as the closer, when its 2 VP take the empire to 10.
   - **Where:** `crowns.rs::objective_effort` and `chosen_objective`, `goal_actions.rs`.
   - **Measure:** seat-only benchmark (VP at T200, win rate), plus the new Scenario Lab baseline below (Hard gains 0 VP in 30 turns from T160).
3. **Break a hold through its live crowns.**
   - **The gap:** Sparta's hold needed Largest Economy (216 against your 173) and Strongest Army, and nobody contested either. Alliances against it formed at T223, three turns into the hold.
   - **The change:** the hold response should list the crowns the holder can lose as targets: an economy push past the holder, or strikes on its army's HP.
   - **Where:** `hold.rs`, `crowns.rs`.
   - **Measure:** a hold war scenario (playbook "Nobody contests the hold").
4. **UI, for the lead and the user (not AI): the "Next VP" list in How you win** (section 4).
5. **Rule questions for the user (not AI):**
   - **(a) Objective stacking:** one recapture scored three eras (T146), and one city count scored two (T211). Fight objectives count from the turn you entered each era, so an empire that enters several eras before its first capture scores them all at once. Intended?
   - **(b) Recapturing a city the leader took from you** counts as "a city from the VP leader".
   - **(c) Sparta's desertion peace after each capture** makes it flip between peace and war (Nagoya changed hands 11 times).
6. **Tooling bug, for the lead (not AI or rules):**
   - **The bug:** `core::scenario::validate_state` checks the 4-hex city gap (`MIN_CITY_GAP`) for every faction, but Japan may found cities 3 hexes apart (`data::min_city_gap`). Every position with Japan's closer cities is rejected ("Okayama is within 3 hexes of Sapporo"), in `cut.sh` and in the app's scenario loader (`crates/wasm/src/scenario.rs`).
   - **The fix:** use the smaller of the two founders' gaps, a one-line change.
   - **Its effect here:** the scenario below was cut with that fix on a throwaway worktree and is held back (`lab.requires`) until it lands.
