# Playtest 8.9.2, Switzerland on Hard: coach's review

VP race, treasuries and decadence (archived outside the repository)

## Coach's verdict

You won this one on economy and patience, and the AIs let you. From T78 you sat on 4 VP against everyone's 2, with 1,000+ Gold in the bank, and for 50 turns nobody treated you as a threat. When five AIs finally declared war on you (T126 to T132), none of them had an army that could take a city, and four of them signed peace within 40 turns. The one AI that could fight, Sparta, spent those turns besieging Byzantium on the far side of the map and turned on you only at T183, ten turns before you started your winning hold. Your play was sound and learnable: early Settlers, Walls first, Markets to the era objective, Gold as insurance, stolen Settlers, no wasted wars. The AI's failures were strategic, not tactical: it didn't see an economic runaway, it declared wars it couldn't fight, it never aimed at your capital where the hoard lives, and nobody contested your hold. The rubber bands slowed you (decadence took about a fifth of your output and pushed your winning Great Work back about 9 turns) but they didn't decide the game; the AIs' choices did. In the what-ifs, one different decision by Sparta at T133 would have beaten you almost every time.

## 1. Result and margin

- **Result:** VP win at T202, 11 of 10, held for 10 turns from T193. Final: you 11, Sparta 5, AI Swiss 5, Mongols 3, Japan 2, Germany 2, Byzantium 0; Athens eliminated by Sparta at T26.
- **Your 11:** five era objectives (eras 2–6), the Swiss reserve goal (2), the Grand Bazaar Great Work (2), Largest Economy (held from T23 to the end) and Most Advanced (from T179, by one tech).
- **The race:** you took the lead at T74 and never lost it outright (tied at T127 and T159). 4–2 at T80 and T100, 5–3 at T150, 7–5 at T170, 9–6 at T190.
- **When it was clear (pillar 11):** on VP it stayed close for longer than it felt: the coalition's pressure knocked your reserve goal off, and the gap fell to 0 at T127 and T159, and to 1 at T153–158 and T174–178 (Sparta 6 to your 7). Underneath, though, it was clear by T100: your Largest Economy score was 108 against the next best 74, and decadence was already cutting your yields 12% for being far above the median empire. Pillar 11 held on the scoreboard and failed on the economy: an AI that read the economy would have known.

## 2. Turning points

1. **T23–T30, a cheap early war.** You declared on Germany at T23, stole its Settler at T24 and signed peace at T30. A free city's worth of Settler, at no cost. Meanwhile Sparta ate Athens (T26) on the far side and became the strongest AI.
2. **T74–T79, the economic break.** Markets in 4 cities scored the era-2 objective at T74 (the AIs scored era 2 at T114 and later), and the treasury crossed 1,000 at T78–79 for the reserve goal: 4 VP to 2, and a lead nobody contested for 50 turns. Your hoard kept compounding to 1,791 Gold at T110 (1,668 Gold of interest over the game).
3. **T126–T132, the coalition that wasn't.** The Mongols (T126), AI Swiss (T130, leaving its alliance with you), Germany (T131), Japan and Byzantium (T132) all declared war. They took no city. Their only real effect: wartime purchases pushed you under 1,000 Gold, so you lost the 2 VP goal from T132 to T155 (and briefly T127–T131 and T156–T160).
4. **T143–T172, peace one by one.** Byzantium (T143), AI Swiss (T153), the Mongols (T169) and Japan (T172) signed peace with you while you sat at 5–7 VP. You rebuilt the hoard, took the goal back at T160, Most Advanced at T179 and era 6 at T182. (The Mongols re-declared at T172; another agent is investigating that flip.)
5. **T183–T202, Sparta arrives too late.** Sparta declared at T183 and took Thun (T187) and Bellinzona (T199) with a real army: proof that a committed AI could take your cities. But you finished the Grand Bazaar at T193 for 11 VP and held. Losing two small cities cost you 162 Gold of bounty and no VP.

## 3. Your edge: what you did that the AI doesn't

| Army strength T80 | 40 | Sparta 178, Mongols 174 | 94 |
| Settlers stolen / lost | 3 / 0 | | 0 / 1 (to you) |
| Units lost all game | 6 | Sparta 17 | 10 |

- **Expansion first, safely.** Three Settlers from the capital before anything else, Walls first in nearly every new city, and Settlers moved only when it was safe. The AIs settled slower and lost Settlers.
- **The Swiss signature, used the way it's meant to be.** Markets beelined, the hoard grown well past the 1,000 line so you could spend and still hold the goal, and Gold spent on emergencies (13 purchases: Walls, Libraries, ranged units at the door). The AI Swiss treats 1,000 as a ceiling, not a floor: it parks exactly at the threshold, so any war spending knocks its goal off (it lost it T106 and T141–T163), and it never builds the buffer that let you spend through a five-front war. Sims agree that the AI Swiss rarely hoards at all: at T150 in 8-empire games its median treasury is 151 Gold and only 8% of AI Swiss seats hold 1,000 or more (2026-10-17 interest-cap runs).
- **Interest dodges the rubber band.** Interest isn't a city yield, so decadence doesn't cut it and it doesn't count toward Power. Your 1,668 Gold of interest replaced about 80% of the 2,084 Gold decadence took. That's by design (the user's call on 2026-10-17), but it means a hoarding Swiss feels the rubber band less than anyone else.
- **Diplomacy without waste.** One war you chose (Germany, 7 turns, a Settler), then pure defence: you accepted every peace offer, so each coalition member dropped out as soon as it asked.
- **Era objectives.** Five of your 11 VP. You scored eras 2, 3, 4 and 6 first or tied first, because Expand/Build objectives (Markets, territory, Mills, population) are what a builder does anyway.

## 4. The AI's mistakes

**Strategic (the ones that decided it):**
1. **Nobody saw an economic runaway for 50 turns.** You had the Largest Economy crown from T23, the public reserve goal from T78 and a public Power lead big enough that decadence was cutting you 12–18%. The AIs' threat model scores conquest (captures, eliminations, armies at the border) and only adds the VP race once a leader passes 40% of the target. Every AI's top threat stayed Sparta until T132, when you reached 5 VP ("VP race +45"). The AIs spent T60–T125 proposing alliances against Sparta, not you.
2. **Wars declared without an army.** The T126–T132 wars came from Contain plans valued on "the leader's race". Each campaign started, found `insufficient_army` or `infeasible` and stopped within 1–3 turns (the AI Swiss switched plan 17 times between T130 and T186). Germany, Japan and Byzantium attacked you 2–3 times each in 70 turns. A war you can't prosecute costs you diplomacy and gives the leader nothing to fear.
3. **They signed peace with the leader.** Four of the five made peace while you were at 5–7 of 10 VP. Peace was priced as free again, as in 8.8.1.
4. **They aimed at the wrong cities.** Every campaign target was a 3–6 population border town (Fribourg, Lugano, Schaffhausen, Winterthur, Thun, Bellinzona; bounties 67–209 Gold). Nobody targeted Basel, where the capital doubling puts about 43% of your treasury (about 630 Gold) and the 2 VP goal.
5. **The strongest AI fought the wrong war.** Sparta named you its top threat at T132 but its plan scored a campaign on Byzantium's Nicaea higher (capture at 19%, T133–T171). Its threat score discounted you for distance (proximity 45%; capitals 22 hexes apart).
6. **Nobody contested the hold.** From T193 you held 11 with Most Advanced by one tech (6,027 against 6,026; ties keep the incumbent). Two techs for any AI, or Basel, would have dropped you below 10.

**Tactical:** Settlers left exposed (you stole three); the Mongols' T126 snipe missed and their three campaigns on Fribourg never assembled; Byzantium lost five units to bankruptcy while at war with you.

**Cheating-adjacent ideas, rejected:** letting the AI read your exact treasury (it's private; the public capture bounty is enough), targeting the human because he is human (target the leader by public VP and Power, whoever it is), or giving Hard production bonuses.

## 5. Was your win fair?

Yes. It was skill the AI can learn, plus some map luck:
- **Skill:** early expansion, safe Settlers, a coherent faction plan, Gold as insurance, taking every peace. All learnable from the AI's own information.
- **AI blind spots, not rules holes:** the late threat read, token wars, wrong targets, uncontested hold. All are AI decisions.
- **Luck:** your neighbours (the Mongols, the AI Swiss, Germany) were the weak half of the table; Sparta, the one conqueror, started 22 hexes away.
- **Not an exploit:** the hoard did what the design intends (it made you a target from T126 and cost you the goal for 27 turns). It just never got captured.

## 6. Smarter AI or a weaker leader?

**Short answer: in this game, mostly AI decisions, not the rubber bands. Roughly two-thirds of how close it got came from what the AIs did (their war pressure knocked your 2 VP reserve goal off and tied the race at T127 and T159), and about a third from the rubber bands (about 9 extra turns before you could finish). The bands didn't change who was likely to win, because they taxed Sparta almost as hard as you. Uncertainty is wide: treat the split as a direction, not a measurement.**

**What the rubber bands took (direct accounting from the replay):**

| | You | Sparta (the challenger) | Everyone else |
|---|---|---|---|
| Decadence, share of Production + Gold + Science cut, whole game | 20.5% (6,455 of 31,492) | 17.8% (3,720 of 20,872) | 0–4% |
| Decadence rate at T50 / T100 / T150 / T200 | 16% / 12% / 23% / 25% | 0% / 11% / 21% / 26% | |
| Crown upkeep paid | 610 Gold (Largest Economy, held from T23) | 181 Gold | 0–6 |
| Swiss interest earned (not cut by decadence) | 1,668 Gold | | AI Swiss 1,320 |
| AIs' research discount from techs you knew first (diffusion) | 3–6% of each AI's research | | |

- **In tempo:** your Grand Bazaar, the Great Work that took you to 11 VP, took 34 turns in Basel at a 24–26% decadence cut. Without the cut it would have taken about 25, so you'd have started your hold around T184 instead of T193. Over the whole game decadence cost you about 30 turns' worth of what you were producing at T150.
- **But it's two-sided.** The cut is measured against the median empire, so the challenger pays too. Sparta, the only AI that could catch you, lost 17.8% of its output, and the five small empires paid nothing. The band pulled both leaders toward the pack; it didn't hand Sparta the gap.
- **Interest is your escape hatch.** Interest replaced about 80% of the Gold decadence took from you, because it isn't a city yield. This is by design; it just means a hoarding Swiss feels the band least.

**Switching the bands off (what-if, from his own save).** Same states, 96 runs per arm, only the AI's dice changed; your seat played by the human-proxy playbook v4 (the one that reproduces your result best: it wins 85–88% from T100, T133 and T150 like you did) and by Hard:

| Branch turn | Seat | Bands on | Bands off | Change |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| T100 | proxy v4 | 88% | 72% | −16 (Sparta, untaxed, wins more) |
| T150 | proxy v4 | 85% | 100% | +15 |
| T172 | proxy v4 | 64% | 100% | +36 |
| T150 | Hard | 81% | 21% | −60 |
| T172 | Hard | 25% | 100% | +75 |

The sign flips with the turn and the seat: switching the bands off helps whichever of you and Sparta is better placed to use the extra output. Decadence and crown upkeep pull opposite ways late (from T172, decadence off alone takes your Hard seat to 100%; crown upkeep off alone, which frees Sparta's Strongest Army crown, takes it to 0%).

**The AI-smartness side (human-seat benchmark, 9 maps × 64 runs, rules fixed).** Reverting this release's post-8.8 AI genes (`troop_runway`, `restless_far_war`, `swiss_market_priority`, `crown_free_aware`, `sparta_search_only`):
- in the AI's own seat: −4.9 pp (CI −8.3 to −1.4): the 8.9 genes make Hard play its own empire better;
- in the opponents only: −1.7 pp (CI −5.4 to +1.9): no measurable change in how hard the AIs are to beat. Code-level AI changes since 8.8 (e.g. the Mongol assault logic) can't be reverted this way.
- For scale, the bands off for every seat: −1.2 pp (CI −5.0 to +2.6), also noise.
- **Your map added to the benchmark:** Hard playing your seat from T1 wins 0 of 64 in every arm. You won it. The gap between you and Hard in your own seat is still large.

So neither "smarter genes" nor "the bands" shows up as a big effect on the benchmark. What does show up, in the what-ifs below, is that single strategic decisions by the AIs swing your win chance by 15 to 87 points.

## 8. Coach's recommendations for the AI

Ranked by expected effect on how often you win on Hard. All three are AI decisions from public information (pillar 6).

1. **Treat the economic runaway as the main threat, and send the strongest army at it.**
   - *Mechanism:* add the public signals that already flagged you by T80 (VP progress from about 30% of the target, a crown held for 30+ turns, a live faction goal, the public Power lead that sets decadence) to the threat score's race term, and stop discounting the VP leader by distance for the empire with the strongest army.
   - *Code:* `crates/ai/src/threat.rs` (race term: `(progress - 40).max(0)`; proximity weighting), `crates/ai/src/plan.rs` (`contain_candidates`, `leader_race`), `crates/core/src/diplomacy/coalition.rs::target_view`.
   - *Evidence:* Sparta at T133 (−87 pp for proxy v4), coalition at T80 (−13 to −19 pp).
   - *Measure:* turn this game's T80 and T133 branches into regression scenarios; human-seat benchmark opponents-only (now with this map).
2. **A war on the leader is a commitment: no peace while he's near the target, and no token wars.**
   - *Mechanism:* price peace with a leader by the VP it lets him keep (the same asymmetry found in 8.8.1, where only war plans carried the cost of being attacked); declare only once a force that can take a named city is staged, and let coalition members pool on one target instead of five separate infeasible campaigns.
   - *Code:* `crates/ai/src/plan.rs` (peace plan terms, contain vs campaign switching), `crates/ai/src/conquest.rs`, `crates/ai/src/military.rs::campaign`.
   - *Evidence:* T126 no-peace branch (−16 pp Hard, −43 pp proxy v6); campaign end reasons `insufficient_army`/`infeasible` within 1–3 turns all game; the AI Swiss switched plan 17 times T130–T186.
   - *Measure:* the T126 branch; war-scenario suite; human-seat benchmark opponents-only.
3. **Aim at what the leader's VP hangs on, and contest the hold.**
   - *Mechanism:* value a Swiss capital's doubled capture bounty and the goal loss in target choice (`hoard_denial_value` only fires per city and was dwarfed by distance); before and during a hold, value each of the leader's VP sources by its cheapest removal (a one-tech crown by research, the capital by siege).
   - *Code:* `crates/ai/src/victory.rs::hoard_denial_value`, `crates/ai/src/conquest.rs` target choice, `crates/ai/src/hold.rs`, research valuation in `crates/ai/src/development.rs`.
   - *Evidence:* every campaign aimed at a 3–6 population town; Most Advanced held by one tech; untested in branches (needs a target hook), and T193 shows it must happen before the hold.
   - *Measure:* a T150 branch with the target hook; benchmark.

**Also worth fixing (smaller):** Settler escorts (you stole three); the AI Swiss parks at exactly 1,000 Gold, so any spending loses its goal: it should hold a buffer above the line, as you did (`victory.rs::hoard_held`).

**Design questions for you, not AI fixes:**
- Decadence is measured against the median empire, so it taxes the runner-up nearly as hard as the leader (Sparta 17.8%, you 20.5%). If the rubber band is meant to keep #2 in the race, it doesn't do that today; it compresses both toward the pack. Worth a look, not a removal.
- Swiss interest isn't cut by decadence (your call on 2026-10-17, unchanged here): it replaced 80% of what decadence took from your Gold.
