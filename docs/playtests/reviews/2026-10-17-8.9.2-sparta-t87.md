## Coach's verdict

You're right on both counts, but the first count isn't the one you named.
- **(a) The AI's Sparta does under-play the strategy, but not by building too few units.** Put the Hard AI in your seat from turn 1 and it declares its first war on T18, the same turn you did, with nearly the same army: 3 units to your 4 at T20, 5 to your 8 at T40. What it can't do is turn that army into cities. By T87 it has taken 1.1 cities on average to your 7.
  - **It picks the wrong victim.** Its first-war code only lets a one-city Sparta attack Japan, or a Byzantine city within 7 hexes. Nicaea, the city you took first, is 8 hexes away, and the Swiss are never allowed.
  - **It makes peace.** About 2 peace treaties by T87, against your none.
  - **It doesn't storm cities the way you do.**
  
  Even handed your exact T20 position (army, war with Byzantium, Nicaea half dead), it takes Byzantium like you did, then stalls at 3.4 captures and loses a city of its own.
- **(b) AI defenders really don't defend properly.** Every city you took was unwalled, with one defender or none. Three of them had Walls half or nearly built when you arrived, and swapped them for a Ranger. Kyoto was 52 of 60 Production into its Walls.
  - Five AIs declared war on you, but between them they had 7 units at T30. Not one of them attacked a city of yours.
  - The rules aren't missing the counter. Where an AI did build it, you lost: walled Tara cost you 4 Hoplites in two turns (T47–48), and the Mongols' three or more shooters at Sarai killed 6 of your units in T69–81. You took neither city.

So the rush works because both sides fail at once:
- the AI attacker doesn't choose the soft target, doesn't stay at war, and doesn't commit melee to an unwalled city;
- the AI defender drops its Walls for a Ranger and fields one unit per city.

One telling detail: the same defenders survived the same rush when an AI ran it. With an AI Sparta or AI Mongols forced to declare on Byzantium and Switzerland at your turns, Byzantium and Switzerland were alive at T87 in 30 of 30 runs. AI defence is tuned against AI attackers.

**Pillar 10 holds in the rules: the counter exists (units first, then Walls and two shooters). The AI doesn't execute it.** One design question remains for you, on Byzantium (section 6).

## 4. The AI's mistakes

**Strategic:**
1. **Hard-coded first targets for AI Sparta** (`crates/ai/src/conquest.rs`, the one-city filter, and the matching `military.rs` sorties). With one city it may only target a Japan (Japan) city, or an unwalled Byzantium (Byzantium) city within 7 hexes.
   - In this game that ruled out the two best targets.
   - It breaks the design rule that AI behaviour comes from value scoring, not faction-name rules.
2. **Peace after a capture.** AI Sparta signs peace in about half the runs within 5 turns of taking a city, so the victim gets time to build Walls.
3. **AI Sparta builds capital Walls at T20.** That's about one Hoplite less, while no rival army stood near its capital.
4. **Contain wars with no army.** Five AIs at war with you for 35–60 turns. Their only offensive was the Mongols' campaign on Sellasia: 5 starts (T50–T86), each ended `insufficient_army`. Alliance offers between your enemies were refused every time (T23, T24, T55, T56, T57, T62, T84, T86).
5. **The AIs fought each other while you ate them.** The Swiss had three wars at once (T18–T28); Japan sniped a Swiss Settler at T25.

**Tactical:**
1. **Walls swapped for a shooter** (`crates/ai/src/threat.rs::plan_threat_defense`).
   - When no shooter stands within one hex of an exposed, unwalled city, it orders a Ranger, even over Walls already in progress. A garrison stepping out is enough to trigger it.
   - The `keep_walls` gene only guards the separate emergency path in `production.rs`.
   - Nicaea (25 of 60, T20), Kyoto (52 of 60, T32) and Edo (33 of 60, T39) all lost their Walls this way and fell 2–7 turns later.
2. **One defender per city.** At capture, six cities had one unit and Edo had none.
3. **The Swiss spent 138 Gold on a Ranger at T24 instead of Walls.** It had 184 Gold, and your army had just taken Nicaea, five hexes from Thun.
4. **What worked:** the Mongols shot your wounded units at Sarai and the Celts' walled Tara shot back. AI defence is fine once it has Walls or 3+ shooters.

**Cheating-adjacent ideas, rejected:**
- "Target the human."
- Letting defenders see your production queue.
- Giving Hard free Walls.

## 5. Was the win fair?

Mostly skill the AI can learn, helped by the AI's blind spots and some map luck.
- **Skill:** commit to one plan, pick the softest target, focus fire, never give peace, and use Sparta's heal-on-kill. A value-scoring AI can do all of it from public information.
- **AI blind spots, not rules holes:**
  - the hard-coded target list;
  - Walls swapped out mid-build;
  - peace after a capture;
  - token coalition wars.
- **Luck:** your three nearest neighbours were the three AIs worst placed against an early rush.
  - Byzantium: levy cap, and no Gold to buy peace.
  - Switzerland: pays upkeep per unit beyond two, and was at war with three AIs.
  - Japan: 2.9% AI-only win rate, the weakest AI.
  
  Germany, the strongest AI here (it wins 13–17 of 30 AI-only continuations of this map), started 22 hexes away.

## 6. Coach's recommendations, ranked by expected effect on your win rate on Hard

Every one is a value or priority change from public information, never a bonus. How to measure each one:
- **This game's what-if branches:** rerun the five branches below and compare captures by T87 and peace treaties.
- **A new war scenario from this game,** "Sparta 8.9.2 first strike": T18, take Nicaea by T26. You took it in 4 turns.
- **The human-seat benchmark,** seat-only and opponents-only.
- **The war-scenario suite** (`tools/sim/war-scenarios.sh`); Sparta's First Blood must stay at 100%.

1. **AI Sparta picks its first target by value, not by faction name** (`crates/ai/src/conquest.rs`, the one-city filter; `military.rs` Byzantium and Japan sorties; `production.rs` assault-wing triggers around lines 1272 and 1298). Score every reachable city:
   - its public softness: Walls, visible garrison, the public levy cap and the owner's treasury against the tribute price;
   - march turns;
   - the owner's army and its other wars;
   - an elimination bonus for a last city.
   
   This merges with idea #15a ("pick the softest city") and replaces the branch `claude/japan-diagnosis` workaround.
   - Expected: AI Sparta's captures by T87 in this game go from 1.1 to 3 or more.
   - Measure: branch `t1-hard`, the new scenario, and seat-only on the benchmark.
2. **Walls under way are never swapped for a shooter** (`crates/ai/src/threat.rs::plan_threat_defense`).
   - Count a shooter within two hexes as present.
   - If Walls are in progress, keep building them and buy the rest at exposure ≥ 2, as `keep_walls` already does in `production.rs`.
   - Train the shooter only in a city with neither.
   
   This makes Hard harder for you as the attacker, so it measures as opponents-only on the benchmark. Also measure Byzantium, Switzerland and Japan's survival in branch `t1-proxy` (the scripted rusher) and in your seat replayed from the tape where the branch allows.
3. **A conqueror stays at war while it's winning** (`crates/ai/src/diplomacy.rs::wants_peace`, the plan's peace candidate). No peace for N turns after a capture while a soft city of the same enemy lies inside the march window (idea #15c). Test the existing `sparta_peace_guard` (default 0), and check against `peace_credibility`, which just landed on master.
   - Measure: peace treaties by T87 (1.9 now) and captures in branches `t1-hard` and `t20-hard`.
4. **Sparta storms unwalled cities with melee** (`crates/ai/src/city_assault.rs::city_assault_succeeds`, `opportunity.rs::forecast_siege`). Two changes to the siege forecast for an Sparta melee wing:
   - credit wound immunity and kill healing;
   - credit the split counterattack (`apply_stage`), so 3–4 Hoplites commit on an unwalled 1–2 defender city as you do, instead of waiting for shooters.
   
   Measure: the new scenario and `t20-hard` (3.4 captures now; your 7).
5. **Lower priority:**
   - Skip capital Walls for an attacking Sparta when no rival army is within reach. That's the `rush_walls` threat, tested as a per-faction gene: one more Hoplite at T20.
   - Make coalition wars pool on one target (idea #17).

**Design questions for you (not AI fixes):**
- **Byzantium against an early rusher.** A one-city Byzantium may field only 2 armed units (thin levies). Its counter, bought peace, costs 10% of the attacker's army value, which was more than its 17–20 Gold at T18. As things stand, an early Byzantium neighbour is free food for a T18 rush, whatever the AI does. Is that intended? Options:
  - leave it (it's Byzantium's drawback);
  - a minimum tribute it can always afford in the first third;
  - +1 levy while at war in the first third.
- **One-city empires at T18.** Every AI still has one city when your rush lands, so each capture is an elimination. In AI-only games the per-faction median is 1–2 cities at T25 and 2–3 at T50. Better AI expansion is the AI fix. Whether the start should give a second Settler (the pillar-10 note "start with more Settlers") is your call.
