# Aztecs, 8.9.29: three Swiss towns, eight walled cities, and a coalition that never attacked a wall

Playtest 8.9.29 (`playtest-8.9.29`, engine `0b0c1924f`, rules version 25). Six empires on a 48x32 map, every AI on Hard: Sparta, Byzantium, the Celts, India and Switzerland. The first game on the new Hard AI: training round 2 tactics, the siege-forecast calibration (`siege_stance` 6, `forecast_garrison_holds` scoped to the stance, `stance_home_guard`) and the hidden-wariness coalition (`runaway_coalition` 55). You lit the New Fire on T107 with 11 of 10 VP and won on T116. Byzantium had 2 VP, the Celts 4, India 3, Sparta 0, Switzerland was gone. 29 active minutes in one session (0.1 to 0.4 minutes a turn).

The replay at the tag reproduces the game exactly: 116 turns, 0 hash mismatches, every AI order matched. The coalition numbers below come from a throwaway trace of `Planner::wariness`, the bloc draws and the second-front gate, printed for every AI on every turn.

VP race and cities (archived outside the repository)

## Coach's verdict

You won with **6 to 8 military units** and **8 to 10 of your 11 cities empty**, and no AI ever took a city you cared about. The new coalition did what it was built to do on paper:

- Every AI marked you as the runaway from T42.
- Their hidden wariness of you sat at 48 to 82 from T50 to the end.
- Three AIs ran strike operations on your cities.

None of it turned into a city hit on a walled town. Hard attacked your cities 10 times in 116 turns. Sparta hit once (T74). Byzantium hit nine times, and took two new colonies of 1 to 3 population, without Walls, during your hold. Neither colony was worth a VP.

The Celts are the clearest failure of the game. From T99 to T114 a Celtic strike had **7 of its 8 units within three tiles of St. Gallen**: seven Spearmen, a Knight and two Composite Archers. St. Gallen had no Walls and one Spearman inside. They made **0 attacks** in 15 turns. From T98 to T116 the same army stood three to nine tiles from **Solothurn**, your Hagia Sophia city. Solothurn had Walls, no military unit, and 3 of your 11 VP (the Great Work and the Swiss capital). Taking it, or sacking it, would have broken your hold. The Celts never moved on it.

Hard in your seat can't do what you did from the start (0 of 48 from T2 and T20). From T65 it wins two games in three. The game was decided between **T55 and T70**, when you grew from 4 cities to 8 and nobody contested it. You said so yourself: "pretty early. i jsut kept conquering and getting points and nobody stopped me."

## 3. Hard's siege and defence: new behaviour or the old failures?

**Mostly the old failures.** Checked against the Hold Biel and Nicomedia entries (`docs/learnings.md`, 2026-11-07 and 2026-11-08):

- **Never attacking walled cities: still true.** Your walled, empty cities (Tlatelolco, Chalco, Solothurn, Tlacopan) had Spartan or Celtic units parked three tiles out for 10 to 20 turns and took 1 hit in the game. Three tiles is just outside a walled city's strike, so the staging ring is safe, and it stays the stance's resting place. The siege stance's job is to step in when the forecast says the storm wins. Against a walled city with an era lead it never says so.
- **Never attacking an unwalled city with an obsolete army: new evidence.** St. Gallen above.
- **Byzantium storms weak cities, and it did.** Colony 8 (pop 1, no Walls, 200 → 0 HP in 4 hits over two turns) and Colony 7 (pop 3, no Walls, 5 hits over two turns). The campaign valued them on "supply" (+2,552 and +1,480), not on VP. **It chose the two cities worth 0 VP over Solothurn, worth 3** and seven to eleven tiles away.
- **Wrong purchases with a big treasury: not seen; nobody had a treasury.** The richest AI was Byzantium at 338 Gold (T102). Sparta bought Spearmen (era 1) for Therapne in era 4. Sparta spent turns on Wonders in a capital under siege: Therapne queued the Great Library, then Hagia Sophia, while you stood four tiles out.
- **Last garrison leaving: seen at Therapne.** It had no unit inside on T84-T88 and T90-T91 while five Spartan Spearmen stood one to four tiles away. It was regarrisoned from T92, too late for the Musketmen.
- **Shooters on the wrong targets: not measurable here;** Sparta had one Archer near Therapne.
- **Sparta's research collapsed** (Science 3 to 4 a turn from T100, 15 techs against your 23). Sparta defended its capital with Spearmen against your Musketmen. That's the era gap the curve was designed to punish.

**Your own siege, by contrast:** you waited 13 turns (T84 to T96) three to five tiles from walled Therapne until the Gold upgrades to Musketmen were in, then stormed for four straight turns. That's exactly what Hard's stance is meant to do, and Hard's Celts didn't do it because their forecast said no. Yours said yes because you had upgraded first.

## 7. What would have stopped you (and the AI fixes)

Ranked by how directly they would have cut this win. Each fix is AI-only and uses public information.

1. **Price a city by the VP it holds, and send the coalition's strike there** (`runaway.rs::second_front_why` and the campaign target in `conquest.rs`). Solothurn, empty, walled, 3 VP, sat seven to nine tiles from ten Celtic units and seven to eleven from Byzantium's six during the whole hold. A strike that ranks cities by `need/power` alone picks the weakest city, and the weakest cities are worth nothing. During a hold the VP a capture removes should dominate. Gate: the new `byzantium-break-the-fire` card, then the designer seat.
2. **A strike that can't win should upgrade, call in help or leave, not wait 15 turns** (`operation.rs`, the stance in `city_assault.rs`). The Celtic operation on St. Gallen forecast 6% for its whole budget. Either spend the budget on upgrades (the Celts couldn't afford them) or retarget after a few turns with no forecast capture. Gate: the war suite and a combat-lab drill with an obsolete besieger.
3. **Make the bloc affordable when the threat is real** (`diplomacy.rs` alliance scoring, the upkeep term). India and the Celts refused each other on a 2 to 5 Gold upkeep while both were at wariness 50 to 70. Scale the fee's weight down by the shared wariness, or let a bloc at Peace skip the upkeep until a war starts. **Design question for you:** should an Alliance against a runaway cost Gold at all?
4. **End the side war that blocks the bloc** (`diplomacy.rs` Peace scoring). India refused Sparta's Peace nine times in a war that was going nowhere. With a shared runaway, Peace between the two members should get the runaway's VP lead as a bonus.
5. **Era-aware defence for the weak capital** (`production.rs`). Sparta bought era-1 Spearmen and queued Wonders in a besieged capital, at 3 Science a turn. That's the "obsolete armies" row again.
