# Germany, a VP win on T169 (Playtest 8.9.43, rules 36), 2026-11-15

VP race and treasury (archived outside the repository)

## Coach's verdict

He won on T169 with 10 VP to Byzantium's 5, in one 29-minute session (34 minutes of wall clock). He scored nothing until T110, then 9 VP in 29 turns: the Foundry goal (T110), Hagia Sophia (T112), and four points from the four Aztec cities taken T118 to T135. The Aztecs started that war, not him: they declared on T111 against a neighbour with **2 military units for 9 cities, an empty unwalled capital six tiles from Chalco, and 2,097 Gold in the bank**, and never attacked once. Two turns later he turned 2,041 of that Gold into three Cavalry and took Tlatelolco five turns after that.

His point about money is right, and it is the clearest valuation error in the AI across all his games. In 25 games since 2026-10-25 his median treasury was 461 Gold and his median peak 2,842; the median Hard seat sat at 72 and peaked at 241. That is not only because he earns more (his median net income was 33 Gold a turn, Hard's 7): Hard spends 99% of what it nets as it arrives, mostly on one upgrade at a time, and **243 of the 265 units Hard bought in those games were Ancient-era Spearmen, Archers and Horsemen**, 164 of them after T120. His 51 spending bursts (300+ Gold, at least 40% of the treasury in one turn) were followed by a capture within 10 turns 30 times; Hard's 33 bursts, once. The replays below show the gap is in what Hard buys and when, more than in how much it has: giving the Aztecs or India 1,300 to 2,600 extra Gold changed nothing, because Hard spent it on Siege for another war, single upgrades, Mines and Spearmen; and even a burst forced into the threatened cities bought only obsolete units (the Aztecs had no Niter, India no Iron or Coal) and saved no city. In this game a bank would have paid as an attack on him while he had two units, not as a defence against his tanks (scenario `aztecs-take-cologne`).

The Machine Age did what he said: no "god tech" moment. The Aztec war was won with Industrial Cavalry before anyone had Combustion; the Indian war with Early Tanks and Panzers against Musketmen and Cuirassiers. No AI ever fielded a Biplane, Tank, Fighter or Bomber.

The game was not in doubt for long. Hard playing his seat wins 12 of 24 from T2, 18 from T40, 21 from T80 and every run from T120 (median win T181 to T208). This seat on this map was strong for Hard too, and he was faster with it.

### 1b. The rule Hard follows, and why the line is flat

- **The reserve is capped by income.** `crates/ai/src/gold_hold.rs::held_gold` (gene `buy_hold` 3): routine purchases leave Gold for the next upgrade bill, one or two cheapest defenders while at War, and the dearest unit for up to four cities, but the total is capped at `INCOME_TURNS` = 25 turns of net income (`gold_per_turn.total × 25`). At Hard's median net of 7 a turn the cap is 175 Gold; the measured 90th-percentile treasury is 24 turns of income. Hard cannot bank a war chest by construction.
- **The passes that spend don't see the hold.** Per the gene's own comment, the upgrade, rescue, door, threat, victory, goal and hoard passes spend through it. Upgrades are bought one unit at a time whenever a unit's next form is researched and the Gold is there (the 1,032 single upgrades across the 25 games), so the treasury is drained every few turns, long before any war.
- **The army buy is the cheapest unit.** The emergency quote in `held_gold` is the minimum `buy_cost` among military units; `hard_defense.rs::second_troop_buy` takes `min_by_key(cost)` among buyable troops; `production.rs` (the troop strength check near line 1100) also takes the cheapest military option. Every era's units stay buildable, so the cheapest is always the Ancient Spearman or Archer. That is where the 243 Ancient-era purchases come from.
- **The decisive buy waits for a reserve that never forms.** `decisive_buy.rs` (gene `decisive_buy` 1) prices one unit against a live war's capture odds and must leave the deficit reserve; in the takeover traces it was gated by "NotEnoughGold" on 9 seat-turns and by "earlier military purchase spent this turn's buy slot" on 10.
- **Net income is low because the army is paid for all game.** India paid more upkeep T100 to T149 (520) than it netted all game (382); the Celts paid 1,229 upkeep over the game against his 239. Hard's army-quota rules keep 6 to 13 units through peace (the "army it never uses" pattern), so there is little left to save even with a different reserve rule.

### 1d. How Hard should save (proposal, not implemented)

One idea: **a named fund tied to the plan, released in a burst.** Hard keeps one savings target at a time, chosen from its own plan and its own sight, and stops routine Gold spending (single upgrades, improvements, buildings) while the fund is short:
1. **Campaign army.** With a Campaign or Contain plan chosen, the fund is the purchase that flips the forecast for the target: the current era's best units of the class the forecast lacks (`decisive_buy.rs` already prices this; the fund is what lets it fire). Spent the turn the force reaches staging range.
2. **Upgrade wave.** When a unit technology is under research and due within N turns, the fund is the whole upgrade bill for the units it will upgrade; spent the turn it lands, all at once (his 7 upgrades in one turn), instead of 1 a turn for 20 turns.
3. **Emergency defence fund.** Sized to the nearest visible army that can reach a city (his rule from the Byzantium game: distance decides), at two of the era's best defenders for the most exposed city; spent the turn that army is within 3 tiles. Never the cheapest unit: the cheapest unit that can survive one turn against what is visible.
4. **The cap is in turns of the plan, not of income.** Drop the 25-turns-of-income cap while a fund is named; keep a ceiling of what the fund costs.

**Scenario to test it (short, disposable):** the T111 Aztec position with the Aztecs' pre-war upgrade Gold returned as a treasury (1,300) and Germany's three bought Cavalry on the board at T113: "keep Tlatelolco, Chalco and Texcoco for 15 turns". Hard today against Hard (in his seat) and against his recorded orders; then the fund rule. Success: the fund rule holds the three cities in most runs where today's Hard loses at least one, and the benchmark shows no seat-only loss (the money it no longer drips into upgrades has a cost in peacetime).

## 3. Where the 169 turns went

- **The shape:** 0 VP for 109 turns, then 9 VP in 29 turns, then 20 turns at 9 waiting for one more capital. Five of his ten points came from captured cities, four of them from the Aztec war alone; two of those were old-era objectives (eras 2 and 3) that stayed open from T16 and T53 until his first walled capture paid them.
- **Against the 250-turn target:** Hard in his seat finishes at a median T181 to T208 from any branch turn (takeover curve), so even Hard closes this map well before T250; he closed it 12 to 40 turns faster than Hard. The rivals peaked at 5 VP (Byzantium: Pyramids, the Heir goal, one objective). The game is short because one empire converts captures into VP quickly and no rival scores more than half the target, not because of the era count.
- **When it stopped being in doubt** (Hard taking his seat from his exact position, 24 runs each, `takeover_gap.py`): 12 of 24 wins at T2, 14 at T20, 18 at T40, 19 at T60, 21 at T80, 19 at T100, 20 at T110, **24 at T120**, 21 at T130, 18 at T140, 24 at T150. ## 5. Five Hard changes, ranked

Ranked by expected effect on how often he wins on Hard with any faction. Each names the evidence, the expected effect and a short scenario. None is implemented.

1. **Save toward a named fund; spend it in a burst; never buy the cheapest obsolete unit.** (`gold_hold.rs::held_gold`, `decisive_buy.rs`, `hard_defense.rs::second_troop_buy`, the cheapest-troop pick in `production.rs`, the single-upgrade path.)
