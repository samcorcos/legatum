# Byzantium, a win by elimination (Playtest 8.9.40 AI, rules 36), 2026-11-15

VP race (archived outside the repository)

## Coach's verdict

He won on T181 as the last team standing, 12 VP to Sparta's 4, in one 62-minute session. The game was decided between T100 and T140 (Hard in his seat wins 6 of 24 from T100, 14 from T120, 20 from T140), by the conquest of Athens and the tech jump that followed; after Piraeus and Biel fell (T140, T142) it took 41 more turns and 21 more captures to finish. His own account holds up against the tape: Sparta was 28 to 34 hexes away all game and never came near him before he went to it, his "early attention on Athens" was one Horseman and two Scouts that sniped Athens's first Settler on T17 (and the same unit, as a Knight, its next one on T96), and the rest of his first 100 turns was population, Science and Gold behind **two military units for up to nine cities**. No AI punished that. He used none of Byzantium's specials: no Mercenary Company, no Heir, no melee-line unit; the thin-levy cap never came within seven of binding.

**This game was not played on the 8.9.41 AI.** The save says "Playtest 8.9.40", the app that recorded it never restarted (one session, from 12:15 to 13:17 UTC; the 8.9.41 app was installed at 12:46 UTC under the running one), and the replay proves it: the 8.9.40 AI reproduces every AI order on the tape (0 strategic-order mismatches over 181 turns), the 8.9.41 AI does not (46). Neither 8.9.41 fix could show in the result. What the tape can show is where they would have fired, below.

Not run in this pass (scoped by the lead to the six questions): the decision diff, rubber-band accounting, what-if branches and a Scenario Lab cut. The takeover curve was run (section 1).

## 1. How and when it ended

- **End:** T181, "Player 1 is the last team standing" (Sparta's last city, Pylos, fell that turn). His 10-turn VP hold had started on T177 at 12 VP (target 10), so a VP win would have come on T187: elimination ended it 6 turns sooner.
- **Play time:** 62 minutes wall clock in one session. The turn log stops at T150 (32 active minutes for T1 to T150: 0.2 min a turn in eras 1 to 4, 0.3 in eras 5 to 7); T151 to T181 took 15 minutes of wall clock, about 0.5 min a turn, the slowest stretch of the game.
- **VP race:** nobody scored until Sparta's Pyramids (T57, 2 VP). He led from Hagia Sophia (T113, 3 to 2 by T119) and was never behind again: 5 to 3 at T140, 6 to 5 at T150, 9 to 6 at T170, 12 to 4 at the end. Sparta was the only rival above 2 VP after T140.
- **When it stopped being in doubt:** between T100 and T140. At T120 he had 15 cities to the best AI's 8, population 79 to 51, Science 85 to 53, army strength 951 to Sparta's 1,012. At T140 he had 17 cities to 10, techs 30 to 25 and army 2,364 to 1,956, and had just taken Piraeus (T140) and Biel (T142), two of the five original capitals. **The takeover curve agrees** (Hard at the 8.9.41 tag playing his seat from his exact position, 24 runs per turn to T220, `tools/decision-audit/takeover_gap.py`): his seat wins 3 of 24 from T2, 4 from T20, 2 from T40, 7 from T60, 9 from T80, 6 from T100, **14 from T120 and 20 from T140**. Before T100 Sparta is the likeliest winner from his position (11 to 16 of 24 at T20 to T40, 13 at T100); the steepest rise is T100 to T140, the Athenian conquest and the tech jump. So the early snipe and the 2-unit economy set the game up but did not decide it for Hard, and pillar 11 held to about T120.
- **Decided to over: about 41 turns (T140 to T181).** In them he captured 21 cities (Piraeus T140, Biel T142, Aarau T146, Laurion T147, Lucerne and Basel T149, Phaleron T153, Alesia T157, Bibracte T159, Celts Colony 7 T160, Emain Macha and Khanbaliq T161, Shangdu T167, Sellasia T169, Sparta Colony 8 and Amyclae T171, Sparta and Gytheio T172, Sparta Colony 7 T173, Therapne T177, Pylos T181), razed three of them by his own order (Zürich T140, Piraeus T141, Aarau T147), and took five Settlers (T158, T163, T167, T178, T180). Two of the eliminations hung on a Settler: the Celts' last unit was Settler 209, which sat on one tile at (28,13) for seven turns (T160 to T167) until his tank took it; the Mongols lost Khanbaliq on T161, founded Shangdu with a Settler on T164 and were out only when he took it on T167.
- **Original capitals (for the capitals-victory rule):** Piraeus (Athens) T140 by him, razed by his order T141; Biel (Switzerland) T142 by him; Karakorum (Mongols) T133 **by Sparta**, razed by Sparta T166; Bibracte (Celts) T159 by him; Therapne (Sparta) T177 by him. He never held every original capital at once: one he razed himself, one was captured and razed by Sparta. **No rival held its own original capital from T177**, 4 turns before the real end and 10 before the VP hold would have closed. A "take all the capitals" rule needs two definitions this game exercises: a razed capital, and a capital another rival took. Counting "every rival has lost its original capital" ends this game on T177. Counting only capitals he holds never ends it.
- **What a capitals rule would have saved here:** 4 turns, Pylos and the two Spartan Settlers. The longer chase (T150 to T177, 15 cities and three Settlers) was on the road to Therapne, 34 hexes from his capital, and he needed Therapne anyway: its capture gave him his 10th and 11th VP (capital +1, the captured Pyramids +2).

## 3. Byzantium's specials: what he used and what it says

Rules 36 Byzantium: Mercenary Companies (hire a met, non-warring empire's unit for Gold at 100% of its Production cost, 30 turns, outside the levy and the supply), thin levies (at most one armed unit per city plus one), the Tagmatic Soldier (+4 strength and +10 Production on the Pikeman and every later melee-line unit), and the Heir (800 Production, held for 2 VP).

| Special | His use | Would using it have beaten what he did? |
|---|---|---|
| Mercenary Companies | **0 hires.** He bought 7 military units at 200% instead (2 Cuirassiers T101, 3 Cavalry T129, 2 Riflemen T150). | Probably a little, not decisively. The same 7 units hired at 100% would have cost about half (about 2,100 Gold saved on the 4,232 he spent on units), but a Company leaves after 30 turns, can't be upgraded, and only sells what a seller at Peace can field. His bought units were the ones he kept and upgraded into his tank army (Cavalry to Landship to Tank to MBT), which a Company can't do. He also ended with 4,858 Gold unspent, so Gold was never his limit. Design signal: the Companies don't fit a player who keeps a small veteran army and upgrades it; they fit a player who needs a burst army now. |
| Thin levies | Never close to binding: 2 units against a cap of 2 to 9 to T100, 11 against 38 at the end. | The drawback cost him nothing. A drawback that never binds doesn't push the faction anywhere (pillar 8). With 1 to 3 units most of the game he would have played the same under any faction. |
| Tagmatic Soldier | **Never used.** His one melee-line unit was the starting Spearman upgraded once to a Pikeman (T74 to T80); everything else was mounted. | Unknown; his mounted army lost 4 units in 181 turns, so there was nothing to fix. The bonus is on the line the AIs' cities are defended with (Spearmen, Pikemen, Musketmen) and the triangle gives mounted units no edge against them; he won anyway with 64 kills to 4 losses. Design signal: the unique unit doesn't shape his army. |
| The Heir | **Never trained.** He set Biel (T150) and two other cities (T159) to the Heir and switched away each time. | No. He needed 10 VP and got there by conquest at T177 with capitals and captured Great Works; an Heir (800 Production, 2 held VP) at T150 would have moved his hold start by a few turns at most. The Heir is the faction's only goal and he won without it, as in his 8.9.37 game. |

His own read was right: "I didn't really use any special abilities ... but that's probably ok." For the faction designer: this is the second Byzantine win in a row with none of the kit. Byzantium's drawback (thin levies) never binds for a player who keeps 2 units, and its strengths (hired armies, a melee unique unit, a 2-VP civilian) all reward a style he did not need. That is a `legatum-factions` question for the user, not an AI fix.

## 4. What Hard did wrong against him

Every claim names the turn and what the AI could see. "Replayed" means traced on his tape at the 8.9.40 tag with every AI order reproduced; "untested" means not measured by a branch.

**Athens (the snowballer he targeted).** Athens never snowballed: one city until T37, 3 at T50 (he had 5), 5 at T80 to T120, 0 at T158. Its effective strategy was "Economy" with no army: 1 military unit at T20 and T50, 4 at T80.
1. **T15 to T17, the first Settler (replayed).** Athens's Settler left Piraeus on T15 for a site two tiles north. On T16 the plain-Hard escort guard (`hard_defense.rs::guard_exposed_settlers_duel`) read the trip as threatened only by fog (`snipe_fog_risk` 90, triggered by Sparta's T15 surprise war on the Celts, an empire Athens had never seen), found no escort in reach this turn and one in reach next turn, and made the Settler wait in the open, two tiles from Piraeus, while Soldier 18 walked over. The wait tile was checked only against **visible** units (`settler_capturable_this_turn`), and Athens saw none of his units within 6 hexes; his Horseman stood 3 tiles away in fog. On T17 Athens saw the Horseman adjacent and sent the Settler home, the escort stepping the other way onto the tile; he declared and took it the same turn.
2. **T18 to T96, Economy beside an empty neighbour (replayed plans; the attack is untested).** Athens planned "Peace with Byzantium" T18 to T30, then Economy T31 to T96. He had 2 military units for 5 to 8 cities, most of them empty, 4 to 9 hexes from Athens's cities from T80. Athens's threat read of him was 42 to 50 ("declared 1 wars, +3 cities") and Athens named no top threat until T97, then Sparta, and him only from T101. No campaign was ever priced on his empty cities. Whether Athens with 4 to 5 units could have taken one is untested.
3. **T86 to T96, the production alternation (replayed).** Athens's city 24 was set eight times in T86 to T96 and switched six times in six turns (T91 to T96: Knight, Crossbowman, Knight, Crossbowman, Knight, Crossbowman, Walls); Athens completed one military unit in T80 to T99. This is the alternation the 8.9.41 production fix targets, and the one-turn replay at 8.9.41 confirms the fix would have acted here: it drops Athens's switches on T86, T88 and T90 to T96 (below).
4. **T88 to T96, the second Settler (replayed).** See section 6, group B.
5. **T96 to T158, a war it never fought (replayed plans).** Athens held Contain on him from T101, at values that flipped between -152 and +293, then a Campaign to retake Eleusis T132 to T140 at value 0 or below. One hit on his cities in 62 turns at War (T125), 24 attacks on his units. It refused his T101 Peace (he had one Knight and two Cuirassiers) and he refused its T119 Peace after Marathon. Its Alliance with Switzerland (T113) was the only coalition that formed against him in time; Switzerland declared on him (T111), took and razed Ravenna (T132 to T133) and was eliminated by T149.

**The other AIs, in one line each.**
- **Sparta** (closest on VP, 5 to his 6 at T150): conquered the Mongols and Celts (Karakorum T133, Lugdunum T143, Tara T145, Gergovia T147) with the game's biggest army, at Peace with him until he declared on T164. It named him top threat from T120 but never planned against him before T165. The "strongest AI busy elsewhere" pattern again.
- **Switzerland:** declared T111, 10 city hits, took Ravenna T132; out T149.
- **Celts:** about 1,100 Gold unspent at T140; declared on him T152 with 7 units within 4 hexes of his cities, 2 city hits; no city from T161, alive on a Rifleman and two Settlers until T167.
- **Mongols:** lost Sarai to Sparta T51 and Karakorum T133; never fought him; out T167.

**The two 8.9.41 fixes.** He played 8.9.40, so neither was in the game. Replaying the 8.9.41 AI one turn at a time on his tape (the same world each turn) shows where they would have acted:
- **Production finish bonus:** different production or purchases on 42 seat-turns across all five AIs, mostly dropping mid-build switches: Athens T86 to T96 (city 24's Knight/Crossbowman flips), Sparta T125 to T142 (Cuirassier, Cavalry and Field Cannon switches in city 6). Its effect on the game is untested.
- **No Peace while an offensive pays:** one change against him: on T169, the turn Sparta took Emain Macha from him, 8.9.40 Sparta offered him Peace (he refused) and 8.9.41 Sparta would not have. It also changes Sparta's T78 to T84 diplomacy with the Celts (8.9.41 accepts and offers Peace there). Neither would likely have changed his result: he refused every Peace offered after T30.

## 5. Playbook candidates from this game

- **His milestone, the distance rule:** with the nearest rush-capable rival (Sparta) 28+ hexes away and at war elsewhere, he ran 2 military units to T95 for up to 9 cities and spent it all on Settlers (13), Walls (8 of 10 cities by T100) and growth. Hard has no such rule: AI defence quotas don't read the distance to the nearest army that could reach a city, so Hard in a safe seat over-builds units (the 8.9.30 economy-gap row).
- **The AIs never priced a hollow neighbour.** Athens, 4 to 9 hexes away with 4 to 5 units T80 to T96, never planned a campaign on his 8 empty cities.

**Grouped by cause: three holes, two of them early.**

**A. A fog-triggered wait parks the Settler where an unseen unit can take it (T17; replayed).** Code path: `civilians.rs` picks the site and orders the move; `hard_defense.rs::guard_exposed_settlers_duel` (plain Hard, `settler_safety` 0) reads the trip as threatened by `snipe_fog_risk` (gene `snipe_read` 90, 11-03), finds no escort reaching it this turn, and in the `settler_escort` 2 rendezvous branch (11-02) turns the move into `SkipUnit` because `settler_capturable_this_turn` (visible units only) says the current tile is safe. The threat that triggered the wait was fog; the safety check for the wait tile ignores fog. Then on the capture turn the Settler retreats home while its escort steps onto the tile it is leaving, so they cross instead of stacking. Which earlier fix should have covered it: `snipe_read`'s "waits up to two turns for an escort" and `settler_escort` 2's "waits where nobody can take it this turn". Both did fire; both trust a visibility check against a fogged threat. The fix: a Settler made to wait for fog reasons waits in a city or on the escort's tile, or goes back, never on open ground with unseen tiles within a mounted unit's reach.

**B. The escort is reassigned when the threat read flickers, and the frozen Settler then walks alone (T96; replayed).** Horseman 89 escorted the Settler out of Piraeus on T89. On T90 the guard read trip risk 0 and fog 0 (Athens saw nothing of his), so it was "not threatened" and the Horseman was released to other work 8 tiles away. On T92 to T94 the arrival tile read exposed (risk 50), no troop stood within `escort_search_distance` 6 (nearest 7 to 8 tiles), so the 11-14 freeze fix (`a_waiting_settler_calls_an_escort...`) found nobody to call and the Settler waited 3 turns. On T95 the read flickered back to 0 and the Settler stepped onto its site, next to his Knight in fog. Fog risk was 0 because `snipe_read` remembers a surprise war for only 20 turns (`SNIPE_RECORD_TURNS`): he had sniped Athens's own Settler on T17, 78 turns earlier, and Athens had forgotten. Which earlier fixes should have covered it: the 11-14 escort call (bounded at 6 hexes), `snipe_read` (bounded at 20 turns), and `settler_escort` (escort chosen per turn, never kept). The fix candidates: keep an escort assigned to its Settler until it founds or returns, extend the escort call beyond 6 hexes for a waiting Settler, and keep a rival that sniped **us** on the sniper list for the game.

**C. Homeless Settlers of a dying empire (T158 to T180; replayed).** At War, with no legal site (`chosen None`, "not a known legal site", "border risk"), the guard has nothing to protect (`no move or found order, guard skipped`) and the Settler sits or shuttles in the open until a tank takes it. These are low value (two small cities, no swing) but they are the "tracking down every settler" he complained about: the Celts' elimination waited 7 turns on one such Settler, and the Mongols' was extended by one that founded Shangdu. Fix candidates: a Settler with no site found in a city it can reach (or joins one), and the elimination rule question for the user (section 1).

**The Swiss Settler he took from barbarians (T104)** is a fourth, unrelated loss: the Swiss Settler dithered between sites and retreats for 10 turns (T58 to T67, trip risk 100 to 350) and was taken by barbarians beside Biel.

**One hole or several?** Several. The two that cost games (A and B) share a root: **the plain-Hard Settler guard decides each turn from what it sees this turn.** It waits on a tile because no visible unit can reach it, releases an escort because no visible unit threatens it, and forgets a sniper after 20 turns. His Horseman/Knight 23 was the same unit both times, parked in fog beside Athens's settling lane, and both snipes were surprise wars declared the turn of the capture. The early one was worth a city by T24 (Constantinople, 10% of his population at T100) and cost Athens its second city for 20 turns (one city until T37).
