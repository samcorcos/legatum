## Coach's verdict

You won on turn 159, 10 VP to the Aztecs' 7, in 29 active minutes (about 11 seconds a turn, one session, about 470 orders). This game is different from the last eleven. **For the first time an AI out-built you.** The Aztecs had more cities (12 against your 10), four times your army, as much Science, and all three crowns, and they led the VP race until T115. You beat them on conversion, and then the AIs failed to stop your finish:

1. **You turned a smaller empire into permanent VP.** Your ten points were a Great Work (Hagia Sophia, T104), five era objectives in a row (T82, T111, T116, T134, T147; four of them cheap building or city-count cards), Sparta's capital (T82) and Raise the Warbands (T127). Seven of the Aztecs' points sat open on the table at the end: three crowns are held VP, and they left four cheap objectives unscored for 40 to 80 turns (a Castle in 2 more cities, a Bank in 2 more cities, a University in 4 more cities, or 1 city taken from you once you led).
2. **Nobody touched your warband camps.** Your hold rested on 2 VP from camps, and your own cities had shot their guards, so they stood empty. Four AIs were at War with you. One camp was cleared in 32 turns (the Mongols, T149, which cost you one hold turn). With the AIs' camp-clearing pass moved to the front of their turn, your own orders (with every re-call you would make) win 0 of 32 from T130, T140 and T148, against 32 of 32 as played.
3. **The Aztec war was a shooting gallery.** For 50 turns (T83–T133) the Aztecs walked 15 units one at a time into your city fire and Ambusher-line archers. They hit your cities twice and never killed a unit of yours.

**Is it the Celts or the AI?** Mostly the AI. The Celtic kit gave you the closing 2 VP and a flank that no barbarian ever threatened. The rework did not make the faction strong for the AI. In 300 AI-only games with this roster the AI Celts win 18.3% (the even share is 16.7%; Mongols 20.3%, Aztecs 19.0%). In the balanced 8-empire league they win 8.5% (even share 12.5%). Barbarian immunity is the largest single piece of the kit: switching it off drops the AI Celts to 13.0%. The sack carried off 261 Gold, two camps and no technology in your game, because Sparta knew nothing you didn't. So the kit is not overtuned. But the one-way sparing has side effects the designer should see (findings 4 and 5 in section 7).

## 5. Was the win fair?

- **Skill the AI can learn:** scoring the cheapest objective of every era, timing the faction goal, fighting only where he chose, and the Settler surge to close. Each is a valuation the AI can compute from its own view.
- **AI blind spots he used:** an AI Sparta with its army away and its capital held by one Archer; attackers who arrive one at a time; nobody contesting a goal that public camps hold.
- **Rule effects:** barbarian immunity (a quiet flank) and the sack camps' raids on his rivals (findings 4 and 5).
- **Luck:** Sparta, the weakest neighbour, was next door and collapsed early. That gave him two VP and a quiet south. The Aztecs, the strongest AI, were next door to the east (Tlatelolco 5 tiles from Lugdunum), but his walls held them.
- **No exploit or rule hole in his play.**

## 6. Coach's recommendations

1. **Contest a hold with orders, not just a plan term** (ideas #44, extends #18). When a rival holds or is one point from the target and a public VP source of his can be removed in reach (a warband camp, a held Great Work tile, a goal unit), run that denial before defence and garrisons, with any unit that can reach it in 2 to 3 turns, and buy one if none can. Today the plan scores "contest the hold" at +1,000 (the Aztecs from T149) but no pass turns it into a move. `clear_barbarian_camps` runs fourth from last in `military.rs::plan_military`, with free units only, within `CAMP_RANGE` + `DENIAL_CAMP_RANGE` (12) of the AI's own city and a `WILD_APPROACH_STEPS` (8) walk (`crates/ai/src/barbarians.rs`, `goal_sparta_wild.rs`). Gates: his T130/T140/T148 tape-with-re-call branches (now 32/32), a war scenario cut from this save, and the human-seat benchmark seat-only and opponents-only (expect a small move: few benchmark games end on a Celtic hold, but the same pass covers Byzantium's Heir and held Great Works).
2. **Score the cheapest open card** (extends #26 and #34). The Aztecs left Castle ×2, Bank ×2, University ×4 and "Take 1 city from the VP leader" open for 40 to 80 turns at 6 to 7 VP, while chasing territory counts. Rank every open card of every entered era by VP per turn of effort, counting what is already built (`crates/ai/src/crowns.rs::objective_effort`, the chosen objective in `production.rs`). The objective genes did not change the choice. Gates: the new Scenario Lab `aztecs-three-crowns` (Hard gains a median score of 770, about 0.7 VP), AI-only objective counts per era, and the benchmark.
3. **Attack together or not at all against a walled ranged defence** (extends #42). About 15 Aztec units died one at a time at his walls with no return damage. A unit should not enter city range unless the force that can take the city arrives the same turn, or it can retreat out of range after firing (`military.rs` staging, `city_assault.rs::coordinate_city_assaults`, `tactics.rs` exposure). Gates: the war-scenario suite, the Scenario Lab `byzantium-celtic-walls` (Hard 0/50), and the benchmark.

Also: Hard Celts can't finish a Wild plan. It calls only at 70% of the game or when 2 VP cross the target, and it won't found while it wants the goal (`goal_actions.rs`, `wild_allows_settling`). From his T127 position Hard wins 12 to 16% even with the call forced. A faction-AI fix, lower priority than 1 to 3.

**Design and rule questions for the designer (not AI fixes)**

- The sack camps and the one-way sparing make a free army against rivals. The camp beside Sparta's last city raided it (T84), and two Mongol Settlers were captured next to his camps (T90, T126). Sparta's Settler sat in Gytheio for 31 turns. Is a camp that raids everyone but the Celts the intended drawback, or a hidden strength?
- The technology steal fired 0 times in 2 sacks here, and 0.41 times per game for the AI Celts (12% of sacks). The empire you can sack is usually behind you, so "a technology the victim knows and you could research now" is usually empty. Is the steal meant to matter?
- Bands: the only one left is diffusion, and it was a wash. If the designer wants a brake on a runaway he cannot see, it isn't there now. That is fine while the AI is the brake.

## 7. Bugs, rule holes and odd behaviour, ranked

1. **Nobody contests a Celtic hold.** At T159, 10 of his 10 goal camps were unguarded, with four AIs at War with him. There was one clear in 32 turns of goal (Mongols, T149). The Aztec plan read "contest the hold" at +200 to +1,000 from T149 to T157 while its 16 units stayed 4 to 6 tiles away. (Recommendation 1.)
2. **His own cities shot his own camp guards.** There were 57 Celtic city shots and 28 unit attacks at barbarians, and 29 barbarians killed, 18 of them in the two turns after his first call (T128–T129). This is the known auto-fire bug (`claude/celts-barb-peace`). The other consequence: his goal camps stood empty from about T128, so the Celts' own goal becomes trivially clearable. The AIs didn't notice (finding 1), but a human rival would. His units also take free XP from the camp guards that respawn.
3. **Two of his cities sat at 0 HP and nobody took them.** Celts Colony 7 on T92 (a Mongol Cataphract at 11 HP beside it) and Celts Colony 10 on T153, during his hold (one Mongol Cavalry at 16 HP). In both, one Celtic Marksman inside survived. The Mongols had the "Take 1 city from the VP leader" card open at T153.
4. **Sack camps raid only his rivals.** Camps from his sacks raided Sparta (T84) and Byzantium (T79, T139) and captured Mongol Settlers (T90 at (9,20), T126 at (11,18)). 6. **The Aztecs proposed Peace to him while their own plan was Contain.** Plan Peace on T129, Contain +369 on T130, their own Peace proposal on T133 (he accepted), a new War on T147. This is the Peace flip pattern again.
7. **Sparta's war machine pointed at nothing.** A value-0 "forced expedition before local siege forecast" ran T37–T86 against Emain Macha, swapping 4 times with Mongol Bukhara, while its capital fell to 1 to 2 archers. ## 4c. Decision diff (summary)
