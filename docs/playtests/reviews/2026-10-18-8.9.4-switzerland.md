## Coach's verdict

This was a real race, and you were behind for most of it:
- **Byzantium led the VP race from turn 99 to turn 201.** It sat on 8 of 10 from T186 and on 9 from T216.
- **You first led outright on turn 237,** the turn your hold began. You won 11 to 9.
- **Pillar 11 held this time:** at T100 you had 0 VP, and nobody could have called the winner before about T225. That's the big change from 8.9.2, where you led from T74.

The war AI, though, didn't get better against you:
- **Five AIs were at war with you at once from T171 to T193,** and four or five most of the way to the end.
- **They attacked your cities 12 times in the whole game, and not once between T171 and T225.**
- **You lost two cities, both ones you had just captured:** Almaliq the turn after you took it, and Geneva 24 turns after.
- **The Celts are the clearest case:** they had the second-strongest army and were at war with you for 76 turns. They never ran a single campaign, because the Celtic AI is coded never to campaign.

The race was close for three reasons:
- **You played a slower game:** 4 cities at T100 against 6 in 8.9.2, and the reserve goal at T202 against T78.
- **Byzantium played a strong economy and VP game:** the Heir, four era objectives, the Great Work and Most Advanced.
- **The rubber bands took 21% of your output and only 9% of Byzantium's.**

The "weird choices" you noticed are real. Each one is a war decision, and each has a code-level cause (section 4). The one change that most likely beats you is the Mongols keeping their war on you at T193 instead of making peace to go after Byzantium. From your own position, with your own orders replayed, that drops your win chance from 30 of 30 to 11 of 30, because Byzantium then takes Geneva for its fifth city and its tenth point.

## 2. Turning points

1. **T99: Byzantium's Heir.** That put Byzantium ahead, and it stayed ahead or tied for 100 turns. The Celts led briefly (T116–T130, 4–5 VP). You were on 0 VP until T131: you were building (Walls everywhere, Markets, 4 cities by T100) and buying Settlers with Gold (T80, T98, T128).
2. **T120: the Mongol attack you crushed.** The Mongols bet on a 72-turn-old picture of Lugano (pop 3, no Walls). It was really pop 7 and walled. A pricing bug made every campaign look like a near-win; see idea #24 and the `mongol-attack-t120` write-up in `docs/learnings.md`. They lost 3 of 11 units, and the war ran on, toothless, to T194.
3. **T142–T165: Japan's war became your expansion.**
   - Japan declared on you (section 4), and its campaign died in two turns.
   - You refused its three peace offers (T145, T152, T159).
   - You took Sapporo (T154) and Okayama (T165) and eliminated Japan, going from 6 cities to 9.
   - That earned the era-4 and era-5 objectives, and it made every AI name you its top threat within six turns (T160–T166: "eliminated 1, captured 2 recently").
4. **T166–T194: the five-front coalition that never attacked a city.**
   - Byzantium (T166), the Celts (T170) and Athens (T171) joined the Mongols and Sparta. Sparta had been at war with you since you answered the AI Swiss's call to arms at T159.
   - Their only lasting effect was forced spending: 1,815 Gold on four emergency units (T163–T190) kept you under 1,000 Gold until T202.
   - At T193 the Mongols retook Almaliq the turn after you took it, made peace with you, and declared on Byzantium, the game's coalition target at 8 of 10.
5. **T186–T246: Byzantium one point short.**
   - It sat at 8, then 9, for 60 turns, with no plan that could reach a tenth point (section 4).
   - You took the reserve goal (T202) and the 8-city era objective (T226).
   - At T227 you spent 2,848 Gold: the Relay Uplink for 1,050 and two Tanks for 899 each (you lost both at T232).
   - The Tanks dropped you below 1,000, so the goal came back only at T237. That made 11 VP and the hold.

## 5. Was your win fair?

Yes, and fairer than 8.9.2:
- **Skill the AI can learn:** Gold as a standing defence, conquest when a neighbour offers it, a Gold-bought Great Work, and peace when it's free.
- **AI blind spots, not rules holes:** the idle Celtic army, the value-0 Spartan war, Japan's and the AI Swiss's suicidal second fronts, Athens's peace in your hold, Byzantium's one-step VP planning.
- **Luck:**
  - Your first conquest target (Japan) declared on you.
  - Byzantium, your real rival, has thin levies and couldn't conquer its fifth city.
  - The one strong conqueror (Sparta) spent T148–T224 eating the AI Swiss on the far side.
- **One rules note for you:** Byzantium's thin levies cap it at 5 armed units with 4 cities. Its Bombers and Fighters aren't counted (aircraft aren't map units), and it built five. That may be intended; it's the one place its army grew.

## 6. Coach's recommendations for the AI

Ranked by expected effect on how often you win on Hard. All of them use public information only (pillar 6).

1. **No war plan without a way to fight it: let the Celts raid, and stop value-0 declarations.**
   - *Mechanism:*
     - Let Celts run sack campaigns. Value each target by its sack Gold (for a Swiss city, its share of the public treasury, which can break the reserve goal) plus the VP denied, and let a Celtic Contain war stage and open a front like any other.
     - Where a faction truly can't campaign, give its Contain plan no "leader's race" value.
     - Remove the value-0 `sparta_force_campaign` declaration, or require a funded forecast before it declares.
   - *Code:* `crates/ai/src/plan.rs` (`campaign_candidates` Celts return, `contain_war`, forced expedition ~1610–1700), `crates/ai/src/military.rs` (`open_front`, staging ~2655), `crates/ai/src/campaign.rs` (Celts exits), `crates/ai/src/opportunity.rs::sack_gold_value`.
   - *Evidence:* the Celts at war for 76 turns with a 561 army and 0 campaigns; Sparta's 46-turn value-0 war.
   - *Measure:* a war scenario from this game's T170 state (Celts against the Swiss hoard); the human-seat benchmark, seat-only and opponents-only; this review's T166 branches with the tape seat.
2. **Price peace by what it frees the enemy to do, and never make peace with a team in its hold.**
   - *Mechanism:*
     - A Peace plan with an enemy at war carries the value that enemy gains from it: its army freed against our partners, the VP race it can then run. That's the 8.8.1 asymmetry again.
     - The leader's victory hold is a hard veto on proposing or accepting peace with that team.
     - Turn on and remeasure `peace_retaliation`, which already exists. The AI Swiss at T208 and Japan at T151 opened second fronts because Peace carried no cost of being conquered.
   - *Code:* `crates/ai/src/plan.rs` (peace candidate terms, `leader_peace_forfeit`), `crates/ai/src/diplomacy.rs` (acceptance), `params.rs::peace_retaliation`.
   - *Evidence:* no peace from T186: 100% → 20%; the Mongols alone: 100% → 37%; Athens's peace in your hold.
   - *Measure:* the two branches above (tape seat), the war suite, and the benchmark opponents-only.
3. **A VP planner that looks two steps ahead, for an empire one or two points short.**
   - *Mechanism:* when the cheapest remaining VP needs a precondition (a fifth city for Walls in 5 cities, 8 cities for the Future objective), cost the precondition: capture the softest reachable city (unwalled, damaged, public), settle any legal site, or hire an army with Byzantium's Gold. Feed that target into `conquest.rs` with the VP as its prize.
   - *Code:* `crates/ai/src/crowns.rs` (`objective_effort`, `chosen_objective`, `objective_capture_value`), `endgame_push`, `crates/ai/src/conquest.rs`, `crates/ai/src/goal_actions.rs` (hire).
   - *Evidence:* Byzantium at 9 of 10 for 30 turns. In the branches where it got Geneva it won 19–23 of 30.
   - *Measure:* the new Scenario Lab cut `byzantium-last-point` (Hard meets it 14% of the time); the T186 tape branches.

**Also worth fixing (smaller):**
- Price a public Swiss treasury as defenders that can be bought at the door. `price_bought_walls` already does this for Walls (`opportunity.rs::forecast_siege`). That's Japan at T142.
- Fix the campaign-pricing bug (#24d) before tuning any of the above: every capture value in this game's traces (2,000 to 3,500) carries it.

**Design and rule questions for you, not AI fixes:**
- **The coalition target and the AIs' threat read disagreed for 30 turns.** The game named Byzantium, the strict VP leader. The AIs went after you, the conqueror. When the Mongols followed the game's target at T193, it saved you. Should the AI follow the formal coalition target, its own threat read, or both? In this game, its own read was the better bet.
- **Byzantium's thin levies cap it at 5 armed units with 4 cities, but aircraft are exempt.** That's intended or not.
- **This time decadence hit the leader 2.5 times as hard as the challengers** (21% against 9%). The 8.9.2 concern, that it taxes number 2 as hard as number 1, didn't show up here.
