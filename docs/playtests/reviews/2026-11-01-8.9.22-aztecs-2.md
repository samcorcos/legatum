## Coach's verdict

You won on T153, 11 VP to 4, after 35 minutes of play. **It was the AI, not the Aztecs.**

- **Flower Wars barely mattered.** It gave you 13 population all game, 13% of your peak of 97. Switch it off from T90 and your own recorded orders still win, on the same turn (T153).
- **The New Fire was your closer, not your engine.** It gave you 2 of your 11 VP on T144 and started the hold. Without it, Hard playing your seat still wins 31 of 32 from T112, two turns later.
- **Hard playing the Aztecs doesn't snowball.** Given your seat from T1, it wins 3 of 32 and has 3.5 cities at T100 to your 9. In AI-only games the Aztecs win 17% (third of nine factions; 12.5% would be even), and 15.8% with Flower Wars switched off.

**What made it easy is what you did and what the AIs didn't do.**

- **What you did.** You expanded with a tiny modern army: 9 cities at T100 and 14 at T130, against the best AI's 6 and 7, with 3 to 6 military units all game.
- **What the AIs didn't do:**
  - Four AIs were at War with you for 56 to 128 turns each, and between them they hit your cities 13 times in 153 turns.
  - Athens, the only AI that could have won, valued its war on you at over +1,000 for 70 turns. It sent two campaigns, and both were called off within five turns as too small.
  - The Celts, who had the strongest army by T150, never went to War with you and never saw one of your cities.

**When it was decided:** between T90 and T112. Hard in your seat wins 7 of 32 from T68, 23 from T90 and 31 from T112. Athens is the AI that usually wins from the earlier points. By T100 the winner was known, which fails pillar 11.

**The in-flight AI fixes.** Of the three Codex branches, `coalition-push` is the one that would have mattered: from T90 it cuts Hard in your seat from 23 to 12 of 32. `see-the-leader` cut it to 16 (a weaker signal), and `war-era-resources` did nothing here. None of them changes anything from T112.

**Design questions for you (not AI fixes):**
- **The faction.** The data doesn't show the Aztecs too strong (17% for the AI; Flower Wars about neutral). If you want the rite to feel riskier, these options deepen drawbacks and leave the strength alone (pillar 8):
  1. **The New Fire is announced.** It is announced a few turns ahead, so rivals can strike the cities that will shrink.
  2. **The offering cities pay longer.** Cities that gave to the New Fire can't grow, or have weaker Walls, for N turns, which deepens "smaller cities after the rite".
  3. **Flower Wars hardens rivals.** An empire that loses a unit to a Flower War won't make Peace with the Aztecs for N turns, which pushes the Aztecs further into constant war.
- **Pillar 11.** The winner was clear by T100 in a game that ended at T153. With decadence gone, the only brake on a leader is AI decisions.
